Planting Density: 50 cm between rows, placing one seed every 10 cm; or 0.2 seeds every 20 cm. The recommended amount of seed for apple planting is 80 pounds.

Fertilization: Fertilization should be based on soil analysis. If no analysis is available, apply a general dose of 2 qqImz of 18-46-0 and 6 qqImz of 15-15-15 at the time of planting, and 0.5 qqImz of urea approximately 25-30 days after planting. Additional foliar fertilizer applications will increase the vegetative and reproductive vigor of the variety and should be applied preferably before flowering.

## PEST CONTROL

Weeds: Control weeds through proper soil preparation. Hand-weed at 15-20 and 25-30 days after planting; and use recommended pre- and post-emergent herbicides.

Bean Weevil: In areas with severe infestations, apply two applications of specific insecticides (at the beginning and one week after flowering).

Grain Weevils: Store in airtight silos after thoroughly drying the grain (13% moisture); fumigate if storing for long periods.

## DISEASE CONTROL

Yellow Mosaic Virus and Common Mosaic Virus: Under normal vector presence (whiteflies and aphids), these viruses do not require control due to the variety's resistance.

Bacteria and Fungi: It is recommended to use clean seed; remove diseased plants, and use recommended bactericides or fungicides.

## VARIETY ORIGIN

CENTA CHAPARRASTIQUE was introduced to El Salvador in 2005 and developed by the El Zamorano Agricultural University. It is derived from the simple cross SRC 1-12-1-47/ EAP 9510-77. ## FIELD TRIALS

In 2005, as a result of the evaluations conducted, MER 2226-41 was selected for the Central American Adaptation and Performance Trials (ECAR 2005), where it stood out for its resistance to the yellow mosaic virus and its potential yield, achieving average yields of 1897 kgh/ha (29.21 qq/ha). During 2006, this line of bean was evaluated in regional trials at the national level in nine locations in El Salvador, showing good yield potential, superior to the improved variety CENTA San Andrés, with values of 2200 kgh/ha (33.9 qq/ha). Starting in 2008, the material entered the validation phase on farms, where this material confirmed its characteristics compared to local and improved varieties. In that year, the genetic material achieved average yields of 1523.2 kgh/ha (23.45 qq/ha), exceeding the test varieties by 20.1%; and in 2009, yields of 1437.2 kg/ha (22.13 qq/ha) were obtained, exceeding the comparative varieties used that year by 412.9 kgh/ha.

Participatory evaluations with producers indicate the acceptance of this new variety due to its soft texture and reduced grain hardness for consumption.

The economic analysis conducted during validation shows a Marginal Rate of Return (MRR) of 15.73 when this variety is used.
