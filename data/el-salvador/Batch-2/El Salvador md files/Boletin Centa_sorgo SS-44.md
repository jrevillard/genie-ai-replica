The sorgo hybrid forage "CENTA SS-44" is a result of the CENTA-INTSORMIL agreement, which is a collaborative program between the National Center for Agricultural and Forestry Technology (CENTA) and the United States Sorghum and Millet Research Institute (INTSORMIL), signed on November 29, 1999, at the Ministry of Agriculture and Livestock. Through this agreement, technicians from the CENTA sorghum program have been supported by scientists from various universities in the United States in research, in order to generate technology that benefits livestock producers and, at the same time, consumers of meat and milk.

To generate this hybrid, CENTA has used the most advanced conventional genetic improvement technology. In this way, it was possible to cross a sorghum (Sorghum bicolor) and a Sudan grass (Sorghum sudanense) to accumulate genes for increased protein content. As a result, this new forage sorghum has the same protein content as a legume and, consequently, can increase milk and meat production in livestock.

In this opportunity, CENTA is pleased to release this new forage sorghum to provide livestock producers with a more nutritious and efficient feed, which will increase the production and productivity of their livestock. For consumers, as a result of this innovation in the production chain, it will be available to them more milk and meat to improve nutrition.

## I. INTRODUCTION

One of the main problems in livestock operations in El Salvador is the lack of good quality forage, which translates into a decrease in milk and meat production and increases production costs by seeking alternatives to feed livestock.

Many livestock farmers, in their search for feed to meet the needs of their herds, use sorghum or corn hybrids without knowing their behavior, which sometimes results in losses. In the case of sorghum, the common problem in livestock operations is that they do not have varieties that are early, with rapid growth, good plant height, resistant to diseases, and high potential for yield and green matter quality. In this situation, the generation of the CENTA SS-44 forage sorghum hybrid is a forage option to increase milk production.

The CENTA SS-44 forage sorghum hybrid should be supplied as green feed or hay. It does not have any potential for grain production.

## II. ORIGIN OF THE HYBRID

The CENTA SS-44 forage sorghum hybrid is the result of a specific intercrossing between sorghum and Sudan grass, research carried out by the National Center for Agricultural and Forestry Technology (CENTA) with the support of the International Sorghum and Millet Research Institute (INTSORMIL).

After 5 years of work, the CENTA SS-44 forage sorghum hybrid was selected from many hybrids generated by CENTA for its adaptation and forage quality (Deras 2002). It was validated at the national level in medium-sized farms where feed is provided with grass.

The validation was carried out using a commercial hybrid with the same characteristics as a witness. The results of the validation (Table 1) show that the CENTA SS-44 sorghum was superior in yield, quality, adaptability, tolerance to pests and diseases compared to the witness.

- Abundant foliage; sweet; juicy and palatable.
- Good recovery after each cutting
- Fast growth (5 cm/day)
- Good tillering
- Good dry matter yield
- High in protein
- Low cyanide content (60 cm or more)
- Tolerant to the Mildiú Lanoso disease and other foliar diseases.

## VI. CULTIVATION

The soils suitable for establishing a CENTA SS-44 sorghum crop are those that have good drainage and a franco-clayey texture; good water retention, level or slightly level; with a neutral or moderately acidic pH.

## 2. Planting

The best time to plant is from the third week of May, which allows for a greater number of cuts (up to 5), taking advantage of the rainy season and the regrowth ability of the material: If irrigation is available, it can be planted during the dry season, which will allow for a greater number of cuts.

The CENTA SS-44 is affected by the light period, so that when planted on short days (November to February), the plant height, flowering time, vigor and yield potential are reduced.

To ensure good crop development, it is necessary to have proper soil preparation and timely weed and pest control.

It is not recommended to plant the advanced generations (second planting) of this material, as it can segregate into sorghum-weeds.

## 3. Planting spacing.

The amount of seed to use per acre is 25-30 pounds.

When planted in rows, the options are: a) broadcast followed by, leaving 25-30 seeds per linear meter and a spacing of 60-70 cm between rows; b) in patches (separated by 20-25 cm), 5-6 seeds per patch in rows with a spacing of 60-70 cm between them; if desired, thinning can be done 15 days after planting, leaving 15-18 plants per linear meter.

## 4. Fertilization

Before making any fertilization plan, it is recommended to have a soil analysis and fertilize based on those results. In general, it is suggested to apply at planting or 8 days after emergence, 3 tons of Formula 16-20-0; the second fertilization at 25 days after emergence with 3 tons of Ammonium Sulfate per acre and 8 days after each cut apply 3 tons of Ammonium Sulfate per acre or 2 tons of Urea.

## VII. HARVEST

Sorghum should be harvested before the plant reaches the flowering stage. The cutting should be made at a height of 10 cm from the ground to encourage regrowth. Generally, this sorghum should not be harvested when it is less than 60 cm tall, as the concentrations of cyanogenic acid and nitrates are higher, making it toxic to animals. However, if harvested at less than 60 cm, the forage should be left in the field, exposed to the sun for 5 hours, to allow the release of cyanogenic acid and nitrates.

<!-- image -->

Also, grazing should not be allowed after a prolonged drought that has affected the growth of the crop. It is necessary to wait for the plant to recover normal growth before feeding the animals.

The first cutting is made 47 days after planting. For subsequent cuttings, observe the flowering stage of the plant, which occurs 48 days after the last cutting.

The forage produced from 5 cuts of the CENTA SS-44 sorghum variety yields 150 tons, providing forage to feed 10-12 adult animals for a year. If the crop was established during the dry season with irrigation, it has the advantage of obtaining a greater number of cuts.

Basic grains have enormous importance for nutrition and food security. The National Center for Agricultural, Livestock, and Forestry Technology (CENTA) pursues this through the Basic Grains Program, aiming to meet the national need with domestic production, in order to maintain a high degree of self-sufficiency in the country.

Currently, there are sorghum varieties and hybrids with excellent yields, intended for forage production, offering alternatives for dairy cattle feeding.
