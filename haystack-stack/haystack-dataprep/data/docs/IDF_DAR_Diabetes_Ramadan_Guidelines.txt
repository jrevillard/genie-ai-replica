IDF-DAR Practical Guidelines — Diabetes Management During Ramadan
Source: International Diabetes Federation — Diabetes and Ramadan Alliance (IDF-DAR) Practical Guidelines 2021
Updated with DaR-ADA-IDF Ramadan Guidelines 2026
Reference: https://idf.org/media/uploads/2024/07/IDF_DaR_Practical_Guidelines_Ramadan.pdf

1. RISK STRATIFICATION FOR FASTING DURING RAMADAN

LOW RISK (0-3 points on IDF-DAR risk score):
- Well-controlled Type 2 diabetes on lifestyle or low-risk medications (metformin, DPP-4 inhibitors)
- No history of hypoglycemia
- No complications
- Fasting is probably safe with medical evaluation, medication adjustment, and strict monitoring

MODERATE RISK (3.5-6 points):
- Type 2 diabetes on sulfonylureas or multiple medications
- Safety uncertain; requires evaluation, adjustment, and monitoring
- Fasting is permitted but patients may decline after consulting physicians

HIGH RISK (>6 points — fasting is probably unsafe, advise against fasting):
- Severe hypoglycemia within 3 months before Ramadan
- Recurrent hypoglycemia or hypoglycemia unawareness
- Type 1 diabetes in adolescents/young adults
- Unstable cardiovascular disease or stroke
- Chronic kidney disease stage 4-5
- On dialysis or post-kidney transplant
- Multiple antidiabetic medications (4 or more drugs, or 3+ with specific high-risk combinations)
- Pregnancy with diabetes
- Poorly controlled diabetes (HbA1c >9%)

2. PRE-RAMADAN ASSESSMENT (6-8 weeks before Ramadan, maximum 12 weeks)

Assess:
- Type and duration of diabetes
- Current medication regimen and glycemic control (HbA1c)
- Hypoglycemia history and awareness
- Self-management capability (can patient check own glucose?)
- Diabetes complications and comorbidities
- Recent severe illness
- Renal function (eGFR)
- Access to glucose monitoring

Educate patient on:
- Risk quantification and religious exemptions (Islam permits medical exemption from fasting)
- Blood glucose monitoring techniques
- Fluid and dietary guidance
- Physical activity advice during fasting
- Medication adjustments explained clearly
- Hypoglycemia/hyperglycemia recognition
- When to break the fast immediately

3. WHEN TO BREAK THE FAST — MANDATORY THRESHOLDS

BREAK FAST IMMEDIATELY if:
- Blood glucose < 70 mg/dL (3.9 mmol/L) — HYPOGLYCEMIA
- Blood glucose > 300 mg/dL (16.6 mmol/L) — HYPERGLYCEMIA
- Symptoms of hypoglycemia occur: shakiness, sweating, anxiety, trembling, rapid heartbeat, tingling around mouth, irritability, difficulty concentrating
- Symptoms of hyperglycemia occur: excessive thirst, dry mouth, tiredness, blurred vision, headache
- Symptoms of dehydration or acute illness

RECHECK if: blood glucose between 70-90 mg/dL (3.9-5.0 mmol/L) — recheck within 1 hour

4. MEDICATION ADJUSTMENTS DURING RAMADAN

METFORMIN:
- No dose reduction required (low hypoglycemia risk)
- Once daily: take at Iftar (sunset meal)
- Twice daily: take at Iftar and Suhoor (pre-dawn meal)
- Three times daily: morning dose moved to Suhoor; combined evening dose at Iftar
- Prolonged release formulation: take at Iftar

SULFONYLUREAS (use 2nd generation — gliclazide or glimepiride preferred):
- Once daily with good control: reduce dose, take at Iftar
- Twice daily with good control: Iftar dose unchanged, Suhoor dose reduced
- AVOID older agents (glibenclamide) — higher hypoglycemia risk
- Use gliclazide, gliclazide MR, or glimepiride instead

DPP-4 INHIBITORS (sitagliptin, vildagliptin):
- No dose modification required
- Once daily: take at Iftar
- Twice daily (vildagliptin): take at Iftar and Suhoor

SGLT2 INHIBITORS (empagliflozin, dapagliflozin):
- No dose modification required
- Take at Iftar
- Must be started 2-4 weeks before Ramadan for blood glucose to stabilize
- Monitor for dehydration during fasting

GLP-1 RECEPTOR AGONISTS (liraglutide, semaglutide):
- Low hypoglycemia risk when used alone
- Dose must be titrated 2-4 weeks before Ramadan
- Higher risk if combined with insulin or sulfonylureas

BASAL INSULIN (Glargine, Degludec, Detemir):
- Reduce dose by 30-40%
- Take at Iftar
- Monitor with 5-7 point glucose checks

RAPID-ACTING INSULIN (Lispro, Aspart):
- Suhoor dose: reduce by 30-50%
- Skip pre-lunch dose entirely (patient is fasting)
- Iftar dose: adjust based on 2-hour post-Iftar blood glucose

TWICE-DAILY NPH/REGULAR INSULIN:
- NPH at Iftar: no modification
- NPH at Suhoor: reduce by 50%
- Regular at Suhoor: reduce by 50%
- Regular at Iftar: unchanged (adjust per post-Iftar glucose)

INSULIN + SULFONYLUREAS COMBINATION:
- Highest risk combination: reduce insulin dose by 25-50%
- Consider switching sulfonylurea to DPP-4 inhibitor if possible

SMBG-GUIDED INSULIN TITRATION:
- BG < 70 mg/dL or symptoms: reduce insulin by 4 units
- BG < 90 mg/dL: reduce by 2 units
- BG 90-126 mg/dL: no change
- BG > 126 mg/dL: increase by 2 units
- BG > 200 mg/dL: increase by 4 units

5. BLOOD GLUCOSE MONITORING SCHEDULE

HIGH-RISK PATIENTS (7-point monitoring):
1. When fasting begins (after Suhoor)
2. Post-breakfast (before fasting)
3. Pre-lunch (mid-fast)
4. Post-lunch (mid-fast)
5. Pre-dinner (before Iftar)
6. Post-dinner (after Iftar)
7. Midnight
Plus: whenever symptoms occur or patient feels unwell

MODERATE-TO-LOW RISK: minimum 1-2 daily readings; 5-point monitoring preferred

6. NUTRITION GUIDANCE DURING RAMADAN

MEAL DISTRIBUTION:
- Suhoor (pre-dawn): 30-40% of daily calories, 3-5 carbohydrate exchanges
- Iftar snack: 10-20% of calories, 1-2 carbohydrate exchanges
- Iftar main meal: 40-50% of calories, 3-6 carbohydrate exchanges
- Healthy snack (if needed): 10-20%, 1-2 exchanges
(1 carbohydrate exchange = 15 grams carbohydrate)

MACRONUTRIENT TARGETS:
- Carbohydrate: ≤130 g/day (40-45% of calories) — low glycemic index preferred
- Protein: ≥1.2 g/kg body weight (20-30% of calories)
- Fat: 30-35% of calories, saturated fat <7%
- Fiber: 20-35 g/day
- Cholesterol: <300 mg/day

SUHOOR recommendations:
- Eat as late as possible (close to Fajr)
- Include fiber-rich foods for sustained energy (whole grains, beans, vegetables)
- Include protein (eggs, beans, yogurt)
- Drink water adequately
- Avoid salty foods (increase thirst during fasting)

IFTAR recommendations:
- Start with dates and water (traditional, low glycemic)
- Avoid high-carbohydrate calorie-dense meals
- Avoid fried foods and sugary desserts
- Include vegetables and lean protein
- Drink 2-3 liters of water between Iftar and Suhoor

GAMBIAN CONTEXT:
- Suhoor: chere (millet porridge) with milk + banana — good sustained energy
- Iftar: dates + water, then light domoda with vegetables, reduce white rice
- Avoid: heavy benachin with excess oil at Iftar, sweet attaya with 4+ spoons sugar
- Encourage: bouye (baobab) drink, bissap (unsweetened), moringa in stew

7. PHYSICAL ACTIVITY DURING RAMADAN

- Continue normal physical activity but avoid rigorous exercise during fasting hours
- Taraweeh prayers (bowing, kneeling, rising) count as daily exercise
- Avoid heavy activity in last few hours of fasting (highest hypoglycemia risk)
- Best time for exercise: 1-2 hours after Iftar
- If exercising during fast: carry glucose tablets/sugar

8. SPECIAL POPULATIONS

PREGNANT WOMEN WITH DIABETES:
- Blood glucose targets: fasting 70-95 mg/dL, post-meal <120 mg/dL
- Break fast if: BG <70, reduced fetal movement, feeling unwell
- Insulin and metformin are treatment of choice
- Avoid glibenclamide during Ramadan
- 7-point glucose monitoring

ELDERLY (≥65 years):
- Higher risk of hypoglycemia and emergency visits
- Use medications with lower hypo risk (avoid glibenclamide)
- Increase SMBG frequency
- Ensure caregiver support for medication management
- Individualized diet and activity plan

9. HOSPITALIZATION RISK DATA
- Type 1 diabetes patients: 4.7-fold increase in severe hypoglycemia requiring hospitalization during Ramadan
- Type 2 diabetes patients: 7.5-fold increase in severe hyperglycemia requiring hospitalization
- Pre-Ramadan assessment and medication adjustment significantly reduces these risks
