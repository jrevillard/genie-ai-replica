## The Asphalt Stain Disease in Corn Cultivation

The asphalt stain disease, which has been prevalent since the 1990s, is a major limiting factor in corn production in humid, sub-humid, and transitional zones in various regions of Mexico and Central America, primarily in areas with high relative humidity and altitudes ranging from 1300 to 2300 meters above sea level. The impact of the corn asphalt stain disease causes significant yield losses and deteriorates forage quality.

## What is the corn asphalt stain disease?

Figure 1. Asphalt stain on corn. Photo: CIMMYT.

It is a disease caused by the synergistic interaction of three fungi: *Phyllachora maydis*, *Monographella maydis*, and *Coniothyrium phyllachorae*. These fungi feed on the plant's sugars, causing the leaves to die and eventually the entire plant. The disease has been observed in Mexico to varying degrees, primarily in the states of Jalisco, Nayarit, Michoacán, Veracruz, Guerrero, Chiapas, and Oaxaca. It is also widely distributed in Central American countries such as Guatemala, El Salvador, and Costa Rica.

## Symptoms and stages of the disease

At the early stages of the disease, the plant exhibits small black spots on the leaves. Around these spots, a yellow halo appears. As the infection progresses, this halo expands, grows, and eventually covers the entire leaf, causing wilting and ultimately the death of the plant. If the disease appears in the early stages before the ears of corn are fully developed, the ears lose weight, and the kernels become loose and underdeveloped, often germinating prematurely even while still on the stalk.

Generally, the disease tends to appear after flowering. However, under continuous rainfall conditions, it can occur before flowering, causing losses ranging from 30 to 100%. When the infection is severe during tasseling or flowering, the loss of grain yield can be total. If the disease occurs after flowering, the yield can decrease by 50% due primarily to the fact that the kernels do not complete their development and have a lower specific weight (Marino et al., 2008).

## Integrated management of the disease

Effective control of the corn asphalt stain disease is achieved through an integrated management approach that includes monitoring, appropriate agronomic practices, and the use of resistant varieties. The following recommendations are for managing the disease:

- The International Maize and Wheat Improvement Center (CIMMYT, 2012) recommends crop rotation and the incorporation of green manure in areas where the disease has been observed to prevent the presence of the causal agents in the next cropping cycle. Incorporating crop residue reduces the inoculum load due to the presence of beneficial microorganisms in the soil.
- Use the recommended planting density for each region, as higher densities favor the development of the disease.
- Planting at an early date and avoiding staggered plantings are practices that help to prevent the incidence of the disease.
- Use the recommended fertilizer doses, especially for nitrogen.
- For chemical control, it is necessary to manage fungicide groups, which helps to prevent pathogens from developing resistance. In areas where the disease is recurrent, it is recommended to apply a fungicide starting 35 days after planting or when the first symptoms appear and the environmental conditions are favorable for the increase in the incidence of the disease. Fungicides that have been reported to be effective against the disease include propiconazole, carbendazim, benomil, a mixture of poxiconazole+carbendazim, and benomil (González, 2005, as cited by CIMMYT, 2012).
- The use of tolerant or resistant varieties helps to reduce damage. Currently, various research institutions have developed hybrid varieties that are resistant to the disease.
