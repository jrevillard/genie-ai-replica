Biochar (from the English term, derived from "bio charcoal," also known as "charcoal" in Spanish) is the name given to charcoal when it is used as a soil amendment. In other words, it is processed plant biomass through pyrolysis. It is a technology that dates back 3,500 years -

The origin of biochar: Amazonian black earth (Terra Preta de Indio)

## Super Soils

Terra preta is characterized by the presence of low-temperature charcoal in high concentrations; large amounts of ceramic shards; organic matter such as plant residues, animal feces, fish bones and other materials; and nutrients such as nitrogen (N), phosphorus (P), calcium (Ca), and zinc (Zn).

It also exhibits high levels of microbial activity and other specific characteristics within its particular ecosystem. It is less prone to nutrient leaching, which is a major problem in most tropical rainforests.

## BIOCHAR VS: CHARCOAL VS: ACTIVATED CHARCOAL: {WHAT ARE THEY AND HOW DO THEY WORK?

<!-- image -->

<!-- image -->

Biochar is designed for agricultural use, and is typically applied as a soil amendment, which is defined as any material added to the soil to improve its physical properties, such as water and nutrient retention.

## Charcoal

- Charcoal is also a carbon-rich solid derived from biomass in a similar way.
- Charcoal is typically used for heating or cooking, and is commonly associated with barbecues.

## Activated Charcoal

- Activated charcoal is a carbon-rich solid derived from biomass and other carbonaceous materials such as charcoal or tar, through pyrolysis.
- In the process, a carbon material is also activated through processes that greatly increase the surface area of the material, allowing it to capture (or "adsorb") a greater number of molecules.
- This high adsorption capacity makes activated charcoal effective for removing contaminants from water and air, which is why activated charcoal is typically used in remediation or purification projects.

## Biochar Application

## (A Mixed with soil in the entire field

For best results, thoroughly mix biochar with soil during ploughing or 1 week before planting. A depth of 30cm is highly recommended. Use 200-400 g/m².

<!-- image -->

<!-- image -->

## Trees, Coffee, etc.

- For tree establishment: directly add to the planting hole - same as (B)
- For topdressing: create a 30cm trench along the drip line of the tree and apply the biochar into the trench.

It is better to combine biochar with compost.

<!-- image -->

## (B Planting hole - before transplanting

Mix biochar and basal fertilizer and apply to planting holes. Cover with a thin layer of soil, then transplant. Use 80-100g/hole.

<!-- image -->

Recommended for plants with long roots: tomatoes, watermelon, cassava, pumpkins, eggplants, etc.

<!-- image -->

## Flowers

- When applying with basal mix, lightly cover with soil. Same as (B). Use 200-400g/plant.
- For topdressing, choose 2 to 3 points in the planter and apply only the biochar to the soil. Use 80-100g/plant.

Soils lacking biological matter and organic matter are poor and have inadequate physical characteristics for plant growth.

When biochar is added to the soil, it can improve crop yields. For farmers, biochar represents a unique opportunity to improve soils using locally available materials.

<!-- image -->

BIOCHAR is an eco-friendly, solid, stable form of carbon that can correct and improve acidic and low-fertility soils. It returns biology to the soil! BIOCHAR significantly increases crop yields and biomass development. BIOCHAR stores more than 3 times its weight in CO2 from the atmosphere, contributing to a net reduction in greenhouse gases.
