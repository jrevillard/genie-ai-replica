## Design of agroforestry systems: a sustainable alternative for agriculture in the state of Guerrero, Mexico

Cadena-Zamudio, Daniel A. 1* ; Cadena-Iñiguez, Pedro 2 ; Arispe-Vázquez, José L. 1 ; Noriega-Cantú, David H. 1

- 1 Experimental Field Iguala-INIFAP. Carretera Iguala-Tuxpan Km 2.5, C.P. 40000, Iguala de la Independencia, Guerrero.
- 2 Experimental Field Centro de Chiapas-INIFAP. Carretera Ocozocoautla-Cintalapa Km 3.0, C.P. 29140, Ocozocoautla de Espinosa, Chiapas.

## Problem

The state of Guerrero, Mexico, is one of the states with the highest levels of social marginalization and poverty at the national level. This means that 64.4% of the population lives in poverty, and 27.8% suffer from food insecurity. In this region, economic activities have declined as a result of the loss of natural resources, which has affected the yields of staple crops, increasing food prices and reducing the quality of life for families. This has led to malnutrition, poverty, migration, and abandonment of land, severely impacting the next generation. In light of this situation, it is essential to develop models and proposals for the appropriate use and management of natural resources, such as agroforestry systems that integrate key species in association with other ecologically and commercially valuable species as a sustainable way to produce food and reduce dependence on monocultures. This is to break the seasonal income cycle and improve the living standards of rural communities.

## Proposed solution

Two methodologies are proposed for agroforestry systems: Model 1 integrates cultivation in alleys, whose main function is to increase the productivity of a cultivated species and associate it with nitrogen-fixing species that incorporate organic matter (green manure and leaf litter), promoting the uptake of nutrients such as phosphorus (P), potassium (K), calcium (Ca), and magnesium (Mg). Model 2, known as the "Taungya" system, consists of establishing agricultural crops during the early years of a forest plantation. Its main function is to produce ordinary or medium-sized timber, associated with transitional crops for self-consumption and commercialization during the initial stages of tree development.

These innovative agroforestry models offer both a new way of producing and commercializing food, making self-employment sustainable and improving the living standards of rural communities by generating income from various crops throughout the year.

## Involved species

Maize (Zea mays L.) is the most important staple crop in Mexico. In the state of Guerrero, 439,281 hectares are cultivated under temporary conditions with an average yield of 3.037 t/ha in 2017. Maize is of great social, cultural, and nutritional importance in the national diet.

Trees of commercially valuable timber species, such as "Red Cedar" (Cedrela odorata L.), "Lináloe" (Bursera linanoe (La Llave) Rzed., Calderón & Medina), and "Mahogany" (Swietenia macrophylla King). The presence of associated trees increases organic agriculture, prevents soil erosion, and provides organic matter and nutrients for the soil.

Species from the Fabaceae family, such as Leucaena macrophylla Benth., allow for crops with high nitrogen content (100-600 kg N/ha/year), benefiting the associated crop and reducing costs. In addition, it provides firewood and biomass for potential use as forage due to its high nutritional quality and rapid decomposition.

Nopales and nopales (Opuntia ficus-indica L.; Nopalea sp.) are extremely profitable crops due to their ease of cultivation at high densities (up to 160,000 plants/ha), with a production of 15 nopales per square meter per weekly harvest in a domestic production network. In a study conducted in Ayoquezco, Oaxaca, the production reached a competitive level, maintaining an exportable supply equivalent to 1050 boxes of 40 pounds each per week.

Jamaica (Hibiscus sabdariffa L.) is a very important agricultural crop in Mexico (the seventh largest producer in the world). The state of Guerrero is the first producer with 13,913 hectares and more than 3,000 tons of dried calyxes per year. The municipalities of Costa Chica and the Montaña account for approximately 65% of the national production of Jamaica.

Achiote (Bixa orellana L.) is an economically viable option for many indigenous communities in the southeast of Mexico, such as Guerrero, Chiapas, Oaxaca, and the Yucatán Peninsula. It is used to produce food coloring and flavoring, which is sold locally, regionally, and nationally.

## Agroforestry Model 1

The perimeter of the agroforestry system is formed by the tree species cedar and Lináloe, which, due to their closed planting arrangement at 4 x 4 meters, retain soil erosion and nutrients.

57

500 m² plots are established with furrows of Leucaena macrophylla of 20 m in length in a density of 0.5 x 0.5 m between furrow and plot. The L. macrophylla forms the alleys in which the maize plot is sown at 0.80 x 0.20 m. Each seed is inoculated with biofertilizer (Azospirillum brasilense) to reduce the proportion of chemical fertilizers and reduce costs and improve sustainability compared to conventional agriculture (Figure 1).

## Agroforestry Model 2

The "Taungya" system consists of producing timber, so the agroforestry design will be composed of tree species such as cedar and mahogany at the perimeter of the system at 4 x 4 meters. Within the

plot, furrows of Jamaica (H. sabdariffa) and achiote (B. orellana) will be established at a distance of 1.20 to 1.80 m between plants (Figure 3). This allows the producer to obtain products for their own consumption or for sale as colorants based on Jamaica and achiote in the first year, and as permanent (arbusts) in the long term, while the trees benefit from management and cleaning for their subsequent sale as timber in the medium and long term, generating income that is not seasonal.

## Social benefits

These agroforestry models are available to rural producers, especially small farmers with low economic resources and in agro-climatically depressed areas of the state of Guerrero. Currently, their expansion is being planned.
