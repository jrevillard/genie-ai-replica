## Disease and Pest Control:

The golden yellow mosaic and common mosaic varieties, under normal conditions, do not require control due to the variety's resistance.

Bacteria and fungi: Use of clean seeds; removal of diseased plants; and use of recommended bactericides and fungicides.

Bean weevil: In areas with severe infestations, apply two applications of specific insecticides (at the beginning and one week after flowering). Warehouse weevils: Store in airtight silos after thoroughly drying the grain; fumigate the grain if it is stored for long periods.

## ORIGIN OF THE VARIETY

The CENTA-Nahuat bean variety was introduced to the country in 2002, within the Central American Adaptation Nursery (VIDAC), originating from the Zamorano Agricultural School (EAP). It was developed from the simple cross SRC 1-12-1 / CENTA 2000. During the research and validation phase, it was managed using the SRC 2-18-1 variety. The selection, evaluation, and validation process began in 2002 and was completed in 2007 through various studies at the San Andrés Experimental Station and in fields of farmers in different environments throughout the country.

## AGRONOMIC CHARACTERISTICS

The growth habit of CENTA-Nahuat is type IIB (indeterminate, bushy), with flowering times of 33 days, and a physiological maturity of 68-70 days. Yield potential of 1,600 kg/ha (35 qq/mz).

CENTA-Nahuat is resistant to the golden yellow mosaic and common mosaic viruses. It exhibits tolerance to fungal diseases: downy mildew, angular spot, and rust. Studies conducted at the Zamorano EAP also show that it is tolerant to the green bean weevil, bean weevil, and warehouse weevils. In addition, it is tolerant to drought and high temperatures. It adapts from 100 to 1,500 meters above sea level and can be sown in May, August, and November, in the following systems: monoculture, association, and relay with maize.

The seed is a bright, light red color, with an average of 23 pods per plant, and 6 grains per pod.

## RESEARCH AND VALIDATION

In 2002, it was selected at the San Andrés Experimental Station within the Central American Adaptation Nursery (VIDAC) for its high potential yield, red bean grain color, resistance to the golden yellow and common mosaic viruses, and tolerance to downy mildew, angular spot, and rust.

In 2003, it was incorporated into the preliminary yield trial, where it was again selected for showing the same initial performance. Based on this, it was decided that in 2004, it would be included in regional trials to be evaluated in different environments throughout the country, in fields of farmers, obtaining an average yield of all trials of 1,503 kg/ha (33 qq/mz), exceeding the CENTA San Andrés variety, whose average was 1,330 kg/ha (29 qq/mz).

In 2005, in the CENTA-Nahuat variety trial, it showed an average yield of 1,973 kg/ha (43 qq/mz), exceeding CENTA San Andrés by 183 kg/ha (4 qq/mz).

In 2006, during the national validation stage, with a total of 33 plots, the average yield was 1,276 kg/ha (28 qq/mz), exceeding local varieties by 139 kg/ha (3 qq/mz).

In 2007, in a total of 40 plots, distributed throughout the country, the average yields of CENTA-Nahuat were 1,605 kg/ha (35 qq/mz), compared to local varieties, which yielded an average of 1,109 kg/ha (24 qq/mz).

## RECOMMENDATIONS FOR CULTIVATION

## Seed:

It is recommended to use improved seed produced by CENTA or produced by recognized farmers, to ensure the use of clean seed free of pathogens and with good germination.

## Planting density:

Row spacing: 50 cm, placing one seed 10 cm apart, with 6 seeds spaced 20 cm apart. The amount of seed required to sow 1 hectare is 80 pounds.

## Fertilization:

It is recommended to fertilize based on soil analysis; if no soil analysis is available, apply 2000 kg/ha of 18-46-0 or 15-15-15 at planting, and 0.5 t of urea per hectare, approximately 25-30 days after planting. Additional application of foliar fertilizers increases the vegetative and reproductive vigor of the variety; this should be done before flowering.

## Weed control:

Control weeds through good soil preparation. Manual weeding 15-20 and 25-30 days after planting, and use recommended pre- and post-emergent herbicides.
