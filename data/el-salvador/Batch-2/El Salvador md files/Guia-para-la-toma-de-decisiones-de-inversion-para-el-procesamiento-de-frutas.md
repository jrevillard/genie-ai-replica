## Introduction

The Executive Board of the National Center for Agricultural and Forestry Technology Enrique Álvarez Córdova" (CENTA), through the Technology and Innovation Management, presents this guide to fruit producers in El Salvador, in response to their concerns and information needs regarding fruit processing. Even on a small scale, processing requires investments and efforts in marketing and administration, activities that require new skills and resources. Given that these are limited or in their initial stages, it is important to examine the alternatives and their feasibility, based on a good technical understanding of the market, the environment, and the producer's capabilities. It will undoubtedly be necessary to consult additional sources of information and seek specialists in each area, especially when considering a specific plan. The purpose of this document is to offer a basic but comprehensive method, as a first step in seeking opportunities for fruit processing entrepreneurship.

Edgardo Reyes Calderón, Executive Director

## Introduction

Fruit processing allows for its preservation, preventing losses and adding value by achieving the desired characteristics for consumers. When successful, fruit processing generates higher profitability than traditional agricultural production, which is why fruit growers and their associations are interested in this activity. However, it is not easy. Many requirements must be met for this activity to be sustainable. For example, a comprehensive plan must be developed before making investments. By "comprehensive," we mean considering various aspects: first, the commercial, organizational, and finally, the technical and economic aspects. All projects should be planned in all these areas, in an integrated manner, as the four aspects mentioned are interrelated and interdependent. A multidisciplinary approach is required from the outset. It would be a mistake to focus heavily on the technical planning or execution without considering the other aspects. Similarly, commercial planning should not be done without considering the other topics mentioned. To select and decide wisely, the biggest challenge is to find information and advice in each area and, above all, to integrate them into a global vision. This is the only way to find the best path in the search for what is most viable for fruit processing.

Regarding the organization: there are also several possibilities, for example:

- Personal business.
- Private company.
- Family business.
- Cooperative with its own administration:
- Cooperative with a contracted manager.

The owners and future managers of the fruit processing plant have their own way of interacting and making decisions. In order for the planning outcome to align with the owners' goals, it is important that the main organization of the fruit processing plant defines the key points in the planning. While it is necessary to have advisors, the owners and designated managers should be the main players; only then will the plan have their trust and motivation. Consequently, once the operation is underway, they will be responsible for the company and will have to make many more decisions.

This is presented as a guide to help fruit growers and their associations shape their ideas and intentions, and to be able to, with the help of experts in the various areas, choose from the options available and make the decision about where to invest.

Another point to consider before starting is the availability of own resources, as well as investment money or credit. Given the size of the market and the operations to be carried out, it is important to know what resources are available, as this will limit the scale and other characteristics of the business. It is necessary to estimate the availability of:

- Suitable personnel:
- Land or buildings.
- Raw materials (in quantity and time).
- Capital:
- Market and sales experience.

## 4. Methods for planning an economic activity

Planning and predicting the results of new projects is not easy, especially for small initiatives. Therefore, it is important to follow the recommendations of professional people in the field. Several organizations use their own methods and formats, for which they have published guides; some even offer training.

A recent and recognized method is the "Business Model Canvas," which helps to identify the key elements of a future business (Fig. 1). This and other methodologies can be found on the internet and are very useful and practical; some are available for free (https://businessmodelcanvas.com/download-templates/).

Fig. 1 Diagram of the Business Model Canvas

<!-- image -->

Since it is not possible to know in advance which option is the best, the search is carried out gradually and in stages. In the first stage, all aspects are considered in a general way, and the possible outcome is estimated. In the second stage, all aspects are considered again, incorporating more information and details, in order to project the possible outcome with calculations, which will be more accurate. This process can be repeated in subsequent stages, each time investigating the alternatives that arise, rejecting the least interesting ones, and determining more and more, until the necessary level of definition is achieved to make a reliable prediction of the project's results.

The word "model" refers to the description of a generic type of business that allows it to be shaped, its dimensions adjusted, and scenarios simulated to estimate how it would behave. Models from others can be used, or one can be created from scratch. Models are the intermediate step between an idea and a concrete plan (Fig. 2).

## 5. Proposed method for turning ideas into plans

With the aim of adding value to fruit production and creating business, income, and development opportunities for rural families and associations, the CENTA Agroindustry Program proposes a method that contains elements adapted from information from various sources.

Most methods recommend starting with the marketing aspect, i.e., knowing the demand and the market for the product in question, before deciding on one or the other product.

Therefore, the first step is to identify the products that customers demand and the general needs of these customers, and to see which ones can be met with the resources available in the organization. The other steps follow a logical sequence, with each step generating the inputs necessary for the following steps. The flowchart (Fig. 4) represents the six proposed steps and their respective inputs and results.

Assuming that those interested in the method are starting from scratch, the activities described here are those of the first stage, in which all possible options and ideas are considered. Even new ideas may emerge during the exercise. In the end, two or three plans will be left, to compare them, draw conclusions, and choose one or two.

The next stage can continue with the same steps, with some adaptations according to the case, and with more advanced tools and activities, which are not mentioned in this guide. For large, complex initiatives, it will be necessary to have specialists in the relevant areas.

## Step 1: Pre-selection

## Introduction

Since this is the beginning of the cycle, the goal is to gather the available information and ideas, in order to discard those that are not viable due to characteristics that do not require further verification. In this first step, the market criterion is the main one, without ignoring technical, organizational, or economic requirements. This can be considered as applying the method in a single step, in order to obtain a selection of ideas that are worth subjecting to the complete method. There should be at least three ideas, since it is not known which ones are the best at the beginning.

## Step 5: Economic analysis

## Introduction

Since the purpose and criterion of any economic activity is profitability and sustainability, it is necessary to combine the initial investments, recurring costs, and expected revenues into a single table, to calculate the probable result (annual profit or loss). Other financial costs, such as taxes, rent, interest on loans, and legal and administrative costs, should also be included. Since investments are at the beginning and revenues are later, the financial movements are projected for each year, and their value is adjusted according to the time elapsed. This allows for comparing the results of different investment options and calculating economic indicators, which help in analyzing the options and verifying whether the operational capacity can be viable.

Within the framework of a project supported by the South Korean government, a research project was carried out to identify mango products that could be produced and marketed by producers in the La Paz department. Part of the report is included below, as an example of how the method is applied, not as a suggestion for specific products. Each individual or group will need to conduct their own analysis and draw their own conclusions for their particular case. The Food Technology Laboratory, the Agroindustry Program of CENTA, and the Center for Research and Innovation in Applied Sciences (CIECA-MINED) can offer specialized technical assistance in defining formulas and producing samples. The personnel of the Ministry of Agriculture's Agricultural Business Division can provide advice to producers. The work was carried out in cooperation with the three organizations, and this can be helpful for addressing the concerns of fruit producers.

Step 1. Pre-selection of products based on the chosen market, with a technical and economic comparison, suitable for the type of mango producer, their investment capacity, cultivated areas, varieties, etc.

Due to their greater feasibility, intermediate products were prioritized, which are the basis for producing refreshing drinks or other foods for cafeterias, snack bars, and schools; healthy snacks, refreshment sales, etc.

The products prioritized in this exercise are:

- a) Dehydrated green Indian mango powder: for flavoring snacks, sorbets, or refreshing drinks.
- b) Frozen Panadés mango pieces: for making refreshing drinks with a blender, as an ingredient, or for decorating food.
- c) Frozen mature Ciruela mango pulp: for making refreshing drinks without a blender, as an ingredient, or for decorating food.
- d) Ciruela mango syrup: for flavoring desserts or making beverages.

Step 2. Prepare samples using the available equipment and packaging at CENTA and evaluate the characteristics of the products (microbiological quality, shelf life). Describe (flowcharts) the processes, define packaging.

Based on the experience of the staff and using the equipment of the Food Technology Laboratory, batches of the four products were produced, obtaining data on the capacity of the equipment, the yields in the processes, and the quality of the products obtained. This information was used to define the flowcharts of each process (Fig. 5 and 6) and the corresponding quantities for an initial commercial operation in batches of 500 pounds of mango.

The mangoes used were from trees at CENTA, and during processing, relevant characteristics (temperatures, pH, Brix, % moisture, color) were recorded. In addition, practical experience was gained with the equipment and processes, which will be useful for future trials and technical recommendations for interested parties.

Microbiological quality analysis (E. coli and Salmonella spp. counts) was carried out, which were below the permitted limit according to the Central American Technical Regulations.

The products were packaged in sealed bags or plastic containers, to store them at room temperature or frozen, as appropriate. The samples were observed for 8 months, and their shelf life reached the period without deteriorating their quality.

## Step 5. Basic Economic Analysis

Based on the results of steps 3 and 4, the total cost per unit of product was estimated at different levels of production (units per day multiplied by the number of days per season), and compared to an estimated selling price, in order to find the level where the operation would be profitable with the planned investment (break-even point).

To do this, the variable and fixed costs were calculated for various commercial operation modes, in spreadsheets that allowed visualizing and analyzing the cost components under different conditions and assumptions. Economies of scale were estimated to choose the capacity and type of equipment in order to reduce costs and ensure competitiveness.

In reality, businesses don't start at full capacity, but grow gradually, potentially incurring losses in the first few years. To determine the possibility and time required to recover investments, a matrix was created, considering the results per year, assuming gradual sales growth in the first years and a reasonably high volume above the break-even point for the following 10 years. Some additional investments were included to replace or expand equipment, and the final value of the investments (recovery value).

## 7. Final observations

The final step may coincide with the first step of the next planning cycle and the choice between the different alternatives. This is the time to draw conclusions, primarily based on market demand and economic viability. It is important to remember that the models and calculations are based on estimated values and assumed conditions, which may or may not be the case in the future. There are tools to include these risks in projections and minimize them; however, they do not disappear, they are part of any business. It is important to be aware that starting a business is a gamble, and its success cannot be guaranteed.

The conclusions and decisions should be made in a group setting, with the leaders or representatives of the organization, investors, and the people in charge of the processing plant when it is operational. This may be different in the case of a personal business, although when a single person makes a decision without consensus, it is more likely to make a mistake.

When seeking a loan or other type of financing from third parties, it will be necessary to present the business plan and the required documentation. This is another reason to plan in a formal and data-driven way.

The good news is that there are several organizations that support the development of a business plan, which follows the selection and specification of a model, and the conversion of initial interest into the decision to invest in an agro-industrial and commercialization project with processed fruit.

For technical assistance or training in processing, you can contact the Agroindustry Program.

## Annex: Explanation of the tool used (spreadsheet)

For the example reported in item 13, an Excel file with several sheets was created to help implement the methodology. It is in editable format, so it can be used for other fruits and processes; with the idea that it can also be used by other people and in other situations. The file can be downloaded from the CENTA website (https://www.centa.gob.sv/servicios/la-agroindustria/) and filling in the sheets with information and estimated values requires little skill in using spreadsheets.

The first sheet shows "<General description of the product and its marketing>" and a table for entering initial investment and annual volume values. To be used in steps 1 and 3.

The "Production" sheet includes the product formulation and its raw material prices, as well as labor, energy, and depreciation costs per batch; to obtain the production cost:

The "Total" sheet automatically projects the annual production costs and adds storage and marketing costs, as well as fixed costs such as administration, maintenance, taxes, and sales promotion (step 4).

The "Yearly" sheet combines expenses with sales revenues to obtain annual results, economic indicators, to support step 5 of the method.

The "Summary" sheet has a simple format as an example and a table for entering the results obtained (step 6).

The "Alternative Comparison" sheet is a table for systematically evaluating and prioritizing the main data from the sheets.
