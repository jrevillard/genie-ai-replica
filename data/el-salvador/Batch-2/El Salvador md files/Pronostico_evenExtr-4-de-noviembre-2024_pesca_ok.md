## High Tides

High tides will be observed on the following days: Friday, November 15th, Saturday, November 16th, Sunday, November 17th, Monday, November 18th, and Tuesday, November 19th.

High tide will occur between 2:00 AM and 5:00 AM, and 2:00 PM and 5:00 PM, flooding the upper part of the beaches, while low tide will occur between 8:00 AM and 11:00 AM, and 8:00 PM and 11:00 PM, exposing the lower part of the beaches.

High tides occur when the difference between the high and low sea levels exceeds a predetermined threshold. In Acajutla, the difference will be 2.4 m, in La Libertad 2.5 m, in El Triunfo 3.2 m, and in La Unión 3.7 m.

These tides will be observed because the moon will be closer to the Earth (perigee) on Thursday, November 14th, and aligned with the Earth and the sun (full moon) on Friday, November 15th.

## Wave and Wind Conditions at Sea

For the week of Friday, November 1st to Thursday, November 7th, 2024, along the coast of El Salvador, the expected wave and wind conditions at sea are suitable for activities such as fishing, transportation, and maritime tourism.

The waves arriving at the Salvadoran coast are generated by extratropical storms in the South Pacific, off the Antarctic coast, between Oceania and South America. The wind at sea off the coast will be influenced by the easterly flow (trade winds), which blow over the Caribbean Sea, crossing the Central American isthmus over the lakes of Nicaragua, and by the southerly flow (monsoonal winds), which shifts the Intertropical Convergence Zone (ITCZ) northward, bringing it closer to Central America.

Along the coast of El Salvador, the waves will come from the southwest at speeds of 35 to 60 kilometers per hour and with a maximum height of 1.4 to 1.7 meters, while the wind at sea will predominantly come from the southeast, with a maximum speed of 20 to 24 kilometers per hour.

Off the coast of El Salvador, typical values for wave height are 35 kilometers per hour and 1.5 meters, while for wind at sea they are 15 kilometers per hour.
