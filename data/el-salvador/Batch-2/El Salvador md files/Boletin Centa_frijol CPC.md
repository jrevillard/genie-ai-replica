## Fertilization:

It is recommended to fertilize based on soil analysis. If no such analysis is available, apply 2 qqlmz of formula 18-46-0 and 6 15-15-15 at planting, and 0.5 49 of urea at the time of planting, approximately 25-30 days after planting. Additional foliar fertilizer application increases the vegetative and reproductive vigor of the variety; this should be done before flowering.

## Weed Control:

Control weeds through proper soil preparation. Manual weeding should be done at 15-20 and 25-30 days after planting. Use recommended pre-emergence and post-emergence herbicides.

## Disease and Pest Control:

The variety is resistant to yellow mosaic virus and common mosaic virus, and does not require control due to the resistance of the variety.

## Bacteria and Fungi:

Use clean seeds, remove diseased plants, and use recommended bactericides or fungicides.

## Bean Pod Damage:

In areas with severe infestations, apply two applications of specific insecticides (at the beginning and one week after flowering).

## Warehouse Beetles:

Store in airtight silos after thoroughly drying the grain; fumigate the grain if it is stored for long periods.

## ORIGIN OF THE VARIETY

The CENTA-CPC bean variety was introduced to the country in 2002 through the Central American Adaptation Nursery (VIDAC), originating from the Pan-American School Zamorano, and was selected that same year for its high yield potential, high resistance to yellow mosaic virus and common mosaic virus, and tolerance to common rust, angular leaf spot, and common bacterial blight.

CENTA-CPC is derived from the cross Concha Rosada / SRC 1-1-18 SRC 1-2-12. During the selection phases at the San Andrés Experimental Station, and evaluation and validation in fields of farmers, it was managed with the code PPB 11-20-MC.

## AGRONOMIC CHARACTERISTICS

The growth habit of CENTA-CPC is type IIB (short, indeterminate bush) with 33 days to flowering and 68 days to physiological maturity; with an average yield of 1,600 kg/ha (35 qq/mz).

CENTA-CPC is resistant to yellow mosaic virus and common mosaic virus; tolerant to common rust, angular leaf spot, and common bacterial blight, and tolerant to drought and high temperatures. It can be grown from 100 to 1500 nsnn and can be sown in May, August, and November; in monoculture, association, and relay with maize.

The seed is a bright, light red color with an average of 23 pods per plant and 7 grains per pod.

## RESEARCH AND VALIDATION

In 2002, it was selected at the San Andrés Experimental Station within the Central American Adaptation Nursery (VIDAC) for its yield potential, bright, light red bean color, resistance to yellow mosaic virus and common mosaic virus, and tolerance to common rust, angular leaf spot, and common bacterial blight, and was evaluated the following year at the same station in the preliminary yield trial, where the same characteristics were confirmed.

In 2004, it was part of the regional adaptation and yield trials to be evaluated at the national level in fields of farmers, with an average yield of 1,452 kg/ha (32 qq/mz), exceeding the CENTA San Andrés variety, which had a yield of 1,330 kg/ha (29 qq/mz).

In 2005, it was evaluated at the national level within the variety trial, with an average yield of 1,965 kg/ha (43 qq/mz) compared to the CENTA San Andrés variety, which had an average yield of 1,790 kg/ha (39 qq/mz). In 2006, it moved to the validation stage at the national level in a total of 33 plots, with an average yield of 1,417 kg/ha (31 qq/mz), exceeding local varieties by 281 kg/ha (6 qq/mz). In 2007, it was validated again at the national level in 40 plots, with an average yield of 1,439 kg/ha (32 qq/mz), exceeding local varieties, which had a yield of 1,109 kg/ha (24 qq/mz).
