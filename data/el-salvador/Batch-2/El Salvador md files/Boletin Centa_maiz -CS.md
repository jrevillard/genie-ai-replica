## INTRODUCTION

Maize is a strategic crop due to its fundamental role in the Salvadoran population's food supply. For this reason, it has been re-established as an important pillar within the Government Plan, the "Strengthening Food Sovereignty and Security" axis, with the aim of achieving "Increased food production and employment in the agricultural sector," translating into greater economic benefits and improved nutritional quality for small and medium-sized Salvadoran farmers.

For the National Center for Agricultural, Livestock and Forestry Technology "Enrique Álvarez Córdova" (CENTA), it is of vital importance to contribute to the Government Plan's objectives. In this sense, as a result of years of research in collaboration with the International Maize and Wheat Improvement Center (CIMMYT), CENTA is now making available to the country's farmers a new maize variety called CENTA - CS: Comandante Susana (Virginia Peña Mendoza, 1952 - 1986), as a tribute to the memory and social struggle of a woman who fought for the benefit of the Salvadoran people. This variety stands out for its high yield and high protein quality, making it an option to improve the productivity of this crop and enhance human nutrition.

## I. INTRODUCTION

More than half of the diseases in humans worldwide can be attributed to hunger, insufficient energy intake, or deficiencies in vitamins and minerals; which manifest in various forms of malnutrition in humans.

Malnutrition has negative effects on various dimensions:

- In health: increases the likelihood of the appearance and/or severity of various pathologies, as well as death in different stages of life. There is a greater susceptibility to chronic diseases such as heart disease, diabetes, and hypertension in adulthood, when malnutrition was experienced in childhood.
- In education: malnutrition affects school performance as a result of the deficits caused by diseases and limitations in learning capacity; this translates into greater probabilities of late entry, repetition of school years, and dropout, and consequently, low educational level.
- In the economy: malnutrition and its effects on health and education also have significant economic costs for society as a result of increased healthcare costs, inefficiencies in educational processes, and lower productivity.

In El Salvador, the main nutritional problems are deficiencies in energy, protein, iron, iodine, and vitamin A. These deficiencies have been identified as significant public health problems, despite the advances in their control in recent years, high rates of protein-energy malnutrition and nutritional anemia have persisted, mainly due to a diet based on low-quality grains or grains that do not contain all the essential nutrients to ensure the health and nutrition of the population.

According to MINED-MINSAL (2008), the country has shown a significant and sustained reduction in chronic malnutrition, from 1998 to 2007. In 1998, the prevalence was 29.8%, and in 2007 it was 15.5%, meaning that of every 100 children, 15 have improved their quality of life.

CENTA, committed to food security and nutrition in El Salvador, in collaboration with the International Maize and Wheat Improvement Center (CIMMYT), has joined forces to generate maize germplasm with High Protein Quality (HPP), as a sustainable alternative to contribute to reducing malnutrition in marginalized rural and urban areas through the consumption of this grain. Therefore, as a result of years of research, farmers will be able to count on a new technology, which is expected to increase the productivity of maize cultivation and improve the nutritional quality of the Salvadoran population.

## II. ORIGIN

The synthetic maize variety CENTA - CS, initially named SO6TLWQHGA-B02, is a High Protein Quality (HPP) germplasm generated by the National Center for Agricultural, Livestock and Forestry Technology "Enrique Álvarez Córdova" (CENTA) in collaboration with the International Maize and Wheat Improvement Center (CIMMYT) through conventional breeding methods, it is not genetically modified. A synthetic variety is one that results from the cross between parents that have been selected for their combined general fitness (Cubero, 1999).

## III. RESEARCH STRATEGY

## Primary Research

The evaluation of the synthetic variety CENTA - CS began in 2012 in fields of farmers in the departments of San Miguel and La Unión, in areas corresponding to the so-called "dry corridor." This effort was carried out with the support of the "Adaptation of Maize and Bean to Climate Change in Central America and the Dominican Republic: An Alternative to Mitigate Poverty" project (PRACCA). As a result, the synthetic variety CENTA - CS was identified, which showed good agronomic characteristics and grain yield.

## Field Validation

During 2016, the variety was validated in farms, establishing 17 plots nationwide, where this germplasm outperformed local varieties used as controls by 26% in grain yield. In addition to the good potential for yield, it showed good characteristics of closed ears and low grain rot. ## IV.       FARMERS' PERCEPTION OF THE CENTA - CS VARIETY

The farmers who participated in the validation process indicated that the variety possesses: good foliage and health, good plant and stalk height, stalk thickness, and good maize coverage, semi-dentate grain, and good weight, easy threshing. They considered it to be a drought-resistant variety, as there were more than 19 days without rain in some areas. Regarding culinary aspects, they stated that the corn flavor is excellent, as well as the color of the tamales and the color of the tortilla.

## V.       NUTRITIONAL QUALITY OF THE GRAIN

In order to verify the nutritional quality of the grain produced in the fields by the farmers, samples of both the synthetic and common corn were analyzed. The results of the analyses are shown in Table 2.

The quality of the high-protein maize grain is not visually distinguishable from common maize, however, laboratory analysis demonstrates that it contains twice the essential amino acid tryptophan. According to the International Maize and Wheat Improvement Center (CIMMYT), for maize to be considered biofortified (high-protein), the Protein Quality Index must be superior to 0.80.

VII.       SOIL PREPARATION

<!-- image -->

<!-- image -->

It is recommended to incorporate the straw from the recently harvested crops into the soils where they are cultivated, or in soils with slopes (hills), leave the straw spread or lying on the soil surface. This practice will improve the soil's texture and structure, allowing for better water retention and reducing runoff.

- In cultivated soils: Perform two passes with a harrow, one to incorporate the straw into a depth of 30 cm; and the second pass before planting to homogenize the soil.
- On slopes: Lay the straw on the soil surface and remove weeds, either manually or using chemical weed control products (herbicides).

## VIII.       CULTIVATION MANAGEMENT

## 1) Before Planting

- a) It is necessary to use certified seeds. For maize ACP seeds, it is necessary that the seeds meet the minimum quality standards for essential amino acids such as lysine and tryptophan.
- b) Seed treatment is necessary to prevent various soil pests, such as: stalk borer, wireworm, and other pests.

## 2) Planting Season

- i) First: May 15th to June 15th, which may vary depending on the onset of the rainy season.
- ii) Second: August 1st to August 31st. This depends on the rainfall conditions in each area.
- iii) Irrigation: With irrigation, planting can be carried out whenever it is deemed convenient, but it is recommended from December 1st to January 15th.

## 3) Planting Density

The recommended spacing is:

Between rows:

0.80 meters

Between plants:

0.40 meters, two plants per hill

To achieve a density of 62,500 plants/hectare or 43,750 plants per plot.

## 4) Fertilization

It is recommended to conduct a soil analysis before establishing the crop to carry out appropriate fertilization. It is of utmost importance to carry out fertilization based on nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium, as these are essential for the normal growth of the plant, as they participate in physiological processes that allow for the production of foliage and grain.

In general, the following is recommended:

- a) First fertilization: carry out at planting or no later than 10 days after seedlings emerge. Apply 3.0 qq/m2 of a 15-15-15 fertilizer. Regardless of the method of fertilization, timing, and type of fertilizer, these should be incorporated for better utilization.
- b) Second fertilization: carry out with 3.0 qq/m2 of ammonium sulfate, 25-30 days after planting, if the crop is mechanized, carry out the fertilization at the time of sowing.
- c) Third fertilization: carry out 40-45 days after planting, applying 1.5 qq/m2 of urea 46% N.

## 5) Pests

The maize crop, from the emergence of seedlings, is exposed to the attack of soil pests such as: stalk borer, wireworm, and other pests. On the foliage, such as: earworm, soldier bug, aphids, etc. There are factors that favor the development of pests, such as the climate, the different soil preparation activities, weed control, and the alternation of different crops in their environment. It is necessary to carry out preventive controls to prevent economic damage to the crop, so it is recommended to carry out periodic inspections to decide the appropriate time for the application of insecticides; if chemical products are used, they should be environmentally friendly. In areas where the maize earworm (Dalbulus maidis) is known to affect, which transmits the achaparramiento virus, it is necessary to carry out controls, as it can cause losses of 100%; the periods of greatest presence are in August and December, especially in the coastal area.

## 6) Weeds

Maize cultivation is very susceptible to the presence of weeds in the early stages of development (10-25 days after planting). Weeds compete with the crop for light, water, and nutrients, so they must be controlled manually or chemically, depending on the farmer's economic conditions. It is important to carry out good weed control from the beginning to avoid future problems. In areas with the possibility of using animal traction, it is recommended to avoid damaging the crop's root system. If chemical products are used, all recommendations for their use must be followed, from the preparation of the mixtures to the removal of the packaging.

## 7) Diseases

Foliar diseases in maize cultivation in the country have not been of major economic importance, as they generally occur after the grain filling stage (hard corn). The presence of foliar diseases depends on three factors: a suitable climate for the pathogen, a susceptible host (cultivar), and the presence of the pathogen.

In the years 2007-2012, the incidence of the disease known as 'Asphalt Spot' was observed, its presence was visible in the inter-valley areas where it occurred due to high temperatures and relative humidity, these factors that favor the pathogen. The control of diseases in maize cultivation is generally not recommended; however, the crop must be monitored to avoid possible damage that reduces production.

## IX. HARVEST

The harvest of high-protein maize is carried out in a similar way to common maize, that is, when the ear reaches its physiological maturity. In tropical countries, physiological maturity is reached between 40-50 days after flowering. The presence of the 'black layer' in the grain is an indicator of physiological maturity.

The harvest can be carried out with or without a husker, depending on the preference or taste of the farmer. The threshing can be carried out mechanically and manually. The advantage of harvesting without a husker and manual threshing is that it allows for the selection of healthy ears without affecting fungal diseases, which, when consumed, pose a risk to human health.

It is recommended to store the grain in metal silos or sacks in a dry and ventilated place; if chemical products are used to prevent grain pests, it should not be stored in the living quarters.

To preserve the protein quality of the grain, it is recommended not to mix high-quality maize with normal maize and, preferably, to keep it for family consumption. CENTA - CS is a variety of free-pollinating maize, which allows farmers to save grain from one harvest to be used as seed for future plantings; for this, they must select plants with good characteristics and health, preferably before flowering and continue until the harvest. It is important that every two or three years, the seed is renewed to preserve the quality of the variety.
