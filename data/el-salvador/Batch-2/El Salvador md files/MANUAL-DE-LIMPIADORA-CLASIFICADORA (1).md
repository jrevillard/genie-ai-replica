## INTRODUCTION

There are many options for grain cleaning; this manual explains about the type of cleaning machine introduced in 2014 for producer associations with sales in the formal grain market. There are simpler and more advanced alternatives, all with the function of removing debris, dust, and small or broken grains. Some equipment can classify the grain by size or perform other functions. The capacity and quality of classification vary depending on the type and size of the equipment, and the way it is used and maintained. For this reason, it is very important to properly understand the equipment and train the operators. This manual aims to explain the basics of cleaning and sorting machines, with a focus on the types of equipment available in storage centers.

Importance of using a cleaning and sorting machine

Depending on the harvesting method, the grain may contain little or a lot of unwanted material; therefore, a cleaning and sorting process is usually applied. The main reasons are:

1. Grain is better preserved when clean (in general)
2. If it is to be used as seed, classification by size is required
3. Clean grain has better acceptance in the market and a higher price than dirty grain.
4. To comply with quality standards according to agreements and other customers in the formal market.

Grain handling can be gradually mechanized, and the use of a sorting machine represents a significant advance. The following sequence of steps represents a logical progression in the mechanization process, moving from bottom to top.

## Basics of grain quality analysis

Normally, a purchase of basic grains is accompanied by a specification of the expected quality, and verification upon receipt, especially in the formal market.

To determine the quality of grain, there are different methods, depending on the type of customer, but typically include:

1. Sampling
2. Visual observation, by smell and touch
3. Determination of moisture content
4. Manual sieving and classification
5. Quantification of damaged grains, debris, and insects
6. Other parameters, such as density, color, cooking time, and toxins, etc.

Even in the informal market, the quality of the grain is an important factor in its acceptability and the price that the buyer is willing to pay.

## Efficiency of separation

The separation of "bad" material from "good" material in practice is never perfect, because it would be too slow and expensive. The cleaning and sorting process reduces the amount of unwanted material in the grain, but it never removes it completely. Similarly, it is normal for some good grain to be removed along with the "bad" material. The percentage of "foreign" material depends on the requirements of the buyer; for example, for the maize agreement, up to 2% of impurities and 2% of broken grains are allowed.

## PARTS OF A CLEANING MACHINE AND ITS FUNCTIONS

The design of cleaning and sorting machines can vary greatly; however, in most cases, there is a hopper for placing the grain to be cleaned, a mechanism for sorting, and several sieves, which are typically moved to allow the grain to flow to the outlet. The byproducts are also directed to their respective outlets, often into a bag.

## GENERAL DESCRIPTION OF THE EQUIPMENT

Several grain storage and service centers in the country have equipment from Brazil, which was designed for use in the field and can be operated by a small tractor: For "stationary" use, a 1.1 to 1.5 horsepower electric motor has been adapted, which is connected to the voltage indicated on the motor's label. If it has both 110/220 volts, it is important to have a qualified technician check the connection and make any necessary adjustments.

Most machines use three sieves, which allow for the separation of large and small grains.

## INSTALLATION OF THE CLEANING MACHINE

Due to the vibration generated by the machine, it should not be used on wet, weak, or delicate surfaces. If necessary, wooden pads should be placed under the "legs," and rubber pieces should be placed under the pads to prevent the machine from moving. No "legs" should be left unsupported.

The electrical connection should be a 14-gauge cable, protected by a double 10-ampere thermal fuse if using 220 volts. For 110 volts, use a 20-ampere fuse and a 12-gauge cable. Avoid connections that are too long (more than 20 meters). For safety, a third line for the ground should be present, connected to the motor body. It is best to have a qualified electrician connect the equipment.

Dust is expelled from the opposite side of the motor and is released into the environment. Therefore, the machine should be used in open areas or at least have an outlet facing outwards. When working in an enclosed space, the air will become saturated with dust.

Space should be provided around the machine for placing the grain to be cleaned, the cleaned grain, and the waste, as well as for weighing and closing the bags, if applicable.

## OPERATION OF THE CLEANING MACHINE

The machine is relatively simple to operate. After making the necessary adjustments to achieve the desired cleaning of the grain, the main tasks are to:

1. Keep the feed hopper full.
2. Change the bags in the outlets when they are full.
3. Check that the sieves remain open and, if necessary, clean them (by lowering them).
4. Remove waste from the feed roller.

## 1. Sieves (shape and size)

The sieves have marked on one side the dimensions of their holes in millimeters (mm). Those with long rectangular holes (e.g., 4 mm x 22 mm) are for rice or corn. Sieves with fine perforations (e.g., 1.5 x 22 mm) are for removing fine debris and are placed at the bottom.

**STEPS FOR CHANGING SIEVES**

1. Loosen the butterfly nuts of the guides and remove the door of the sieve drawer.
2. Remove the door, rotate the wedges, and remove the guides.
3. Lower the sieves, making sure to place them in the correct order and that they do not drop rubber balls.
4. Clean the rubber ball containers.
5. Replace the sieves with the indicated ones.
6. Mount the sieves in the correct order, making sure to secure them properly (see next to the motor).
7. Place the two guides and rotate the wedges.
8. Place the cover.
9. Tighten the butterfly nuts of the cover and the guides.

## VARIOUS OPTIONS

- To prevent large grains from separating from small grains, place the lower sieve in the middle, and use a sieve with a large hole on the bottom to allow everything to pass through, so that no small grains are left behind (you should always have 3 sieves to ensure proper pressure).
- To prevent dust from flying too far, place the outlet against a wall or cover it with a tarp.
- To increase the flow through the sieves, tilt the machine towards the outlet, placing supports underneath the "legs" next to the motor.
- If you want to increase the capacity of the material being cleaned, you can increase its height with a wooden or metal frame.
- If you don't have 220V, change the motor to 110 volts, or if that's not possible, change the motor.

## CONCLUSIONS

The equipment allows for faster grain cleaning than manual methods, and also meets the quality requirements of the formal grain and seed market. However, the investment is significant. To recoup it, you need to clean thousands of quintals of grain per year.

To provide the service, you need to consider the costs of electricity, labor, and the investment, depending on the situation in each case.

## RECOMMENDATIONS

- The sieves may wear out, so it may be necessary to have them repaired. There are specialized workshops that can repair agricultural equipment.
- Lubricate the points on the machine according to the instructions in the manufacturer's manual.
- Avoid getting the equipment wet. Before storing it, clean it and protect the metal parts with paint or oil to prevent rust, especially the sieves.
- Maximize the capacity per hour to reduce the costs of electricity and labor per quintal.
