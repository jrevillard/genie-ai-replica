## INTRODUCTION

The traditional agricultural production model practiced in El Salvador continues to rely on intensive tillage, which leaves the soil exposed to erosion, continuous loss of fertility, and low water retention capacity, making it poor soil that requires increasing doses of fertilizers to achieve good yields.

The monoculture system (planting the same crop repeatedly in the same area) combined with the burning of crop residues and the lack of implementation of soil conservation practices, has increased soil degradation. In light of the above, to achieve sustainable production, it is necessary to incorporate conservation practices as well as restore soil fertility.

The use of green manure is one of the low-cost, short-term results alternatives. It can improve soil fertility and life, while also reducing dependence on chemical fertilizers.

## WHAT IS GREEN MANURE?

These are plants with the ability to adapt to various soils and climates, with rapid growth and high vegetative production. They are cultivated to protect and restore the soil. They are found naturally as weeds in uncultivated areas.

The varieties recommended as green manure are primarily those belonging to the legume family. These plants have a special ability to form nodules in their roots. They harbor bacteria that have the ability to coexist with plants, providing them with nitrogen. They take it from the air and fix it in the soil, making it available to crops that will subsequently be planted.

## FUNCTIONS OF GREEN MANURE

They improve soil fertility through two mechanisms: fixing atmospheric nitrogen in the soil and by providing vegetative material, which is transformed into organic matter, improving the physical, chemical, and biological properties of the soil.

<!-- image -->

## Because they are grown as soil cover and develop a large amount of foliage, they provide the following benefits:

*   Increase the soil's ability to retain moisture.
*   Protect the soil from the impact of rain, reducing erosion.
*   Reduce water evaporation from the soil.
*   Contribute to soil warming.
*   Reduce weed populations, reducing the cost of crop weeding.
*   Contribute to the restoration of highly degraded soils through the addition of organic matter.
*   Reduce the need for chemical fertilizers due to nitrogen fixation.
*   Interrupt pest and disease cycles by including green manure in crop rotation plans.
*   Allow for the detoxification of soils to initiate organic farming, removing toxic residues from chemical materials.

## CULTIVATION SYSTEMS

## INDIVIDUAL OR MONOCULTURE

The green manure is grown in the field, with only the green manure crop being planted. It is used during periods of soil rest or in crop rotation.

## ASSOCIATED CULTIVATION

Some producers associate it with maize, planting two seeds of *Cannavalia* every 3 rows of maize.

## PLANTING AND FIRST USE TIMES

After the rains have established in the months of May to June, it can be sown to produce vegetative material and use it as ground cover.

Incorporate into the soil with a plow or leave as ground cover on the soil surface when it is flowering.

Cows (image or photo) or agricultural machinery can be used to prepare the soil, and sowing can be done in furrows.

## POST-SEASON PLANTING

Planting in August or the post-season should be for seed production, which is harvested during the dry season, in December and January.

The crop ends when the plants reach physiological maturity, or when the pods change color and the seeds are fully mature.

## RECOMMENDATION

For producing vegetative material with the purpose of adding organic matter to the soil:

It is recommended to cut the plant when it is flowering, 90-100 days after planting. At this point, it reaches its maximum nutrient content. In this case, the vegetative material is used as a cover crop or for incorporation into the soil.

## WEED CONTROL

Weed control is only needed in the first 30 days, as mucuna, gandul, and dolichos have a high capacity for ground cover. Crotalaria and gandul also require additional weeding.

## OTHER USES OF LEGUMES RECOMMENDED AS GREEN FERTILIZERS

For human consumption, the young grains of gandul or peas are prepared with rice, meat, or tamales. The dry grain of gandul can be prepared and consumed in the same way as red and white beans.

As forage for ruminants, gandul, dolichos, and cannavalia are used, provided in green form. Mixing legumes with grasses for silage and hay production improves the protein content (10-15%).
