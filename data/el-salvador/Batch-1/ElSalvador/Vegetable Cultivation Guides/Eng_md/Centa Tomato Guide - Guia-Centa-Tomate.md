## I. INTRODUCTION

The Ministry of Agriculture and Livestock, through the National Center for Agricultural and Forestry Technology, CENTA 'Enrique Álvarez Córdova', has the objective of ensuring food security for the population, reducing imports, and promoting the development of technological innovation processes that increase productivity, competitiveness, and profitability.

In the vegetable program, a great deal of effort has been devoted to the cultivation of TOMATO, due to its importance as a major economic and demand item in the national market. These efforts have focused on the search for new technologies, such as identifying better genetic materials with high yields and tolerance to pests and diseases, using different organic sources, irrigation, fertirriego, hydroponics, and general crop management, in protected and open conditions.

This technical guide is the result of research, experience, and dedication by the technical team of CENTA. It is intended to serve as a tool for dissemination and consultation for producers, professionals, horticulturists, students, and the general public, on the most recommended techniques currently available, generated and/or validated by CENTA for the production of this crop, taking into account the possibility of acquisition and adaptability to the edaphoclimatic and cultural conditions of our country.

## II. GENERAL INFORMATION

## IMPORTANCE

Tomato is one of the most consumed vegetables at the national level, adapting to warm and temperate climates, and cultivated in areas with altitudes between 100 and 1,500 meters above sea level. It can be sown throughout the year in areas with irrigation. It is considered one of the most important vegetables due to its economic value and high vitamin and mineral content.

Due to various climate and soil factors, production is limited. The 2014-2015 agricultural statistics report the planting of 1,060 hectares of tomato at the national level, with a production volume of 19,396,600 kg, which means that this production does not cover the apparent demand ($109,554,703 kg); with imports of approximately 90,158,103 kilograms with a value of $12,165,670.00.

To cover the apparent demand, it is necessary to sow approximately 3,990 hectares - 1 hectare. This requires several lines of action such as: a) the implementation of new technologies using protected infrastructure (microtunnel, macrotunnel, mesh house, greenhouse), irrigation, fertirriego, hydroponics, organic agriculture, varieties tolerant to pests and diseases; b) increasing the area of planting; c) producers have the technology.

## ORIGIN

Tomato (Lycopersicon esculentum) is a plant originating in the western coastal plain of South America. It was first introduced to Europe in the 16th century and began to be commercially cultivated in the 19th century, a period in which industrialization and differentiation of varieties for table and industrial use began.

In recent decades, the introduction of improved varieties from the United States and Europe, particularly hybrid types, has eliminated the inferior quality native varieties.

## BOTANICAL DESCRIPTION

Annual or perennial herbaceous plants, autogamous, with an erect growth habit and up to 1.5 m in height, hairy and smooth.

Leaves compound; with 5 to 9 leaflets per leaf, ovate or oblong, 5-7 cm long, with toothed or lobed edges, acute apex, and oblique base.

Flowers with pedicels of 9-12 mm.

Berry red, pink, or yellow, oblong, globular, depressed or pyriform, more than 2 cm in diameter, multi-chambered.

Numerous seeds, flattened, and yellow. It is cultivated for its fruits, which are consumed as vegetables and in salads. It is also used to prepare condiments, sauces, and juices. It is propagated by seed (LEON, J. 1987).

## III. BOTANICAL ASPECTS

The cultivated tomato belongs to the Solanaceae family, the genus Lycopersicon and the species esculentum. It is cultivated as an annual plant, of arboreal stature. It develops in a sprawling, semi-erect or erect manner, depending on the variety.

The first stem is herbaceous, although it tends to lignify in the older branches; it is fragile, round and erect, semi-woody, with glandular hairs that give it the characteristic odor.

The leaves are compound, alternate, imparpinated, with toothed or smooth leaflets and glandular hairs (Figure 1).

The inflorescences are flower heads with five petals, five long lanceolate sepals and five stamens in columns surrounding the style; the calyx is persistent.

The flowers do not open simultaneously, so there are always buds, flowers and fruits on the same stem or branch. The anthesis (opening of the flower) usually occurs in the first hours of the day and 24 hours later, the pollen is released, and it appears on the inner side of the anthers and falls

directly onto the surface of the stigmas (Figure 2).

Self-pollination is normal for a tomato plant flower; cross-pollination occurs with the presence of insects or by wind. The fruit is a bilocular or plurilocular berry, red or yellow in color, with very varied shapes (Figure 3).

Commercial cultivars are selected for the maximum number of locules and their thickness. It is common to find five to ten cells (AgroEs.ES 2014) (Figure 4).

## SEED

It is flat and oval with dimensions of approximately 3x2x1 mm. If stored for prolonged periods, it is recommended to store it at a humidity of 5.5%. A quality seed should have at least 95% germination.

- a) Rapid water absorption, which lasts 12 hours.
- b) Rest for 40 hours, during which it absorbs water again.
- c) The layers of cells surrounding the seeds dissolve in maturity, forming a gelatinous mass rich in starch granules (Figure 5).

## IV. PLANT GROWTH

From a systematic point of view, the tomato plant is divided into two groups based on its growth habit:

## DETERMINATE GROWTH

These are plants whose vegetative growth is continuous. Its main stem can reach up to about 10 m in length, if managed to a single growth axis. The inflorescences appear laterally on the stem. The stem develops from the penultimate bud, pushing the terminal inflorescence outwards, so that the lateral stem appears after the main stem that gave it origin. Culinary and salad varieties belong to this order and are preferred for greenhouse cultivation (Figure 7).

## Figure 7. Indeterminate tomato growth.

## INDETERMINATE GROWTH

Plants with indeterminate growth have a bush-like shape, and the lateral branches grow continuously.

These plants have a floral bud at each end of growth and have defined periods of flowering and fruit set. The size of the plant varies depending on the variety; they can be compact, medium, and tall plants, and the latter require the use of stakes.

Most tomato varieties, whether paste or cooking varieties, grown in the country fall into this category, and production is obtained in a relatively short period. This characteristic is very important because it allows for harvesting to be concentrated in a specific period according to market needs.

## FLOWERING

The tomato flower is perfect, yellow in color, consisting of 5 or more sepals, 5 or more petals, and 5 to 6 stamens; they are grouped into inflorescences of the type of cyme, consisting of 4 to 12 flowers (Figure 9).

Temperatures above 30°C cause the pollen not to mature, so there is no fertilization, with the observation of floral abortion or flower drop, recommending the selection of varieties that adapt to this type of environmental conditions.

## FRUITING PATTERN

For a good fruit set (ripening), the nighttime temperature must be lower than the daytime temperature, approximately 6°C. The nighttime temperature should range from 13 to 26°C for most varieties, as if the internal temperature of the fruit is greater than 30°C, it inhibits the synthesis of lycopene (the compound responsible for the red color of the fruit), resulting in fruits with uneven ripening and coloration.

The start of fruit set occurs between 65 and 75 days after planting, and the first harvest can be carried out between 85 and 90 days.

The number of cuts will depend on the management given to the tomato crop, the prevailing climatic conditions during its growth cycle, and its growth habit. However, in open field conditions, an average of 7 to 8 cuts can be made for determinate varieties and 12 to 15 cuts for indeterminate varieties.

## V. CROP PHYSIOLOGY

The tomato plant does not require a photoperiod, as far as its ability to form flowers is concerned; however, it is important for its vegetative growth.

The increase in vegetative growth is favored by the increase in light and temperature. To fruit normally, it needs daytime and nighttime temperatures between 23-17°C.

A continuous temperature will not allow for normal fruit set. Night temperatures below 17°C cause flower drop; this physiological process can be avoided with the external addition of growth regulators (in this case, auxins), which induce the formation of parthenocarpic fruits. Night temperatures above 23°C cause flower drop due to the lack of carbohydrates. The daytime temperature should not exceed 35°C.

There are a large number of substances that allow for the control of a multitude of characteristics of the tomato plant's development. Some of these substances will allow us to control aspects as fundamental as improving the uniformity of the planting, stimulating the production of roots, delaying or advancing the transplant, obtaining precocity in production, increasing the number of flowers or influencing the set of fruits.

Temperature, light, and humidity play a determining role in the correct formation of pollen, and that this can pollinate the largest number of flowers.

In addition to the application of plant regulators to obtain better results in fruit set, mechanical agitation and vibrators are used to achieve a greater release of pollen with their shaking.

The tomato seed does not have a dormancy period and retains its viability for approximately four years at low temperatures (3° C to 5°C) and between 5 to 10% humidity. After planting, with optimal temperatures of 16-25°C, germination occurs between 3 to 6 days.

## VI. CROP PHENOLOGY

Tomato is sensitive to low light conditions, as the crop requires at least six hours of direct sunlight per day to flower. ## VIII. VARIETIES

The type of tomato to be sown will depend on the purpose of consumption and the market; since they can be classified as table tomatoes and paste tomatoes.

Salad tomato hybrid are consumed fresh and are commercialized in supermarkets in bulk or packaged in trays, and are also found in municipal markets where they are commercialized in plastic jugs.

## X. CULTURAL PRACTICES

## TRANSPLANTING

## PREPARATION OF SOIL

Proper soil preparation or plot to be sown is essential to ensure the success of planting.

The preparation should include, in addition to good soil loosening, ensuring the health of the soil and, in particular, the removal of the majority of economically important weeds. When possible, one or two months should be initiated before transplanting, in order to efficiently control weeds and soil pests.

Soil preparation practices include:

## SUBSOILING

This is done to break up the compacted layers of the subsoil, caused by the passage of machinery, which helps to promote better root development. It should be carried out during the dry season, in a cross pattern, and it is recommended to do it every 3 to 5 years.

## PLOUGHING

This involves removing the top layer of soil to depths of 0.40 m. This practice

Technical Guide

helps to incorporate previous crop residues, destroy weeds, retain more moisture, and improve fertilization efficiency. It should be carried out whenever the crop is established in the field.

## HOEING

This practice aims to pulverize the clods that have remained after passing the plow, and it should be carried out when the soil has enough moisture for the clods to break down easily. Heavy hoes and cultivators can be used.

The number of passes will depend on the size of the clods and the desired level of pulverization, but it is recommended to leave the soil as loose as possible, because this work depends on the quality of the bed that will be made and the efficiency of the transplant.

Before the last hoeing, it should be used to incorporate lime amendments and organic matter inputs.

## TRANSPLANTING MODES

## RAKING

This is the final practice in soil preparation and consists of forming the bed where the tomato will be transplanted. The goal is to raise it at least 25 to 40 cm, leaving a width between 80 to 100 cm, with a distance of 150 cm between each bed, with a population of 11,667 plants per bed.

Raking provides better drainage, better soil aeration (the roots receive more oxygen), and the soil is looser for better root development. Beds are made using implements such as border discs, pre-rakers, or furrow openers, using a tool bar at the recommended spacing.

## LIVING BARRIERS

This practice is used to prevent severe damage to the crop from strong winds or as a barrier to the movement of vector insects, such as the whitefly. Recommended species include corn, sorghum, King Grass, sugarcane, and Crotalaria.

## LEVEL ROWS

This is recommended for sloping terrain. This practice helps to reduce soil erosion.

## GREEN FERTILIZERS

This is recommended for soils with low organic matter content. They are used to improve soil structure, nitrogen input, and increase soil microorganisms. Among the most recommended are Dolichus lablab, Canavalia ensiformis, Mucuna pruriens, and Crotalaria juncea.

They should be sown before transplanting or at the beginning of the rains, and incorporated into the soil when they bloom, because at this stage there is a higher concentration of nutrients in their vegetative parts.

## PLANTING DENSITY

Planting density influences competition between the crop and weeds. It can also modify the soil microclimate, thereby preventing some diseases caused by fungi and bacteria.

The recommended spacing is 1.50 between rows and 0.50 m between plants in the rainy season, resulting in a planting density of 9,334 plants per bed and 1.20 x 0.50 m in the dry season, 11,667 plants per bed.

To establish a fertilization program that allows for high tomato yields at the lowest possible cost, it is necessary to know the availability of nutrients in the soil; this is achieved through chemical analysis. ## MACRONUTRIENTS

## POTASSIUM (K)

This element is necessary in tomatoes for the formation of stems and fruits, carbohydrate synthesis, increase in solid substances, coloration and brightness of the fruits. It helps to eliminate the harmful action of other elements, favoring the assimilation of essential minerals. Its deficiency is manifested in the reduction of stem growth. K plays an important role in the amount of sugars that the fruit accumulates; just like phosphorus, K helps to increase the amount of dry matter and vitamin C.

## NITROGEN (N)

It is the main nutrient element in the formation of plant vegetative organs. Tomatoes are sensitive to nitrogen deficiency in the vegetative phase and during maturation. The lack of this element affects the development of the plant; the foliage becomes pale green or yellow, young leaves and branches are very thin. Late flowering and decrease in fruit weight occur.

Excess N unbalances the availability of K and P, and as a consequence, excessive vegetative development occurs at the expense of fruit production; hollow and light fruits with little juice, few seeds, succulent stems, leaves grow excessively and the plant becomes susceptible to diseases. In sandy soils, organic fertilizers and fertilizer should be applied in fractions.

## CALCIUM (Ca)

The apical rot is caused by a localized calcium deficiency, the green fruits show the base of the fruit sunken and hard, its color changes from green to black. Deficiencies are manifested in very acidic soils or with low humidity.

## SULFUR (S)

This element is vital for plant growth and for the development of proteins and seeds. The youngest and closest to the buds leaves are most affected; under conditions of deficiency, not only the yield is reduced, but also the quality of the fruits.

## MAGNESIUM (Mg)

It is a component of chlorophyll, it is the green pigment of plants. Chlorophyll is essential for the process of photosynthesis. In the growth stage, chlorosis appears at the tips of the lower leaves, evident between the veins, but in advanced stages, all leaves turn yellow. This symptom extends to the middle leaves, in the fruitification stage, the chlorosis becomes more evident, and the lower leaves of the plant turn purple.

<!-- image -->

## PHOSPHORUS (P)

In tomato cultivation, this element must be applied before transplanting or sowing, because it has problems with assimilation by the plants. A good availability of phosphorus accelerates root development, fruit production is early, improves production and fruit quality.

The lack of phosphorus reduces the absorption of nitrogen and reduces growth. It reduces flowering, fruit production and fruit development. The most characteristic symptoms of phosphorus deficiency are reddish or purple (violet) in young leaves and on the back or dorsal side of the leaves.

## MICRONUTRIENTS

It is a group of chemical elements necessary for the good development of plants. The lack of a microelement can be caused by the excess of another, which performs an action on the plant that blocks it. The pH of the soil also influences: a high pH (7.5) causes the lack of manganese (Mn), copper (Cu), zinc (Zn), iron (Fe), boron (B), and molybdenum (Mo) in the plant; a low pH (&lt;5.5) can cause a lack of molybdenum.

In sandy soils, manganese, copper, zinc, boron, and sulfur may be absent, as they are easily washed away. The micronutrients that the tomato most demands are: boron, manganese, zinc, and iron.

## BORON (B)

It is essential for good pollination, favors the setting of flowers and fruits and the development of the seed.

Excess boron produces chlorosis and burns on the edges of the leaves and the tissues turn a dark black, the cortex is swollen, the fruits are deformed and ripen prematurely.

## MANGANESE (Mn)

In addition to promoting resistance to pests and diseases, manganese acts as a catalyst in enzymatic and physiological actions; in addition, it is related to respiration and chlorophyll synthesis. The deficiency is observed as a pale green color and chlorotic tissue between the veins of young leaves.

## ZINC (Zn)

It is a very important element in growth and production; it can act as a limiting factor in the performance of these functions if the availability is scarce. The deficiency is more frequently observed in sandy soils and with high phosphorus content. The internodes are considerably reduced in size, which makes the terminal growth appear in a rosette shape. It causes deformation in new leaves.

## IRON (Fe)

The deficiencies of this element are first seen in the young leaves of the plant; growth stops when the element does not move from the adult leaves to the meristems. The young leaves show chlorosis that extends to all of them; finally, a completely white color appears. In soils with coarse texture, low organic matter content and high pH, the deficiency of iron is most observed.

## XIV. INTEGRATED PEST MANAGEMENT

The integrated management of tomato pests is the combination of different strategies, with the purpose of managing pests in a more rational way.

The philosophy of this management is the coexistence with pests at levels that do not affect the crop, so it is of great importance to carry out sampling in order to improve the control strategies.

## PLANTING DATES

The choice of planting date allows to offset the susceptible periods of the crop with the peaks of pest populations, thus reducing the damage.

## SEED SELECTION

F1 hybrids are superior to open-pollinated varieties due to their high yield, earlier maturity and uniformity, in addition to maintaining the quality of the fruit and resistance to some pests and diseases.

It is recommended to sow resistant or tolerant varieties to pests and diseases in order to reduce the use of pesticides.

## PLANTING DENSITY

The planting density influences the competition between the crop and weeds. It can also modify the soil microclimate, thus preventing some diseases caused by fungi and bacteria. The recommended spacings are 1.50 x 0.50 m in the rainy season and 1.20 x 0.50 m in the summer.

Technical Guide

## CROP ROTATION

The continuous sowing of the same crop tends to concentrate pests in the soil, so it is advisable to program the sequence of sowing of different species in order to break or alter the life cycles of the pests; for example, the rotation of crops with legumes reduces the reinfestation of Spodoptera frugiperda; the rotation with maize reduces the inoculum of bacterial wilt (Ralstonia solanacearum).

## INTERCROPPING

Intercropping plays an important role in pest control, contributes to the elimination of weeds, better use of soil nutrients and improves productivity per unit area.

This practice is carried out by intercropping the main crop with some repellent plants such as the dead flower, oregano, basil, thyme, mint, sage. It can also be intercropped with other crops to repel pests, and the favorable association can be with: onion, carrot (in cold climates), parsley and asparagus.

## WEEDS

Weeds compete for water, light, nutrients and physical space, they are hosts for pests, which causes a reduction in production or the formation of fruits of poor quality.

Inadequate weed management can increase the production costs of the crop, reducing the profitability obtained by the farmer.

## XV. CULTURAL CONTROL

## SOIL PREPARATION

To control high-growing weeds in flat soils, it is advisable to carry out a hoeing first, in order to then incorporate them into the soil. If the weed is of low growth, it can be removed manually or incorporated directly by mechanical or animal traction.

## CROP ROTATION

Planting crops such as corn and sorghum one year before the establishment of the tomato planting contributes to the reduction of weed populations through the cultural practices carried out in the corn crop. In addition, the straw left on the soil forms a cover that prevents the germination of weeds.

## COVER CROP PLANTING

The most recommended cover crops are legumes. These are left to grow until the formation of pods, then they are cut and spread on the ground as cover or incorporated at the time of soil preparation for the next planting.

## MECHANICAL CONTROL

In soils with abundant weed populations, it is recommended to prepare them three weeks before transplanting, performing one plowing and two harrowing passes with intervals of one week between each operation.

## CHEMICAL CONTROL

This method has advantages over others due to its speed, economy, and efficiency. The efficiency of herbicide use depends on the type of herbicide, proper management, dosage, amount of water for its application, nozzle used for its application, and the weed to be controlled.

## XVI. PEST MANAGEMENT

## SOIL INSECTS

The attack of soil insects is a limiting factor during the first few days after transplanting, as when populations are high, they feed on the root system, causing losses of up to 80% of the plants.

The most damaging pest is the blind beetle.

## BLIND BEETLE (Phyllophaga sp.)

Most species of Phyllophaga spp. Oviposit in the soil; the eggs are white, initially elongated and then become spherical.

The larvae are whitish or creamy in shape, resembling 'C', and can complete their life cycle in one or two years.

## Sampling

Sampling should be done after soil preparation. To do this, a hole of 0.30 m x 0.30 m and 0.20 m deep is made in 5 different locations. The critical level is 1 large larva or 2 small ones found in four samples.

## Physical-mechanical control

At the beginning of the rains, the adults emerge from the soil, and they can be captured at night by placing light traps. On the other hand, the soil is calked, which makes the environment unfavorable for the development of the blind beetle.

The effect of solar rays on the soil acts as a lethal agent for the control of the blind beetle, so it is recommended to remove the soil during the dry season (March-April) so that the solar rays control the pest.

## Biological control

Entomopathogenic fungi such as Beauveria bassiana and Metarhizium anisopliae, or other natural enemies such as some insects, birds, reptiles, and small mammals, which feed on the blind beetle, can be used.

## NEMATODES

In nematodes, there are two types of parasitism:

Ectoparasites are species that normally do not penetrate into the roots, but feed only from the outside of the cells near the root surface. Endoparasites are species that enter the host and feed from the inside, for example, Meloidogyne incognita, Meloidogyne javanica.

They can be migratory, which move freely in the soil and feed on the root. Or sedentary, which once inside the root, do not move.

## Symptoms

They form lesions in the root, fungal and/or bacterial infections, root galls, weakening of the plants, yellowing, wilting, nutritional deficiencies, and low productivity.

Once the females produce the egg mass, they come out and the cracks that they cause when emerging from the roots, serve as an entry for pathogens such as: Fusarium, Phytophthora, Rhizoctonia, and Pythium.

## WHITEFLY ( Bemisia tabaci )

Whitefly is a sucking insect, of which there are many species; Bemisia tabaci is the most widespread, possibly the most damaging; it has a distribution practically throughout the tropical area of the world, although recently it has exceeded these limits and colonized areas located in other latitudes.

## SYMPTOMS AND DAMAGE

Adults and larvae of whitefly feed on the plant. This influences the physiological processes and can cause a reduction in its growth. They are viral transmitters.

## BEHAVIOR OF WHITEFLY ( Bemisia tabaci )

It is responsible for transmitting geminiviruses, which are located in the phloem and require the vector to pass through a feeding period of 2-24 hours (virus acquisition),

followed by a 2-3 day feeding-inoculation period.

It remains protected in the underside of the leaves throughout its life, has a high capacity to develop resistance to insecticides, shows great genetic plasticity to develop biotypes and adapt to new or adverse agroclimatic conditions, has migratory habits, constantly colonizing new crop fields, both the vector and the virus have multiple hosts (cultivated plants or weeds).

When the infected plants are shaken, the adults first fly, to return later to the underside of the leaves. The larvae are found on the underside of young leaves; the pupae in older leaves.

Bemisia tabaci larvae can appear both in old and young leaves (Figure 28 and 29).

## EARLY BLIGHT ( Alternaria solani )

Another name it is known as: Early blight. This disease is favored in areas where there are alternating periods of rain and heat. This pathogen survives in the soil, in crop residues, and affected fruits, and can spread by seed. Its spread is favored by wind, wounds or injuries caused by tillage or insects that favor the penetration of the fungus.

## BIOLOGY AND TYPES OF DAMAGE

In the leaves, irregular brown spots with a yellowish halo are observed; as the disease progresses, concentric rings are observed in the center of the spots. The lesions can merge, and when there are a large number of them, the leaf turns yellow, dries, and falls off.

In severe attacks, it can cause defoliation of the plant. At the stem and leaf level, the lesions are deep, elongated, and brown in color.

On the fruit, the damage starts at the calyx, which completely necroses; the lesions can be well defined and reach up to 2 cm or can extend and cover the entire fruit. In advanced lesions, a layer of black color (like soot) is observed, consisting of the hyphae of the fungus.

Crop rotation, removal of crop residues, balanced nutrient application, sanitary pruning to remove damaged leaves and remove them from the area of the crop.

## TOMATO WILT (DORMILONA, BACTERIOSIS, MOLD, BACTERIAL WILT)

Ralstonia solanacearum

## BIOLOGY AND TYPE OF DAMAGE

This disease is observed in tomato production areas that have alternating conditions of high temperatures and high relative humidity. It is a bacterium that persists for years; soils with poor drainage and low pH favor the development of this pathogen. It is spread by irrigation water, tools, stakes, wounds caused during planting activities, soil and seed dragging, among others. At the field level, it has been observed that the crop is more affected when it is in flowering and has suffered from drought stress. Initially, a fall of the basal leaves is observed, and subsequently, the plant wilts completely, due to contamination at the root level. A rapid diagnosis can be made in the field by cutting a portion of the stem about 7 cm long, then placing it in a glass container containing water. It is held by one end with a piece of wire and left for 10-20 minutes; after that time, a whitish thread is observed, which is the bacterial exudate.

## BIOLOGY AND TYPE OF DAMAGE

## WATERING METHODS IN THE COUNTRY

The most used method is gravity irrigation, but in the last few years, drip irrigation has gained considerable popularity as an alternative for horticultural crops.

## DRIP IRRIGATION

In drip irrigation, it is important to consider the hydraulic design of the pumping system and the practical conditions of the place where the system is installed.

The irrigation interval for drip irrigation is calculated daily, but it can vary from 2-3 days for light soils, and from 3-5 days for heavy soils, depending on the agroclimatic conditions of the area and the advantages that can be gained by varying the number of days in the irrigation application.

One of the main advantages of this system is the efficiency in water application, so with small sources, this crop can be established. According to the type of soil where it is established, the irrigation interval will be.

Before establishing the planting, it is recommended to perform a saturation irrigation to the depth that the roots will reach. When there are problems due to water shortage, excess, or sudden variation, the following symptoms may appear in the crop:

## EXCESS OF WATER

Green and ripe fruits crack due to cell turgor. Increased susceptibility to fungal and bacterial diseases. Excessive apical growth and little stem development (thickness).

## WATER DEFICIENCY

Fruit and flower drop. Yellow to purplish coloration. Vegetative growth stops, specifically at apical and fruit points. Necrosis at leaf tips and apical ends.

## ADVANTAGES

a) Partial soil wetting, which becomes an important water saving, b) wide and precise uniform water distribution, c) it is possible to use localized fertilization, along with irrigation, d) flexibility in irrigation schedules, normally application times are low, e) low discharge volumes, which translates into a pumping economy.

Additionally, it can be mentioned that the possibility exists for small producers to have access to this method through the use of low-cost unconventional technologies, combining conventional irrigation materials and accessories such as drip tape (Figure 44).

## FERTIRRIGATION

Irrigation and fertilization are the most important management factors through which the development of plants, yield, and fruit quality can be controlled. The introduction of drip irrigation and fertigation opens new possibilities for controlling water and nutrient supply to crops, while maintaining the desired concentration and distribution of ions and water in the soil.

Fertigation is the technique of applying fertilizers through the drip irrigation system, which allows for a rational dosage based on the crop's demand, soil characteristics, water, and specific environmental conditions. Rational nutrient management of the crop requires a mastery of the physiological and edaphic principles of nutrition and the aspects related to production systems (Etchevers, 1997; Castellanos, 1997).

Technical Guide

Improving control under drip irrigation and soil fertilization is due to different factors such as: a) appropriate and uniform application under different circumstances, b) application of nutrients only in the moistened area, where the most active roots are concentrated, c) ease of adaptation of specific nutrient quantities and concentrations according to the crop's requirement, according to the phenological stage and climatic conditions, d) convenient use of soluble and balanced liquid fertilizers with small amounts of minor elements.

Two factors define the amount of nutrients needed for the plant: a) optimal daily water consumption rate during the crop cycle, b) the rate of dry matter production and the optimal nutrient concentration of tissues define, in conjunction, the daily nutrient consumption rate during the growth period, which results in the maximum yield and quality of the product.

## XIX. HARVEST AND POST HARVEST

## HARVEST INDEX

At the time of harvest, the degree or index of maturity must be considered. Two types of maturity are distinguished: physiological and commercial. The former refers to when the fruit has reached maximum growth and maturity. The latter is that which meets the conditions required by the market.

## WHEN TO HARVEST

For industrialization, the tomato must be fully ripe on the plant. For fresh market, the tomato is harvested in its green ripe or pink stage, in order to reduce losses due to quantity and quality, caused by poor transportation and inadequate handling.

The harvest must be carried out when it is free of moisture from dew or rain, because this favors the decomposition and putrefaction of the fruit. It is also recommended to harvest in the morning and keep the tomatoes in shaded places.

## HOW TO HARVEST

This activity must be carried out with great care to avoid producing damage to the fruits, which, although not noticeable visually, are the origin of high percentages of losses that manifest as microbial caused rot.

The harvest of the tomato can be done manually, with a scissor to avoid damaging the fruit.

The tomato for fresh consumption can be harvested with or without the peduncle, depending on the preferences of the markets.

They are collected in baskets or shallow bags with smooth surfaces to avoid damage by abrasion and compression; then they are poured onto sacks spread on the ground, under the shade of trees or structures built to protect the harvest from direct sunlight.

Here, the intermediary classifies and fills boxes with 55 lbs capacity. It is necessary to dedicate the maximum attention to the operations of collection and transport, as these are considered the ones that cause the most internal damage due to bruising.

## SELECCIÓN Y CLASIFICACIÓN

The tomatoes are carefully selected before being shipped to market, so that they have a good appearance and presentation. The deformed, overly green or very ripe, those that present sunburn, bumps, cuts, scratches, bruises or those that are damaged by worms or microorganisms, must be separated.

This activity is important to be carried out immediately after harvesting. This will prevent contamination of the rest of the production. Tomato fruits can be classified according to: size (small, medium and large), skin color, and other characteristics required by the market.

## TRANSPORT

The transport of the tomato to the destination market must be carried out as soon as possible, preferably in fresh hours, to avoid that the fruits remain under the effects of the sun, wind and high temperatures, which are factors that accelerate the ripening and senescence processes.

It is also important that the speed of the vehicle is moderate, to avoid damage caused by the vibration and bumps, as a consequence of the irregularities of the rural roads.

## STORAGE

The storage temperature of the tomatoes in the refrigerator varies depending on the degree of maturity in which they have been harvested. The tomato when it has reached its physiological maturity, can be stored at temperatures between 12 and 15°C, when it is desired to temporarily delay ripening; prolonged periods at these conditions affect the color and flavor when the fruits ripen.

It is not recommended to store the tomato in a state of development (physiological maturity) at temperatures below 10°C, because it suffers damage, which is characterized by the development of a slow and abnormal maturation.

## XX. COMMERCIALIZATION

## NATIONAL MARKET

In the wholesale market, 89% of the national production and 31% of the imported are commercialized.

The wholesalers distribute to other markets in San Salvador and throughout the country.

The maximum offer of fresh tomatoes occurs in the months of May, August and December. The commercialization channels include: intermediaries, wholesalers in the market, supermarkets, hypermarkets, stores, municipal markets, hotels and restaurants.

Direct sales: on the farmer's plot where the wholesalers or 'coyotes' come. Also

In addition, today the commercialization is carried out directly with supermarkets. They determine the delivery conditions - purchase terms: Agro-markets chains, which provide the fruit with a post-harvest process: washing, selection, packaging and labeling.

The commercialization is done in boxes, which have a weight of 22.7 kg and their price depends on the external offer from Mexico, Guatemala and Honduras. These prices vary from $8.00 to $20.00 per box; it is also influenced to a lesser extent by the time of year.

## QUALITY STANDARDS

The tomato fruits must be free of impurities. In the country there are no standards to classify them by quality; however, the most common problems are:

- Inadequate maturity
- Wilting or loss of firmness
- Mechanical damage
- Insect and mite attack
- Diseases.

## PRICE BEHAVIOR

The prices in the domestic market fluctuate depending on the season, but historically the best prices are in November and December of each year.

- Wholesalers transporters.
- Wholesalers-retailers.
- Retailers.

## XXI. SALES PRESENTATIONS

The tomato is commercialized at the level of producers, wholesalers and retailers, mainly in wooden boxes with a capacity of 55 lbs. Tomato for salad is also commercialized at the level of wholesaler and retailer in wooden boxes, but with a capacity of 13 kg net. There are other presentations at the level of retailers and supermarkets, which are in baskets, plastic bags and in some cases for demanding clients, they are presented in protected trays.

## XXII. TOMATO PASTA SEASONALITY

## SEASONALITY

The best prices to the wholesaler of tomato are historically in the month of November and December, as a consequence of the reduced offer due to the reduction in production due to the cessation of rains. Also, there is a relative increase in the months of June and July.

## HARVEST

If the farmer wants to take advantage of the December price window, the crop must be sown in the first fortnight of August to transplant in the second fortnight of September.
