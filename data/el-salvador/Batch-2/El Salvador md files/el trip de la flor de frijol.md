## Notice

This thrips, known as the bean flower thrips or "Asian bean thrips," is an invasive pest and was recently reported in Puerto Rico by the Diagnostic Clinic of the Agricultural Experiment Station of Juana Díaz, UPRM. This pest is very aggressive towards Fabaceae plants, and can cause yield losses of up to 30% to 60%. Therefore, it is recommended to use control methods for managing this pest.

## Sampling

Samples should be taken weekly, on flowers, at growth points, leaves, and pods. Use blue traps to determine the presence and increase of the thrips.

## Control Methods

## Chemical Control:

- The following products are considered effective in controlling this thrips in its adult stage: Spinetoram, Acetamiprid, Methomyl, Cyantraniliprole, and Abamectin.
- While for larvae or nymphs, Nuvaluron and Spirotetramat have been considered more effective.

## Biological Control:

- The use of neem, mineral oil, garlic extracts, papaya, and ginger could be used for management, after evaluation.

## Control Methods

## Micro-pathogenic Products:

Bacillus thuringiensis, Metarhizium anisopliae, and Akanthomyces attenuatues have been reported as micro-pathogens of this thrips.

## Biological Control

Some predators evaluated are: Ceranisus menes, Orius maxentis, Orius strigicollis, Orius sauteri.

## Cultural Management

- High rainfall reduces the population of bean flower thrips.
- High temperatures (86°F or higher) reduce the populations of nymphs or larvae.
- Remove crop residue from previous seasons and control wild hosts before planting.
- Planting barriers such as milo, sorghum, and castor beans helps to slow the arrival of the thrips.
- Planting with other crops interspersed reduces the movement of the thrips, as well as rotating with other crops that are not legumes.
