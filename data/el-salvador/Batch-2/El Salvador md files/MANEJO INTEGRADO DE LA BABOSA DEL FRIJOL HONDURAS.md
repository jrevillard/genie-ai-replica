## Introduction

Don Tomás Ramírez, a farmer from the El Pedregal community, grows beans and other crops on his farm, which allows him to have a diversified source of food for his family, and, as he usually has good harvests, he always has surpluses to sell.

He says that this success is due to the fact that he performs all the farming tasks at the right time and is very careful with pests, which can damage his crops.

One of the pests that affects him the most is the bean beetle. He says that this pest causes a lot of damage to the late-season crops, when in the community, farmers plant larger areas of this grain.

Don Tomás controls the bean beetle from the moment he prepares the soil for planting. He removes weeds, as weeds provide a habitat for the beetle.

He explains that he has learned to control the bean beetle, which he considers to be a very damaging pest, as if left unattended, it leaves no bean plant standing.

He recommends that the weeds and crop residues be thoroughly chopped, as this is where the pest makes its nest. "Bean beetles like damp places. This is the key. A bean beetle that doesn't exist is a bean beetle that doesn't cause damage."

He assures that if there are many bean beetles in the bean crop, they are removed manually, and if the number increases, they make poisoned baits and place them.

Today, we want to share the experience of Don Tomás and his actions to control the bean beetle, whose results allow him to harvest more beans.

## How does the bean beetle live?

Don't you all know the bean beetle? This animal lives in beans, gardens, and vegetable patches.

The bean beetle reproduces on its own, it doesn't need a male to lay eggs for the new bean beetles to hatch.

Every year, I've seen that during the rainy season, the bean beetle can lay up to three clutches of eggs. These are clutches of eggs, one on top of the other. Can you imagine? I've counted up to 50 and sometimes more.

I'll tell you that a single bean beetle can lay more than 200 eggs during its life, which is 12 to 18 months. This means that for every bean beetle, we have 200 more in just a few months.

The eggs are laid under leaves, stones, and branches. That's why it's important to remove or chop the debris in the field.

The bean beetle doesn't disappear. As the young beetles grow, they have the same characteristics as the adults. They start to lay eggs or reproduce between 2 and 5 months after hatching.

## What damage does the bean beetle cause?

The bean beetle feeds on the leaves, stems, and tender pods of the bean plant.

I have observed that the bean beetle causes the main damage in the first 20 days of the plant's life.

When the bean beetle eats the leaves, only the stem remains. This prevents the bean plant from developing, and in these conditions, there is no harvest.

The bean beetle is nocturnal. It attacks at night and on cloudy days. The sun's rays cause a lot of damage to it, that's why it's not seen during the day.

## Which crops does the bean beetle attack?

The bean beetle mainly attacks the bean crop. But it can also damage other crops such as cabbage, lettuce, cilantro, sweet potato, potato, pumpkin, peanuts, and other plants grown in the garden and in vegetable patches.

## How to control the bean beetle?

The best way to control the bean beetle is to prevent its presence, rather than spending money on chemicals and preparing poisoned baits.

The key is to control the snails in the early stages and not waste money on traps later.

## What practices should be used to prevent snails in the early and late stages?

- Thoroughly clear and remove weeds, debris, and crop residue.
- Keep the field and surrounding area clean.
- Regularly inspect the field to check for the presence of snails.

Manual removal: We also practice manual snail removal in the community. If there are a lot of snails in the field, they can be removed manually in two ways:

- 1. Night removal: At night, we go to the bean field with a flashlight and, using a stake, we kill the snails.

2. Daytime removal: We place trash in piles, where snails tend to hide from the sun. Using trash as a trap has given us good results in controlling snails.

- Place medium-sized trash or crop residue piles as traps.
- Place the traps in the bean field, at a distance of 10 steps apart.
- Lift the trash piles every three days and, using a stake, kill the snails that are found.

## When should chemical products be used to control snails?

This method should be carefully planned, as it is only used when the number of snails in the bean field warrants it, otherwise, it is a waste of money. There are two ways to control snails with chemical products:

One way is to purchase products like caracolicides, and the other is to make and place poisoned traps.

With chemical products, the growth and reproduction of snails are prevented, killing as many as possible. These products are already prepared and are called traps.

In agricultural stores, you can purchase ready-made traps such as matalapa, matababosa, or the same caracolicides.

However, to decide on chemical control, it is necessary to take samples or calculate the number of snails found in the bean field. For example, if I only see two, it is better to use a stake.

## Why is it necessary to take samples of snails in the bean field?

This is a way to determine whether it is advisable to use chemical products to control snails or any other pest.

It is simply a review of the damage and the extent of the damage to proceed to count how many snails are found in the most damaged parts of the crop.

This is called sampling because it is only done in certain parts of the crop.

## How to take a sample?

Select 10 bean plants from the area damaged by snails, and only inspect 5.

Count the number of snails found in the selected area.

Check if the number of snails found is more than 5. In the 10 plants inspected, apply poisoned traps.

## How to prepare poisoned traps?

A trap is a poisoned food to kill snails. It is a mixture of several ingredients that serve as filler with an insecticide and an attractant.

The attractant is to make the snail reach the trap and, when it eats it, it dies.

It is prepared with the hands, well protected with a plastic bag. We have also learned to prepare traps using plants that are in the community, such as the piñón.

Below, you will find several types of traps that are easy to prepare:

## Trap 1. Piñón seeds:

## Ingredients:

- 1 pound of piñón seeds.
- 1 pound of cornmeal, rice flour, oat bran, or tusa. These are substitutes or are used in the mixture.
- 4 ounces of sugar or 1/2 pound of sugar dissolved in water.

1/2 bottle of beer.

## Procedure:

Grind and crush the piñón seed (seed).

Pour the ground seed into a liter of hot water and then strain.

Mix the juice extracted from the piñón with beer and sugar, adding it gradually to the cornmeal mixture.

Fill a plastic bag with the mixture and, in one end, make a hole to form a cord with the mixture.

Cut the cord of mixture into small pieces and dry it in the shade.

## Bait 2. with chemical products:

## Ingredients:

2 ounces of Formaldehyde (1%).

- 9 pounds of wheat, rice, oat, or barley meal.
- 1 pound of molasses or brown sugar.
- 5 liters of water.

## Bait 3. with chemical products:

290 gr. of Methomyl 90 Ps (Lannate or Carbaryl 80 (Sevin).

Wheat, rice, oat, or barley meal.

5 liters of water.

5 liters of molasses or 5 pounds of brown sugar.

## Procedure for preparing these poisoned baits:

Thoroughly grind the meal, oat, or barley.

Dissolve the molasses or brown sugar in liters of water.

Add the pesticide to the sweet solution.

Mix all ingredients to obtain the bait.

## How to place the poisoned baits?

The first thing to keep in mind is that the soil should be moist, and there should be evidence of the bean weevil.

## Procedure:

1. Place the poisoned bait pouches every two rows and between each planting, taking two long steps.
2. Monitor the crop and, if the damage continues, reapply bait if necessary.

## Special Project for Food Security (PESA) in Honduras

The Special Project for Food Security (PESA) emerged as a commitment by the Government of Honduras, adopted at the World Food Summit in 1996 and subsequently ratified in the Millennium Declaration in 2000.

PESA is structured within the framework of the National Sustainable Rural Development Project (PRONADERS), which is promoted by the Ministry of Agriculture and Livestock, with funding from the Spanish Agency for International Cooperation (AECI) and technical assistance from the FAO.

Since its inception in 2000 as a pilot phase in the municipalities of San Lucas and San Antonio de Flores in the department of El Paraíso and Morolica in the department of Choluteca, PESA has contributed to the development of policies and strategies aimed at capitalizing on human resources, strengthening organizational structures, and improving production systems, in order to guarantee food security for the most vulnerable populations.

In its expansion phase 2005-2009, covering 29 municipalities in the departments of El Paraíso, Choluteca, Valle, Francisco Morazán, Yoro, and Intibucá, the aim is to reverse the degradation of natural resources, ensuring their sustainable management and use, and improving the quality of life, in accordance with the objectives of the State of Honduras in terms of food security. Activities will be based on local and municipal governance to combat poverty and improve human development indices.
