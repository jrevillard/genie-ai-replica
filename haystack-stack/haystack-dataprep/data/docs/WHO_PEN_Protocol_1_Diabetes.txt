WHO PEN Protocol 1 — Prevention of Heart Attacks, Strokes, and Kidney Disease through Integrated Management of Diabetes and Hypertension

PROTOCOL 1: DIABETES MANAGEMENT

1. SCREENING AND DIAGNOSIS
- Screen all adults aged 40+ for diabetes during any health facility visit
- Screen earlier (age 30+) if: family history, obesity (BMI > 25), history of gestational diabetes
- Diagnostic criteria:
  * Fasting plasma glucose >= 126 mg/dL (7.0 mmol/L) on two separate days
  * Random plasma glucose >= 200 mg/dL (11.1 mmol/L) with symptoms (polyuria, polydipsia, weight loss)
  * HbA1c >= 6.5% (48 mmol/mol)
- Pre-diabetes (impaired fasting glucose): 100-125 mg/dL (5.6-6.9 mmol/L) — lifestyle intervention

2. TREATMENT TARGETS
- Fasting blood glucose: 70-130 mg/dL (4.0-7.2 mmol/L)
- Post-meal blood glucose (2 hours): < 180 mg/dL (10.0 mmol/L)
- HbA1c target: < 7.0% (53 mmol/mol) for most patients
- HbA1c target: < 8.0% for elderly, frail, or those with frequent hypoglycemia
- Blood pressure target for diabetics: < 130/80 mmHg (stricter than general population)

3. LIFESTYLE MANAGEMENT (FIRST-LINE FOR ALL PATIENTS)
- Diet: reduce refined carbohydrates, increase vegetables, reduce salt to < 5g/day
- Physical activity: at least 150 minutes per week of moderate-intensity activity (brisk walking)
- Weight management: aim for BMI 18.5-24.9, or 5-10% weight loss if overweight
- Tobacco cessation: essential — smoking increases cardiovascular risk 2-4x in diabetics
- Alcohol: limit or avoid — increases hypoglycemia risk, especially with sulfonylureas

4. PHARMACOLOGICAL TREATMENT STEPWISE APPROACH
Step 1: Metformin 500mg once daily with food, increase to 500mg twice daily after 1-2 weeks
Step 2: If fasting glucose not at target after 3 months, increase Metformin to 1000mg twice daily
Step 3: If still not at target, add Gliclazide 40-80mg daily (sulfonylurea)
Step 4: If still not at target after 3-6 months on dual therapy, REFER for insulin initiation

IMPORTANT: Metformin is contraindicated in renal impairment (eGFR < 30), heart failure, liver disease
IMPORTANT: Sulfonylureas cause hypoglycemia — patient education on symptoms is essential

5. HYPOGLYCEMIA MANAGEMENT
- Symptoms: sweating, trembling, confusion, hunger, weakness, palpitations, blurred vision
- Blood glucose < 70 mg/dL = hypoglycemia
- IMMEDIATE ACTION: eat 15-20g fast-acting sugar (3-4 sugar cubes, half glass of juice, 1 tablespoon honey)
- Recheck blood glucose after 15 minutes — repeat if still < 70
- If unconscious: do NOT give anything by mouth, place in recovery position, call emergency (199)
- After recovery: eat a proper meal to prevent recurrence
- Severe hypoglycemia (< 50 mg/dL): MEDICAL EMERGENCY — needs IV dextrose at health facility

6. HYPERGLYCEMIA AND DKA WARNING SIGNS
- Blood glucose > 300 mg/dL with: nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, fruity breath, rapid breathing
- This is Diabetic Ketoacidosis (DKA) — MEDICAL EMERGENCY
- ACTION: Go to hospital IMMEDIATELY (EFSTH or nearest facility with IV fluids)
- Do NOT wait. Do NOT try to manage at home.

7. MONITORING SCHEDULE
- Self-monitoring blood glucose: daily if on insulin, 2-3x/week if on oral medications
- HbA1c: every 3-6 months at health facility
- Blood pressure: every visit
- Foot examination: every 6 months (check for sensation loss, ulcers, calluses)
- Eye examination: annually (diabetic retinopathy screening)
- Kidney function (urine protein, creatinine): annually
- Lipid profile: annually

8. FOOT CARE (CRITICAL FOR PREVENTION OF AMPUTATION)
- Wash feet daily with warm (not hot) water
- Dry thoroughly, especially between toes
- Apply moisturizer to prevent cracking (NOT between toes)
- Wear closed shoes that fit well — never walk barefoot
- Cut toenails straight across, not curved
- Check feet daily for cuts, blisters, redness, swelling
- Any non-healing wound on foot → visit health facility within 24 hours
- Loss of sensation in feet → higher risk of unnoticed injury

9. DIABETES IN PREGNANCY
- Pre-existing diabetes: tighten glucose control before conception
- Gestational diabetes: screen all pregnant women at 24-28 weeks
- Treatment: diet and exercise first, insulin if needed (NOT oral drugs in pregnancy)
- After delivery: recheck glucose at 6-12 weeks postpartum

10. RAMADAN FASTING WITH DIABETES
- HIGH RISK (should NOT fast): Type 1 diabetes, poorly controlled Type 2, history of severe hypoglycemia, renal impairment, pregnancy
- MODERATE RISK (fast with medical supervision): well-controlled Type 2 on metformin or diet alone
- Adjustments: move Metformin dose to Iftar, reduce sulfonylurea dose, check glucose 2-3 times during fasting
- BREAK FAST IMMEDIATELY if: glucose < 70 or > 300, symptoms of hypo/hyperglycemia
- Consult doctor BEFORE Ramadan to adjust medication schedule

11. REFERRAL CRITERIA (REFER TO HOSPITAL)
- Newly diagnosed Type 1 diabetes (any age)
- Blood glucose persistently > 300 mg/dL despite medication
- Recurrent severe hypoglycemia
- Suspected DKA (vomiting + high sugar + fruity breath)
- Pregnancy with diabetes
- Non-healing foot ulcer
- Progressive kidney disease (rising creatinine)
- Need for insulin initiation
- HbA1c > 9% despite dual oral therapy

GAMBIAN CONTEXT:
- Essential medicines available at health centres: Metformin, Gliclazide, Insulin (some centres)
- Blood glucose testing available at most health posts (glucometer)
- HbA1c testing available at regional hospitals and EFSTH
- CHW role: education, adherence support, foot checks, referral when needed
- Cost barrier: insulin requires cold chain — may need referral to facility with refrigeration
- Diet guidance: reduce white rice portions, add leafy vegetables (moringa, bitter leaf), reduce palm oil, use whole grains (millet/chere)
