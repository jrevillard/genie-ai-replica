KDIGO 2024 — Chronic Kidney Disease Evaluation and Management in Primary Care
Source: KDIGO 2024 Clinical Practice Guideline for the Evaluation and Management of CKD
Reference: https://kdigo.org/guidelines/ckd-evaluation-and-management/
Published: Kidney International, Volume 105, Issue 4S, April 2024

1. WHY CKD MATTERS FOR NCD MANAGEMENT

- CKD affects 10-15% of the global adult population
- Leading causes: diabetes (40%) and hypertension (30%) — the same NCDs AMINA manages
- CKD is largely SILENT until advanced stages — screening is essential
- Untreated CKD → kidney failure → dialysis ($10,000/year in The Gambia) or death
- Early detection and treatment can PREVENT progression to kidney failure
- CKD increases cardiovascular risk (heart attack, stroke) by 2-4 times
- In Sub-Saharan Africa: large gap between CKD burden and available healthcare

2. WHO TO SCREEN FOR CKD

Screen these patients at EVERY health facility visit:
- All patients with diabetes mellitus (any type)
- All patients with hypertension
- All patients with cardiovascular disease (previous heart attack, stroke)
- All patients aged 60 and above
- Patients with family history of kidney disease
- Patients with obesity (BMI > 30)
- Patients with systemic diseases affecting kidneys (lupus, HIV)
- Patients on long-term NSAIDs (ibuprofen, diclofenac) or other nephrotoxic drugs

HOW TO SCREEN:
1. Blood test: serum creatinine → calculate eGFR (estimated Glomerular Filtration Rate)
2. Urine test: urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio (uACR) — spot urine sample, morning preferred
3. Both tests needed — eGFR alone misses early kidney damage

3. CKD STAGING — GFR CATEGORIES

G1: eGFR ≥ 90 mL/min — Normal or high (kidney damage may be present if albuminuria positive)
G2: eGFR 60-89 mL/min — Mildly decreased
G3a: eGFR 45-59 mL/min — Mildly to moderately decreased
G3b: eGFR 30-44 mL/min — Moderately to severely decreased
G4: eGFR 15-29 mL/min — Severely decreased
G5: eGFR < 15 mL/min — Kidney failure (may need dialysis or transplant)

4. CKD STAGING — ALBUMINURIA CATEGORIES

A1: ACR < 30 mg/g — Normal to mildly increased (normal)
A2: ACR 30-300 mg/g — Moderately increased (microalbuminuria — early kidney damage)
A3: ACR > 300 mg/g — Severely increased (macroalbuminuria — significant kidney damage)

CKD is diagnosed when:
- eGFR < 60 mL/min sustained for > 3 months, OR
- Albuminuria (ACR ≥ 30 mg/g) sustained for > 3 months, OR
- Other markers of kidney damage (structural abnormalities on imaging)

Repeat testing is required for confirmation — single values cannot diagnose CKD.

5. RISK ASSESSMENT — PROGNOSIS BY GFR + ALBUMINURIA

COLOUR-CODED RISK MAP (GFR rows × Albuminuria columns):

GREEN (low risk): G1-G2 + A1 — monitor annually
YELLOW (moderate risk): G1-G2 + A2, or G3a + A1 — monitor every 6-12 months
ORANGE (high risk): G1-G2 + A3, G3a + A2, G3b + A1 — monitor every 3-6 months, consider referral
RED (very high risk): G3a + A3, G3b + A2-A3, G4-G5 any albuminuria — refer to nephrologist

6. MANAGEMENT BY STAGE

ALL STAGES — LIFESTYLE:
- Blood pressure control (target < 130/80 for CKD patients)
- Blood glucose control if diabetic (HbA1c < 7%)
- Dietary sodium restriction (< 5g salt/day — half Maggi cube)
- Maintain healthy weight (BMI 18.5-24.9)
- Regular physical activity (150 minutes/week moderate intensity)
- Stop smoking (accelerates kidney damage)
- Adequate hydration (2-2.5 liters water daily)
- Avoid NSAIDs (ibuprofen, diclofenac) — these worsen kidney function
- Avoid nephrotoxic herbs and traditional remedies — tell your doctor what you take

EARLY CKD (G1-G3a, ACR < 300):
- Lifestyle interventions above
- ACE inhibitor (Enalapril) or ARB (Losartan) if: albuminuria present + hypertension or diabetes
  * These PROTECT the kidneys — continue even if eGFR dips initially (up to 30% dip is expected and acceptable)
  * Monitor potassium and creatinine 1-2 weeks after starting
- Statin for cardiovascular protection (Simvastatin 20-40mg or Atorvastatin 10-20mg)
- SGLT2 inhibitor (if available): proven to slow CKD progression AND reduce cardiovascular events
  * Dapagliflozin 10mg or Empagliflozin 10mg daily
  * Can be used down to eGFR 20 mL/min
  * Benefits: slows kidney decline, reduces heart failure risk, modest weight loss, BP reduction
  * Side effects: genital infections (thrush), dehydration risk during Ramadan fasting

MODERATE CKD (G3b-G4):
- All above plus:
- Anaemia screening: check haemoglobin every 6 months (CKD causes anaemia)
  * If Hb < 10 g/dL: oral iron first, refer if not responding
- Bone health: check calcium, phosphate if available
  * CKD disrupts calcium-phosphate balance → bone weakening
- Dietary protein: moderate restriction (0.8 g/kg/day) — do NOT over-restrict in malnourished patients
- Potassium monitoring: CKD + ACEI/ARB can cause high potassium
  * Avoid potassium-rich foods in excess: bananas, oranges, groundnuts, tomatoes
  * If potassium > 5.5 mmol/L: reduce or stop ACEI/ARB, refer
- Medication dose adjustments: many drugs need dose reduction in CKD
  * Metformin: reduce dose if eGFR 30-45, STOP if eGFR < 30
  * Glibenclamide: AVOID in CKD (hypoglycemia risk increases)
  * Use Gliclazide instead (safer in CKD)

ADVANCED CKD (G5 — eGFR < 15):
- Refer to hospital (EFSTH) for kidney replacement therapy planning
- Options: dialysis (hemodialysis or peritoneal dialysis) or kidney transplant
- Conservative management may be appropriate for elderly/frail patients (focus on symptom control)
- Advance care planning discussion with patient and family

7. BLOOD PRESSURE TARGETS IN CKD

- All CKD patients: target < 130/80 mmHg (stricter than general population)
- First-line: ACEI (Enalapril 5-20mg) or ARB (Losartan 50-100mg) — especially if albuminuria present
- Second-line: add CCB (Amlodipine 5-10mg) or Thiazide (if eGFR > 30)
- For eGFR < 30: use loop diuretic (Furosemide) instead of Thiazide
- Monitor potassium and creatinine 1-2 weeks after starting/changing ACEI/ARB
- Expected initial eGFR dip of up to 30% is acceptable — do NOT stop medication for this

8. DIABETES + CKD MANAGEMENT

- Most important: blood glucose AND blood pressure control together
- HbA1c target: < 7% (but < 8% acceptable if hypoglycemia risk high or eGFR < 30)
- Metformin: safe if eGFR > 45, reduce dose if 30-45, STOP if < 30
- Gliclazide: preferred sulfonylurea in CKD (safer than glibenclamide)
- Insulin: may be needed earlier than usual (CKD affects drug clearance)
- SGLT2 inhibitors: dual benefit — protect kidneys AND control glucose
- ACEI/ARB: essential — protects kidneys from diabetic damage
- Monitor glucose more frequently: CKD changes insulin clearance (hypo risk increases)

9. MONITORING SCHEDULE

Low risk (green): annually — eGFR + uACR
Moderate risk (yellow): every 6-12 months — eGFR + uACR + BP + medication review
High risk (orange): every 3-6 months — eGFR + uACR + BP + potassium + haemoglobin
Very high risk (red): every 1-3 months — full panel + specialist involvement

WHAT TO CHECK AT EACH VISIT:
- Blood pressure
- eGFR trend (stable? declining? how fast?)
- Albuminuria trend
- Medication adherence
- New symptoms: fatigue, swelling (oedema), nausea, foamy urine, reduced urine output
- Review all medications for kidney safety

CONCERNING TRENDS (investigate or refer):
- eGFR decline > 20% from baseline
- Albuminuria doubling
- New or worsening oedema
- Potassium > 5.5 mmol/L
- Haemoglobin < 10 g/dL

10. REFERRAL CRITERIA — WHEN TO SEND TO HOSPITAL

REFER to nephrologist/hospital if:
- eGFR < 30 mL/min (CKD stage 4-5)
- eGFR 30-60 with significant albuminuria (ACR > 300)
- Rapid eGFR decline (> 5 mL/min/year or > 20% decline in 6 months)
- Persistent proteinuria > 1 g/day
- Uncontrolled hypertension despite 3 medications at adequate doses
- Persistent potassium > 5.5 mmol/L
- Anaemia not responding to iron therapy (Hb < 10)
- Suspected glomerulonephritis (blood + protein in urine + declining eGFR)
- CKD in pregnancy
- CKD in children or young adults (< 30 years)
- Uncertainty about cause of CKD

11. PATIENT EDUCATION — KEY MESSAGES

- "Your kidneys filter your blood like a water filter. When the filter gets damaged, waste builds up."
- "Diabetes and high blood pressure are the two biggest enemies of your kidneys."
- "Taking your BP medicine and diabetes medicine PROTECTS your kidneys — even when you feel fine."
- "Kidney damage is SILENT for years. By the time you feel sick, much damage is already done."
- "Reducing salt and controlling your blood pressure is the single most important thing you can do."
- "Do NOT take ibuprofen or other painkillers without asking your health worker — they can hurt your kidneys."
- "Tell your doctor about ALL medicines you take, including herbs from the marabout."
- "Drink enough water — but not too much. 2-2.5 liters per day is right for most people."

GAMBIAN CONTEXT:
- Dialysis available only at EFSTH — extremely limited capacity and costs ~$10,000/year
- Prevention is the ONLY sustainable strategy
- Creatinine testing available at health centres and regional hospitals
- Urine dipstick (for protein) available at most health posts — basic screening tool
- ACEI (Enalapril) and ARB (Losartan) on essential medicines list
- NSAIDs (ibuprofen, diclofenac) widely available OTC — patient education critical
- Traditional remedies may contain nephrotoxic substances — patients must inform their doctor
- CHW can monitor BP and check for oedema (swelling of feet/ankles) during home visits
- Refer early — late referral to EFSTH means worse outcomes
