## Origin

The origin center of the potato is located between Peru and Bolivia, near Lake Titicaca for the sub-species andigenum, although there are many wild species in Mexico, Guatemala, Ecuador and Chile; in the latter, Isla Chiloe is considered the secondary center of the tuberosum sub-species (Bukasov, 1933). In 1537, Juan de Castellanos made the first reference to the cultivation of potatoes in Peru.

## Key aspects for the development of the sector

- Planting of specialized varieties for the agro-industry, such as the production of flakes (chips) using varieties that possess a high content of dry matter such as: Atlantic, Alpha, Norchip, and for the preparation of french fries (a la francesa), the varieties: Idiafrit, Santa Ana and Mondial, which are demanded by restaurant chains and fast food.
- Production of varieties for fresh consumption with high yield potential and with the culinary characteristics that consumers like, such as: elongated potato with light skin, with superficial eyes and creamy pulp.
- Promotion of potato consumption, as it is a cheap food that can be used to prepare more than 1000 different dishes.

## Botanical description

The potato belongs to the Solanaceae family. The cultivated species are Tetraploid (2n=48) which belong to the species Solanum tuberosum and Solanum andigenum.

Solanum tuberosum is the potato that was brought to Europe by the Spanish and domesticated in these countries, it is generally of days and short cycles; (90 to 100 days) with an elongated shape, smooth skin, superficial eyes, the pulp color is cream to yellow and the skin is pink, red or beige, and has short stolons.

Solanum andigenum is of long days, with a late cycle (round shape, and deep eyes, skin color variable (purple, red, white, black and combined); the pulp is white or yellow, and is cultivated by the countries of South America. There are varieties that are a mixture of both species.

## SEED

Generally, the term seed refers to the tuber selected or intended for reproduction and potato production; however, the true seed is produced in a round, oval or elongated cone-shaped berry with a diameter of 1 to 3 cm, with a green color, in which the sexual potato seed is found, the shape and color of this is similar to that of the tomato, but with half its size; it is dicotyledonous, with a weight of 0.5 mg. In one gram there are 1600 seeds and an average of 200 seeds per berry and 20 berries per plant.

## Categories of seed

Commercially, there are different types of seed and potato categories, including:

Genetic seed: is the seed or plant that has been produced under the supervision of a technical improvement program and constitutes the fundamental initial base of the basic or nuclear seed.

Basic seed or nuclear seed: is the one that is produced under the supervision of a plant improvement technical program, and maintains its specific genetic identity and purity. It can be given to producers to use it in the production of registered or certified seed.

Registered seed: is the one that comes from basic or registered seed and is treated to maintain the original identity and genetic purity.

Certified seed: is the seed that is produced under the supervision of a certification service. It can originate from basic, registered or certified seed, always maintaining its variety identity and complying with the requirements for this category.

Improved seed: is the one that does not meet the requirements of the certified category, maintains its variety identity and good production capacity.

## Germination

- a) The tuber seed, to germinate, must go through a dormancy period of 2 to 3 months; after that period, it emits sprouts of 0.5 to 1 cm in length, and when the tuber is ready to be planted. The emergence of the plant occurs after 12 days of being planted.
- b) The sexual seed germinates generally after 8 to 10 days of being planted and having passed through a dormancy period of 4 to 6 months. This seed is used in genetic improvement programs for the obtaining of new varieties. It has the advantage of its low cost and ease of transport,

## ROOT

In plants derived from sexual seeds, the main root is fibrous, originating from which lateral branches form a fibrous system.

The root formed from a tuber seed is fibrous, there is no main root and it has many adventitious roots. Its greatest growth occurs in the first 0.20 m of depth, extending laterally from 0.30 to 0.60 m. The fibrous lateral roots can reach up to 1.20 m in depth, in well-drained and deep soils.

## Plant Development

- a) The plant derived from sexual seed has slow initial growth, with a single stem that sometimes branches, reaching a height of 0.40 to 0.90 m at 60 days, when flowering begins (this is when it flowers). The leaves are compound, similar to those of the potato tuber
- b) Plants derived from tuber seed produce herbaceous, erect stems, which can explain or determine their trailing or semi-trailing growth, and sometimes branch. The leaves are compound, presenting a terminal leaf, some secondary lateral leaves, leaf sheaths, a rachis and pseudo-stem leaves; it reaches its maximum growth at 35 or 40 days. The height of the plant varies from 0.40 to 0.90 m.

## Flowering

The flower is pentamerous and zygomorphic, it has 5 yellow, orange and a single pistil stamens. The potato inflorescence is a terminal top that can be simple or compound. The color of the flowers is variable: pink, white, purple (various shades) or a mixture of 2 colors.

Not all varieties derived from potato and sexual seed flower and form berries, in varieties derived from sexual seed, flowering is delayed by about two weeks.

## Fructification Pattern

In potato cultivation, two patterns of fructification are distinguished: the production of berries, where the true sexual seed is found, and the production of tubers (fructification)

## Berry Production

- a. Flowers generally self-pollinate at 98%; and a 2% of cross-pollination. The flowers remain open for 3 to 5 days, then the petals fall and the ovary begins to grow and form a green, round berry, generally reaching a diameter of up to 2.5 cm. Inside it grow the seeds, 200 per berry. The maturation time of the berries is 45 to 60 days after flowering.
- b. Production of tubers (fructification) The tubers begin to form from stolons, which are lateral stems that grow within the soil and are emitted by the main stems, when the plant begins to flower (in varieties that flower); this generally occurs from 35 to 45 days after planting. The tubers are formed at 60 days, developing until the plant reaches its physiological maturity: 90 days for early varieties, and 110 to 120 for intermediate varieties, and more than 120 for late varieties.

## Phenological and Growth Stages

Seed dormancy or rest: is the period that occurs between harvest and sprouting. For the tuber seed, this stage lasts 2-3 months, and for the sexual seed, 4 to 6 months. Dormancy can be broken or induced by wounds or some disease in the tuber; in these cases, sprouting occurs in a shorter time. It can also be induced by chemical treatment, using gibberellic acid, at a dose of 1 to 5 ppm.

Sprouting: occurs when the buds of the tubers begin to emerge; it lasts 2 to 3 months, then the potato is ready to be sown; it is ideal for the tubers to have at least 3 short and strong sprouts, and have a length of 0.5 to 1 cm.

## Emergence

The sprouts emerge at 10-12 days in tubers, and 8 to 10 days in sexual seed, when planted in the field and have the appropriate temperature and humidity in the soil, for their development.

## Stem Development

At this stage, there is simultaneous growth of foliage and roots; it lasts between 20 to 30 days.

## Tubrification and Flowering

Flowering is a sign that the potato begins to produce stolons or initiates tuberization. In early varieties, this occurs 30 days after planting; in intermediate varieties, between 35 and 45 days; and in late varieties between 50 and 60 days. This stage lasts for about 30 days.

## Tubers Development

The tubers reach physiological maturity at 75 days, in early varieties, 90 days for intermediate and 120 days for late varieties. At this stage, the tubers can be harvested and stored.

## Climatic and Soil Requirements

## Temperature

The production of potatoes in the tropics is favored by the climate conditions that occur in high-altitude lands, where the temperature is relatively cool due to the fact that potatoes require temperatures of 15 to 20°C for tuberization (formation of tubers) and growth.

Potatoes are considered a thermo-periodic plant, which means that there is a need for a variation, between the daytime and nighttime temperature, of at least 10°C. If the difference is smaller, the growth and tuberization are affected. When this situation occurs frequently, throughout the growing season, the yield and quality are affected, as high temperatures are ideal for the growth of stems and leaves, but not for the tubers.

Temperature influences the sprouting of seed tubers, the utilization of nutrients, water loss, and the phenological stages of the crop. According to Fulgueira (1982), the best yields in the temperate region are obtained under the conditions of the temperatures mentioned above, and with 12 to 16 hours of light (depending on the species cultivated).

## Light Hours

In the country, potato cultivation behaves better with periods of 8 to 12 hours of light. The luminosity that the plants receive during the day affects the function of the chloroplasts and triggers a series of reactions in which carbon dioxide and water intervene, which help in the formation of the different types of sugars that pass on to form part of the tubers. In addition, the luminosity

influences photosynthesis and photoperiods required by the plants.

## Precipitation

The precipitation or optimal amount of water required is 600 mm, distributed throughout its growing cycle; the largest demands occur in the germination and growth stages of the tubers, for which it is necessary to carry out supplementary irrigation during critical periods or when there is no rain.

## Wind

The wind should be moderate, as plants do not tolerate winds with speeds greater than 20 km/h, without causing damage or affecting yields.

## Altitude

The ideal altitude for the development and production of potato crops for consumption is between 1,500 and 2,500 meters above sea level, but it can be cultivated at lower altitudes such as Zapotitán, which is located at 460 meters above sea level, during the dry season (November to February) when there are conditions of low temperatures.

## Soils

The best soils are loamy, sandy loam, franco-limy, and franco-clay, with a light texture, good drainage, and an effective depth greater than 0.50 m, which allow the free growth of stolons and tubers and facilitate harvesting.

## Soil Preparation

Soil preparation should be carried out as early as possible before planting, in order to favor the decomposition of the residues of the previous harvest and to induce the early germination of weeds, for their good control at the time of planting. These practices vary depending on the topographic conditions of the land.

## Soils on Slopes

Soil preparation is carried out manually with a hoe, first performing a plowing; then a harrowing of the soil, and subsequently, at the time of planting, it is preferred to furrow in curves at the level. Some soil conservation works should be implemented such as: live fences and terraces. Sometimes the use of animal traction is feasible.

<!-- image -->

## Flat or Low-Sloping Soils

In these soils, animal or motorized traction can be used, or both. The preparation works depend largely on the crop that has been sown previously.

When using machinery, the soil is prepared with a reverse-reversible furrow plow (or disc plow) at a depth of 0.30 m.

If you plan to use gravity irrigation, the direction of the furrows should be in the direction of the ridges, in addition to leveling the soil.

Then apply two passes with a heavy-duty plow in a cross pattern. After that, a rotavator can be used to leave the soil well tilled, to obtain good growth of roots, stolons and tubers, and to ensure good aeration in the soil. Before this work, herbicides can be applied to control weeds.

## Planting Systems

## Planting with Sexual Seed

In this planting system, the use of seed trays is necessary to produce minitubers or seedlings for transplanting. Based on the experiences in this planting system, the CENTA suggests the production of minitubers, as it is possible to obtain seed for planting of commercial potatoes (in large quantities) in a small space.

## Seedling Bed Preparation

## Seedling Production

180 m 2 of seedling bed is required to produce seedlings or minitubers to plant one manzana (0.7 ha). This requires 60 g of seed.

Finally, the furrowing is carried out, whose spacing will depend on the variety to be used, the end of the production and the planting season. Currently, two planting systems are being used, using: sexual seed or asexual seed (seed tuber).

Seedlings can be produced for transplanting, either using trays with an appropriate substrate (growing mix) or in beds, in a ratio of soil, fine sand, bovine manure 2:1:1, respectively. Seed that is not yet dormant is used.

## Planting in Rows or Beds

* Run the soil with a suitable implement over the moistened or substrate; leaving 0.20 m between each row.
* Deposit the seeds at a distance of 2.5 cm between each other, and at a depth of 0.5 to 1.0cm.
* Cover with the same substrate.
* Prevent the problem of pathogens (Rhizoctonia, Fusarium, Pythium) in the soil. Apply Benomyl to the soil and the base of the seedlings immediately after transplanting, at a dose of 5g/ 4l of water.
* The emergence of the seedlings occurs between 8 to 10 days after planting, under appropriate temperature conditions (optimal temperature - oscillates between 15 to 20 o C).
* Approximately 7 to 10 days after emergence, thinning or weeding should be carried out, leaving seedlings 5 cm apart.
* Seedlings with any type of abnormality should be removed.

In places where the temperature is above 25 o C, it is recommended to protect the seedlings with shade, but avoiding that the light intensity is less than 50% to not produce plants with very thin stems.

The shade should be gradually removed, exposing the plants daily to sunlight, starting from the early morning hours.

After 25 or 30 days of emergence, the seedlings have between 5 to 6 well-defined leaves and measure from 8 to 14 cm in height.

Planting of seedlings with sexual seed, in Las Pilas, Dept. of Chalatenango.

The level of fertilization should be applied, every 15 days, 30 grams of formula 15-15-15 per m 2, plus applications with foliar fertilizer that contains minor elements, at a dose of 7 ml/m 2, considering that the plants will remain in the beds until harvest.

The tubers-seeds produced can be used as seed material for potato production for consumption or to start a new multiplication cycle as seed tuber.

## Seed Potato Renovation

If the seed potato is cared for properly, it should be renewed every 2 to 3 years, otherwise (and this usually happens in El Salvador) the producer will renew it year after year.

## Storage of Seed Potatoes

The potato intended for seed must undergo curing, selecting healthy material, free of wounds or damage caused by pests. The selected tubers should be treated with Phoxin 1% (Volatón), 50 g for 45.45 kg of seed, and stored in seed trays, silos, or in boxes or plastic bags, stacking them on top of each other, no more than five. Approximately 45.45 kg of potatoes can be stored in one square meter.

The storage place must be well ventilated, with low humidity, indirect light (this promotes sprouting, with a temperature of less than 20°C and a relative humidity of more than 70%, in these conditions the potatoes quickly turn green and short, robust green shoots appear).

It is essential to maintain the vegetative vigor of the buds, as these, in the absence of light, become long and weak and produce plantations with low yields.

The potatoes should sprout 2-3 months after being stored. The use of traps with potato moth pheromones to detect and control them is recommended. In addition, it should be sampled weekly to remove any tubers infested with fungi or bacteria, and to detect the presence of aphids or any other type of pest.

## Predominant Production Systems in the Country

Potatoes are grown in monoculture, although in the past, in the Zapotitán valley, Ciudad Arce, Dept. de La Libertad, the potato-maize association was used with very good results. Maize was sown in an intercalated manner 45 days after planting the potatoes.

## Planting Season

In the country, in the Las Pilas area, Chalatenango, the potato can be cultivated throughout the year, as the nighttime temperature in the area is always less than 18°C at night and more than 18°C during the day; the only requirement is to have a sprouted seed potato.

In Zapotitán, Ciudad Arce, Dept. de La Libertad, located at 460 msnm, the potato can only be cultivated during the dry season (November to February) when the temperatures are cool at night (up to 15°C), and during the day, up to 30°C.

## Fertilization

The cost of fertilizers represents between 7 and 10% of the production cost; in some cases up to 30%, this is according to the level of technological development of the crop.

To decide how to fertilize the potato and add the necessary and appropriate amount of nutrients to the soil, it is necessary to carry out a soil analysis, determining the content of Nitrogen (N), phosphorus (P), potassium (K), calcium (Ca), magnesium (Mg), aluminum (Al), organic matter, total acidity, apparent density, texture and pH.

The nutritional requirements of potatoes (according to Christiansen 1967) are: 150 kg. N, 120 kg. P2O5 and 90 kg K2O. In addition, it requires moderate amounts of Mg, S and some micronutrients such as: B, Ca, Mo, Mn, Fe, Cu, and Zn.

When very high amounts of nitrogen are applied in relation to the availability of the other elements, it induces the production of very large potatoes, but it reduces the starch content. In addition, it increases the susceptibility of the plants to pests, especially those caused by fungi and bacteria.

P2O5 and K2O should be applied in full at the time of planting; Urea 45% or Ammonium Nitrate 33% can be used as a source of nitrogen; triple superphosphate 46% and simple phosphate 20% can be used as a source of P2O5; and Muriate of Potash 0-0-60, Potassium Sulfate (formula 0-0-5017), or complete formulas such as 15-15-15 or 18-46-0, 12-24-12 can be used as a source of K2O.

<!-- image -->

In the Atzimba variety, the greatest nutrient absorption occurs during the first 56 days after planting. The plants absorb nitrogen during the period before flowering, so it is advisable to fertilize when the first flowers appear. In relation to the absorption of phosphorus by the plants, this occurs throughout the crop cycle.

In acidic soils such as those in Las Pilas, Chalatenango, the availability of minor elements (Fe, Mn, Zn, Cu, and B), except for Mo, is greater; therefore, the crop will extract more of these micronutrients per unit of production, which is why it is necessary to apply foliar fertilizers to maintain the extraction of these micronutrients; for example, to produce 1 ton of potatoes, it is necessary to apply 0.6 to 0.8 kg of Ca, S and Mg; 80 to 120 g of Fe and 5 to 10 ppm of Zn, Mn, Cu, Mo and B.

## Irrigation

In the country, potato cultivation thrives in areas with abundant rainfall or with water availability for irrigation, as the effective root system of the potato is between 0.20 and 0.60 m deep, requiring 500 to 700 mm of water during its vegetative period.

During the dry season, the crop requires frequent and light watering to maintain the soil at field capacity, as low moisture levels negatively affect the yield, size and quality of the potato.

The sudden changes in soil moisture content cause deformations in the tubers and increased attack by potato fly larvae. Excessive moisture favors the spread of bacteria (Ralstonia solanacearum), fungi (Phythopthora infestans), and it is recommended to maintain a permissible depletion of 30 to 35% of the usable water in the soil.

The irrigation methods used are: gravity (in straight furrows and ridges): applying water through channels and siphon tubes; sprinkling: in which commercial and handmade sprinklers are used; these latter have the disadvantage that the discharges (flow rate) and the operating pressure vary along the irrigation line.

In flat soils, where potatoes are grown, it is recommended to level them and leave them with a very gentle slope of 1:1000, depending on the type of soil; using appropriate machinery for this purpose; in addition, the water distribution system within the plot should be designed, for example: channels, furrows and drains.

Potatoes are relatively sensitive to water deficits, so they should not be depleted more than 30 to 35% of the total available, especially during the formation and growth of the tubers. It is recommended to use tensiometers to determine the time to irrigate; calibrating them according to the type of soil and the location.

## Critical stages of the crop

All stages are critical in potato cultivation, especially during the formation and growth of the tubers, which is from 35 to 80 days after planting.

To decide on the water delivery method to use, it is necessary to evaluate the technical, economic and social feasibility for its choice; however, gravity methods, such as in the case of straight furrows on soils with slopes not exceeding 2.5% or furrows on contour with slopes of the order of 5%, are feasible.

## Additional information

The term "aporco" or "calza" refers to the deposition of soil in the trunk or neck of the plant, to improve its support and tuber production. In short-stem varieties, it is recommended to apply one aporco at 35 days after planting, subsequently a fungicide application should be made to prevent late blight damage.

For long-stem varieties, it is advisable to apply two aporcos: the first at 25 days after planting and the second at 40-45 days after planting (at tuberization).

## INSECTS

## WEEVIL, CATERPILLAR, BLIND BUTTERFLY, CHORONTOCO

(Phyllophaga sp,) (Coleoptera: Scarabeidae).

The adult, known as weevil; is light brown or dark in color, has quite long legs. It is abundant in May and June (the beginning of the rainy season). The females lay eggs that are pearl-white and more or less rounded. After 2 or 3 weeks, the larvae hatch, which go through 3 stages. The larva, known as chorontoco, caterpillar, white worm and blind butterfly, is a curved worm, creamy white, about 3 cm long, the head is light brown; it subsequently goes through the prepupa and pupa stages. In these last stages it spends the dry season and emerges as an adult at the beginning of the rains in the following year.

## CATERPILLARS OF BLIND BUTTERFLY (Phyllophaga spp).

The biennial species are those that cause the greatest damage, because in the sowing period (May-June) there is already a large number of large larvae in the soil that cause damage to the forming roots, to the newly planted tubers, and during the formation of tubers. The damage they cause is lacerations in the already developed tubers, which lose their quality and become susceptible to damage from soil fungi and bacteria.

<!-- image -->

The species that have been identified in El Salvador are: Phyllophaga menetriesi and Phyllophaga elenans, with annual and biennial cycles, respectively.

## Control

* Crop rotation (the most used)
* Soil preparation in advance of sowing to allow biological controllers to act: chickens, birds, insects etc.
* Adults can be controlled with light lamps
* Sowing in the last week of May and first week of June.
* Chemical control is used when sampling indicates that there are more than 4 large larvae per m2, and Phoxin 2.5 GR can be used at a dose of 64.9 kg /ha or Chlorpirifos 2.5 G at a rate of 21 to 28 kg/ha

WHITEFLY (Tecia solanivora and Phthorimaea operculella) (Lepidoptera: Gelechiidae).

They are known as whiteflies, their cycle is completed in 21 to 25 days, passing through the stages of egg, larvae (4 stages) pupa and adult.

The damage they cause oscillates between 5 to 15% of the total production, they cause the damage by perforating those tubers that are close to the surface of the soil or uncovered.

The stage in which a large number of adults are found in the field is between the sixth and eighth week after sowing, which coincides with the formation of tubers.

The populations of Tecia solanivora are always greater than those of Phthorimaea operculella, in a ratio of 60 to 40, both in the field and in storage.

The greatest damage is caused during the dry season and during storage.

## Control

The integrated control of these pests includes:

* Use of traps with pheromones (8 traps per apple tree is recommended).
* High altitude.
* Irrigation during the dry season.
* Use of insecticides when passing the critical point (60 adults per trap per week) Phloxin can be used at a dose of 1.5 lt/ha (in the field) or Acetamiprid at a dose of 0.5 kg/ha.

## LEAF PESTS

## WHITEFLY

(Bemisia tabaci, Trialeurodes sp)

The whitefly has become the main
problem for many crops. It is a sucking insect, with the species *Bemisia tabaci* being the most widespread, distributed throughout the central and coastal zone of the country, at altitudes ranging from 0 to 800 m above sea level. Recently, the species *Trialeurodes sp* has been found at altitudes of up to 2400 m above sea level.

Both species remain in the egg state for 5 to 10 days, placed on the underside of the leaves, and the nymphs for 12 to 28 days. These are translucent, yellow to yellowish-green, passing through four stages from nymph to pupa. The nymphs suck sap from the underside of the leaves.

The adult measures 1 to 2 mm in length, white in color with two pairs of wings, and is very active and agile, flying quickly from its feeding sites, spreading viruses to healthy plants. The most important damage caused by these insects is the transmission of geminivirus and other types of viruses in potato and tomato.

## Integrated Management

Cultural control can be carried out using live barriers such as maize or sorghum, which should be planted around the planting area 40 days before transplanting, thus preventing the pest from reaching the crop at an early stage.

Another option for cultural control is the use of yellow traps with oil or glue, which should be placed at a height of 0.25 to 0.75 m, depending on the size of the crop, installed with the prevailing wind to capture a greater number of possible flies. One of the critical points for carrying out chemical control is when 15 flies are found per trap.

## Biological Control.

There are predators, parasitoids and entomopathogenic fungi of whiteflies such as: *Ectomermis sp*, *Encarcia sp*, *Chrysopa sp*, Coccinellids, Spiders and fungi such as *Verticillium lecanii* and *Bauberia bassiana*.

## Chemical Control.

Applications with chemical products should be carried out when 15 whiteflies are found per plant, recommending the use of insecticides with different active ingredients and in alternating form. Within the recommended insecticides are: Acetamiprid at a dose of 0.37 to 0.5 kg per hectare; Imidacloprid at a dose of 0.21 to 0.36 kg per hectare; Imidacloprid + Cyflutrin at a rate of 286 cc per hectare.

It is necessary to follow the recommendations of the product labels to make good use of them and avoid environmental contamination.

## MINING FLY

( *Liriomyza sp*, Diptera: Agromyzidae).

This pest presents a large number of host plants; in the country it has been found in beans, potatoes, chili peppers, celery and cabbage. Its cycle is 15 to 18 days, the adults lay eggs on the epidermis of the leaves, where the larvae hatch 1 or 2 days later. The larvae begin to feed on the mesophyll of the leaves, leaving only the epidermis. The most critical stage is when the attack occurs just after the crop has emerged. It can destroy plantations with up to 70 days of planting.

## Control

Control can be carried out in an integrated manner, using yellow traps with glue, which are placed (23 traps / ha) around the plantation in this way, taking advantage of the existing parasitoids ( *Halticóptera sp*, *Opius sp*). To control the adults, apply Cartap or Tiociclam-H-oxalate at a dose of 0.71 kg/Ha, and Bifentrin at a dose of 1 liter diluted in 200 liters of water, in early hours (6 to 8 am) or from 16 to 18 pm.

## Aphids

( *Myzus persicae*) (Homoptera: Aphidae).

The life cycle is short. Generally, it reproduces by parthenogenesis. The nymphs are born directly alive from the winged or adult female. This aphid can transmit more than 100 different types of viruses, as it acquires them when sucking sap from a diseased plant.

<!-- image -->

The main damage is caused by transmitting viral diseases to plants (PLRV and PVY), whose effect is noticeable in a reduction in yield up to 50%, and in the non-germination of sprouts in tubers-seeds. Aphids also attack potatoes during storage, when they begin to appear.

Other economically important aphid species commonly found in the country are: *Macrosiphum euphorbiae* and *Aphis gossypii*. They are generally located in the upper third of the plant and on the underside of the leaves.

## Control

Control MIP, using yellow traps with glue or water, taking as a critical point an winged aphid per trap per day.

It can also be controlled with organic or botanical insecticides such as NIM, at a dose of 40 gr/lt of water, or oil of NIM, at a dose of 1.5 lt/Há or using Cyflutrin, 1.5 lt/Há.

## Potato PLANT HOOK

( *Paratrioza cockerelli*)

It is an insect of the order Homoptera and is classified within the family Chermidae, also known as Psyllidae. The insect's body measures about 2mm, its appearance is similar to that of an aphid, with the abdominal segments dark in color and the space between segments yellow. On the dorsal side of the abdomen, you can see a cream-colored stripe, which gives the appearance of a belt. Its habit is jumping and it feeds on the plant's sap.

During its feeding, the nymphs inject a toxin that causes a disease known as 'yellowing by plant hook' (Psyllid yellows) or purple tip, as in advanced stages of the disease, the leaves turn purple.

After mating, the females lay their eggs on the margins of the leaves, which are oval and orange in color and attached to the leaf by an colorless pedicel. Subsequently, it passes through five nymphal stages in the form of a scale and reaches the adult stage.

<!-- image -->

Its threshold temperature is 7º C and requires 336 heat units (HU) to pass from eggs to adults.

Infested plants initially show an upright growth habit, mainly on the younger leaves. The leaves curl towards the apex, starting from the base, it can also acquire a purple or yellow pigmentation. The stem has a more woody appearance and there may be swelling of the nodes, sometimes the internodes are shorter and may appear aerial tubers. When the population of nymphs is very high, the plant presents a yellow color and eventually dries up. For a severe manifestation of the symptoms, it requires more than 15 nymphs per plant. The adults, even in populations of 1000 individuals per plant, do not cause damage.

In the underground part, you can see a cortical discoloration and the tubers can deform or develop very close to the stolon, which gives a rosary appearance. In some varieties, the number of tubers can increase, but they do not reach the commercial size.

In the interior of the tuber, a grayish color is present, which in many cases makes it impossible to consume. On the other hand, the affected tubers emit thin and pointed sprouts, and many of them definitely do not sprout. When the sprouts age, small tubers can form at their apex, which can range from 5 to 15 mm in diameter.

## Control

Use yellow traps with glue for detection, foliage sampling for nymph detection. The critical point is one nymph per plant.

## Chemical Control

Tyociclam H oxalate at a dose of 429 gr per hectare, Imidacloprid + Cyflutrim at a dose of 286 ml per hectare, Imidacloprid 429 g per hectare and Endosulfan at a dose of 1.5 l per hectare.

## ACARO

(Polyphagota rsonemus latus) (Acari: Eryophididae)

It is a mite, whose life cycle is completed in 8 to 12 days, passing through the stages of eggs, nymphs (34 stages) and adults. It attacks during periods of drought, especially in low areas such as Zapotitán (460 msnm). The damage is caused in the terminal leaves, which turn a cobweb-like color when the attack is high. Sometimes this damage can be mistaken for symptoms of virosis.

## Control

Carry out sampling every 3 days during the crop cycle, using products such as: Chlorfenapir at a dose of 1 cc/l of water; Flufenoxuron 100 cc/ 25 l of water.

## Diseases

## LATE TOMATO BLIGHT

(Phytophthora infestans)

It is a fungus of the order Peronosporales, family Pythiaceae. On the leaves, it causes irregular and dark spots, which quickly enlarge, especially during the rainy season.

It attacks the stem and terminal shoots, when it acts in this way, the affected plants do not last more than 3 days, as the attack can reach 100% of the plantation. It also damages the tubers, where brown spots are noticeable on their surface.

The ideal conditions for its reproduction and attack are: high relative humidity 80%, temperature of 15 to 22°C.

## Control

Use of: certified seed and tolerant varieties.

Plant in rows at ground level to ensure good drainage and at spacings of 100 to 120 cm between rows, to ensure good aeration in the crop. Use of systemic fungicides such as: Dimetomorf, Metalaxil + Mancozeb, at a dose of 1.5 to 2 kg/ha, in the initial stages of growth until flowering. After flowering, only contact fungicides such as Chlorotalonil and Mancozeb should be used, at a dose of 2 to 3 kg/ha.

## BACTERIAL WILT

(Ralstonia solanacearum)

It is a bacterium of the family Pseudomonaceae, it has 2 virulent races (race 1 and 3 biovar 2a) that cause damage to the potato crop. The primary source of infestation is the seed tuber. To reproduce, it requires a minimum temperature of 5°C and an optimal temperature of 22°C and a maximum of 37°C. The initial symptom is a wilting of the plant, as if there is a lack of moisture in the soil.

Affected tubers present a dark halo when parted or when exposed to sunlight; from the eyes or buds, some mucuous secretions come out.

There is a test that can be carried out at field level and consists of cutting part of the plant stem and placing it in a transparent glass with water, and if the detachment of a silver thread is observed, it is certain that the bacteria is present. The presence of latent bacteria in tubers can also be detected by the Elisa method at low concentrations of 10 2, this test is carried out in the laboratory, requiring 250 tubers for sampling per hectare.

## Control

- Use healthy seed
- Plant in areas where potatoes have not been planted for 3 years
- Crop rotation with corn, cabbage, beans,

lettuce, etc.
- Drain to avoid waterlogging, excess moisture
- Destruction of diseased plants: burning or burying them.
- Disinfect tools and machinery used in infected fields with a 5% chlorine solution or a 3% formalin solution.

Irrigation through furrows helps a lot in the spread of this disease.

## VIRUS

The potato spindle tuber virus (PSTV) and the soft rot viruses are caused by the PVY and PVX viruses. These are common in the country, in most of the plantations cultivated with solanaceous plants. The vector is the aphid Myzus persicae.

The virus that causes losses of up to 50% of the production in the primary infection is PSTV and in secondary infections it can reach up to 80% of losses.

The symptoms are erect and pale yellow leaves; in some varieties there is a red or purple pigmentation and growth of rolled leaves at the base of the plant.

In secondary infection, caused by diseased tubers, there is a stunted plant, upright and rigid leaves and typical curling of the leaves.

Virus Y (PVY). It is the second most important virus worldwide. It attacks most solanaceous plants, causing necrosis along the veins, on the underside of the leaf. When this virus is combined with PVX, it causes the disease known as rough mosaic; sometimes the secondary symptoms are: strong stunting and the presence of mosaic.

Virus X (PVX). It is present in all places where potatoes are cultivated. Affected plants appear apparently healthy, in some varieties it appears as a soft mosaic; virulent races cause roughness and deformation of the leaves.

<!-- image -->

This virus is transmitted by contact. The losses it causes are generally less than 10% of the yield, but in extreme cases, it can reach up to 50%.

This disease is not transmitted by aphids, but by chewing insects, such as grasshoppers and flea beetles occasionally.

## Control

- Use of healthy, certified seed
- Control of the vector by means of yellow traps with water or sticky traps
- Use of botanical or chemical insecticides.

## Methods of Detecting Viruses

The determination of the presence of viruses in potato crops can be done in three ways:

- a) Visual inspection of the field
- b) Inoculating indicator plants
- c) Using serological methods.

## Harvesting

## Harvesting Time

This stage of the crop is defined by the days of the vegetative cycle of the planted variety (early, intermediate, or late) or when the foliage begins to turn uniformly yellow and the leaves begin to fall naturally.

It is advisable to cut the foliage about 10 days before harvesting, so that the skin of the tubers becomes stronger, and it speeds up their maturity. This practice favors the accumulation of dry matter, an important condition for the quality of the product, and controls the potato blight and any physical damage or loss of moisture.

The harvest should be done in the early hours of the morning and in dry weather; the digging is done manually, with a hoe, a trowel, or a short-handled spade.

It is advisable to harvest carefully to avoid injuries to the surface of the potatoes, as this makes them the main entry point for multiple diseases.

The tubers should be left lying on the ground exposed to the sun for a period of 2 hours to allow them to air out and dry properly, which helps to complete the skin formation of the tuber, which, when rubbed with the hands, should not come off, this helps to prevent damage during handling, transport, and storage, and also facilitates the removal of the soil attached.

## Selection

The tubers that are to be stored must be in good health and should arrive clean in the storage area for the treatment to be effective, as they are in direct contact with the skin of the tubers; within this, the sorting (removal of sick tubers and foreign bodies) is carried out.

The selection can be done manually, mechanically, or in a mixed way.

## Sorting

Generally, this activity is carried out in the collection center, and the product can be sorted in different ways depending on the destination of the tuber, as described below:

By degree of cleanliness or dirtiness that the tuber has:

- Extra clean
- Clean
- With dirt

By size and appearance:

- Large (7-10 cm in diameter and 15 cm in length)
- Medium (4-7 cm in diameter and 10 cm in length)
- Small (4 cm or less in diameter)

By weight:

- 1st grade with 113.5 g weight or more
- 2nd grade between 85.13 to 113.5 g weight
- 3rd grade between 56.75 to 85.13 g weight
- 4th grade of 56.75 g weight and less (less than 28.38 g)

The potatoes with green spots, bitter taste, and those that show signs of sprouting should be removed, the selection by weight is used to facilitate immediate sale or storage.

## Post-Harvest

The purpose of storage is to maintain the tubers in the same conditions as when they were harvested; that is, to keep them firm, without rotting or losses due to disease or germination, and with very little sugar content. In addition, if the potato is harvested at low prices, it can be stored to sell it when the price is more attractive.

Only healthy potatoes, as clean as possible, are stored; preferably using wooden boxes of 28'x14'x12 with an approximate capacity of 45.45 kg or in hessian or synthetic fiber sacks. It is recommended to leave low stacks.

The potatoes should not be washed, nor should they be allowed to get wet with rainwater or condensation, as this causes the penetration of bacteria, resulting in immediate rotting.

The potatoes should be stored in dry and cool places, as ventilated as possible, and with little light, as darkness and low temperatures prevent the skin of the tubers from turning green quickly, forming solanine (a toxic alkaloid that reduces the commercial value of the potato, causing an unpleasant taste and loss of weight due to shrinkage and wrinkling).

The stored potato should be checked weekly to remove the ruined tubers.

<!-- image -->

The optimal conditions for prolonged storage (5 months) should be with temperatures between 4.4 and 7.2oC, and a relative humidity of around 90%. It is necessary to maintain sufficient air circulation, so that there is a uniform temperature and to prevent the accumulation of CO2.

## Processing or Industrialization

The transformation of raw potato material for consumption is a process that adds value to the product.

In general terms, the most important by-products obtained from potatoes are:

- Fresh potatoes: Unpeeled, in bags, ready to be consumed in various soups.
- Frozen potatoes: Which are preserved for use in fried potatoes in the fast food industry
- Potato chips: In flakes of fried potatoes in the fast food industry.
- Alcohol production.
- Dehydrated potatoes: Which are used for making soups, flavorings and flours.

Flours have uses as thickeners for various foods, in the manufacture of dough, pastries and bread.

## DEHYDRATED

The technology of dehydrating potatoes is a preservation process at the level of family or industrial consumption. At the artisanal level, housewives can do it to take advantage of low-quality potatoes for the market.

The process to obtain dehydrated products is as follows:

1. Selection; potatoes are selected according to the final destination of the product.
2. Washing.
3. Peeling and immersion in an antioxidant solution
4. Cutting: The cuts must be thin to allow for faster drying.
5. Blanching. Blanching for a period of 2 minutes at 100°C is carried out.
6. Draining. To remove as much water as possible
7. Spread on trays and expose them to the sun or use solar dryers.
8. Drying
9. Grinding
10. Packaging preferably in paper bags
11. Stored in a dry environment.

## Product Quality

When the potato is ripe, it contains a considerable proportion of water that ranges between 77 and 79%; its starch and sugar (carbohydrate content) is in the order of 12 to 19% and the proteins reach only 2%.

The culinary quality of the varieties with respect to their dry matter content is classified as follows:

For domestic consumption: materials with 20.72% dry matter; for all purposes, 22.1% to 22.7%; and for industrial use, materials with 24.5% dry matter or more.

The best conditions for the potato frying industry are given by the high dry matter content, as long as they do not disintegrate or become powdery; with these qualities they absorb little oil and give greater yield, another important characteristic is that they contain less proportion of reducing sugars. If the content is high, the product takes on a dark brown color which makes it unacceptable. The characteristics mentioned depend on the variety, but everything is affected by the maturity of the harvest, the season and the storage conditions.
