## Maize Phenology and its Relationship with Pest Incidence

Institute for Technological Innovation in Agriculture

One of the main causes of reduced maize yield is pest incidence, as despite having better pest control practices today, there are still losses of up to 31% due to pest attacks, along with some diseases. To help mitigate this problem, it is necessary to have sufficient knowledge about the crop's phenology, which allows predicting the critical periods of incidence for each pest, and thus establishing the most effective control method that ensures the proper development of the crop.

## Why is it important to know the phenology of maize in pest control?

The phenological development of maize refers to the rhythm of vegetative and reproductive growth, expressed as a function of the morphological and physiological changes in the plant, related to the environment. Knowing this development behavior will allow for a prediction of pest incidence, which is essential when planning, structuring, and applying an integrated pest management program, as the susceptibility of the crop to damage caused by pests will depend on its stage of development.

## Pest incidence in relation to maize phenology

Vegetative phase. This phase begins from planting and lasts until just before the appearance of reproductive structures, i.e., when the maize cob (male flower) begins to appear. During the seedling stage, any damage to the foliage or roots is critical and puts the survival of the seedlings at risk. In the vegetative phase, most of the energy is directed towards the formation of foliage; therefore, the plant has some tolerance to foliage loss due to the attack of a pest. During this vegetative growth, pests such as trip (Frankiniella williamsi), larvae of diabrotic weevils (Diabrotica virgifera zeae), corn earworm (Spodoptera frugiperda), soldier bug (Spodoptera exigua), stalk borer (Agrotis sp.), wireworm (Agriotes sp.), cutworm (Phyllophaga sp., Cyclocephala sp., Diplotaxis sp., Anomala sp. and Macrodactylus sp.), weevil (Geraeus senilis), pod bug (Sphenarium purpurascens) and red spider (Oligonychus mexicanus and Tetranychus sp.) (Figure 3) predominate.

Knowing the presence of pests according to the plant's development stage can help the technician or producer to determine the level of economic damage and focus efforts on monitoring, detection, and control, which are more efficient.

With the above, it is possible to more accurately assess the attack of a particular pest and the possible management measures.

Figure 3. Incidence of major pests in relation to the phenological development of maize. Source: CESAVEG, 2008.

Reproductive phase. This begins when the maize cob appears and ends until the crop reaches physiological maturity (black layer at the insertion point of the grain with the stalk). During this stage, pests such as weevil, pod bug, red spider and stalk borer (Helicoverpa zea) appear (Figure 3). The incidence of pests during the vegetative phase is reflected in the reproductive phase of maize, causing significant losses in the potential yield, due to the reduction in the supply of photosynthates for the growth of the grains. The attack of stalk borer on maize grains can cause an infection with the fungus Aspergillus flavus, which is responsible for the production of aflatoxins, highly carcinogenic substances for humans.
