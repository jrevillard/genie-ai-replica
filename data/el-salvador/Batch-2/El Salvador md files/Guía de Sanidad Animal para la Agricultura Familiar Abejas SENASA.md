This guide is intended for family beekeepers. It contains useful information about the bee diseases that have the greatest impact on production and the problems associated with human health, with the aim of maintaining healthy colonies and obtaining safe and quality products from the source.

## ABOUT SENASA

The National Service of Food and Animal Health (Senasa) is the agency dependent on the Ministry of Agriculture, Livestock and Fisheries, responsible for preventing, controlling and eradicating pests and diseases of animals and plants that affect agricultural production. It is responsible for supervising and certifying animal and plant origin products, their inputs and agrochemical residues, in order to ensure the quality and safety of food for human and animal consumption from the source.

## Senasa and family agriculture

To support the development, growth and strengthening of family agricultural production, Senasa has the Coordination of Family Agriculture, whose objectives are:

- to build, in a participatory manner, new regulations that regulate food production and adapt existing ones, taking into account the specific characteristics of the family agriculture sector (AF);
- to recommend preventive measures and health plans, as well as generate different working strategies to comply with the regulations associated with primary agricultural and livestock production as well as food production;
- to disseminate the basic concepts of the existing regulations for the exercise of good agricultural, livestock and manufacturing practices.

In addition, Senasa coordinates the Family Agriculture Commission (Senaf), made up of representatives from the productive sector and public institutions, in order to promote joint and consensual work in the benefit of producers.

In the same vein, each regional center of Senasa has local inter-institutional reference points for family farming, who participate in local discussions.

## Official Sampling

Senasa conducts random sampling (sample collection) in agricultural and processing establishments without charge to producers. The goal of these sampling is to improve the health status of the country regarding certain diseases of importance for health, production, and trade, and to ensure the quality and safety of food.

The results obtained from these studies allow for the planning of actions to prevent, control, and eradicate the main diseases affecting agricultural production.

If your establishment is included in the sampling, cooperate with Senasa staff, who will then inform you of the results obtained.

## Health Advice

Senasa has 375 offices distributed throughout the country, under the jurisdiction of 14 regional centers. Those who need advice on animal diseases can contact the nearest Senasa office or contact the regional reference points for family farming within the organization. The Subsecretariat of Family, Peasant, and Indigenous Agriculture (SAFCI) of the Ministry of Agriculture, Livestock, and Fisheries has offices distributed in the various provinces of the country, assisted by teams of professionals who provide advice, training, and support to family farmers. Senasa has a register of qualified technical and veterinary personnel, specializing in diseases and/or species, and trained on current regulations. Producers can contact these professionals to receive advice on the treatment and control of animal diseases. The list of qualified technical and veterinary personnel can be consulted at Senasa offices or through the organization's website (http://argentina.gob.ar/senasa/family-agriculture).

## ABOUT BEE PRODUCTION

Beekeeping, or apiculture, is a productive activity that can provide economic benefits with little investment and can be carried out simultaneously with other agricultural activities, as it does not require land suitable for cultivation or grazing, or ownership of the location where the hives are placed.

The most popular product of beekeeping is honey, although other products can also be obtained from the hives, such as wax, pollen, propolis, royal jelly, apitoxins, and live material (queen cells, queen bees, nuclei, bee packages), whose trade is growing.

Bees are the quintessential pollinators; they play an essential role in maintaining the health of people and the planet, safeguarding biodiversity and contributing to food security and nutrition. They pollinate 90 percent of the crops we eat (foods rich in micronutrients - such as fruits, vegetables, and seeds - depend on pollination).

Bees are also used to provide pollination services for commercial crops, which improves the yield of agricultural and forestry production. In addition, through their natural work, they contribute to the pollination of wild plants, which helps maintain the balance of ecosystems and the existence of flowering for their own food and development.

## ABOUT BEE DISEASES

The main diseases of bees are caused by external and internal parasites, fungi, bacteria, or viruses specific to the species.

The health of bee colonies can also be affected by various pests, predators, and adverse environmental factors, including exposure to agrochemicals applied to crops and flowering, which can cause intoxication and even mass death of colonies.

The presence of disease in bees reduces the quantity and quality of the products obtained from the hive, and causes significant economic losses for those who are involved in production, due to the depopulation and mortality of colonies.

## The presence of disease in colonies reduces the benefits of the activity.

Most of the diseases that affect bees are distributed throughout Argentina, and there are cases in which they have been diagnosed only in certain areas or regions. Some diseases have never been detected in the country, so they are considered exotic, which positions Argentina in a favorable position for international trade in live bee products.

However, a problem related to apiculture health is the improper application of chemical treatments for their control, as they not only damage the hive, but can also leave residues in apicultural products and affect the health of people who consume them. As a consequence, the presence of residues in honey constitutes an obstacle to its commercialization as a safe product.

In addition, people can contract diseases by consuming food contaminated by poor handling or inadequate hygiene measures in the establishment.

## Prevention and control

Good apicultural practices are essential for preserving the health of the colony. To detect diseases early, it is recommended to carry out regular inspections of the hives, at least twice a year:

- when the entry of pollen begins, which stimulates the development of the brood nest: this corresponds to the beginning of spring and varies from August to November, depending on the area;
- immediately after the last honey harvest: this coincides with the end of summer and varies from February to May.

Regular inspections help prevent damage to colonies.

## Benefits of preventing and controlling bee diseases

- Prevents the loss of bees and brood.
- Maintains a balanced population in the colony.
- Improves the performance of each hive.
- Reduces the costs associated with colony loss and disease treatment.
- Increases the economic benefits of beekeeping.

## How to recognize signs of disease?

There are signs that, through observation, allow you to distinguish a sick colony from a healthy one. It is important to know them in order to:

- Not multiply sick colonies;
- Apply the appropriate treatment and avoid colony loss.

To detect them, you should first observe the brood chamber, as many diseases affect the early stages of bee development.

The visible signs in open brood and in adult bees are as follows:

## Diagnosis and treatment

In order to apply the appropriate treatment, it is necessary to make an accurate diagnosis of the diseases present in the colony, and for this it is important to know the specific signs of each one. However, in some cases, a laboratory analysis is required to confirm their presence.

If there are doubts about which disease may be affecting the colony, how to take samples, or how to apply the treatments, you should consult with a Bee Health Inspector (BHI) or a health advisor (the list of BHIs is available on the Senasa website).

In case of a sudden or massive colony death, you must report it to the nearest Senasa office.

## Varroa

Causes a parasitic mite called Varroa destructor, which sucks the nutrients from the bees and the brood, unbalancing and weakening the colony.

It is a major vector of many viruses that affect bees.

## WHAT ARE THE VISIBLE SIGNS?

- Presence of mites in capped brood.
- Presence of mites on adult bees.
- Adult bees with deformed wings and legs, with reduced body size, and increased defensive behavior.

## WHAT MEASURES SHOULD I TAKE?

- Resolution 81/2015 from Senasa states that producers are required to monitor the varroa population in their hives. To do this, they must:
- Comply with the "Recommendations for Varroosis Control"(*) 1 from Senasa;
- Perform the jar test to determine the level of infestation;
- If necessary, apply an acaricide treatment.

## ORDER, CLEANING, AND DISINFECTION

- Clean and disinfect beehives and floors every year, using direct flame or chlorinated water.
- At the beginning of each season, replace the floors and frames in the beehives that have been cleaned and disinfected.
- Renovate at least 3 frames in the brood chamber of each beehive every year.
- Keep the area around the beehives free of weeds and unused materials.

## HONEY HARVEST AND EXTRACTION

- Avoid harvesting on rainy or very humid days.
- Do not harvest frames from the brood chamber or honey supers that contain brood.
- Utensils used for handling honey should never be placed on the floor and should be washed frequently.
- Honey should be extracted in designated extraction and honey-splitting rooms provided by Senasa.
- Containers for honey packaging must be clean and approved for food use.

## SANITARY REGISTRATION

- Number and mark the beehives in a corresponding manner to allow for identification.
- Record all activities performed in the beehive (monitoring, treatments applied, cleaning, sample taking, laboratory analysis, etc.).
- If the beehives need to be moved, they must be transported with the electronic Transit Document (DT-e).

## DOCUMENTATION AND PROCEDURES

The following steps must be completed by family producers to carry out their agricultural activities in accordance with current regulations.

## Registration in ReNAF

## What is it?

ReNAF is the National Registry of Family Agriculture (ReNAF) 2, managed by the Secretariat of Agriculture, Livestock and Fisheries through the Subsecretariat of Family, Peasant, and Indigenous Agriculture (SAFCI), and created by Resolution ex-SAGPyA N° 255/07, with the aim of making the work of those involved in family agriculture visible and strengthening it throughout the country. The registration is voluntary, free, permanent, and nationwide.

## What is it for?

Based on the information provided by each producer, it is possible to know how many and who they are, where they live and work, what they produce, and their specific potential and needs, contributing to the development of public policies for the sector.

## Who can register?

Families/households of agricultural, forestry, fishing, and/or craft, agroindustrial, or tourism producers, when these activities use agricultural resources, can register, regardless of whether the purpose of these activities is sale, self-consumption, or barter, or whether they are the main or secondary activity of the household.

## How to register?

To register, consult with the provincial delegations of the SAFCI or with their field technicians.

## REGISTRATION IN RENAPA

## WHAT IS IT?

Renapa is the National Registry of Apiculture Producers, managed by the Secretariat of Agriculture, Livestock and Fisheries of the Nation. It was created by Resolution SAGPyA Nº 283/2001 (and its modifications 89/02 and 857/2006) in order to provide better service to the beekeeper and to provide a framework of security for the production.

Registration in this registry is mandatory and free for all natural or legal persons, and has a validity of 2 years.

Producers with a minimum of 5 beehives must register. Registration is voluntary for those with less than 5 beehives.

## WHAT IS IT FOR?

You will receive a credential that identifies you as a beekeeper and that will be required for all procedures related to the activity. Registration in the Registry is an indispensable requirement to be able to market the production, in order to ensure the traceability of your product.

Updating the registration becomes voluntary, so it can be done whenever the producer modifies their personal data, the number of beehives, etc. Registered producers will receive updated information on the evolution of the sector, as well as permanent assistance from the provinces and the national government.

The Renapa number is unique and non-transferable. Beekeepers must identify the apicultural material to facilitate the work of police and sanitary controls on roads.

All natural or legal persons who are involved in the management of beehives, production, and/or the use of bees as pollinators of crops must also comply with these requirements.

Note: It is important to consider that once the registration in Renapa is completed, it will be considered as fulfilled the registration in the NATIONAL SANITARY REGISTRY OF AGRICULTURAL PRODUCERS (Renspa) according to what is established in Article 13 of the Resolution that creates Renapa.

## Identification of Hives

The beekeeper must identify the bee-keeping material with the Renapa number, which must be engraved on the hives using hot iron or with a procedure that produces similar effects.

The mark must have a maximum height of 5 cm and a width of 20 cm. If selling hives or acquiring used hives, the first owner must mark the inverted number on the top to thus cancel their mark and disassociate from the material.

## Issuance of DT-e

The Senasa self-management system allows for the online processing of electronic transit documents (DT-e) for all species.

Once the registration and system activation steps are completed, producers can access the service to process the DT-e at any time, without having to go to the agency's offices.

From now on, producers will receive automatic confirmation of registration without delays and without having to wait for the same via email from the agency. Currently, through the AFIP website, you can complete and enter the data in the Payment Direct Adhesion Form, which will be automatically approved.

Any movement or transfer of live bee-keeping material (MAV) from one farm to another, for any reason, must be carried out under the DT-e. This document is processed at the Senasa office corresponding to the origin of the MAV, informing the Renspa/Renapa of origin and the Renspa/Renapa of destination. Once the MAV arrives at the destination, the producer* must inform the Senasa office at the destination within 5 business days, a process known as 'closing the movement'.

Furthermore, it is important to mention that the transfer of live bee-keeping material (hives, nuclei and packages of bees) and honeycombs requires the authorization of the vehicle used for this purpose by Senasa (Senasa Resolution 503/2022).

Some provinces establish additional requirements for the transfer of hives, nuclei and packages of bees, which must be consulted in the corresponding municipal or provincial office.

* This is the same producer if moving hives. Different producers in case of selling the live material that is being moved.
