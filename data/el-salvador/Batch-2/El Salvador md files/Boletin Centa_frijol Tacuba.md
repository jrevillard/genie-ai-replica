## "CENTA Tacuba" - A High-Yielding Black Bean Variety

National Center for Agricultural and Forestry Technology 'Enrique Álvarez Córdova' - Basic Grains Program

San Andrés, La Libertad, El Salvador, 2018

## INTRODUCTION

The National Center for Agricultural and Forestry Technology 'Enrique Álvarez Córdova' (CENTA), in line with its primary objective of increasing agricultural production and productivity, is making a new variety of black bean, named "CENTA Tacuba," available to producers.

This new variety was developed through evaluations in experimental stations and field trials, demonstrating excellent agronomic characteristics, including high productivity and resistance to diseases.

The purpose of releasing this variety is to promote the cultivation and consumption of black beans in El Salvador, as they were once a staple food in the country. This initiative also aims to capitalize on export opportunities to various countries that consume this type of bean. Therefore, the Board of Directors, Executive Management, and technical staff of CENTA are offering this new variety, which will directly contribute to the food security and sovereignty of the Salvadoran people.

## INTRODUCTION

Adapting to climate change requires relevant technological solutions, and one such solution is the use of improved varieties that are tolerant to the effects of this phenomenon. CENTA is conducting research to address this issue. In this context, the "CENTA Tacuba" black bean variety has been developed for use by producers, as an additional option to improve the diet of Salvadoran families, as it provides 22% protein.

With this new variety of black beans, the goal is to increase consumption of this bean, as it was done in previous decades in our country, and to access international markets through the export of surplus production. The development of this improved variety is the result of the work of bean researchers in the Basic Grains Program and extension workers. The cooperation and participation of farmers have also been crucial.

## 1. ORIGIN OF THE VARIETY

The line BRT 943-20, which gave rise to the commercial variety "CENTA Tacuba," was introduced to El Salvador in 2012, through the Central American Bean Adaptation Nursery (VIDAC Negro), originating from the Agricultural School of Zamorano (EAPI Zamorano) in Honduras, and evaluated in various stages until the Preliminary Performance Trials (EPR Negro) at the San Andrés experimental station of CENTA. This experimental line is the result of a simple cross between Aifi Wuritil IBC306-55, carried out at EAPI Zamorano in 2009.

## II. RESULTS OF THE EVALUATION PROCESS

During the May and August periods of the 2016-2017 agricultural cycle, the BRT 943-20 line was evaluated in regional performance trials of common black bean in the municipalities of Santa Ana, Chalchuapa, El Triunfo, Ciudad Arce, San Pablo Tacachico, and Armenia. The line showed the highest adaptation and yield (Table 1).

Table 1. Yields of the "CENTA TACUBA" variety obtained during regional trials of black bean in El Salvador, 2016.

| Year | Season | Average yield (kg/ha) |
|-------|--------|-----------------------|
| 2016 | May    | 2,068                 |
| 2016 | August | 2,049                 |

The "CENTA Tacuba" variety was validated in the 2017-18 agricultural cycle on farms in the municipalities of San Miguel, Sonsonate, San Salvador, Santa Ana, and Ahuachapán, showing an average increase of 38.3% compared to local varieties (Table 2). The BRT 934-20 line was identified as tolerant to high temperatures in trials conducted by EAPI Zamorano in 2015-16, in the municipality of Nacaome, Valle Department, Honduras, as part of the activities carried out through the "Resilient Beans to Climate Change" (CRIB) project, sponsored by Feed the Future/USAID and coordinated by Pennsylvania State University.

## Participatory evaluations with producers

Field evaluations of the validation plots were conducted with producers, who were consulted about the characteristics of the new variety compared to those used in the area. They expressed their satisfaction with the high yield, early maturity, tolerance to drought and high temperatures, greater tolerance to pests and diseases, the plant's growth habit, and the size of the medium to large beans.

Regarding the culinary characteristics of the variety, producers indicated that the soup had a better color and was thicker. In terms of taste and texture, producers said that both varieties were similar.

## IV. CROP MANAGEMENT

- Seed: It is recommended to use improved seed produced by CENTA or authorized Seed Producer Associations.
3. Fertilization: It is recommended to conduct a soil analysis to use a recommended fertilization for its cultivation. For general fertilization, it is suggested to use 2-3 quintals of 18-46-0 formula at planting; and 1 qq of urea 25-30 days after planting.
- 2 Planting density: The suggested amount of seed to plant one manzana (a unit of measurement) is 80 pounds, with a planting spacing of 50 cm between rows; 2-3 seeds per hill, with a spacing of 20 cm between hills.

## V. DISEASE CONTROL

In the case of yellow mosaic and common mosaic viruses; when there is a low presence of vectors for these diseases, the variety does not require control due to its resistance to these diseases. During the planting season, it should be planted before December 15th, and applications of a specific insecticide for whitefly control should be made 15-20 days after planting.

## VI. PEST CONTROL

Bean weevil: In areas with severe infestations, it is suggested to make two applications of insecticides before and after flowering (one week apart).

Storage pests: Store in metal silos with a maximum humidity of 13%. It is recommended to fumigate the grain if it is to be stored for a long period.

## VII. WEED CONTROL

Weeds should be removed through good soil preparation, manual weeding 15-20 days and 25-30 days after planting, and the use of recommended pre- and post-emergence herbicides.
