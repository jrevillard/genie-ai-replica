? GENIE.AI walkthrough · Public-sector briefing
An open-source framework for public services, from the source document to the system that answers the question.
! GENIE.AI is an open-source framework for public services, and this walkthrough follows one question — what a citizen needs to bring to renew a permit — from the answer on screen back through the service, the knowledge and the infrastructure that produced it. The framework is aimed at people who need both its public-service value and how it works. The permit, its guide and the instructions that go with it are fictional: they are a thread for explaining the framework, not policy advice. The closing slide leaves time for questions.

@spread|Local knowledge changes the service.|+light
? THE PUBLIC-SERVICE CHALLENGE
What the model already knows|A general model can explain what permit renewal usually means and help write a clear response.
What the institution knows|The local authority knows which form applies, which proof it accepts and who publishes a change.
What the user needs|A reliable answer for this jurisdiction, with enough evidence to check or challenge it.
! A fluent, plausible explanation is not the same as an answer that applies to a particular jurisdiction today. A general model can help phrase an answer; the local requirements have to come from the institution's own maintained sources. When the policy changes, the knowledge and its publication process must carry that change into the service. The first design question follows from this: who owns the source, and how does a change reach the users?

@ingestion|A source document has to become searchable.|+light
? FOLLOW THE PERMIT RENEWAL GUIDE
Upload formats|Uploaded files stay pending until the administrator starts ingestion.|PDF|DOCX|XLSX|CSV|MD|HTML|TXT
Example chunks|Bring your current permit and|a recent proof of address|. Submit both with your renewal form.
Deployment settings|Upload limits and antivirus scanning. Chunk size by file type and overlap. Optional context and the embedding model.
= The highlighted text appears in both chunks to preserve context across the split.
! A source document has to become searchable before it can answer anything. An authorised user uploads a file; it stays Pending; an administrator triggers Ingest from the dashboard or the API. The configured pipeline scans uploads with ClamAV when enabled, extracts supported formats with the available parser, splits the text into chunks, creates embeddings and stores the searchable result. Docling handles supported formats such as PDF and DOCX in the reference setup. The highlighted overlap is why adjacent chunks can share context: smaller chunks may improve focus but can also cut the context in half, so size and overlap have to be evaluated against real documents. These are deployment settings, not per-question choices. When the chunking or the embedding model changes, the affected files are re-ingested and retrieval is checked again. The sample guide and the chunks are fictional.

# How knowledge is governed|24:00–33:00
@graph|OKF makes curated knowledge versionable.
? OPEN KNOWLEDGE FORMAT · CONCEPTS, LINKS AND SERVING GRAPHS
Concepts|Each concept is a Markdown document with YAML frontmatter. It captures a distinct topic such as proof of address.
Relationships|Typed links connect concepts across the knowledge base. A stewardship team can review the content and its links.
Serving graph|Enable OKF and select OKF-only or hybrid retrieval to search published, ingested repositories as separate graphs.
! OKF is a structured way to curate concepts and their typed relationships, with the content stored in a versioned repository. Three actions stay distinct: editing a draft, publishing a version, and making the resulting knowledge available for serving after the ingestion. OKF is configurable and can be disabled; the existing free-form corpus remains a separate path. When OKF is enabled, the selected retrieval mode decides whether the queries use the OKF serving graph alone or combine it with the legacy corpus. The benefit is a governed structure and a visible lifecycle, not automatic correctness.

! Three levels, next to the access control of the previous slide. Users act on their own data: a conversation can be deleted from the saved chats, and the settings page offers a data reset and an account deletion. Make no compliance claim here: the platform provides the mechanisms; the record of processing, the retention periods and the boundary of a remote GPU API are for the institution and its data protection officer to assess.
