## RED SORGO CENTA-CF: THE FIRST RED SORGO VARIETY WITH TANNINS IN EL SALVADOR

<!-- image -->

National Center for Agricultural, Livestock and Forestry Technology 'Enrique Álvarez Córdova' - A VARIETY WITH HIGH FORAGE, GRAIN AND GOOD SILAGE QUALITY

San Andrés, La Libertad, El Salvador, September 2016

## INTRODUCTION

With the aim of supporting producers in the country, the National Center for Agricultural, Livestock and Forestry Technology (CENTA), led by Enrique Álvarez Córdova, continuously works on developing technologies that produce the best results when used.

This opportunity marks the culmination of several years of research, and CENTA is now making a new variety of red sorgo available to farmers. This variety stands out for its high forage yield, red grain color, and tannin content. These two characteristics significantly reduce the consumption of grain by birds, contributing to higher quality silage.

For CENTA, it is a source of pride to produce new technological alternatives that contribute to solving the various problems in the agricultural sector, particularly in the livestock subsector of El Salvador.

Santos Rafael Alemán, Executive Director of CENTA

## I. INTRODUCTION

In El Salvador, 60% of livestock farms use sorghum as a primary feed for silage, which is mostly of low nutritional quality due to a lack of sufficient grain, as it has been consumed by birds.

An alternative to this problem is the use of red sorghum varieties with tannins. These varieties have a bitter taste and dark color, making them less attractive to birds. This characteristic ensures that the feed remains available for the silage to have enough grain, improving fermentation and providing the necessary amount of starch to improve the nutritional quality of the animal's diet, which translates into better milk production.

In addition, it is known that all grain sorghum varieties, regardless of their color, contain hydrolyzable tannins (gallic acid and ellagic acid), which are factors that affect their nutritional value. In some cases, there is sufficient condensed tannin content that can precipitate or increase protein levels in the same crop (Acurero, 1983).

Sorghum cultivation has gained significant importance in El Salvador due to its contribution to the development of the livestock sector, providing a significant contribution in terms of social impact, generating 43 days of work per hectare, with an income of $1328.25 per month for the value of biomass for silage. CENTA, in line with its objectives, has developed this new variety, which responds to the demand for red sorghum with tannins from farmers. This contribution is expected to improve the nutrition of cattle and, consequently, increase milk production.

## II. VARIETY ORIGIN

In 2010, the sorghum breeding program, supported by the CENTA-INTSORMIL agreement, initiated initial crosses to create a red sorghum variety with tannins. These varieties are less attractive to birds. The crosses were carried out using male parents, ICSV-LM-905038 and ICSV-LM-90509 lines from the University of Texas A & M in the United States, and female parents, the main sorghum varieties developed by CENTA (CENTA S-2, CENTA-3, CENTA RCV) and others such as TORTILLERO, PINOLERO and VG-146.

The SORGO RED CENTA-CF experimental variety was identified as CI-013261. This code identifies the agreement that existed at the time between CENTA-INTSORMIL (CI). 013 is the year of the variety's development, 26 is the corresponding number, and "T" refers to the tannin contribution of the new variety.

## III. EVALUATION AND RESULTS PROCESS

The process of formation and evaluation began in the 2012-2013 cycle, within the sorghum breeding program of the CENTA Basic Grains program. Through various evaluation processes, including crossing and selection nurseries (from which 36 uniform lines were obtained), preliminary yield trials (from which 10 lines were identified), and regional trials in which the CI-01326T line (Hernández, 2014) stood out, which was subsequently validated in four different climates in El Salvador, where sorghum is used as feed. The following traits were evaluated: forage yield and grain yield.

## SEEDING AND CULTIVATION MANAGEMENT

The red sorghum variety with tannins can be sown from 0 to 1000 meters above sea level, in any type of soil (sandy, loamy, and clayey), and is very tolerant of adverse conditions such as drought.

## 1. Land Preparation

The soil should be prepared in such a way that it is loose and well-drained, so as not to hinder the covering and germination of the seed.

## 2. Planting

Rows should be spaced 0.7 and 0.8 meters apart, whether mechanized or done by hand.

## 3. Seed Treatment

Seed treatment is essential, as it ensures germination by controlling soil pests such as weevils and cutworms, among others. Any commercially available seed treatment can be used.

## 4. Planting Density

It is recommended to sow between 20 and 28 pounds of sorghum seed per acre (40-56 pounds per hectare) and to plant between 10 and 12 plants per linear meter. This density helps to avoid competition for nutrients, light, and water, and also allows the plant to reach its full potential. With this amount of seed, the density is approximately 80,000 plants per acre (114,286 per hectare).

## 5. Thinning

This practice is related to the skill of the person doing the planting. If the planter sows the necessary amount of seed per plant, thinning is avoided, as it increases production costs. If thinning is necessary, it is recommended to do it between 10 and 15 days after germination.

## 6. Pest Control

It is important to first control soil insects using seed treatments. Then, conduct frequent monitoring to determine if the level of pest infestation warrants control.

The main pests of sorghum foliage include: stalk borers and, currently, the sorghum stem borer (PAS), which causes total crop loss. To control these pests, use systemic band herbicides, as they are environmentally friendly.

## 7. Weed Control

It is recommended to use pre-emergent herbicides depending on the type of weeds (grasses or broadleaf weeds). If only grasses are present, use glyphosate at a rate of 36 SL (2 liters per hectare), and if only broadleaf weeds are present, use atrazine at a rate of 2 kg per hectare. If both types of weeds are present, it is recommended to mix these products.

After 25-30 days of planting, if weeds are present, they can be controlled manually or with burning herbicides such as ammonium glyphosate at a rate of 2 liters per hectare. This should be done directly on the weed, being careful not to spray the surrounding area, as this is where the plant absorbs nutrients.

## 8. Fertilization

It is recommended to conduct a soil analysis before planting, which will indicate the type of fertilization required. If this is not possible, two fertilizations should be carried out: the first 8 days after planting with 4 kg/hectare of a 16-20-0 fertilizer, and the second 25-30 days after planting or before harvest with 4 kg/hectare of ammonium sulfate.

## 9. Harvest for silage

The harvest should be carried out when the grain is in the milky stage, cutting the plants 10 cm above the soil surface. After the harvest, the bales should be handled with weed control, either manually or with burning herbicides. Then, 15 days after the cut, fertilize with ammonium sulfate (4 kg/hectare) or 3 kg/hectare of urea.

## 10. Second silage harvest

This is always carried out when the grain is in the milky stage, which occurs 75-80 days after the cut.

## 11. Grain Harvest

If the purpose is to obtain seed or grain, it is recommended to plant in August or late autumn, as the harvest and processing will be done during the dry season.
