## THE RADISH

## It is a root vegetable that is easy to grow; that does not take up much space and grows very quickly.

Appreciated for its scarlet color and its spicy flavor. The color of the root varies from white to black, passing through pale red to bright scarlet. The size of the roots varies from small to large, with the small ones being the most appreciated in the Spanish market. Radishes are commercially grown in many parts of the world. Radishes are usually eaten raw, and sometimes the tender leaves are cut and added to salads. Long-leaf varieties are used for harvesting in late spring and summer. - 3) ORIGIN

<!-- image -->

The origin and provenance of radishes is still uncertain after the latest studies provided by expert botanists and researchers; however, there are numerous hypotheses that consider China as the possible cradle of this food. It is certain that the Egyptians and Babylonians consumed this tuber more than 4,000 years ago, as it is represented on the interior walls of the Pyramid of Cheops. During the first millennium BC, the Greeks and Romans made radish a very appreciated food, extending its consumption throughout Europe thanks to the provinces conquered by the latter, while the large radishes may have originated in Japan or China. In inscriptions found in the Egyptian pyramids, dated 2000 years ago, reference is already made to its culinary use.

- 5) PLANTING

The seed that is kept in good condition maintains its viability for 6 years.

It is sown in the field, preferably in autumn, spring and winter.

The radish seed is generally sown broadcast at a rate of 12 kg of seed per hectare.

In contrast, radishes are usually sown in rows at 50 cm, using about 8 kg per hectare.

When growing radishes, it is common to associate them, intercropping other longer-cycle vegetables such as carrots, beets, etc.

## 6) IRRIGATION

Because these are early-maturing crops, they need a good amount of water, distributed evenly and with well-adjusted watering intervals: The soil moisture should be between 60% and 65% of the field capacity during the vegetative cycle.

Lack of water causes the root to become harder, and if this is accompanied by high temperatures, it stimulates premature flowering. On the other hand, extreme fluctuations in soil moisture cause the roots to crack, losing their commercial quality.

It is recommended to water regularly every 3 to 5 days in the absence of rain.

## 8) HARVEST

Radishes are highly appreciated by amateur gardeners because they only need 3 to 6 weeks from sowing to harvest.

Harvest the summer varieties as soon as they reach the appropriate size (15 cm for Japanese varieties).

Leave the immature ones in the ground and collect them when you need to use them.

In summer, the harvesting of small roots is carried out at 45 days, medium-sized roots about 10 days later, and large roots at 70-80 days.

During the winter season, the plants can be left unharvested for a certain time from the optimal harvest time; but if this is prolonged, the roots become excessively large; and if it rains, they crack and then hollow out:

In summer, it is necessary to harvest immediately, as they quickly hollow out, especially early varieties.

## 9) COMMERCIALIZATION

There are two ways to commercialize: in bunches with leaves; and clean, that is, without leaves or roots. Radishes and radishes are transported to processing units in plastic containers or trailers. First, they are subjected to a pre-wash with water to remove soil and other impurities, achieving complete cleanliness thanks to a subsequent washing system with air in water. Subsequently, they are rotated in a way that the leaves are trapped between two rollers and are removed. Defective radishes are separated on the roller belt. The roots are cut with blades. Finally, calibration is carried out. Water is necessary throughout the process to prevent deterioration.

They are stored between 2 and 5°C and a relative humidity between 90 and 96%.
