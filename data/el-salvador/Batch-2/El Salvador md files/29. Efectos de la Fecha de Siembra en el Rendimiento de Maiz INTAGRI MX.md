## The Impact of Planting Date on Corn Yield

The planting date is a crucial decision that affects the environment the crop will experience throughout its life cycle, especially during critical development stages, and ultimately impacts yield.

Due to various climate phenomena, such as El Niño, as well as global warming, planting dates have been modified in different corn-producing regions of Mexico. Therefore, it is important for commercial companies and research institutions to conduct experiments to determine the optimal planting date for different regions, based on the current climate conditions.

In Mexico, there are two main corn production cycles: spring-summer (SS) and autumn-winter (AW). The main producing states for the SS cycle are: Jalisco, the State of Mexico, Michoacán, and Chiapas. For the AW cycle, the producing states are: Sinaloa, Sonora, and Chihuahua, where it is worth noting that most of the planted area in this cycle has irrigation systems.

Some effects of the planting date include:

*   Delayed or reduced germination. Changing the optimal planting date can lead to problems such as low soil temperatures that limit germination. In addition, in rain-fed crops, there may be problems with water stress.
*   Extended growing season. Without the appropriate temperature and moisture conditions, the growing season tends to lengthen, resulting in reduced production.

Carrying out the planting within the optimal dates recommended by institutions such as INIFAP, allows you to take advantage of the best environmental conditions and aim for the highest possible yields, and primarily, to reduce risks of production due to climate and pest events. However, the changing climate conditions currently demand more research in this area.
