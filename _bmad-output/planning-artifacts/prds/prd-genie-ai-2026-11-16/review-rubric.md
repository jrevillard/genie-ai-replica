# PRD Quality Review — Agentic Foundations (OPEA-free), prd-genie-ai-2026-11-16

## Overall verdict

This PRD holds up as a launch-stakes capability spec: the decision spine is explicit (two operator decisions in §1, five decisions wired to nine verification items in §10, staged gates S0–S4 in §9), the trade-offs are named with what was given up (addendum A), and 27 of 29 FRs carry testable consequences — including two named acceptance scenarios (FR-16 rollback continuation, FR-28 retained-data authorization). What is at risk is downstream extraction, not decision quality: there is no glossary, the core noun shifts shape ("capability contract" / "capability boundary" / "agent-facing document contracts"), and FR-21 is a constraint with no verification hook. Both risks are cheap to fix and none of them threatens the iteration-1 scope, which the PRD keeps honest at every boundary (§4.2, §9 deferred-initiative list, addendum E/F).

## Decision-readiness — strong

The two framing decisions are stated as decisions with dates and consequences, not as considerations: "the agentic path is OPEA-free from the first iteration" and "OPEA exit … is deferred" (§1), with FR-6 explicitly superseding the 10-November delegation option and addendum A recording that supersession with its rationale. The D01–D05 table (§10) does what an open-decision table should: each decision names the verification item that feeds it and the gate before which it closes (e.g. D03 "Decision at S4 entry from V06 + pre-adoption licensing evidence (V07a)"). The one live tension — the operator's stated DSH preference versus the research's provisional last-place ranking — is not smoothed over: the D03 cell carries the AM-1 correction ("the original 'not embeddable' premise was corrected … what stands: developer preview, no security audit, zero evidenced adoption"), and addendum D marks the screen's G4/G5 verdicts as mechanism-level and pending re-evaluation. A decision-maker pushing back on DSH finds their objection acknowledged, not dodged.

The seven Open Questions (§13) are genuinely open — each names its decision path (OQ-2 decides whether UX artifacts exist at S2; OQ-7 sets a research-freshness deadline of 2026-12-01 that binds D03). The §8 posture block goes further than most PRDs: it states that missing thresholds *block* promotion rather than defaulting to pass. No `[NOTE FOR PM]` callouts exist anywhere; that mechanism is replaced by the D-table, the operator-confirmed posture blocks, and OQ list — the tensions reach the surface through those channels, so the absence is stylistic, not a suppression of dissent.

## Substance over theater — strong

No personas — §3's actor table has three roles, and each one drives requirements: the administrator drives FR-15/FR-16/FR-18, engineering drives FR-29, the end user drives FR-10/FR-13. Nothing in the table exists to look thorough. There is no differentiation section claiming novelty; the closest thing, the query-class note (§4.1), does the opposite of innovation theater — it downgrades the literature's agentic benefits to "hypotheses pending E02" on our own corpora. The NFRs are the anti-boilerplate: NFR-2 grounds sovereignty in a specific documented failure mode (default telemetry keyed on the self-hosted credential) and converts it into a defaults-not-capabilities gate; NFR-3 names the concurrent-load saturation problem that per-run bounds do not solve; NFR-7 explicitly rejects building a second engine to demonstrate flexibility — a requirement against theater, written into the NFR table. The Vision (§2) is not swappable: it names the public-administration trajectory, the OPEA-archival driver, and the non-closing constraint toward iteration 2+. Success metrics are structural gates plus separately-reported measured dimensions, with counter-metrics (excessive abstention, limit-hit rate) that argue against the PRD's own feature — no activity metrics anywhere.

## Strategic coherence — strong

The thesis is stated and everything serves it: grow from a fixed RAG pipeline into a governed agentic system, iteration 1 = OPEA-free document capability contract + one governed agent + activation/rollback alongside the legacy path + the evaluation infrastructure to prove trust. The feature groups map one-to-one onto that thesis (FG-1 boundary, FG-2 agent, FG-3 coexistence, FG-4 governance, FG-5 evaluation), and the prioritization follows the thesis rather than ease — the OPEA-free implementation of the contract is deliberately the harder path (FR-6 supersedes delegation), and evaluation infrastructure is in scope from iteration 1 rather than bolted on after. The E01 (A/B) and E02 (B/C) design in FR-23 is the strongest coherence signal: the two experiments directly test the PRD's two bets — the migration preserves behaviour, and agentic behaviour earns its cost — with held-fixed variables enumerated so each delta is attributable. Counter-metrics are named where the SMs exist (§8). Scope kind is coherent: a platform/foundations initiative whose iteration-1 scope is a problem-solving use case (SC-01) with the platform contract extracted from it, and the PRD resists the backlog-with-headings failure mode by deferring every capability that does not serve the foundations (addendum A rows 2–5).

## Done-ness clarity — strong

The FR set is testable to an unusual degree for a PRD. FR-10 splits acceptance into two independently checkable criteria and says which one is mechanical ("reference validity — checkable mechanically against structured tool results with stable source identifiers") and which is judgment ("grounding — an unsupported assertion citing a genuine source fails grounding"), rejects prompt-level instructions as enforcement with a cited counter-result (REC-5), and surfaces the streaming release-policy problem (text chunks forwarded ahead of final metadata cannot be unsent). FR-11 defines *counting semantics* — retries, parallel tool invocations, auxiliary model calls all count against budgets; the wall-clock deadline applies end-to-end — which is exactly the specification an engineer needs and most PRDs omit. FR-16 names its acceptance scenario outright (agentic conversation started, routing disabled, conversation continued on legacy without data loss), and its rollout record enumerates fields down to "a named person who pulls the trigger." FR-19's licensing tiers define edge semantics (NOASSERTION = unverified, not incompatible). FR-23–FR-27 fix evaluation-set composition, hostile-injection scenarios, and separate reporting dimensions. The post-baseline numeric thresholds (§8, FR-11) are gated rather than hand-waved: absence of thresholds blocks promotion to the corresponding stage, so "done" stays well-defined at every gate even before numbers exist.

Two FRs fall short of that bar.

### Findings

- **medium** FR-21 has no verification hook (§6 FG-4) — "The agentic path adds no new OPEA coupling that makes the deferred OPEA exit harder" is a review stance, not a testable requirement: FR-20's CI checks cover only the *request path*, and coupling that worsens the exit (a new `comps` dependency in build tooling, shared code, or scripts outside the request path) escapes both. *Fix:* name the mechanism — extend FR-20's greppable CI check to the whole repository diff of the initiative, or require an explicit architecture-review sign-off recorded as an ADR (FR-29) at each stage gate.
- **low** FR-14's "the selected existing client API" presupposes a selection that has not happened (§6 FG-3) — V05 inventories the contracts but the PRD never states who selects "the selected" one or when; an engineer cannot yet know which contract compatibility is claimed against. *Fix:* one clause naming the selection owner and gate (e.g. "selected at the S3 compatibility gate from the V05 inventory, per D04").

## Scope honesty — strong

Omissions are explicit, load-bearing, and paired with their review trigger: §4.2 lists seven non-goals including the OPEA-free constraint itself with its definition cross-ref, and adds "These exclusions require scope review before iteration 2; they are not assertions about permanent product limits" — de-scoping proposed honestly in both directions. Non-goals are also placed inline where a reader could silently assume otherwise (FR-15 "Granularity per user/tenant is a deliberate non-goal of iteration 1"; FR-13 "no new frontend citation surface in iteration 1"; FR-18 "No dedicated admin UI in iteration 1"). The "no pilot designated" gap is stated in the body (§3), not buried, and its consequence is drawn (evaluation against an agreed fixed reference, not a live pilot; D05 owns pilot scope). Addendum E carries the deferred-initiative list so nothing is lost silently, and addendum F preserves the retrieval-strategy guard verbatim in intent. Assumptions are tagged (`[ASSUMPTION]` in §3 and §12) and each is falsifiable with a stated consequence if wrong (versioned change, not silent absorption).

Open-items density is high in absolute terms — 5 decisions + 9 verification items + 7 open questions + 5 assumptions ≈ 26 — but each D is wired to the V that feeds it and to the gate that requires it, so the density is the tracking system working, not unresolved sprawl; for launch stakes with verification-gated decisions this is the designed shape, not a blocker.

## Downstream usability — adequate

This is the PRD's weakest dimension, and it matters because the PRD is chain-top: it feeds the architecture doc (D01/D03/OQ-3/OQ-6 are explicitly architecture-doc decisions) and story creation. Three frictions stand against real strengths: FR-1..FR-29 are unique and complete, cross-references to FRs/V-items/gates resolve, external references all check out (see Shape fit), and each FR restates its constraint inline rather than pointing at the framing issue (e.g. FR-12 quotes the degraded-honesty rule and cites framing §11 only as provenance).

### Findings

- **medium** No glossary; the core noun drifts (whole document) — the PRD's central artifact is called "document capability contract" (§4.1, FR-1), "document capability boundary" (FG-1 heading, D01), "agent-facing document contracts" (FR-7) and "document/client contracts" (D04); secondary terms ("agentic path", "legacy RAG path", "reference", "working names") are defined only at first use or by implication. §5 does glossary work for the one most-contested term ("OPEA component") and proves the value of doing it. Story and architecture writers extracting sections standalone will have to resolve the boundary/contract distinction themselves. *Fix:* add a compact glossary (§ before §3) fixing contract = the interface, boundary = the deployment/logical placement of that interface, plus agentic path, legacy path, reference, reference validity, grounding, SC-01; or state the distinction once in FG-1 and use the terms consistently after it.
- **low** Success metrics and counter-metrics carry no IDs (§8) — downstream artifacts citing them ("excessive abstention rate") have no stable handle the way FRs do; the gate structure references E01/E02 but the counter-metrics are only reachable by name. *Fix:* number them (SM-1.., CM-1..) in §8.
- **low** The D03 table cell is a dense narrative, contradicting addendum D's own policy (§10 D03 row) — addendum D says the candidate matrix is "maintained here so the PRD's D03 section stays a pointer, not a copy", but the D03 cell itself packs the whole ranking saga (operator preference, AM-1 correction, what stands, revisit trigger, screening pick, runner-up, decision inputs) into one table cell — the hardest-to-extract text shape in the document. *Fix:* collapse the cell to decision + status + pointer to addendum D, and move the AM-1 narrative there.

## Shape fit — strong

The shape matches the product: an internal, Level-2 framework-capability initiative, so the capability-spec form is right — actors instead of personas, operational/structural gates instead of user-facing metrics, no user journeys (they would be furniture here; §3's actor table plus OQ-2 covers the only user-facing question, whether the surface changes at all). Constraint traceability, the non-negotiable for a governance-heavy initiative, is present end-to-end: OPEA-free gets a definition section (§5), a labelling discipline (FR-22/R12), a two-tier licensing gate (FR-19/V07a/V07b), and an ADR chain requirement (FR-29). As a brownfield PRD its existing-code references are accurate — spot-checked against the repository: ADR 0001 does record E2E suspension (2026-11-03, no runner capacity; `docs/adr/0001-gitlab-registry-build-scan-pipeline.md`), okf-011 is indeed "No raw AQL exposed to agents" (`docs/adr/okf-011-no-raw-aql-to-agents.md`), okf-034 is indeed still `Proposed` (`docs/adr/okf-034-knowledge-hierarchy-canonical-store.md`), and the research-extract (`research-extract.md`) contains every REC-1..REC-7, AM-1/AM-2, OQ-6 and the AG-UI taxonomy the PRD cites, with the full research folder at `research/technical-opea-free-agentic-document-qa-architectu-2026-11-16/`. The one shape risk — a broad vision dragging iteration-1 scope — is actively managed (§2 "no orchestrator groundwork is delivered in this scope"; §4.2; risk table row "Scope creep toward workflow/orchestration").

## Mechanical notes

- **FR ID continuity:** FR-1..FR-29 all present, unique, no duplicates — but presentation order is non-monotonic: FR-28/FR-29 appear in FG-4 *before* FR-23..FR-27 in FG-5. Harmless for extraction (IDs resolve), mildly confusing on linear read. Renumbering would break the two already-applied review rounds' references; prefer leaving as-is and noting it, or renumber only if the PRD is regenerated.
- **Assumptions roundtrip:** five `[ASSUMPTION]` tags — one inline (§3, "inherits the existing chat surfaces, multilingual behavior … and error contracts") and four in §12. The §12 list is the de-facto index, and the §3 inline assumption is only partially mirrored there (§12 item 2 covers multilingual; the "error contracts" and "chat surfaces inherited" halves have no §12 entry of their own). *Fix:* add a fifth §12 entry covering the §3 assumption verbatim, or fold the §3 text into item 2.
- **Cross-reference resolution:** all internal refs (FR↔FR, FR↔V-item, FR↔gate, NFR↔FR) resolve; NFR-2's "research §5.2" resolves to the full report via the extract's §3 (the extract itself has no numbered subsections — a reader following the citation from PRD to extract hits one indirection through the extract's own §5.2 pointer).
- **UJ protagonist naming:** not applicable — no UJs, correctly for this shape.
- **Required sections for stakes:** present — non-goals, risks with mitigations that name evidence (not aspirations), staging table with exit evidence, decision + verification tables with roles, assumptions, open questions. No `[NOTE FOR PM]` callouts (tensions surfaced via D-table/posture blocks instead — see Decision-readiness).
