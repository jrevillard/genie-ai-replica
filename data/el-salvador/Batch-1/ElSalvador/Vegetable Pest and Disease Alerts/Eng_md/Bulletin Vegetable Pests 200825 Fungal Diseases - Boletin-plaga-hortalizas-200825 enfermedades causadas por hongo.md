## Fungal Disease in Chili Cultivation

The seedling disease is the common disease in the first stage of plant development, also appearing in seedlings. It is caused by fungi that are generally present in all soils. It occurs more frequently when there is excess water in the irrigation or natural flooding.

The symptoms observed are strangulation at the base of the stem, turning reddish-brown; when gently rubbed, the entire bark or skin of the plant in the affected area becomes rotten, causing death.

Control begins by using high-quality seeds, efficient irrigation, establishing good drainage, disinfecting the soil before transplanting, and primarily rotating crops. At the time of transplanting, a dose of 13 grams of Azoxistrobin per 20-liter pump should be applied, this application should be made at the base of the plant, ensuring that the product reaches the plant's roots.
