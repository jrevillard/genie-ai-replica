## Fungal diseases in corn stalks

<!-- image -->

Fungal diseases typically appear during the post-harvest and storage period. They cause the decay of the kernels, due to the metabolism of these microorganisms that grow and reproduce when environmental factors, such as weather conditions, are favorable.

Fungi begin to appear when the relative humidity reaches 65%, producing enzymes that break down carbohydrates, fats, and proteins in the kernels, and degrade their quality. Fungi are capable of invading and causing infections in the field and during the storage period; they require a relative humidity of 65% to 90% and a high moisture content in the grain (24% to 25%) to grow. Diseases in corn stalks are known as corn stalk rot.

Corn stalk rot is caused by fungi and can be toxic to certain animal species, such as mammals and birds.
