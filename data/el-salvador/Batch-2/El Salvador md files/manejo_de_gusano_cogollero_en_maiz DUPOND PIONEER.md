## SPODOPTERA FRUGIPERDA PEST MANAGEMENT IN CORN CROPS

<!-- image -->

While *Spodoptera frugiperda* is a polyphagous species, it primarily affects corn and sorghum crops. During the early stages of plant development, the larva can damage the plant by cutting it near the ground (acting as a cutter), or by defoliating it partially or completely, which can lead to plant death. During the vegetative development period (6 leaves or more), the damage typically occurs in the whorl (acting as a whorl borer). In the final stage of the crop, it can affect the ears, stigmas, and kernels. Corn crops planted in warm areas are the most affected by this pest, as are late-season crops in temperate areas.

## LIFE CYCLE

Depending on the temperature, the entire life cycle of the pest can last between 30 and 70 days, being shorter in warmer conditions and vice versa. In each generation, the pest's life cycle is divided into four stages. The duration of these stages varies: (i) as a pupa (barely buried in the soil or on the stalks), lasts between 6-13 days; (ii) as an adult, 6 to 20 days; (iii) as an egg, between 2-5 days, and (iv) as a larva, between 17 to 32 days (in this stage it passes through 6 to 9 instars). In Argentina, it typically develops 3-4 generations per year in the field, with up to 6-7 observed when favorable environmental conditions are present. This aspect of the insect's life cycle can be considered key: exposure to low temperatures (&lt; 2°c) for short periods (&lt; 4 days) kills the pupae.

In general, pupae die at a very high percentage when exposed for short periods, for example, from 15 days at temperatures below 8°c (Murúa, 2014). In some regions of the northern part of the country, where conditions are favorable (temperature), the insect will overwinter in the soil as a pupa. In the central and southern agricultural areas of Argentina, the first infestations will come from migrations from warmer areas.

## INTEGRATED PEST MANAGEMENT

Monitoring is a necessary and important agricultural practice in the management of any corn crop, whether conventional or Bt. In areas with high pest pressure, the use of insecticides may be necessary as a complement.

The optimal time for application should be determined based on proper monitoring of the crop. It is important to consider the level of pest incidence, its development stage, the crop stage, and the existing environmental conditions.

## RECOMMENDED PRACTICES FOR PROPER INTEGRATED PEST MANAGEMENT:

- Crop rotation.
- Planting a refuge area in Bt crops.
- Early desiccation of host weeds.
- Early monitoring of fields and, if necessary, chemical control of pests before planting.
- Use of Bt corn treated with seed coatings.
- Maintaining the crop free of weeds and volunteer plants.
- Monitoring the crop from early stages.
- Evaluating the level of damage and chemical control if the damage threshold has been reached.
- Applying products at the recommended doses and times.

## MONITORING

It is common for the pest to affect the crop in patches. From the location of the oviposition, the larvae spread in a circular pattern, colonizing neighboring corn plants.

Therefore, it is recommended to use a monitoring pattern forming a 'X' that covers the field in five sampling stations with ten plants each.

Depending on when the crop is affected by the pest, the damage to the plant will vary, and the solutions to use will be different.

## LARVAL STAGES AND OPTIMAL CONTROL TIME

- Rotation of insecticide active ingredients and preservation of natural enemies using selective products.

The potential for damage will vary depending on the stage of the insect and the eventual chemical control will require different active ingredients, doses, and/or application methods. The optimal time for control is before the larva develops more than 1.5 cm (L3), as from this size they enter the whorl, making them difficult to control as they are not reached by the applied product. If the larvae are already in the whorl, higher volumes of spray are required to reach the target.

## DAMAGE ASSESSMENT METHOD

GRADES

<!-- image -->

0-1: No damage, or with damage similar to that caused by a pin. These damages are caused by the first instar larvae (L1). It is common to find that during this stage there are more eggs than hatched larvae.

2-4: Optimal control time: Damage similar to "window" damage or small circular lesions (approximately 1 to 1.5 mm in diameter) and/or few small elongated lesions (1 to 3 cm) without consumed epidermal membrane (scratching without a hole). These damages are caused by second and third instar larvae (L2-L3).

## RECOMMENDATIONS FOR SPODOPTERA CHEMICAL CONTROL

## 1. ESTABLISHMENT STAGE OF THE CROP.

THE MAIN OBJECTIVE IS TO PREVENT PLANT LOSS

- 5-6: Holes of different sizes, visible damage in the whorl with signs of soft consistency feces (not yet a "sawdust" plug). Ineffectiveness in control as larvae are inside the whorl. However, some of them may exhibit some nocturnal mobility, allowing them to be reached by the applied insecticide. Larvae of L4 and L5 stages.

7-8-9: Obvious destruction of the whorl in different degrees, with "sawdust" type feces plug. L6 larvae with "sawdust" plugs that completely prevent chemical control.

It is recommended to monitor the fields during the pre-planting period, before sowing, to detect the presence of *S. frugiperda* in the previous crop's weeds, grassy weeds, or volunteer plants.

Control of "Green Bridges": control weeds at least 30 days before planting. This prevents damage from large larvae that act as cutters in the newly planted crop.

## Detection of Larvae in Weeds Before Planting:

If the larval population is high, it is advisable to reduce it before planting. However, if the presence is not significant, the decision can be delayed to apply the product at the time of planting to control the newly hatched larvae.

During the growing season of the crop, we can use the Davis scale to identify the level of damage and relate it to the size of the larvae present at that time. For example, values on the Davis scale of 5 indicate larvae in the L4-L5 stage, already residing in the developing ear. Values on the Davis scale of 1, 2, and 3 indicate larvae smaller than 1.5 cm in length (larval stages L1-L3), which are generally more mobile and easier to control.

## MONITORING THE CROP BETWEEN V2 AND V6:

Application threshold: 20% of plants showing leaf damage (degree 3 on the Davis scale) and the presence of live larvae (L1-L3), or in the presence of the first cut plants. Observing the presence/absence of ovipositions will help determine the control strategy.

Control: Pyrethroid-based insecticides (contact) formulated for cutworm control, which are readily soluble, MC (micro-encapsulated), or oil-in-water emulsions (EW). In addition to leaf damage, if egg masses are also observed, apply Pyrethroid + IGR (Insect Growth Regulator). At this stage, acting as a cutworm, it is very important to return to the field three days later to verify the control.

## 2. FOLIAR EXPANSION PHASE.

THE MAIN OBJECTIVE IS TO BREAK THE PEST CYCLE BEFORE IT REACHES THE EAR

## MONITORING THE CROP BETWEEN V6 AND V10

Application threshold: 20% of plants showing leaf damage (degree 3 on the Davis scale) and the presence of live larvae (L1-L3). At the same time, we may find multiple larval stages coexisting (L6 larvae, which are found in ears with "sawdust" type feces, will not be controlled). Larvae in the L4 stage, which are located inside the ear but without the presence of feces, may be partially controlled by insecticides due to their limited mobility (they move towards the late afternoon/evening).

Control: With multiple larval stages and new ovipositions, apply Pyrethroid/Phosphorated/Carbamate + IGR. If the monitored larvae are in stages L3 or below, only an IGR can be applied.

## MONITORING THE CROP BETWEEN V10 AND VT.

Application threshold: 20% of plants showing leaf damage (degree 3 on the Davis scale) and the presence of live larvae (L1-L3). It is essential to avoid the establishment of new generations in the "late ear". This will ensure that: (a) the foliar area of the upper third of the plant, which impacts yield, is not affected; (b) the colonization of larvae during flowering is avoided in the future ears (R1).

Control: The present larvae should be controlled and future populations minimized by using next-generation residual insecticides, such as Diamides or Spinosines, which provide coverage of 15-20 days. While these products control different larval stages, the greatest benefit will be achieved by applying them to the younger stages of the pest.

Application: It is recommended to apply at a high pressure to achieve small droplets and use hollow cone tablets. Applications should always be made with oil.

Desired impacts: 40-50 per cm2, it is always recommended to use water-sensitive cards to verify the quality of the application.

Application and desired impacts are valid for phase 1 and 2.

* FORMULATED FOR CUTWORM CONTROL

The entire monitoring and control strategy should aim to suppress the pest during the growing season of the crop, as once it has established in the ear, the possibility of controlling it is significantly reduced, and it is difficult to reach the plant with the product at that height with proper coverage (number of drops per square centimeter containing the active ingredient). Due to the migratory nature of the pest, damage may not be observed during the vegetative period, but ovipositions may be recorded during the reproductive period. In this case, it is recommended to consult with your local technician, as the management will depend on multiple factors that cannot be addressed in this document due to the diversity of possible situations.
