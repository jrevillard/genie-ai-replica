## For Fermentation

The dryer provides a protected space from insects, wind, and rain, suitable for fermenting cocoa beans. To begin the process, the pods are opened at their optimal maturity stage, and the beans are separated, removing those affected by mold, pests, or other defects. The "wet" cocoa beans are placed in rohan boxes or similar containers, layered with leaves, and compacted to prevent air bubbles. The boxes are then covered with leaves, stacked, and wrapped in plastic to minimize heat loss. After two days, the temperature should have risen to approximately 40°C. After 48 hours, the boxes are turned every day, using wooden pallets, always covered again to retain heat, but allowing some air to enter. When the temperature starts to drop and observing the cocoa inside, if the beans appear brown and shrunken or hollow, the fermentation process is complete, and drying can begin.

For draining the liquid, a floor with a slope and a drainage pipe leading to a collection or absorption point in the ground, protected with gravel, are required.

## For Drying After Fermentation

After the fermentation process, the cocoa beans should be spread out in wooden boxes, maximizing the space available. They should be turned, preferably with a wooden rake, every two hours during the day to speed up the drying of the water film that the cocoa beans contain at the beginning of the process. To dry the beans, the maximum amount of available sunlight should be utilized, distributing the cocoa in the boxes throughout the dryer.

To achieve good results and uniform drying in the shortest possible time, it is recommended that the layer of beans be no more than 3 cm thick for even drying. The drying point can be detected by touch and with humidity measuring equipment, and should be 6-7%, which is the optimal level for storage or distribution.

## Solar Drying System for Cocoa

## Importance of a Solar Drying System for Cocoa

A solar drying system, similar to a greenhouse, is a structure used to carry out the fermentation and drying of cocoa beans, utilizing solar energy. The prototype being recommended is constructed with a metal frame and a polycarbonate cover. Inside, wooden boxes are used to ensure optimal fermentation. In these boxes, the beans are placed, covered with leaves, and turned every 24 hours until temperatures above 40 degrees Celsius are reached. The fermentation time depends on the type of bean.

After verifying that the cocoa has reached a temperature of 40 degrees Celsius, the beans are uncovered for drying.

The importance of this type of drying system is that it allows the temperature to increase by 5°C compared to the external conditions, which helps to accelerate the fermentation and drying process.

## Characteristics of the Solar Drying System

The solar drying system built at CENTA has the following characteristics:

- It has 8 fermentation boxes.
- Each box has a capacity of 286.34 lbs of wet beans.
- 8 boxes of fermentation yield 826 lbs of dry cocoa beans.
