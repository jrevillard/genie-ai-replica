## INTRODUCTION

For over a decade, the Centro Nacional de Tecnología Agropecuaria y Forestal (CENTA), through its Basic Grains Program, has been conducting research on CENTA A-8 rice, involving our researchers, extension agents, and producers.

Over the years, researchers have accumulated positive advancements, discovering the excellent characteristics of this germplasm in terms of grain yield, good milling quality, and resistance to diseases such as Piricularia. Therefore, CENTA hopes to help rice producers in our country with this new technology.

Reaching the release of CENTA A-8 has not been easy, but we are satisfied and proud to make this technological alternative available, which helps to meet the expectations of the main actors involved in the rice agro-productive chain in El Salvador.

CENTA A-8: RESISTANT TO PIRICULARIA AND HIGH-QUALITY

## INTRODUCTION

After ten years of observing a rice variety in the field, the Centro Nacional de Tecnología Agropecuaria y Forestal (CENTA) is making the CENTA A-8 variety available to rice producers, which is highly competitive due to its good agronomic characteristics and favorable reaction to major diseases, as well as its good qualities for milling. This was demonstrated through the validation process, in which this variety showed high potential yield in the localities of Zapotitán, Santa Rosa, and Hacienda Nueva, with yields of 147, 124, and 120 quintals per hectare, respectively. In terms of milling quality, it meets the established quality standards for commercialization, based on the limits of milling yield (68%), whole grain (55%), and broken grain (3%). With this contribution, we hope to contribute to the increase in production and productivity of this important sector.

## ORIGIN OF THE VARIETY

This variety originated in the International Rice Seedling Nursery of the International Center for Tropical Agriculture (CIAT) in 1996, with pedigree CT 12249-3-4-3-3P-1P, resulting from the cross CT 11519/CT 11492, which was subsequently recognized experimentally with the number 2322 and is currently known as CENTA A-8.

## CULTIVATION MANAGEMENT

## PLANTING SEASON

Rain-fed rice is planted in the second half of the rainy season (mid-May and early June); the variety CENTA A-8 is recommended to be planted from May to the first half of July. Transplanting is recommended for areas with high rainfall, such as Zapotitán.

## METHODS

## DIRECT SEEDING

It is recommended to deposit the seed in rows with a spacing of 0.20 to 0.30 m, the soil should be well prepared to facilitate the proper and uniform placement of the seed at a depth of 0.02 m, with a light covering to ensure uniform and rapid emergence of the seedling. In this system, it is recommended to use 98 kg of seed per hectare.

## SEEDING

This involves evenly spreading the seeds over the prepared ground, either by hand or mechanically. The recommended seed rate per hectare is between 98 and 130 kg.

## PLANTING BY TRANSPLANTING

This is done on the flooded and leveled soil. It begins with transplanting seedlings or young rice plants, typically between 18 and 21 days old, with three plants per hill, spaced 0.25 meters apart. This requires 45 kilograms of seed per hectare.

## FERTILIZATION

In soils where rice is grown, there may be deficiencies in nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium, magnesium, manganese, and zinc. When growing rice in direct seeding, the following factors should be considered: selecting a high-yielding variety, controlling weeds effectively, and managing the incidence of diseases.

## PEST CONTROL

Insects:

The insects that can cause economic damage to rice crops are described below:

Soil: rice weevil (Phyllophaga sp.) and cutworm (Eutheola sp.). For control, it is recommended to prepare the soil well, and to treat the seeds with a product containing Imidacloprid 70 WS, at a rate of 2 grams per kilogram of seed.

Foliage:

Rice planthoppers: green planthoppers (Hortensia sp.), rice leaf folder (Steneotarsonemus spinki)

Rice stem borers: rice stem borer (Rupela albinella)

Rice snout weevils: rice snout weevil (Mocis sp.), stem borer (Spodoptera sp.), grasshoppers (Conocephalus sp.)

Rice sucking pests: rice thrips (Tagosodes sp), rice bugs (Blissus sp, Oebalus sp), whitefly (Aleurocybotus occidus). For control of these pests, it is recommended to conduct sampling in the rice fields to verify and quantify their presence, as well as to utilize natural enemies, which will reduce damage through biological control. Chemical control should only be carried out when there are high infestations.

## DISEASES

Rice blast: The CENTA A-8 variety is resistant to this disease, so it is not recommended to apply any fungicide.

Sheath blight: The causal agent is Rhizoctonia solani. This disease can occur in all rice varieties, but to reduce the incidence, it is recommended to use fertilizers appropriately, to avoid high planting densities, and to maintain good weed control. The application of chemicals such as Benomil (0.30 to 0.60 kg/ha) or Tebuconazol-Triadimenol (0.40 l/ha) can be effective.

False smut: caused by Ustilaginoidea virens. This is an occasionally epidemic disease, whose symptoms are visible when the rice begins to mature its grain; the individual grains are transformed into masses of green-colored spores on the outside and yellow-orange on the inside.

It is favored by prolonged drizzle, periods of high relative humidity, followed by hot days, during the flowering period, poor panicle development, and the presence of sucking insects. It is recommended to have good fertilization management and good weed control. The recommended chemical control is Metiltiofanate 70 WP, at a dose of 0.5 to 1.0 kg/ha.

## WEEDS

Rice cultivation should be kept free of weeds during the first 45 days of the crop cycle (to avoid competition). Research has shown that weeds can reduce rice yields by 50% or more at this stage. Since manual weeding is quite difficult, it is recommended to use chemical products.

Herbicide application timing: herbicides can be applied before planting, pre-emergence, and post-emergence.

Pre-emergence herbicides: are generally applied to actively growing weeds, preferably when they reach a maximum size of 20-30 cm. The herbicide Glyphosate, at a rate of 3 to 5 I/ha, is mainly used for this purpose. This is applied during the appropriate times, planting systems, and in most areas where rice is grown in our country.

Pre-emergence herbicides: Pendimethalin (2.50 to 3.50 U/ha) is frequently used. For this type of application, the soil must be moist.

Post-emergence herbicides: products such as propaniles (5.5 to 10 U/ha), bentazon MCPA (1.5 to 2.0 U/ha), bispyribac sodium 40 SC (145 cc/ha), fenoxaprop-ethyl, this last one at a dose of 1.4 to 1.7 U/ha, are regularly used, specifically for difficult-to-control grasses such as Ixophorus, Cynodon, Digitaria, Echinochloa, among others; the halosulfuron methyl, 145 g/ha, which is recommended to be applied 22 days after planting and is specific for controlling Cyperaceae.

For the selection of herbicides, their mixtures, doses, and application times, a good field diagnosis should be carried out, which allows us to verify the condition of the soil, the type and development of weeds, as well as the crop.

## HARVEST

The optimal time for harvesting rice is when the panicle reaches its physiological maturity, that is, when 95% of the grains in the panicles have a brown color and the rest is yellow (which occurs around 110 days); and the moisture content of the grain is between 20 and 27%. The timing of the harvest is crucial, as if it is carried out when the grain has not matured, the yield is reduced, and in the threshing, the grain is very sticky and broken; on the other hand, if it is left to overmature, there is a risk that it will disperse at the time of harvesting, which reduces the yield and in the threshing, it may have broken grains.

In the country, the harvest is carried out manually or mechanically, according to the technological development or production system of the different productive areas.
