## INTRODUCTION

In recent years, the cultivation of lettuce has become one of the most important crops, both for agricultural producers and national institutions that promote vegetable production. This situation has been motivated by a variety of reasons, primarily economic and climatic.

Currently, lettuce cultivation is one of the most consumed vegetables, which has led to an increase in import levels, mainly from Guatemala. The importance of this fact lies in the fact that this situation can be reversed; the conditions that make this possible are as follows: El Salvador has areas with high productive potential, with appropriate climatic conditions, and through improvement, it has been possible to adapt new genetic materials to warm climates. Lettuce cultivation can be an alternative in areas where coffee cultivation has lost its value.

To increase the area of cultivation, information on its management is necessary; this allows producers to have a tool that helps them make better decisions regarding the management of the crop, reducing costs and using appropriate technologies for each location and the economic level of the producers.

With this publication, CENTA aims to provide answers to the most frequent questions faced by technicians and producers, hoping that its content will satisfy the expectations of those who work in this field. guide

## Importance

The cabbage is cultivated for the utilization of the leaves that make up the head, which can be consumed fresh, cooked in various ways, and pickled. Cabbage is also used to prepare sauerkraut (sauerkraut), a food originating in Asia, which consists of the fermentation of chopped cabbage in its own juice, to which salt has been added. Currently, this product is still very appreciated in Germany and the United States. This crop is high in vitamin C, iron, and the content of glucosinates has been proven to be effective against cancer, primarily lung cancer. Similarly, it is attributed to effects in reducing blood cholesterol. One hundred grams of cabbage contain 2.2 grams of protein, 4.1 grams of carbohydrates, 1.5 grams of fiber, 49 milligrams of calcium, 130 international units of vitamin A, and 47 milligrams of vitamin "C". These qualities make it a product that is recommended for incorporation into the family's diet.

Taking into account the trend of prices in the last five years, cabbage is a vegetable that, when managed properly, provides satisfactory profits for the producer; it is a crop that contributes to agricultural diversification, the generation of labor, and the reduction of imports. In the year 2000, the volume of imports in El Salvador was 17,253 tons, which represented a value of $2,514,951. The above data leads to the conclusion that there is a shortage in the planted area at the national level, of approximately 600 acres, which, when produced locally, would supply the national demand, without having to depend on imports.

It is specifically native to the coasts of the Mediterranean and Western Europe, it grows wild, found in places such as Denmark, England, France, and Greece, although always in coastal and coastal areas, but it develops better in cool climates. ## Stem

During the first vegetative cycle, the plant forms a relatively thick, short, juicy, erect, and branching stem; with the outer part woody and short internodes, it does not branch and does not exceed 30 cm, due to the fact that growth in length stops in the initial stages of development. The cabbage head corresponds to a stem that supports a large number of unfolded leaves, resting one on top of the other, and that form a more or less compact set, which encloses the terminal bud and the youngest leaves.

## Leaves

The cabbage head is made up of modified leaves and originates from the stem, with an angle that is different depending on the variety and that defines its compaction.

## Seed

Small, round, and brown, gray-red or black seeds; in one ounce there are 8,900 (300 seeds/g).

## Root

It is cylindrical, pivotable, and has secondary roots that absorb nutrients and water. Like the other botanical varieties of the species, it has a reduced and superficial root system, between 40 and 45 cm, which limits the exploratory capacity of the soil.

## Flowers

The plant produces hundreds of flowers in clusters; the corolla is yellowish with oval petals, measuring approximately 1 cm when open. It is hermaphroditic but with cross-pollination, which is carried out through wind and insects. The plant is auto-sterile due to incompatibility with its pollen, so it has entomophilous pollination. Once pollinated and fertilized, the flowers give rise to thick, straight or curved siliques, 10 cm long and 5 mm wide.

## Fruit

It is a silique elongated, ending in a cylindrical calyx, with numerous seeds. Dehiscent when dry.

## PHENOLOGY

Lettuce plants are biennial, in temperate climates, they take one year to grow and another to produce flowers and seeds. In tropical climates, the plant has a cycle of three to four months, generally does not flower. The first cycle of its life corresponds to the vegetative phase, represented by the development of roots, leaves and stems, and ends with the production of a wide and short stalk that acts as a storage organ. New leaves form a compact mass that develops from the inside and does not contain chlorophyll. These leaves are succulent and contain large amounts of starch and sugars.

## VEGETATIVE PHASE

The life cycle of lettuce, or vegetative growth phase, is the most important for producers and the only one that occurs naturally in our climatic conditions. This phase is divided into four stages, useful for planning crop management practices. First

## First stage

It takes place between eight and ten days, starts with the germination of the seed and ends when the seedling has four to five true leaves; it corresponds to the appropriate time for transplanting: During this stage, the plants develop their root system and the first true leaves.

## Second stage

Starts from the establishment of the plant to transplant until it has six to eight leaves. After recovering from the stress of transplanting, the plants enter a phase of rapid biomass increase. The leaf area increases rapidly, as does the root system and the plant stem.

## Third stage

Called pre-heading, the plant continues to produce leaves with elongated petioles and extended leaf blades, ending when the plant has approximately twelve leaves. The leaves originating at this time will not form part of the head and only some of the ones produced during the last stage will slightly bend to form a protective layer.

## Fourth stage

Is characterized by the production of leaves without petioles, which overlap forming a ball (palla), these grow rapidly, allowing the development of more succulent leaves until the ball or head reaches the appropriate size for each cultivar. At the end of this stage, the leaves have formed a compact ball that, when touched, feels firm and hard; in some cases, the inner leaves can exert a lot of pressure on the outer ones, causing cracks in the head.

## CLIMATIC AND EDAPHIC REQUIREMENTS

## Climate

In El Salvador, lettuce is cultivated in areas with altitudes ranging from 400 to 1800 meters above sea level, with temperatures between 15 and 28 C, the minimum for its germination ranges between 7 and 35*C, while for its growth it must remain between 5 and 24*C. In the last decade, hybrids and varieties have been developed that adapt to warm climates, that is, in a range from 15 to 35*C and altitudes between 100 and 500 meters above sea level.

## Soil

The cultivation of lettuce adapts to a variety of soils, however, good development is obtained in sandy soils, rich in organic matter; in heavy soils (clay), it is necessary to make good drainage to avoid waterlogging. Due to the constant demand for water, especially from the formation of the head to harvest, the cultivation is seriously affected when it is sown in sandy soils, so it is recommended to avoid cultivating in those that drain water quickly, unless it is provided with adequate drainage. The cultivation develops well in soils slightly acidic with a pH between 5.5 and 6.5. However, some diseases find it easy to spread when the pH is acidic, so it must be a priority for all producers to have a pH close to 7, when this is the case, measures must be taken to avoid planting repeatedly in the same soil, in order to prevent root rot, which is difficult to eradicate.

## VARIETIES

Lettuce is classified according to the shape of the head, the color and texture of the leaves or the duration of the growing period. According to the shape of the head, they are grouped into: round, oblong and conical; most commercial cultivars are round.

The texture of the leaves groups the cultivars with smooth leaves and the Savoy type; those of the latter group have their leaves corrugated, which gives them a very particular appearance. The duration of the growing period (harvest) is grouped into late, intermediate and early.

Evaluations carried out by CENTA, in El Salvador, have shown the adaptability of many varieties for the local production of lettuce, mainly for intermediate to high conditions (greater than 300 msnm), showing that there is availability of hybrids that respond favorably even under high temperature conditions. ## Use of trays

Due to the fact that traditional systems do not guarantee the production of healthy seedlings AND the high acquisition costs of seeds, the use of trays has become popular in recent years. Under this technique, the production of vegetable seedlings has been innovated, making it more efficient, as healthy, uniform, and better-rooted plants are obtained, preserving all their roots at the time of transplanting. In the tray, each plant develops individually, without competing with the others, which are better distributed and grow vigorously. Currently, the most widely used trays are those with 200 cavities. Trays can be handled under tunnels, with antivirus mesh or greenhouses with the purpose of protecting seedlings from insect and disease attacks. The only function of the trays is the development of seedlings and at no time do they constitute a means of transport for them. The transfer of seedlings to the transplant must be carried out in plastic pots or in cardboard boxes. This ensures the durability of the trays, reducing production costs per crop cycle. At the end of each production cycle, the trays must be disinfected in a solution based on sodium hypochlorite or another similar commercial product. place

## Bovine manure

The use of completely decomposed bovine manure, mixed with sifted sand and forest soil in a ratio of 3:1:1, has been used by some producers for the production of seedlings. The requirement for successful production is the decomposition and disinfection of the materials. degree

## Rice bran

Rice bran is a lightweight substrate, facilitates drainage and aeration, is easily available in our area and is widely used by producers who have acquired experience in its handling. However, it presents some inconveniences that must be considered, such as the slow decomposition rate; it is difficult to moisten, which requires very frequent watering in the first few days after sowing the seed. A strong limitation is the high concentration of silicon, which hinders its degradation. Analyses carried out by CENTA showed the presence of 42% of silica (on a dry basis) in burnt rice bran. The use of rice bran is recommended mainly as a means to facilitate the drainage of excess water, therefore it must be mixed with materials that facilitate the supply of nutrients and moisture retention. In the CENTA, burnt rice bran and forest soil were used in a ratio of 1:1:3, respectively. To improve the fertility of the mixture, 1500 grams of 15-15-15 fertilizer, finely ground, plus 100 grams of 0-20-0 per cubic meter of mixture were used, with the condition of frequent watering at the beginning, in order to moisten the rice bran as quickly as possible.

## Forest soil

In forested areas, it is possible to collect large amounts of decomposed mulch from leaf residues, which, when completely decomposed and pulverized, provide a substrate material. It has been used in a ratio of 2:1 with sand, which has facilitated good drainage:

## Wood ash

As with forest soil, it is a low-cost, lightweight material, very common in forest and sawmill areas. The use of pine ash, free of gummy exudate, as well as others from white woods, has given good results. ## Handling of trays at the time of sowing

Once the substrate to be used has been selected, it must be moistened before filling the trays. Some producers prefer to moisten the substrate after filling the trays; however, there are some substrates that increase their volume after being moistened, which requires emptying the cells that exceed their capacity. Once the substrate has been applied to the tray, it must be moderately compacted in order to achieve better contact between the moisture and the seed. It is recommended not to fill the tray completely, leaving 3 to 5 millimeters of space at the edge, which is necessary to deposit the seed and subsequently cover to complete the filling. Currently, the seed is acquired in presentations of 7,000 to 5,000 seeds, so it is necessary to apply one seed per cavity in order to reduce the cost of the same.

## Watering of the trays

Unlike production in the final field, in the case of seedling production, it is very difficult to provide a recommendation on how much and when to water: The variability in the materials used in the preparation of substrates, the conditions of seedling production (greenhouse, micro tunnel, ramada, etc.), as well as the climatic differences between areas, make it difficult to provide a general recommendation. However, with an intermediate level of technology, under greenhouse conditions, where producers use backpack pumps for watering seedlings, it is generally carried out between three to five times a day, depending on the substrate used and the temperature; mainly in the first few days. The best thing is to constantly monitor the moisture content of the substrate so as to avoid both excessive water or drying. Once the plant has emerged, the watering is generally reduced to two to three times a day.

## CULTURAL PRACTICES PREPARATION OF SOILS

Some insects and diseases develop through the residues of harvest. In order to eliminate the sources of infestation of previous harvests, prior to the preparation of soils, the removal of harvest residues can significantly help to reduce their incidence, although it is recommended to rotate crops, to prevent the infestation of diseases in the fields, mainly bacterial, this can be reduced by a good soil preparation, and mainly when it is carried out in due time. Tilling reduces the sources of inoculum of Rhizoctonia sp, Mycosphaerella brasssicola, Sclerotinia sclerotiorum and Pernospora parasitic.

The preparation of soil can also eliminate populations of Plutella xylostella that are found in harvest residues. In relation to weeds, early plowing will help the germination of seeds that can be eliminated by other methods of control later.

Minimum tillage should include cross tilling and the number of rows necessary that dig at least 30 cm, to turn the soil. The rows should be carried out with intervals of one week to expose and destroy the sources of inoculum by solar effect.

## TRANSPLANT

The plants adapt very well to transplanting, whether under the trench or furrow system, the seedlings are ready for transplant when they have achieved four true leaves, during the 22 to 28 days after planting. Only healthy and good-looking plants should be used to achieve the desired yield. Special care should be taken to avoid damaging the plants during handling, as they are very sensitive to damage, it should be avoided the use of trays for the transfer of plants to the place of transplant.

## Three-row system

In winter, planting in trenches helps to prevent the spread of pathogens by reducing runoff. Trenching can allow a greater number of plants per unit of area, when the arrangements approach the known "three-row" system however, the cleaning will become difficult. For small head cabbages, it is possible to establish spacings of 1.2 m between rows, while the three-row system can be formed in each row at spacings of 50 to 60 cm. Under this system, up to 24,000 plants per hectare can be managed (Photo 4).

## FERTILIZATION

Due to the fact that the cabbage production areas in the country are very diverse, the recommendations on the requirements of fertilization of the crop must be based on the soil analysis of the country.

## Nitrogen

Nitrogen is one of the main elements required by the lettuce plant. High levels of nitrogen extraction cause low yields in subsequent crops, therefore it is necessary to apply nitrogen fertilization according to the needs of the crop. Plants with deficiencies in this element present elongated leaves and petioles, rosette of leaves, small heads, and maturation is delayed. The correction of this deficiency should be carried out using fertilizers in the form of nitrates to achieve faster effects. A late application of nitrogen causes the formation of small heads and prolongs the crop cycle. An excess affects the appearance of small, compact heads and the rotting of the outer edges of some leaves within the lettuce. The nitrogen fertilization must be fractioned into three or four applications, according to the recommendations of CENTA. The moments of application of fertilizers should be taken advantage of to carry out the weeding and cleaning necessary for the crop.

## Phosphorus

The application of phosphorus should be carried out only once. Its availability favors the formation and increases the percentage of commercial heads. Its deficiency slows growth, the outer leaves acquire a purple color, presenting more intense dark green leaves and reddish edges on their inner part; which affect the presentation and are a cause of rejection in the market.

## Potassium

Potassium increases resistance to low temperatures and maintains turgor during the summer. Its deficiency is characterized by the browning of the edges of the older leaves. It should be applied at the time of transplanting:

## Calcium

High temperatures and variations in soil moisture hinder the movement of calcium in the leaves; in addition, lettuce does not tolerate acidic soils with low calcium content. Calcium deficiency can occur where the available levels of this element are low, as well as where there is a nutrient imbalance in the soil. By using lime, the plant can increase the availability of nutrients. The determination of acidity and exchangeable aluminum can be carried out in the CENTA laboratories, in order to determine and recommend the necessary amendments.

## Boron

A boron deficiency causes the rupture and blackening of tissues, causing slow growth and deformation of the plant. The core of the lettuce shows a brownish color. Boron deficiencies are generally more noticeable during the dry period. In soils where boron deficiencies are known, it is recommended to apply 2 kg/ha of boron. The amount applied to the foliage should never exceed 0.4 kg/ha. boron

## Molybdenum

A molybdenum deficiency in lettuce usually occurs in sandy or acidic soils, the symptoms are characterized by the mottling of the leaves, chlorotic areas, and in general give the appearance of remeo. It is possible to carry out corrections based on sodium molybdate, applied to the foliage.

As a general recommendation, microelements should never be mixed with insecticides, unless recommended by the manufacturers.

## IRRIGATION

Due to the succulence of its leaves, lettuce consumes large amounts of water, therefore it must be provided frequently. An imbalance in the water supply can limit the absorption of calcium. Crops using sprinkler irrigation must be managed carefully, as this method can spread fungal and bacterial diseases such as: Peronospora parasitica and Xanthomonas campestris. Furrows can be very well utilized if they are carried out on well-leveled and drained soils, otherwise they cause waterlogging and the formation of a favorable microclimate for the development of diseases. Drip irrigation is probably the best option, although the initial investment is high, it is easier to control the volume of water that is applied; currently there are drip emitters on the market with fixed flow rates, which allow you to estimate the most suitable time, according to the climatic and soil characteristics of each area. irrigation irrigation irrigation

## USE OF NATURAL COVERINGS

In order to conserve soil moisture and reduce the incidence of weeds, it is recommended to apply vegetal coverings with slow decomposition, corn straw, sorghum, rice, etc., in layers of 5 to 10 cm thick. In the high areas of our country, such as Las Pilas, with this practice it is possible to increase the soil temperature during cold periods. However, there are inconveniences that must be known for its management; the use of natural coverings requires periodic observation, as these can be a refuge for pests that attack the crop and at the same time make it difficult to carry out tillage, in some cases.

## CROP ROTATION

To the extent possible, it should be avoided the successive sowing of lettuce in the same land, as this increases the levels of inoculum of diseases that attack it, as well as the populations of insects. Therefore, it is advisable to sow in the following season a different crop that does not belong to the family Brasicaceae, such as cucumber, tomato, bean, etc.

## Biology and type of damage

Its life cycle comprises four stages: egg, larva, pupa and adult. The eggs are white and shiny, acquiring a round shape before hatching. The larvae and grubs have an elongated, translucent or creamy white shape; they feed on the roots and bases of the stems; the affected plants wither, turn yellow and fall off; if the attack is severe, the plants may die. After feeding on the roots, the larva turns into a brown-gold pupa and is found inside a soil cell. The beetles, grubs or caterpillars that are the adults, are pale brown or reddish-brown. They are attracted to light and seek plants such as grasses, cassava, black wood, guava and charcoal to take refuge, feed and reproduce.

In low and dry areas, the chicken completes its life cycle in two years. During the first year, the beetles lay eggs, and the larvae grow to a medium size. In the following summer, the medium larvae survive in the soil, in the deep layers (up to 1 or 2 meters) where there is sufficient moisture. With the first rains, in May and June, they begin to activate, moving to the less deep layers, where they come into contact with the roots of the plants. By feeding on the roots and bases of the stems, they cause serious damage to the crops, during the months of June to September, the plants die.

## Sampling

It is important to take a sample just before transplanting, to know the population of the chicken. Since this will determine the control measures. Five samples should be taken, well distributed throughout the plot; each should have a width and depth of 30 cm. The soil collected from the samples should be placed on white plastic to count the larvae present. If more than 6 large larvae or 5 medium larvae are found in the five locations, the chicken population in the plot is considered high and control measures must be taken.

## Cultural control

Prepare the soil during the dry season, starting with a deep plowing, or with several plowing passes, to bring the larvae that are in the soil to the surface, exposing them to the air and sun, causing them to dry out or because the chickens, birds and frogs eat them.

## Chemical control

When high populations of chicken larvae are found in the soil, it is necessary to protect the plants to prevent damage. To do this, the roots can be washed and lightly sprinkled with ground neem powder. To protect 15,000 lettuce seedlings, it is necessary to use 2 to 4 pounds of ground neem.

It is also possible to use insecticides such as: Chlorpirifos 2.5G (35 to 40 kgh/ha) or Etoprofos 15 GR (15 to 30 kg/ha), following the instructions of the respective package.

## Black Rot

Causal agent: Xanthomonas campestris p.v. campestris (Pammenl) Dowson

Other names: Bacteriosis, bacterial wilt of cabbage, bacterial rot, black vein, yellow-brown spot.

It is caused by the bacterium Xanthomonas campestris p.v. campestris which can penetrate healthy plants through lesions in the leaves, stem or root, caused during transplanting or during cultural operations, as well as through the opening of the stomata and hydathodes of the leaves.

## Symptoms

The symptoms can be observed approximately 43 days after infection. The infected plants may show: wilting, yellowing of leaves and darkening of the veins; however, the characteristic symptom of this disease is the curling and wilting of the leaf edge and the appearance of "V" shaped lesions.

As the infection progresses, the bacteria invades the vascular system, and the plant shows: darkening of the veins, death of the foliage and general wilting.

## Biology

The bacteria can be transported by: seeds, rainwater, machinery, tools used for cultural operations and equipment for tillage; it can also survive in the residues of previous crops and weeds of the Brassicaceae family.

When newly transplanted seedlings are infected, there is no head formation, while if heads of cabbage infected with are harvested, they deteriorate rapidly, reducing their shelf life.

The disease manifests severely under environmental conditions where there is high heat and humidity; the optimal temperature for development and appearance of symptoms ranges between 20 and 28 %.

## Cultural Control

Purchase certified seeds or completely healthy seedlings, free from diseases. Plant varieties of cabbage that are tolerant to the attack of this bacteria. Practice crop rotation every three years with other vegetable species; disinfect equipment and tools used for crop maintenance.

## Chemical Control

Apply preventative applications of copper hydroxide (1.5 to 2.0 kgha) according to the instructions and dosage specified on the product label.

## Symptoms

Initially, the first symptoms are observed on the outer leaves of the plant in the form of small, wet spots, which quickly spread, becoming dark brown.

## Biology

The Erwinia spp. bacteria survives in the soil and in the remains of previous crops; it penetrates healthy tissue through lesions in leaves, stems, and roots.

This disease develops easily under conditions of high humidity and if the ambient temperature is between 25 and 30%.

Plant cabbage in areas that are free from this disease, harvest the heads completely dry, and avoid impacts and bruises during transport.

## Stem Rot

Causal agent: Pythium spp, Fusarium spp, Rhizoctonia solani

## Symptoms

This disease is common in the seedling stage, causing the sudden death of seedlings before they emerge from the soil, as the fungus invades them, preventing their development.

When the infection occurs in young plants, the lesion is observed on the stem at the neck level, at ground level; the sick tissue is dark brown, watery, and causes a constriction of the stem, causing the plant to wilt. Both in the seedling and in the planting, wilted plants are observed, arranged in circles in rows.

## Biology

These pathogens are favored in soils with abundant moisture for prolonged periods; however, they can survive in the soil for a long time without causing damage until the environmental conditions and the presence of susceptible plants are favorable.

## Cultural Control

Use commercial substrate or properly disinfected homemade substrate when sowing seedlings, water as needed to avoid excess moisture in the soil.

## Chemical Control

Apply Propamocarb (850 to 900 cc/ha) plus Carbendazim (560 to 600 cc/ha) to the trunk of the plants according to the instructions on the label.

## Alternaria Spot

Causal agent: Alternaria brassicae (Berk) Sacc.

## Symptoms

At the onset of the disease, small lesions of circular shape, dark brown or black, are observed, which spread and are bordered by yellowish tissue in the form of a halo. If the environmental conditions are favorable, these lesions can be covered with dark dust-like spores.

If the infection is severe, several lesions merge, causing the yellowing of the leaf and defoliation of the plant.

*Alternaria brassicae* (Berk) Sacc is a fungus that can be transmitted by seed. It can be present either in the form of mycelium or in the form of spores; it is also present in the residues of previous crops in cruciferous family host plants.

## POST-HARVEST HANDLING

The product, when harvested, must be handled with care during transport to the storage area to avoid any damage. Only heads of good consistency, without yellow leaves and mechanical damage, should be stored. Before being stored, three to six loose leaves should be left, as they interfere with ventilation between heads, and ventilation is essential for successful storage. Cabbage rots quickly in dry conditions, so high humidity is needed to keep the leaves green and turgid. Cabbage stored at 0 °C decomposes less when the relative humidity is high (90-95%).

Under the above conditions, the storage life can be more than three weeks, however, in our environment, this is not economically advantageous, as the national market is characterized by being for immediate sale. After storage, the loose leaves should be trimmed again to remove the damaged ones. It is recommended that cabbage not be stored with products that produce ethylene, as they cause the leaves to fall and the green color of the cabbage to be lost. If the heads are to be stacked, they should not exceed 1.5 meters in height to prevent deterioration.

## PROCESSING OF CABBAGE

## Making Sauerkraut (Fermented Cabbage)

At certain times of the year, the price of the product in the market is low, which does not favor obtaining profits. Under these circumstances, knowledge of the processing of the heads helps to add value to the production, significantly improving income. One of the most well-known forms is the fermentation of the leaves.

Starting from the original name Sauerkraut, one of the most well-known forms of preservation has been the lactic fermentation of the cabbage leaves. Lactic acid fermentation is achieved by combining two factors: the concentration of salt and the decrease in the pH of the brine, due to the production of lactic acid by fermentative bacteria. The fermentation takes place in plastic containers with different capacities. These containers are installed in covered industrial sheds, although in some warm areas, the containers are placed outdoors. The cabbage is selected for its firmness and uniform color. Once harvested, it is advisable to let them dry, which makes them soften, operation that is achieved by leaving them for a few days piled up to air, before starting their manipulation. Since their fresh leaves are very fragile, they break not only when cut, but also when deposited in the fermentation containers.

<!-- image -->

<!-- image -->

<!-- image -->

<!-- image -->

Before proceeding to manual or mechanical cutting, the outer leaves and the inner core of the cabbage are removed: They are subjected to a thorough washing with hot water. This operation is carried out prior to fermentation, whose objective is to eliminate the microorganisms, dirt, and soil residues that the fruits have attached. Subsequently, cut into quarters with a sharp stainless steel knife, their leaves are cut into strips of 6 or 8 millimeters. The strips are weighed to add salt in a proportion of 2.5% by weight. They are well mixed with the cut leaves and taken to the containers that are usually lined with a layer of paraffin to prevent the product from darkening. The salt used must contain less than 1% of sodium carbonates or bicarbonates, calcium, and magnesium fine

The salt acts as a desiccant, causing the vegetative juice to leave its cells, while also helping with fermentation, allowing lactic bacteria to predominate. In some cases, it is also added to the product before fermentation, various flavorings to aromatize the preparation, such as pepper, cumin, garlic, etc. regular

The cabbage strips are placed in the buckets with salt and are tightly packed to expel as much air as possible that is trapped between the strips. Once full, almost to the edge, a wooden lid is placed on top and weights or stones of a weight that causes the cabbage juice to come out from the bottom up, without exceeding said wooden lid. To prevent the presence of insects, the top must be covered with a muslin cloth. The juice that is detaching from the tissues due to the action of salt and pressure, in a few hours, is such that it covers practically the entire product.

Once fermentation has begun, a large amount of gas is produced, which then activates the lactic bacteria, resulting in an increase in the mass temperature while also increasing its acidity to 1.75-1.80% in lactic acid, if the ambient temperature is high. The most suitable temperature for the development of lactic bacteria is 30 "C, although this fermentation can be carried out at temperatures of 7 "C, although in these extreme cases the fermentation occurs much more slowly.

As you can see, fermentation is directly related to temperature. At 30 "C, it lasts for about 8 days at 26 "C 12 days, at 23 "C 18 days and at 20 "C 25 days. The fermentation can be considered finished when its acidity reaches 1 in lactic acid. After approximately 20 days of fermentation, the product is in optimal condition for consumption: 8%

Once the fermentation is complete, in order to preserve the product, it must be packaged in conditions that do not alter it due to the action of pathogenic agents. Only glass will be used as packaging material. This choice is due to the following advantages: they are impermeable to water, gases, odors, etc.; they are inert; they can be subjected to thermal treatments; they are transparent and enhance the content.

<!-- image -->

The product is placed in containers that close hermetically, adding its own set; it is pre-sterilized or preheated to a temperature of 70 "C, the containers are closed and sterilized at 100 %C in a steam bath for 30 or 50 minutes depending on the capacity of the containers (500 or 1,000 g). If there is not enough juice from the fermentation itself, fresh brine of 10% or simply water can be added, since the solid substance contains enough acid and salt for its preservation.
