## 1. OBJECT

This standard establishes the physico-chemical and microbiological specifications for honey from Apis mellifera.

## 2. SCOPE

Honey from bees that is marketed in El Salvador, regardless of its origin, presentation, and final destination.

## 3. DEFINITIONS

- 3.1 Additives: a substance that is not part of the product's composition and is added as a technological function.
- 3.2 Adulterant: a substance intentionally added that modifies the natural composition of a product. For example: commercial glucose, fructose, and sucrose.
- 3.3 Hive: a structure prepared by humans for housing a bee colony; it contains the bee population and the honeycomb with larvae and food reserves.
- 3.4 Contaminant: any biological or chemical agent, foreign matter, or other substance present in food that may compromise the safety or suitability of food.
- 3.5 Fructose / Levulose / Fruit Sugar: a monosaccharide commonly found in fruits and in appreciable amounts in honey and in invert sugars.
- 3.6 Glucose / Dextrose: a monosaccharide produced by the hydrolysis of starch and disaccharides such as sucrose; it has a sweet taste, although less than fructose and sucrose.
- 3.7 Larva: the second immature stage in the metamorphic development of insects.
- 3.8 Honey: a food product produced by bees from nectar collected from flowers or from secretions from living parts of plants or from the excrement of sucking insects on plants, which the bees collect, transform, store, and leave to ripen in the honeycomb of the hive.
- 3.9 Creamed honey / paste: honey made from pure bee honey. It has a pasty or creamy consistency due to the crystallization process.
- 3.10 Multifloral / Polifloral / Milleflower honey: a product made from a mixture of nectars from various flower species.
- 3.11 Monifloral / Unifloral honey: a product primarily from flowers of the same family, genus, or species and has characteristic sensory, physico-chemical, and microscopic properties.

## BEE HONEY. SPECIFICATIONS

- 3.12 Capping: a layer of wax produced by bees to seal the cells of the honeycomb, whether with honey or larvae.
- 3.13 Comb: a structure consisting of a series of hexagonal cells that bees build and use to deposit and store some of their products and to develop their offspring.
- 3.14 Uncapped comb: a honeycomb with honey to which the capping has been removed.
- 3.15 Sucrose: a disaccharide formed by the union of a glucose and a fructose molecule.

## 5. CLASSIFICATION AND DESIGNATION

Regardless of its classification, honey must meet the requirements specified in section 6.2, except where otherwise indicated.

## 5.1 CLASSIFICATION

## 5.1.1 According to botanical origin

- a) Flower honey: This is obtained primarily from the nectar of flowers and is distinguished by:
    1. Monofloral or monofloral honey.
    2. Polyfloral, polyfloral, or thousand-flower honey.

- b) Honey from honeydew: This is obtained primarily from secretions of plant parts or from excretions of sucking insects that feed on plants.

## 5.1.2 According to the harvesting method

- a) Filtered honey: This is obtained by filtering from honeycomb without larvae.
- b) Pressed honey: This is obtained by pressing honeycomb without larvae.
- c) Centrifuged honey: This is obtained by centrifuging from honeycomb without larvae.

## 5.1.3 According to presentation

a) Honey: This is in liquid, crystallized, or a mixture of both states.

b) Honey in comb: This is stored by bees in honeycomb cells, constructed by them, that do not contain larvae, and is marketed as a whole comb or in sections.

c) Honey with comb pieces: This contains one or more pieces of comb with honey, free of larvae.

d) Crystallized or granulated honey: This has undergone a solidification process as a result of glucose crystallization, which can be natural or induced.

e) Creamy or creamed honey: This has a fine crystalline structure and may have undergone a physical process that gives it this structure.

## 5.1.4 According to production method

a) Conventional honey: This is honey obtained using traditional production methods.

b) Organic/ecological honey: This is honey that, during its production and processing, meets the certification requirements for this designation.

## 5.1.5 According to destination

a) Honey for direct consumption

b) Honey for industrial use (industrial honey): This meets the requirements specified in section 6.2, except for diastase index and hydroxymethylfurfural content, which may be less than 8 (on the Schade scale or its equivalent on the Gothe scale), and greater than 60 mg/kg, respectively.

## 5.1.6 According to Process

a) Processed honey: This is honey that has been subjected to a conditioning process for commercialization, which may include homogenization, filtration, mechanical crystallization, or thermal treatment.

b) Unprocessed honey: This is honey that has not been subjected to conditioning processes for commercialization.

## 5.2 DESIGNATION

The product must be designated as "honey," "bee honey," "natural honey," or "natural bee honey," and may be accompanied by its classification as indicated in section 5.1.

The term "honey" may not be used in syrups or juices of vegetable origin.

## 6. COMPOSITION AND REQUIREMENTS

## 6.1 COMPOSITION

Honey is composed of various sugars, predominantly glucose and fructose. It also contains proteins, amino acids, enzymes, organic acids, mineral substances, pollen, and may contain sucrose, maltose, melezitose, and other oligosaccharides (including dextrins), as well as traces of fungi, algae, yeasts, and other solid particles, as a result of the honey extraction process.

Pollen content must not be removed by any type of process.

## 6.2 REQUIREMENTS

## 6.2.1 Organoleptic characteristics

a) Color: from light to dark, including various shades of yellow and amber, but uniform throughout the volume of the container it is in. (See Annex A).

- b) Taste: characteristic of its botanical origin.
- c) Odor: characteristic of its botanical origin.

- d) Consistency: liquid, viscous, or completely crystallized.

## 6.2.2 Physical-chemical parameters:

## 6.2.2.1 Maturity

- a) Reducing sugars (calculated as invert sugar):
    1. Flower honey: minimum 60%
    2. Honey from honeydew and mixtures with flower honey: minimum 45%
- b) Moisture: maximum 20%
- c) Apparent sucrose
    1. Flower honey: maximum 5%
    2. Honey from honeydew and mixtures: maximum 10%
- d) Fructose/glucose ratio: greater than or equal to 1
- e) 6.2.2.2 Deterioration
- a) Free acidity: maximum 50 meq / kg
- b) Diastatic activity: at least 8 on the Schade scale or its equivalent on the Gothe scale.
- c) Hydroxymethylfurfural: maximum 60 mg/kg

## 6.2.3 Additives and Contaminants

It must not contain any additives or contaminants.

## 6.2.4 Hygiene

The honey must not exceed the maximum limits for contaminants established in paragraphs 6.2.5 and 6.2.6.

Its processing must be carried out in accordance with Executive Agreement No. 218, dated September 7, 2007, in the area of Agriculture and Livestock, the Salvadorean Mandatory Norm for Good Manufacturing Practices for Honey Processing Plants, and the general principles on food hygiene recommended by NSR 67.00.241:99 "Code of Principles for General Food Hygiene Practices".

## 6.2.5 Microbiological Parameters

The honey must meet the following microbiological parameters:

- Aerobic mesophilic colony count (at 31 ± 1°C): maximum 1 x 10^4 UFC / g
- Salmonella: absence / 25 g
- Total and fecal coliforms: absence
- Fungi and yeasts: 1 x 10^2 UFC / g

## 7. SAMPLING

## 7.1 SAMPLING PLANS

Sampling will be carried out in accordance with the procedure established in the Codex Alimentarius FAO/OMS standard. Sampling Plans for Pre-packaged Foods (CAC/RM 42-1969) Volume XIII

## 8. ANALYSIS METHODS

The physical and chemical parameters and microbiological parameters of the product will be determined according to the following:

- Reducing Sugars: CAC/Vol. III, Suppl. 2, 7.1; 1990.
- Humidity, Refractometric Method: AOAC International, 15 th Edition, 969.38B; 1990.
- Apparent Sucrose: CAC/Vol. III Suppl. 2, 7.2; 1990.
- Insolubles Solids: CAC/Vol. III Suppl. 2, 7.4; 1990.
- Minerals (Ash): CAC/Vol. III Suppl. 2, 7.5; 1990.
- Free Acidity: AOAC International, 15 th Edition, 962.19; 1990.
- Hydroxymethylfurfural (HMF): CAC/Vol. III Suppl. 2, 7.7; 1990.
- Total and Fecal Coliforms: AOAC International. Bacteriological Analytical Manual, 8 th Edition, Chapter 2, 4; 1995.
- Fungi and Yeasts: AOAC International. 1995. Bacteriological Analytical Manual, 8 th Edition, Chapter 18; 1995
- Salmonella s.p: AOAC International. Bacteriological Analytical Manual, 8 th Edition, Chapter 5; 1995.
- Antibiotics: ELISA (Enzyme-Linked Immunosorbent Assay)
- Pyretroids: HPLC (High-Performance Liquid Chromatography)
- Organochlorines, Organophosphates, Thiazolidine Derivatives, Chlorinated and Formamidine: Gas Chromatography.
- Heavy Metals: Atomic Absorption
