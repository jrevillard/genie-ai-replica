Defects to consider during selection:

1. Fruits damaged by birds, rodents, or insects.
2. Fruits with cuts, bruises, or other damage.
3. Fruits with premature or over-ripeness.
4. Fruits damaged by diseases such as finger tip rot (Trachysphaera fructigena, Verticillium dahliae, stachylidium sp.)
5. Fruits that are too large or too small (outside the standard for the target market)
6. Fruits damaged by sun exposure.
7. Fruits with tears.
8. Fruits with broken stems due to mechanical damage.

## Washing, Sorting, and Disinfection

These operations are generally performed simultaneously, but there may be farms where each is carried out in separate containers. After sorting, the fruit should be placed in the washing tank or pile. Avoid touching the fruit with your hands or rubbing it against the edge of the tank to prevent damage and handling marks.

## Packaging

Packaging plays a very important role in presentation, protection, and handling; it helps maintain the quality of the product and facilitates the commercialization process. Packaging is done in agricultural sacks for the domestic market and in cardboard boxes for export, with an approximate weight of 12 kg. Bananas are classified according to quality and weight, according to the buyer's specifications.

## The Banana

The fruit is mainly composed of water (61%), which continues to live after being harvested; internal changes continue, associated with the respiration and transpiration of the fruit. Respiration involves the consumption of oxygen and provides energy for ripening, generating carbon dioxide and water. Transpiration is the evaporation of water, the main cause of weight loss.

## POST-HARVEST OPERATIONS

Post-harvest includes all the activities and treatments applied to preserve the quality of the fruit; from harvesting, transportation, packaging, and distribution.

## Harvesting Indices

These are the parameters or indicators that tell the farmer which bunches are ready to be harvested, according to the demand in the market and according to the conditions in the field (variety, climate, availability).

## Harvesting

This involves cutting the clusters of fruit from the plants when they have reached optimal development, according to market preferences. The harvesting process relies on colored tapes that indicate the age of the cluster.

## The most commonly used harvest indicators are:

Chronological: This refers to the number of weeks from flowering to harvest. This varies depending on the variety.

Fruit color: The change in color of the fruit, from dark green to light green and yellow.

Shape and size of the fruit: The fruit elongates and develops, with the edges disappearing. Once the fruit has reached its physiological maturity, it develops good flavor and aroma.

## Post-harvest care on the farm:

- Avoid exposing the bananas to direct sunlight after harvesting, to prevent damage from bruising and uncontrolled ripening.
- Minimize impacts and injuries, as these not only damage the appearance but also allow microorganisms to quickly deteriorate the product.
- If mixing ripe and unripe bananas, be aware that the latter will ripen quickly due to the effect of ethylene.

## Selection

Selection involves separating the high-quality fruit from those that, due to their external appearance, should not be sold. The fruit that is discarded during selection is sold at a lower price and is used for human consumption, animal feed, or organic fertilizer production on the farm.
