## BOTANICAL ASPECTS

Onion belongs to the genus Allium, which contains a large number of horticultural species, which are cultivated for their bulbs or foliage and are used as a condiment in various dishes.

The great genetic diversity and the ease with which spontaneous crosses occur between several species belonging to the genus Allium, cause some complications for taxonomists, producers and consumers. Some close relatives of the onion (Allium cepa L) are: leek or garlic (Allium ampeloprasum L or porrum); garlic (A. sativum L); chives (A. schoenoprasum L).

## PHENOLOGY

The life cycle of onion plants is of medium complexity, not only because it is a bianual crop, but also because of the large number of factors that regulate the transition from the vegetative growth phase to the bulb formation phase, and from this phase to the reproductive phase.

When the seed germinates, the primary root and the lower part of the cotyledon emerge quickly, turning green and taking an erect position. At this point, the first adventitious root emerges from the stem, and the first leaf grows through the tubular wall of the cotyledon.

Germination and emergence of seedlings are determined by soil temperature and water availability. The optimal temperature for seed germination is between 20 and 25 °C, under these conditions, seedlings sprout between eight and ten days after sowing.

The plant's growth process continues at a constant rate of one new leaf per week, until it reaches the characteristic number of the variety or until changes in the light period or other external factors activate bulb formation. As bulb formation progresses, the older leaves dry up, so that at harvest, the first leaves have disappeared or remain as dry scales on the outside of the bulb. the

At the end of the bulb formation stage, a loss of turgor occurs in the neck region, and the leaves collapse. At this point, onion plants enter a dormant period, whose duration varies depending on the cultivar. This is the optimal time for harvest.

The second period of the onion's life cycle corresponds to the reproductive phase. The plant emits a variable number of flowering stems, which can be between 0.50 and 1.5 m tall; with a terminal spherical umbel inflorescence.

Flower production is determined by temperature, mineral nutrition, the duration of the light period, and, of course, the genetic structure of each cultivar. When the conditions are appropriate, onion plants pass directly from the bulb formation phase to the flowering phase.

## Temperature

<!-- image -->

Excessive temperature is an unfavorable factor for the crop. It is generally developed in cold climates, but there are genetically improved varieties that can grow in a wide range of temperatures. In El Salvador, plantings have been made very close to sea level (35 msnm) in October-November months with temperatures between 15 and 18 °C, obtaining good results. today '

It adapts to different temperature ranges and develops in warm, temperate, and cold climates, between 50 and 1800 msnm. It produces best at altitudes above 900 msnm, with a dry and sunny environment; temperatures between 18 and 25 °C are more appropriate; however, high temperatures can produce undesirable effects, such as: a greater tendency to produce divided and elongated bulbs, increased pungency (spicy and smelly flavor), and loss of sweetness. Low temperatures induce the formation of the flowering stem, and onions do not pass beyond the juvenile stage. To avoid this phenomenon, it is sown in September to October, in addition to using varieties adapted to the area. Sweet onions need cool nights with temperatures between 10 and 15 °C, and during the day, 26 °C to achieve high sugar levels in the bulb.

## Altitude

The height above sea level does not have a greater influence on the crop, as it grows well from 35 to 2000 m above sea level; however, what should be taken into account before planting is that the variety is for planting in areas where the daily light range is between 8 to 12 hours.

## Relative Humidity

Relative humidity has great importance in the incidence of fungal diseases; areas where several months without rain occur are the most ideal for onion production (50 to 70% relative humidity), achieving good maturation and natural curing in the field; the condensation of humidity (fog) during cold hours also favors the development of foliar diseases.

## Soils

Onions can develop in a range of soils, preferably in loamy, loamy-sandy, sandy-loamy, and clay-loamy soils (no more than 30% clay). Clay soils cause problems for water management, it is better to avoid them; in addition, they cause bulb damage and make it difficult for them to develop. The ideal conditions are: good texture, fertile, and well-drained; with a pH between 6.0 and 7.0.

The soils that do not meet these conditions should be incorporated with green manures or residues from different crops; it should not be mixed with manure because it increases the incidence of root rot and pungency due to the high sulfur content, in addition, onions grown in very organic soils have little suitability for storage, the organic matter content should not be greater than 3%. It should be avoided planting this crop in saline soils (no more than 1.2 mmhos/cm).

## Planting Methods

Onions can be produced by direct seeding and transplanting; the largest commercial area is planted using the transplanting method.

## Direct seeding with asexual seed (bulbs):

This method consists of planting small onions, 12-15 mm (bulbs), which are obtained by selection during the normal onion harvest or are produced specifically for this purpose.

When it comes to producing bulbs, the planting is done between November and December. The irrigation should be applied until the moment of starting the formation of the bulbs or until the first stage of their formation, which occurs at 60-70 days:

The harvest is determined by the bending of the false stems, which indicates that the bulbs have reached maturity: They are pulled and the false stems are cut at 0.01 m above the neck and stored to finish their curing. After three or four months, they can be sown to obtain large bulbs.

## Transplanting

This is the most common practice and consists of making a nursery before the final planting. For this, beds or raised beds (1 x 10 m) are prepared; the seeds are sown in rows followed by. The use of raised beds allows to make a furrow with a distance between lines of 0.10-0.15 m and a depth of 0.01 m. The seed is sown in rows followed by, with care to place 3-5 seeds per cm.

This sowing is done by hand, so that 5 to 6 grams are used per m², to produce between 900-1000 plants of quality. One pound of seed is enough to sow 60-80 m² of nursery. If you want to produce a bulb, you need 3-4 pounds of seed, which indicates that you need to prepare 180-240 m² of nursery. Subsequently, the nursery should be protected with rice husk, straw or grass.

The nurseries should be covered with straw or coconut palm, among others, to ensure adequate humidity and to prevent the formation of crust, in addition to protecting them from rain, as they can destroy the nursery. When using this type of protection, it is important not to hinder the entry of light, as onions require a lot of light.

## Soil preparation:

<!-- image -->

In normal growing conditions, the plants will be ready for transplanting in about 40-60 days; plants of good quality are 18-20 cm tall, with three true leaves and a false stem with a diameter of 0.07 m. It is important to plant them at the appropriate depth, so that the white part is covered. It is important to organize and train the person who will do the transplanting before starting the task, especially emphasizing not taking too many plants at once to facilitate their handling.

It is advisable to select plants, discarding the weak and sick ones. The selected seedlings should be placed vertically in boxes, i.e. with their roots facing down, to be taken to the field. When placing the seedlings, it is recommended to place them in a shaded area to avoid dehydration.

Before transplanting, the soil should be at the appropriate moisture level so that the seedlings do not suffer from water stress; otherwise, if the soil has low moisture, a very light watering should be given in the final planting location, which should last for 2 or 3 days to put the soil in the right conditions.

## CULTURAL PRACTICES

The depth of soil preparation varies depending on the nature of the soil. In compact soils, the depth is greater than in loose soils. Normally, this work is carried out to a depth of 30-35 cm, due to the short length of the roots.

## Aporco:

Due to the narrow spacing of the plants, when carrying out the aporco practice, it is important to take care not to overturn the plants or damage the root system.

## Application method

All phosphorus and potassium should be applied at the time of transplanting, in a band along the edges of the rows.

Nitrogen is applied in part, the first at transplanting (25%) and during the growth of the plant, 3-5 applications should be made during the rainy season and 2-4 in the dry season.

When using fertigation, the entire potassium and 30% nitrogen can be applied in a band, the rest can be applied fractionally as needed, taking into account that the more fractionated, the better the plant can utilize it.

Fertilizer should not be applied after the seventh week, to avoid affecting the onion maturation.

## Mildew (Grey Mold)

Produced by: (Peronospora destructor Berk)

Symptoms: It is characterized by yellowish leaf lesions on which a greyish, velvety-looking mold appears, which develops when the environmental conditions are humid.

Survival: Survives as oospores in sick tissues that persist in the soil and on bulbs, as well as in infected bulbs associated with sick plants.

Dispersal: By wind, by infected bulbs or seeds.

Favorable factors: Relatively cool and humid environmental conditions, the optimal temperature for spore formation fluctuates around 10°C.

Integrated management: Establish a crop rotation of at least one year with tolerant or resistant species, use healthy seeds produced in areas free of mildew. Treatment of seeds by immersion in hot water at 50°C for 25 minutes.

## IRRIGATION

<!-- image -->

## Types of irrigation

Onions can be watered by gravity, drip, or sprinkler. However, sprinkler irrigation increases the risk of disease, as it does not regulate the amount of water. If the water is scarce, it slows down the growth of the crop, and if there is too much, it promotes the formation of double bulbs and cracks in them, which reduces the commercial value of the product.

If using drip irrigation, the frequency should be daily, with the practice of fertigation every two days.

In the case of dry onions or mature bulbs, the irrigation should be stopped when 25% of the plants are observed with bent or fallen stems due to their maturity.

## HARVEST AND POST-HARVEST

The harvest of onion bulbs begins when 50% of the stems have bent due to their maturity. In this case, expect 2 to 7 days before starting the harvest, which is done by hand when the soil is loose. If the beds are compacted, it is necessary to loosen the soil by passing a knife under the bulbs.

## Curing and storage

Curing consists of removing excess moisture from the neck, roots, and outer scales. This results in a loss of 3-5% of the bulb's weight. When done correctly, it helps reduce infections and reduce damage caused by excessive moisture loss of the bulbs.
