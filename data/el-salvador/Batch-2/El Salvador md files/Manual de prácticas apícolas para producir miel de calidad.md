## Introduction

The global food market increasingly demands foods that are safe – that do not cause harm when consumed -, 'that are free of…', that are traceable from their origin, that are produced under environmentally friendly conditions, and that do not pose a risk to human health.

Argentine beekeeping is no exception, and that is why, in the area of influence of the Estación Experimental Agropecuaria of the Salado Basin of INTA, work has been carried out for more than a decade on transferring the technological pathway for beekeeping. This primarily promotes the production of honey free of antibiotics. In this sense, a network of Rural Change Advisors was consolidated, who, supported by various INTA referents in the field, accompanied beekeepers in implementing this pathway. This organization of beekeeping in the region led to the need to develop a common document for all those involved, which, on the one hand, would validate the usual practices of producers in the area, and on the other, would define the production and honey extraction conditions to obtain a product of the desired quality.

To carry out this work, the INTA Protocol N· 11 for the production of honey from 1998 was used as a reference, and various meetings were held with the Rural Change Advisors, other INTA referents, and producers to develop the manual in a participatory and consensual manner.

The objective of this manual is to promote, among all the members of the beekeeping chain that make up the Salado Basin cluster, the implementation of the recommended practices to produce high-quality honey and, in this way, contribute to the added value and development of beekeeping at the regional level.

<!-- image -->

This manual applies to honey produced in drums of first use (new or recycled iron drums - according to SAGPyA Resolution 121/98) obtained in the Salado Basin region, under specific management practices carried out in the apiary, during harvesting, transportation, and in the extraction room, which allow for obtaining honey of the desired quality with traceability from the apiary.

## 1. Good Apiary Management Practices

<!-- image -->

The good apiary management practices outlined in this manual are consistent with the technical requirements and recommendations mentioned below, and their implementation and record-keeping will ensure the production of honey of the desired quality. The fundamental pillars of these good practices are based on the technological path that has been implemented since approximately 2002 in the Cambio Rural groups in the area. These pillars are primarily based on viewing the apiary as a management unit; knowing the flowering cycle of the area; basing disease management on inspection for decision-making; avoiding the use of antibiotics for brood diseases; and using acaricides approved by SENASA; implementing strategic energy feeding; and replacing queens at the appropriate time for the apiary's multiplication. Just as the aspects related to good management practices in harvesting, loading, and transporting and extracting honey.

## 1.1. Requirements for the Producer

This section details all the requirements that the producer must meet when implementing this Apiary Management Manual.

-  The producer must register as an apiary producer with national and provincial authorities, in accordance with current legislation, and must operate their apiary in rural areas, away from urban areas or areas with a potential risk of environmental contamination.
-  While transhumance is permitted, apiaries must be based in the Salado Depression area. When moving apiaries outside the province, the SENASA Resolution No. 535/2002 must be respected, which requires declaring the colonies being moved, which will be inspected by the corresponding health authority, and for which a permit for the move will be obtained.

-  At the same time, the producer must register and identify each of their apiaries (number or name) with a code, which will be tracked, in order to demonstrate the traceability of the final product - honey - and the actions carried out in each one.
-  The producer must record, for each apiary, all the practices carried out during each visit (FO - PC 02 Rev.00 - See instructions for use).

## 1.2. General Conditions of the Apiary and Materials to be Used

-  It is recommended to locate the apiary in areas that are not prone to flooding, and that are easily accessible, as well as to place the hives on elevated platforms.
-  If the hives are located in livestock farms, perimeter fences should be installed to prevent breakage and/or potential contamination of the materials, as well as to maintain the area free of weeds.
-  The inert material of the hive should be made of wood and in standard sizes. It is recommended to use ½ and ¾ supers for honey production.
-  The exterior of the inert material can only be protected with products that minimize the risk of contamination within the hive.
-  Only products that do not produce contamination within the hive should be used for combustion. The use of all products derived from hydrocarbons is prohibited, and the use of natural, non-contaminating elements is recommended for this purpose.

## 1.3. Feeding Management

Artificial feeding will aim to prepare the hives for the winter period; to avoid nutritional stress due to the lack of natural resources, and to adjust their development for better utilization of natural flowering periods, in accordance with the production objective. In no case should it pose a risk of contamination of the hive's products.

-  It is not permitted to feed the hive when there is a continuous natural influx of nectar.
-  The level of reserves and the development of the hives should be monitored during the spring and autumn inspections (See PO - IO/P 01 Rev. 00).
-  A frame feeder - type Dolitle - should be used, which should remain inside the hive throughout the season, in order to avoid deterioration and prevent exchange between hives.
-  The use of honey for supplementary feeding is prohibited.
-  The hive can be fed in autumn or spring with a sucrose syrup. The syrup should not be made with granulated sugar or refined sugar, nor with the addition of acids to extend its shelf life (IO - AA 03. Rev. 00).
-  A high-fructose corn syrup can be used in autumn.

-  In case of needing to feed hives during the winter, it is recommended to use candy as a supplement, as the use of syrup adds moisture to the hives during a period of low activity, which is detrimental to the hive. For candy preparation, it is recommended to use finely ground sugar and hot water, in a ratio of 2 to 1, avoiding overheating, which can lead to the formation of Hydroxy Methylfurfural (HMF).

-  All elements used in feeding (hoses, pumps, containers, dispensers, etc.) must be kept clean to avoid contamination of the final product. It is recommended to disinfect the material with sodium hypochlorite after washing.

## 1.4. Health Management

Disease control in the brood is based on the management of the hives, which allows for producing honey without the use of antibiotics. In this sense, it is important to determine the prevalence of different diseases in the apiary, and for this purpose, at least two health inspections should be carried out per year, in autumn before the winter and in spring before the multiplication of the apiary (See PO - IO/P 01 Rev. 00).

-  A recommended practice to reduce the prevalence of diseases is to replace

at least 30% of the frames in the brood chamber annually; do not exchange frames between hives and use ½ or ¾ supers for honey production.

-  In case of detecting hives with American Foulbrood, and depending on the severity of the infestation, the hives should be incinerated or packaged/brushed to recover the live material.
-  All inert material from hives affected by American Foulbrood must be sterilized before use with parafi  na (15´ to 150· C) or autoclave.
-  Hives that have died from any cause must be removed from the apiary at the time they are observed to prevent robbing and the spread of diseases.
-  To determine the level of Varroa infestation and to carry out appropriate control measures, a strategic monitoring system must be implemented that, at a minimum, includes the end of the honey harvest, pre-treatment, post-treatment, and the end of the winter. It is also recommended to carry out monitoring during the autumn treatment. (IO- MV 02 Rev. 00), (FO - TMA 03 Rev.00).
-  Only products approved by the health authority -SENASA- for use in beekeeping should be used to treat Varroa, and they must be used according to their instructions and the corresponding withholding period.
-  If any type of hive with European Foulbrood and American Foulbrood is detected at any time of the year, they must be identified and categorized as observed, and the necessary corrective measures must be taken (replacement of queens, adjustment of feeding, prevention of multiplication, etc.).
-  To detect and quantify the presence of Nosemosis, a sample must be taken (Ver IO - MN 01 Rev.00) and the appropriate management measures must be taken (replacement of queens, adjustment of feeding, prevention of multiplication, etc.).

## 1.5. Queen Replacement and Apiary Multiplication

Systematic queen replacement will help reduce hive mortality, improve genetics, reduce swarming, and improve productivity. In this regard, it is recommended to replace all the queens in the apiary every two years, which can coincide with the multiplication of the apiary. Queen replacement can be carried out in either spring or autumn. (IO - RR 04 Rev. 00).

-  In spring, it is necessary to multiply those hives that allow it, with the aim of reducing swarming and increasing the number of hives in the apiary.
-  Hives affected by American Foulbrood, European Foulbrood, Varroa, and European Foulbrood should not be multiplied.
-  Before multiplication, the apiary must be inspected for health.

## 1.6. Honey Harvesting

This section describes how honey harvesting should be carried out to obtain the desired quality.

-  Only hives with at least 70% of the honey cells capped should be harvested.
-  For harvesting, the smoker can be used (in moderation), the blower, tapping, or brushing. The use of formic acid or any other chemical product for this purpose is prohibited.
-  Hives containing brood should not be harvested.
-  The entire apiary should be harvested in order to guarantee the traceability of the final product from its origin. To do this, a Field Sheet must be kept with each transfer to the extraction room. This sheet should include the room number, the number of supers delivered, the lot number, and the total weight obtained after extraction.
-  In case of unforeseen weather conditions or any other inconvenience that prevents the complete harvesting of the apiary, or if the number of supers (both small and large apiaries) justifies moving more than one apiary in the same transfer, it must be noted on the Field Sheet and informed to the room supervisor.

## 1.7. Loading and Transport of Full Honey Supers

The loading and transport of full honey supers must be carried out in a way that does not damage the quality of the product. Therefore:

-  The vehicle used to transport the honey supers must be in good condition and any contaminating material should not be transported with the harvested material.
-  The honey supers should not be in direct contact with the floor of the vehicle and, for this purpose, it is recommended to use trays. The trays and supers should also be properly stacked and secured to prevent movement and falling.

-  The vehicles must travel covered, so that the honey supers are not contaminated with dust, dirt, water, or a mixture of these.
-  Careful and safe handling is required to avoid damaging the frames and/or honey supers.
-  If more than one apiary is being transported in the same vehicle, the supers should be separated and identified as separate lots so that they can be extracted as such in the extraction room.

## 1.8. Extraction Room

Honey supers and frames must be processed in a honey extraction facility that is authorized by the health authority -SENASA-, and the facility must be of sufficient size to carry out the specific activities under hygienic and sanitary conditions, allowing the application of good beekeeping management practices that this protocol outlines and Good Manufacturing Practices, which are mandatory under the Argentine Food Code for all food processing industries (Resolution SAGPyA 870/06).

- It is recommended that the extraction room has a system for scheduling shifts that allows for a temporal separation between producers who are working under the Protocol and those who are not, in order to ensure the cleanliness of the equipment between them.
- The room supervisor must comply with and complete the mandatory documentation (Honey Extraction Room Movement Log - Honey Extraction Log) required by the health authorities (SENASA Resolution No. 186/2003: Traceability system for honey).
- Similarly, the supervisor must complete the Honey Acceptance Form (FO - RAM 04), documenting the conditions under which the transfer took place and the characteristics of the honey to be harvested, as well as assigning the corresponding batch number. Once the extraction is complete, the supervisor will complete the Extraction Form (FO - E 05), documenting the total kilograms extracted and the number of containers obtained. The identification of these will be carried out - according to SENASA Resolution 121/98 and 186/03 - in the planographed area, with indelible paint marking the official number of the extraction room, and then, on the same line of writing and separated by a bar, the two last digits of the extraction year.

## 1.9. Traceability

The traceability of the final product will be ensured by the correct implementation of the forms proposed in the protocol, which will serve as a record and will allow the final product to be traced from packaging to its origin, and thus to reconstruct its history.
- To do this, all practices carried out in the apiary must be recorded in the Form called Field Sheet (FO - PC 02 Rev.00). This form will accompany the transfer of the material to be extracted to the corresponding extraction room, in which the type and quantity of material sent, as well as the number and name of the room where the extraction will be carried out, will be recorded. In the room and through the Honey Acceptance Form (FO - RAM 04 Rev.00), the received material and the conditions in which the transfer was made will be recorded. Inside the room and at the time of carrying out the extraction, the corresponding Extraction Form (FO - E 05 Rev.00) will be completed, which will record the assigned batch number, the total kilograms extracted and the quantity and identification of the honey containers obtained. At this time, the act of taking the sample must also be recorded.

## 2.1. Autumn and Spring Inspection Procedure

The scope of the procedure covers all inspection actions that will be carried out inside the beehive and which will allow obtaining information from visual observation; as well as the actions that arise from the monitoring of Varroa and Nosema, respectively.

##  How to proceed?

Upon arriving at the beehives, the general condition of the apiary will be observed in an attempt to identify any abnormalities that could affect the product. Subsequently, the sequence in which the beehives will be inspected will be determined, so that after inspecting one beehive, the next and immediate one will be inspected, and so on, without skipping any beehives. Subsequently, for the monitoring of Nosema and Varroa, a minimum of six or a maximum of 10% of the beehives in the apiary will be selected at random, taking into account that the same beehives will not be selected for both sampling. In the case of Nosema, it is recommended not to sample beehives located at the extreme vertices of the apiary; beehives that have suffered from lack of population or beehives that present any clinical signs of the disease. As well as, it is necessary to cover the entrances once the beehive has been selected. Both sampling must be carried out in the order of inspection assigned to these beehives. (See IO - MN 01 Rev. 00 and IO - MV 02 Rev. 00 Instructives)

- Before opening the beehives, some puffs of smoke will be applied through the entrance, and then the roof of the beehive will be removed and placed upside down next to the brood chamber, in order to support the frames extracted. By observing visually, the beehives will be categorized into Type I, II or III according to the number of bees that cover the frames. Understanding Category I as those beehives whose population covers more than 7 frames of bees; Category II, between 5 and 7 frames covered and Type III, beehives whose population covers less than 5 frames. It is necessary to record the time of day in which the inspection was carried out (time) and therefore the categorization, due to the implications that this has on the distribution of bees inside the beehive.

- To begin with the inspection inside the beehive, the second frame on the side that is less populated will be removed - then the third and so on until all the frames are checked, supporting one of the pallets on the roof of the beehive and the top of the brood chamber next to the upper edge of the corresponding brood chamber. In this way, all the frames on both sides - including the empty ones - will be checked visually, in order to detect the presence of any possible diseases of the brood; as well as, dead beehives, swarm cells and orphaned beehives. Once the entire beehive has been inspected, the frames will be returned, respecting the order in which they were removed from the same.

- Once the beehive has been closed, it must be written on the roof and in a visible way what was observed in the inspection carried out using indelible markers or crayons. Each beehive must be identified, detailing its category: I, II or III according to the case; presence of brood disease with LE in the case of detecting European Foulbrood, CY for Brood Disease and H if orphaned beehives are detected. In the case of beehives affected with American Foulbrood LA or swarm cells Z and/or dead M, these must be identified accordingly and will not be categorized, since once the inspection is finalized, they will be removed from the apiary as discarded beehives. It is important that in the case of beehives with LA, for their subsequent management, the LA abbreviation must be added to the roof, as well as the corresponding chamber.

- Once the beehives have been identified, the corresponding form will be completed and the inspection will be considered as finalized. The technician and/or producer responsible for the inspection must keep the original record and deliver a copy to the producer and the technical team of the EEA Cuenca del Salado for their subsequent analysis and return.

These instructions describe how to carry out the management practices outlined in the Manual. This Manual includes five instructions related to Nosema monitoring; Varroa monitoring; artificial feeding; queen replacement, and finally, colony multiplication.

## 3.1. Nosema Monitoring Instruction

Six frames from the apiary should be sampled randomly, and it is recommended not to sample frames located at the corners of the apiary, frames that have been abandoned, or frames that show any clinical signs of the disease.

First, the entrances to the frames should be covered to prevent bees from entering, and then the frames should be numbered from 1 to 6, as well as the containers to be used for taking the samples. These containers should contain pure alcohol at 96% to completely cover the sample. This will consist of 60 to 100 worker bees, which will be collected using a brush.

Once the sample has been taken and labeled, it should be sent to the laboratory.

## 3.2. Varroa Monitoring Instruction

10% of the frames in the apiary should be sampled randomly, ensuring a minimum of 6 frames are sampled. Before opening the selected frames, place the wide-mouth containers used for sample collection on the top of the frames, numbering them from 1 to 6.

When starting the monitoring, count the frames covered by bees and the number of frames with capped brood, recording this information on the frame and corresponding sample container.

Next, collect 200 to 300 nurse bees from both sides of three brood frames, with a predominance of open brood, into a wide-mouth container with detergent or 50% alcohol (depending on the sample processing time). To introduce the bees into the container, turn it upside down and slide it along one side of the frame from top to bottom, being careful not to disturb the queen (De Jong's methodology, modified by Marcangeli).

To process the sample, shake the container for 5 minutes, then invert it over a colander or metal mesh that allows the bees to pass through while retaining the mites (see Figure 1).

## 3.3. Artificial Feeding Instruction

This instruction describes how to prepare and obtain both sucrose syrup and high fructose corn syrup, as well as how to store them. In both cases, it is necessary to plan when to administer the supplement, the volume to use based on the number of colonies, transportation, and method of administration.

Sucrose syrup should be prepared in a clean container, based on water and sugar. It is recommended to use a 66% syrup, which is obtained by mixing 2 kg of sugar per 1 liter of water (yields approximately 2.26 liters of syrup, which corresponds to the dose to be administered per colony). To prepare the syrup, heat the water to above 60°C and below 80°C – as this temperature allows for good solubilization of sucrose – and then pour it into the container, adding the sugar gradually. Once both ingredients have been added, stir the mixture until the sucrose is fully dissolved.

<!-- image -->

Do not overheat the syrup once the sugar has been added, as this can increase the levels of Hydroxy Methyl Fural (HMF), which is harmful to bees.

It is not recommended to store the syrup for more than three days to prevent deterioration.

In the spring, to stimulate the colony and achieve early development, administer syrup at a frequency of between 7 and 10 days until a natural and continuous flow of nectar is observed. Stop administering syrup when this occurs.

In the autumn, to generate sufficient reserves (at least 5-6 frames) and to reduce the queen's activity, increase the frequency of syrup administration.

Feeding the package

<!-- image -->

When using high fructose corn syrup, take all necessary precautions when acquiring it. In this sense, use clean containers – such as empty honey buckets – to avoid contamination.

Once the high fructose corn syrup has been administered, thoroughly wash the containers to prevent fermentation and cross-contamination when the bucket is used for honey extraction.

If you purchase a commercial product that is already solid, you cannot overheat it.

The frequency of application is the same as that of sucrose syrup, during the autumn.

## 3.4. Queen Bee Replacement Instructions

This document aims to describe how to carry out queen bee replacement, by introducing new, mated queen bees in the spring or autumn, and existing brood combs, only in the spring.

To carry out queen bee replacement, the hives must be balanced with four brood combs and six bees. Any remaining combs should have more than 70% of the brood capped, and they should be placed in a technical basket for subsequent multiplication or replacement in unbalanced hives at the time of replacement. This is the only time when it is permitted to move hives with brood between hives.

In the spring, the hives should be "orphaned," 48 hours before introducing the new queen. At

<!-- image -->

this time, the population should be reduced to six full combs and four brood combs. Once the hives have been reduced, sugar syrup at 66% should be provided to the entire apiary, as this is crucial for the successful acceptance of the new queen.

When introducing the queen, the queen excluder should be placed in the center of the brood chamber with the opening facing downwards – to facilitate her release – and gently pressed between the frames, ensuring that no honey is spilled.

Queen bee replacement can also be carried out in the autumn, taking advantage of the last nectar flow. While it is not necessary to reduce the hives as in the spring, the same procedure should be followed.

The acceptance of the new queens in the hives can be verified seven days after placement, by observing whether there are eggs in the brood area. If no eggs are observed, and the queen has been successfully released, the queen should be searched for inside the hive, by carefully removing the frames. If the queen is found, the hive should be closed, and the inspection should be repeated in seven days. If it is confirmed that the hive is orphaned and has the necessary balance (brood/bees), a new queen can be introduced.

If queen bee replacement is carried out using existing brood combs, this should always be done in the spring. On the fourth day after introducing the comb, the presence of eggs should be verified. If no eggs are found, a new comb should be introduced. The acceptance of the new queens can be verified 15 days after introducing the brood combs. After verification, sugar syrup at 66% should be provided to all hives. In the case of an orphaned hive that is in a balanced state, a new comb or a nucleus with a mated queen can be introduced.

## 3.5. Apiary Multiplication Instructions

This document aims to describe the different multiplication methods that can be used, such as the bee package and the production of nuclei using technical baskets or brood chambers.

One of these methods is multiplication by bee package. To carry out this process, the hive should be opened to search for the queen and the combs should be extracted in an orderly manner and placed outside the hive, as described in the inspection procedure.

Once the queen is located, she can be enclosed in a queen excluder or placed in a comb with bees inside the hive. In both cases, the aim is to prevent the queen from accidentally entering the queen excluder when the combs are shaken. The number of combs to shake per hive will depend on the state of the hive and the formation of packages should guarantee 1,250 kg of nurse bees. These can come from one or more hives.

The combs with nurse bees should be shaken over an inverted funnel placed on top of the queen excluder, which should be weighed until the required amount of nurse bees is reached.

Once the package is complete, the hive should be closed, and the queen should be released if she was previously enclosed. After closing the hive, sugar syrup should be provided to all hives in the apiary, at a rate of 1.5 liters per hive.

All queen excluders containing the packages obtained should be sealed. Before installation, they should be placed in a shaded area with good ventilation and at a distance of at least 30 cm from each other for at least six hours. This period before installation is to allow the packages to stabilize and the temperature to drop. At the same time, it is recommended to introduce the new queen into the queen excluder. This should be obtained in a transport cage containing candy and its corresponding plastic lid, in order to prevent the bees inside the queen excluder from consuming the candy and releasing the queen at an inopportune time.

The most appropriate time for installation is when the sun goes down.

To carry out the installation of packages in new hives, the following materials are required: preferably a new or used brood chamber in good condition; four combs with stamped wax; a feeder of the type Dolitle;

a mosquito net to prevent bees from escaping; and a plastic cover (poncho) to cover the combs.

The first step in installing the packages will be to place the brood chambers in the shade, 1.5 or 2 meters apart. Then, four combs with stamped wax should be placed on one side of each chamber, respecting the same side for all chambers. Next, the feeder with syrup should be placed, followed by the package being introduced into the hive, and the transport cage with the queen should be removed.

The following steps should be followed after installing the package:

-  At the end of the first day, the mosquito net should be removed to allow the bees to exit.
-  On the second day, the queen excluder should be removed and the candy lid removed. Sugar syrup should also be provided at 66%, until the feeder is full.
-  On the seventh day, the release of the queen should be verified and the brood area should be observed for signs of eggs. If a package is considered orphaned, a new queen can be introduced. If the queen has not been released, a small opening can be made in the candy to facilitate her escape.
-  At this time, the feeder should be refilled with syrup.
-  On the 14th day, the feeder, stamped wax combs, and a new comb should be placed.

From this point on, sugar syrup should be provided every seven days, and new stamped wax combs should be introduced until the package is fully developed.

If the multiplication is carried out using nuclei, the use of technical baskets is recommended. This multiplication can be carried out in the spring, together with the replacement of queens. The same steps described above for multiplication by package should be followed.

The basket will be constructed from a standard base that is permanently closed at the bottom, with a mosquito net. The lid of the basket will also be made of mosquito net but will be movable. It is recommended to use wire mosquito net for its greater durability. Inside, separators should be placed to prevent the combs from hitting each other during transport.

When introducing the combs into the basket, combs with more than 70% of capped brood and the presence of bees should be selected, leaving four to five combs with brood inside the hive. The combs selected should be introduced into the basket, and it can be completed with combs from different hives, in order to ensure

The sufficient number of bees to cover the capped brood frames should be shaken – in the hive – one by one, another brood frame of bees. Once shaken, these should be placed in their respective hive.

Once the hive is complete, the process will be completed. Just as with the multiplication of bee packages, the hives should be moved to a shaded area and should remain empty for at least 8 hours, until the nuclei are prepared. If it is not possible to prepare the nuclei on the same day, it is advisable to spray the wire mesh roof with syrup.

The nucleus for honey production can be prepared in its respective chamber, starting from 3 or 4 brood frames. It is advisable that the inert materials are in good condition and have been previously sterilized, to minimize any risk of disease.

Each nucleus should consist of 2 wax frames interspersed with the brood frames, and then a Doolitle feeder should be placed. A queen or a cell can be introduced into the center of the brood frames. Once the nucleus is prepared, it should be fed with 66% syrup. The acceptance of the queen or cell should be verified according to the instructions in the queen replacement manual.

If the nuclei are prepared directly in brood chambers or nuclei, without the use of technical hives, the same conditions for extracting the brood frames and the practices described regarding the use of hives and nucleus formation should be respected.
