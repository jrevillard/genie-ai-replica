## SEEDING SYSTEM AND SEED QUANTITY

Generally, the amount of seed, the planting system, and crop management are similar to those for traditional corn; however, to achieve optimal yields, it is recommended to leave 2 plants per hill in plantings with 0.8 meters between rows and 0.4 meters between plants. A recommendation of 20-25 pounds of seed is suggested for a single apple tree.

In El Salvador, there are well-defined areas with dry periods during the rainy season: the northeastern part of the country, the northern part of the Santa Ana department, and the southern part of the Ahuachapán department. These areas are characterized by poor, sloping soils, where the effects of drought are more severe for grain production.

In light of the threat of climate change, especially drought, CENTA considers it appropriate to relaunch the free-pollinating variety, CENTA PASAQUINA, which was identified to help solve the problem of corn production in regions with scarce and erratic rainfall.

## ORIGIN OF THE VARIETY

CENTA PASAQUINA was released in 1988, originating from the International Maize and Wheat Improvement Center (CIMMYT-Mexico). Specialists in physiology and plant breeding from both centers participated in its development.

## ZONING AND YIELD

The research on the CENTA PASAQUINA variety involved agronomic studies conducted from 1984 to 1987. During this period, it was compared with various local varieties in the Eastern region; and included years with both favorable and unfavorable rainfall.

In 1987, CENTA PASAQUINA was validated in 24 plots in seven locations, in areas with a history of drought. A 37% increase in yield was observed compared to local varieties:

Since its release in 1988, CENTA PASAQUINA has been widely used by farmers in the Eastern region and is known as "Planta Baja" (Low Plant) due to its short stature. Due to inadequate methods for farmers to produce their own seeds, its outstanding agronomic characteristics may be lost.
