## ORIGIN AND DISTRIBUTION

It is one of the most cultivated solanaceous plants in the world, especially in the Mediterranean countries. It is a very important fruit in many Spanish-speaking countries, especially the spicy varieties.

The genus Capsicum has approximately 30 species, of which 10 are cultivated for human consumption and the rest are wild with purple and white flowers. Until now, the classification of this genus has undergone numerous modifications by researchers and botanists; most of them attribute the origin of the genus Capsicum to Central America and the central Andean area with the countries of Bolivia, Peru and Ecuador, from where it spread to Mexico, always in climates with favorable conditions for its establishment.

## II. BOTANICAL ASPECTS

## PLANT TYPE

Cultivated annually. It is composed of a main stem, herbaceous in consistency, which later becomes woody; it reaches a height of about 40 cm, bifurcating into 2 or 3 main branches that branch out in a dichotomous manner.

Technical Guide

In suitable conditions and with prior pruning, it provides a harvest in the second year; the new plant formed frequently presents with weak shoots and smaller, lower quality fruits.

## SEED

The seed is attached to the plant in the center of the fruit. It is white-creamy in color, flattened, smooth, reniform, with a diameter ranging from 2.5 to 3.5 mm. In warm, humid environments, once extracted from the fruit, it quickly loses its germination power, unless properly stored.

The number of seeds depends on pollination and also on the size of the fruit. One gram contains between 150 and 200 seeds.

## ROOT

The root system of the sweet pepper is, at the first 20 days of germination, composed of a main, pivot, thin root with abundant rootlets, surrounded by a large crown of secondary and adventitious roots.

The adult root can reach more than 1 meter in depth, depending on the soil texture, with a strong and vigorous main pivot root predominating. In sandy soils and with localized irrigation, the depth of the roots is less. In sandy soils and with irrigation, it can reach 50-60 cm, although 75% or more of the root volume is located at a shallower depth, between 25-30 cm, with a high horizontal density of roots that reach a width of 50-75 cm. 3.4.

## STEM

The stem supports all the plant's organs: leaves, flowers, shoots and fruits, and is responsible for conducting the sap from the root to the leaves. It contains the nodes where the leaves, fruits and branches are inserted.

Initially, the stem is tender, becoming woody later as it develops, but not enough to stand upright as a mature plant with many fruits, so it needs supports. 3.5.

## FLOWERS

The flowers of the sweet pepper are complete, having a peduncle, petals, sepals, stamens and pistil; pendulous, bending downwards during anthesis or flower opening, although depending on their position on the plant, the peduncle is often supported by a pedicel or held between two shoots and remains vertical or inclined. Sweet pepper flowers develop from floral buds or terminal tips and normally one flower appears at the intersection of the stem that produces thick fruits (Figure 1 and 2).

The fruit of the sweet pepper is a hollow, non-juicy berry in the form of a capsule, in a hanging, drooping, or fallen position when the peduncle is curved, which is an advantage in protecting them from the sun. The fruit has a smooth skin, usually green at the beginning and yellow or red when ripe. The length of the peduncle is between 4-5 cm and about 1-1.5 cm in thickness.

## III. PHENOLOGICAL STAGES AND DEVELOPMENT

## GERMINATION AND EMERGING

The pre-emergence period varies between 8 and 12 days and is faster when the temperature is higher. Almost any damage that occurs during this period has fatal consequences because this is the stage with the maximum mortality (Figure 7).

Figure 7. Emerging (4-6 days after planting)

<!-- image -->

## GROWTH OF THE PLANTLET

After the development of the cotyledon leaves, the growth of the true leaves begins, which are alternate and smaller than the leaves of an adult plant.

From this point on, a slow growth of the aerial part is detected, while the plant continues to develop the root system, lengthening and deepening the pivot root and starting to produce some secondary lateral roots.

The plant's tolerance to damage begins to increase, but it is still considered very susceptible.

## VEGETATIVE GROWTH

Starting with the production of the sixth to the eighth leaf, the rate of growth of the root system gradually decreases; in contrast, the rate of growth of the foliage and stems increases, the leaves reach their maximum size, the main stem bifurcates, and as the plant grows, both stems branch out.

Generally, the plant's phenology is summarized as: germination and emergence, growth of the plantlet, rapid vegetative growth, flowering, and fruiting (Figure 8 and 9).

## FLOWERING AND FRUITING

When the flowering stage begins, the sweet pepper produces abundant terminal flowers on most of the branches, although due to the type of branching of the plant, they seem to be produced in pairs in the axils of the upper leaves.

The flowering period lasts until the load of ripe fruits corresponds to the plant's ability to mature them.

Under optimal conditions, most of the first flowers produce fruit, then there is a period during which most of the flowers abort. As the fruits grow, the vegetative growth and the production of new flowers are inhibited (Figure 10, 11 and 12).

The largest number of fruits and the largest size fruits are produced during the first fruiting cycle, approximately between 90 and 100 days.

Subsequent cycles tend to produce progressively fewer fruits or smaller fruits, as a result of the plant's deterioration and exhaustion.

## IV. CLIMATE AND SOIL REQUIREMENTS

## CLIMATE

The crop adapts very well to altitudes from 0 to 2,300 msnm, depending on the variety.

## TEMPERATURE AND RELATIVE HUMIDITY

Sweet peppers develop well with temperatures of 15 to 30°C; at higher temperatures, fruit formation is minimal. The optimal soil temperature for germination is 18 - 30°C. The optimal relative humidity is 70 to 90%.

## PRECIPITATION

The crop requires rainfall of 900 to 1200 mm, well distributed during the growing season.

Heavy rains during flowering cause the flowers to fall due to water impact, and consequently, poor fruit development; and during the ripening period, they cause physical damage that induces rot.

Excessive watering can induce the development of fungal diseases in the plant tissues.

## LIGHT

Sweet peppers need good illumination. If the illumination is low, the growing cycle tends to lengthen; otherwise, it tends to shorten. This indicates that the sowing times and the density of plant distribution must be consistent with the light balance.

## PHOTOPERIOD

This plant is a day-neutral plant. Flowering occurs best and is more abundant on short days (December), provided that the temperature and other climatic factors are optimal. However, due to the great diversity of cultivars currently, the photoperiod requirements vary from 12 to 15 hours per day.

## WATER

The soil must provide a total water layer of 900 to 1,200 mm for the crop cycle, from transplanting to the last commercial cut.

Although sweet peppers can tolerate water stress, if this stress lasts for a long time, it can result in irreversible damage, such as leaf, flower, and finally, fruit drop.

## SOIL

Currently, the choice of soil for sweet pepper production is one of the most important decisions. If an error is made in this regard, the entire crop can be lost; however, sweet pepper cultivation is sown in a very wide range of soils, deep, rich, with an organic matter content of 3-4%.

## TEXTURE

Ideal soils are those with a light to medium texture: sandy loam, deep and fertile with adequate water retention and good drainage; soils that are too clayey should be avoided.

Short periods of waterlogging cause leaf drop due to lack of oxygen in the soil and favor the development of fungal diseases.

## pH

The optimal pH for sweet pepper cultivation is 5.5 to 7.0.

## VI. SEEDLINGS

Ideal soils for establishing seedlings are those that have a flat topography, good drainage, are free of stones and lumps, with low clay content; it is advisable that there is a source of water for the necessary irrigations, in addition, the seedlings must be protected from the wind.

## CONDITIONS FOR PRODUCING STOLONS

## USE OF CERTIFIED SEED

Seeds from locally produced materials, produced in an artisanal way, are less productive and can act as carriers of pathogens, therefore, the use of certified seeds is necessary and advantageous. The production of chili seed lines is an alternative, which producers have employed by collecting fruits that present good characteristics, obtaining the seeds and storing them in an artisanal way, but this activity has been carried out in a few areas of the country, since the viability of the seed is reduced if it is not stored in a cool place.

## SEED TREATMENT

This is the application of insecticides and fungicides to the seed prior to planting to protect it from the attack of soil pests, some suggested products are:

Imidacloprid 70 WG as a seed treatment, 0.40 l/ha

Carbendazim 50 EC at a dose of 0.28 l/ha

Trichoderma sp at a dose of 0.6 - 1.2 g per liter of water

## HIGH QUALITY SUBSTRATES

For the production of seedlings in trays, the use of substrate is necessary, which is the material that will serve as support for the seedling during its development in the nursery. Some substrates are imported from Canada, the United States, New Zealand and South Africa, but it can also be produced locally, among which the following formulations are mentioned:

- Formulation 1: 60% of organic matter; 30 to 40% of fine sawdust; 10% of soil (optional)
- Formulation 2: 50% of decomposed organic matter; 40% of sifted wood charcoal; 10% of fine sawdust from dark woods

## VII. CULTURAL OPERATIONS OF THE CROP

## PREPARATION OF THE SOIL FOR OPEN FIELD

On slopes, minimal tillage is used, which consists of combining planting in level curves and not removing weeds, leaving them as ground cover. Its advantage is that it does not require removing the soil and herbicide is only applied to the remaining weeds.

On flat soils, the soil is removed using animal or mechanical traction, herbicides are applied, a subsoiler (if necessary), one or two passes with a cultivator before planting (depending on the size and population of weeds).

## PREPARATION OF THE SOIL IN PROTECTED AREA

The purpose of soil preparation is to provide the plant with a suitable medium for root development, improving soil aeration and structure.

It is recommended to prepare the soil before installing the mesh structure, otherwise the structure hinders the passage of machinery. The following operations are recommended:

- Leveling. Level the ground before placing the mesh structure, with a rake, a metal frame or heavy board
- Tilling. It is recommended to make one pass with a cultivator that reaches a depth of 25 to 30 cm.
- Tilling. Make two passes with a cultivator with the purpose of breaking up clods, this is carried out between 20 to 30 days after the tilling, if necessary, make a third pass with a cultivator.
- Furrowing or edging. The distance between the furrows will be according to the distances in which the posts of the structure are located. The minimum recommended distance is 75 cm.
- Bed formation. The formation of the beds is carried out by cutting every third edge, subsequently

the bed is shaped with the bed-forming equipment and passing a board over the edge.

- To make the surface of the mesh structure more efficient, it is necessary to start tracing the beds from the lines of the posts, working in this sense allows for an adequate distribution of the surface.

Seedlings from the nursery should be placed in the planting hole with the stem slightly above the soil level and firmly press the surrounding soil to fix the seedling's root ball to the sides of the hole.

## VIII. PLANT MANAGEMENT

## STAKING

The staking process is carried out to provide the plant with a support or anchoring point as it grows. This is especially important in varieties or hybrids whose height exceeds 1.2 m, as the weight they produce can strain the plant itself.

## TIEING

This activity is carried out with the aim of supporting the plant's weight. Wire, plastic tape, jute tape or other material can be used.

## MULCHING

This consists of placing soil around the plant's stem, either mechanically or manually. The aim is to provide aeration and greater anchorage to the root system. This work is recommended to be carried out on land with little slope, as it involves removing a significant amount of soil. The recommended time to do this is after soil fertilization, as it helps to incorporate the fertilizer into the soil.

## PRUNING

Pruning is infrequent, it is carried out when late-season shoots appear on the lower leaves. The occasional pruning that is carried out is the removal of the fruiting shoots, which is carried out when the fruit has ripened and it is necessary to obtain new shoots.

## IRRIGATION

## IMPORTANCE

Water is one of the most important factors that affect the production of crops. Water is required by plants in small amounts for metabolic and nutrient transport processes, and in large amounts in the physiological process of transpiration.

Irrigation is the supplementary application of water with the main objective of providing the plants with the appropriate level of moisture.

There are many factors that influence irrigation management, including the system used, soil characteristics, the crop and its stage of development, and environmental conditions. Each of these factors must be taken into account when determining the frequency and amount of water to be applied. Each crop has particular water requirements and each soil has specific properties that affect the availability of water to the plants.

## WATER REQUIREMENTS

Total water requirements are 600 to 900 mm and up to 1,250 mm for long vegetative periods with multiple harvests. To achieve high yields, an adequate supply of water and relatively moist soils are needed throughout the vegetative period.

Before flowering and at the beginning of the first flowering shoots, the crop is more sensitive to water deficiency or excess. Water deficiency in the soil reduces the growth and development of the plant; conversely, excess reduces the absorption rate.

## TYPES OF IRRIGATION

## Furrow Irrigation

This type of irrigation is suitable for flat, well-drained land. It consists of conducting water over the surface of the land, if the availability of water allows; with the advantage of its low cost.

## Drip Irrigation

This industrial or artisanal system is gaining popularity among crop producers and consists of conducting water through plastic tubes with orifices distributed in the furrow, which moisten the root zone with a controllable dose of water. It can be perfectly adapted to almost any slope, provided that the water source is properly located and the amount of water used is small, but the initial investment is high.

## Sprinkler Irrigation

This consists of conducting water through tubes and sprinklers that distribute it in the form of rain. This system has the advantage of not requiring level soil and contributes to controlling pests such as aphids and whiteflies, reducing the problems caused by these insects. Care must be taken not to apply excess water, as chili does not tolerate waterlogging.

## IRRIGATION PROGRAMMING

Normally, 100% water absorption occurs in the top 0.5 to 1.0 m of soil. To achieve optimal yield levels, the depletion of soil water should not exceed 30 to 40% of the total available water.

The frequency of irrigation will depend on the climate of the area, the type of soil, the age of the plant and the irrigation system used. Point irrigation is essential until the first harvest, after which some applications can be avoided.

## X. PESTS

These are the organisms that affect the crop, either directly or indirectly, causing economic losses. There are invertebrate pests (insects, mites, nematodes, mollusks); pathogenic organisms (fungi, bacteria, viruses); as well as weeds and vertebrates (rodents, birds).

## WEEDS

The most frequent weeds in the sweet chili crop are: yellow flower (Bidens pilosa), Mimosa (Mimosa pudica), chickweed (Eleusine indica), Cyperus (Cyperus rotundus) and Bermuda grass (Cynodon dactylon).

The control of weeds is generally carried out with one to three hoeings during the crop cycle, this will depend on the specific conditions of the place.

There are three critical or competitive moments to control weeds, these are:

- In the vegetative development stage of the crop.
- Prior to flowering, this is more important, because the crop demands a greater amount of nutrients
- After the development of fruits, due to which it can cause fruit losses due to a higher incidence of diseases.

To ensure successful crop cultivation, weed control methods are practiced at different stages of their growth, either individually or in combination.

## INSECTS

To know the situation of the insects in the field, and to take appropriate measures to manage them, mainly with chemical decisions, it is necessary to perform a sampling.

When designing a sampling system, the following aspects should be taken into account: The habitat unit to be sampled.

This refers to the unit or space that the insect occupies for its feeding and development. For sampling the main pepper pests, the following habitat unit can be used: plant, terminal shoots, leaves, fruits, root system.

## THE SAMPLING METHOD.

This is the visual count of the number of individuals of the pest found in each habitat unit. The most commonly used methods are: random sampling, systematic sampling and stratified sampling.

Random sampling consists of taking several points within the area of the crop, and then counting. These points can be taken in various directions, either in the form of 'X', 'W' or other.

Systematic sampling consists of taking samples at a determined distance from the furrows (every 5 or 10 meters). Stratified sampling consists of subdividing the plot into strata and taking samples from each stratum.

## Number of samples to take.

For peppers, a total of 12 samples can be taken, either plants, leaves or terminal shoots, once a week, to measure the evolution of the pest, and to make control decisions based on the critical point of each pest.

- Planting densities:

In nematodes, there are two types of parasitism:

Ectoparasites, are species that normally do not penetrate the roots. They feed only from the outside of the cells near the root surface

Endoparasites, are species that enter the host and feed from the inside of the plant, for example: Meloidogyne incognita, Meloidogyne javanica.

## BACTERIAL CANKER

Causal agent: Xanthomonas vesicatoria

## Symptoms

Symptoms can occur in all parts of the plant (leaves, fruits and stems). Temperatures (24 to 30°C) together with irrigation by overhead or heavy rain, favor the development of the disease, which is why it is very prolific in tropical and mainly rainy environments.

## SOFT BACTERIAL ROT

Causal agent: Erwinia carotovora

## Symptoms

Soft rot usually starts frequently in the tissues of the peduncle and the fruit calyx. Externally, the lesion wrinkles, while internally, the rot progresses, transforming the tissues into a white, watery, colorless mass. While the epidermis remains intact, the rotten fruit hangs like a bag full of water, until it finally breaks, emptying the contents.

In the complex with Neosilba sp., the associated bacteria produce in the fruit a dark spot of two to eight cm in length, which quickly spreads on the surface or inside the fruit, causing a dry rot, contrasting with the 'water bag'. 48 to 72 hours after the infection, the surface of the fruit opens, and in the following 48 hours, the fruit falls.

Technical Guide

Conditions for the development of the disease: The initial symptoms in both rots appear in less than 24 hours after the penetration of the bacteria into the plant tissues, and the rapid destruction of immature fruits is very fast; it also affects mature fruits, but in them the rot develops slowly (Figure 35).

The disease worsens during rainy periods, as the bacteria are splashed by the water droplets from the soil to the fruit, which is more susceptible to the disease due to the high humidity. In addition, the bacteria spread by the action of insects and rain, by irrigation water, wind, and tools. They live as epiphytes on the surface of the fruit and the foliage of the plant. They survive by feeding on the dead matter present both in the harvest residues and in the soil.

## Control of soft bacterial rot

## · Cultural control

Maintain crops in good health, for which insect attacks and diseased fruits in the plantation should be controlled. In places where severe soft bacterial rot damage has occurred, it is advisable to immediately destroy the harvest residues, rotating with maize, beans, or other crops that do not have susceptibility to this disease.

Measures should be taken to avoid injuries during fruit handling. Post-harvest rot can be reduced by harvesting the fruit when it is free of moisture, minimizing injuries during handling and storing at low temperatures.

Washing can increase the number of infected fruits, but these losses can be reduced by washing the fruits with a solution of 25 cc of sodium hypochlorite (bleach) in a cubic meter of water; this eliminates the bacteria, although rinsing with this solution does not stop the infection initiated before washing.

- The fruit has completed its maturity ('completely green, red or yellow' - depending on the variety).

For the commercialization of sweet pepper, there are official standards established by the National Council of Science and Technology (CONACYT). These are:

## PACKAGING AND PACKAGING USED.

Commercially, harvested peppers must meet the characteristics that comply with the requirements and tastes of consumers.

Some packaging and packaging forms used in the country are:

- In bags of approximately 35 pounds.
- In tomato-type boxes of approximately 35 pounds.
- In plastic boxes (jabas) of approximately 60 large peppers or 31 pounds in weight.
- Some characteristics that the sweet pepper must have for packaging include:
- Preferably green with a maximum coloration of 25% (pinto).
- Selected in large and small sizes.
