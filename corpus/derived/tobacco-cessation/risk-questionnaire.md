# Tobacco cessation — risk & readiness questionnaire

Two-part self-assessment for tobacco users:

- **Part A** — nicotine dependence (adapted from the Fagerström Test
  for Nicotine Dependence, the de-facto standard tool, simplified for
  patient self-administration; mirrors the screening items in Annex 1
  of the WHO *Be He@lthy Be Mobile — mTobaccoCessation handbook*).
- **Part B** — readiness to quit (adapted from the Transtheoretical
  Model "stages of change" framework).

This is a triage tool, not a diagnosis. The combined score guides what
kind of support is likely to help most, not whether you "can" quit —
people at every dependence level have successfully quit.

---

## Part A — Nicotine dependence

**A1.** When do you smoke your first cigarette of the day?

- (3) Within 5 minutes of waking
- (2) 6 - 30 minutes after waking
- (1) 31 - 60 minutes after waking
- (0) After 60 minutes

**A2.** Do you find it difficult to refrain from smoking in places
where it is forbidden (a hospital, a cinema, an office, an aeroplane)?

- (1) Yes
- (0) No

**A3.** Which cigarette of the day would you most hate to give up?

- (1) The first one in the morning
- (0) Any other one

**A4.** How many cigarettes per day do you usually smoke? (For other
tobacco products, count comparable use — e.g. one cigar ≈ 2-3
cigarettes; one pipe session ≈ 1 cigarette; one shisha session ≈
many cigarettes — see special-cases below.)

- (0) 10 or fewer
- (1) 11 - 20
- (2) 21 - 30
- (3) 31 or more

**A5.** Do you smoke more frequently during the first hours after
waking than during the rest of the day?

- (1) Yes
- (0) No

**A6.** Do you smoke even when you are so ill that you have spent most
of the day in bed?

- (1) Yes
- (0) No

### Dependence score (Part A total)

| Total | Dependence level | What this typically means for quitting |
|---|---|---|
| 0 - 2 | **Low** | Many people at this level can succeed with behavioural support and a clear plan. Pharmacotherapy is optional. |
| 3 - 4 | **Moderate** | Behavioural support **plus** pharmacotherapy (NRT, bupropion or varenicline depending on what's available and your health worker's advice) noticeably increases success. |
| 5 - 7 | **High** | Withdrawal will be intense in the first 1-2 weeks. Pharmacotherapy is strongly recommended. Plan for daily support during the first month. |
| 8 - 10 | **Very high** | This is the addiction working hard. Quitting is absolutely possible, but it usually needs combination pharmacotherapy plus structured behavioural support (counselling, programme, regular check-ins). Talk to a health worker before Quit Day. |

## Part B — Readiness to quit

**B1.** Which of these is closest to how you feel right now?

- (0) I have no intention of quitting in the next 6 months. *(Pre-contemplation)*
- (1) I'm thinking about quitting in the next 6 months but not in the next month. *(Contemplation)*
- (2) I'd like to quit in the next month and I've already started thinking about how. *(Preparation)*
- (3) I quit within the last 6 months. *(Action)*
- (4) I've been smoke-free for more than 6 months. *(Maintenance)*

**B2.** How confident are you that you could stay smoke-free for an
entire day, if you tried right now?

- (0) Not at all confident
- (1) A little
- (2) Moderately
- (3) Very

**B3.** Do you have at least one person in your life who would actively
support you through a quit attempt (a partner, a family member, a
friend, a colleague, a community group)?

- (2) Yes, definitely
- (1) Maybe — I haven't asked
- (0) No

**B4.** Have you tried to quit before?

- (1) Yes, at least once
- (0) No

(If yes:) **B4a.** What was the longest you stayed smoke-free?

- (2) More than 6 months
- (1) Between 24 hours and 6 months
- (0) Less than 24 hours, or only "cut down"

### Readiness score (Part B total)

| Total | Stage | Recommended next step |
|---|---|---|
| 0 - 2 | Pre-contemplation / very low readiness | Don't pressure yourself. The fact you're reading this is already a step. Learn one new thing about how your body is affected, and revisit this in a few months. |
| 3 - 5 | Contemplation | Pick one specific reason to quit (children, breath, money, a specific health concern). Write it down. Notice when you smoke and what triggers it for a week. No need to quit yet — just observe. |
| 6 - 8 | Preparation | You're ready. Pick a Quit Day in the next 1-2 weeks. Decide on whether you'll use pharmacotherapy (Part A score above moderate? Strongly recommended). Tell at least one supporter. |
| 9 - 12 | Action / Maintenance | Keep going. Recognise the milestones (day 1, week 1, month 1, month 3, month 6, year 1). If a slip happens, treat it as data, not failure. |

## Combining the two scores

| Part A (dependence) | Part B (readiness) | Suggested path |
|---|---|---|
| Low / Moderate | Pre-contemplation / Contemplation | Motivational support only. No quit plan yet. |
| Low / Moderate | Preparation | Behavioural support, Quit Day planning, optional NRT. |
| Low / Moderate | Action | Daily check-ins for first 2 weeks, weekly to month 3. |
| High / Very High | Pre-contemplation / Contemplation | Motivational support; mention that medication exists and works. |
| High / Very High | Preparation | **Pharmacotherapy + behavioural support together.** Strongly recommend a clinic visit before Quit Day. |
| High / Very High | Action | Daily check-ins. Watch for severe withdrawal — recommend NRT if not yet using. |

## Special-case adjustments

- **Hookah / shisha only:** Treat the dependence score with caution.
  Many shisha users score "low" on the cigarette-oriented questions but
  inhale large doses during sessions. If they have hookah ≥ once/week,
  treat the addiction as moderate at minimum.
- **Smokeless tobacco (chewing, snus, dip):** Adapt questions A1, A3,
  A5 to "use" rather than "smoke." Scoring is similar. Add the question:
  do you ever wake up at night to chew/dip? (yes = high dependence).
- **Pregnant or breastfeeding:** Regardless of dependence score, the
  recommendation is to quit immediately, with a clinician-supervised
  plan. Risk to the baby of continued smoking outweighs the
  inconvenience of dependence-level withdrawal in nearly all cases.
- **Recent cardiovascular event (heart attack, stroke) within last 2
  weeks:** Quitting is urgent. Pharmacotherapy choice must be decided
  with the treating clinician.
- **History of depression or bipolar disorder:** Flag for clinician
  involvement. Mood drops in the first weeks post-quit are more common
  in this group and may need adjustment to existing treatment.

---

## What this questionnaire does **not** do

- It does not predict your individual chance of success. People at every
  dependence level have quit; people at every readiness level have, at
  some point, become ready.
- It does not replace conversation with a health worker. If your
  combined picture is "high dependence + high readiness," the most
  useful single next step is a clinic visit to discuss pharmacotherapy
  before your Quit Day.
- It does not assess withdrawal severity directly — that becomes
  visible only after you stop. If withdrawal becomes overwhelming, that
  is also a reason to seek clinical support, not to give up.
