## Guazapa 1

This is a variety of red bean with a bright red grain, selected by farmers from the 'San Miguel de Guazapa' Agricultural Producers Association for its potential yield, resistance to major bean pests and diseases in El Salvador, and erect growth habit.

The Guazapa 1 variety was introduced to El Salvador in 2007 with the code IBC 309-23 and developed at the EAP Zamorano from the cross of: Carrizalito//Carrizalito/Cincuenteño.

The main characteristics of the variety are its intermediate erect growth habit, a vegetative cycle of 75-77 days, physiological maturity occurring at 69-70 days, white flowers, green pods during grain filling, and a uniform creamy color at physiological maturity, with a potential yield of 30 qq/hectare.

## The JF Dam

This variety was introduced to El Salvador in 2007 with the genetic code FP 5-38; it originated from the cross of Marciano/SRS 6-6, developed by the EAP Zamorano. The selection of this variety was carried out by farmers from the La Presa Community Development Association in the municipality of El Congo, in the department of Santa Ana. Its main characteristics include its red bean color, physiological maturity of 68-70 days, an intermediate vegetative cycle of 72-75 days, tolerance to major pests and diseases, and a potential yield of 30 qq/hectare.

## INTRODUCTION

The Collaborative Program for Participatory Plant Breeding in Mesoamerica (FP-MA) began its work in the region in the year 2000, with processes of improving the traditional materials of corn and beans, with its main objectives to increase the productivity of the selected crops in order to ensure a better supply for consumption and acceptance in the market, and to promote the use and conservation of local biodiversity through participatory plant breeding. To achieve these objectives, work is carried out with groups of farmers who practice subsistence agriculture and have limited access to agricultural inputs and credit systems.

The Program is an inter-institutional and interdisciplinary effort that brings together the collaborative work of producers, producer organizations, non-governmental organizations, government institutions and research centers to achieve a central objective: to improve the living conditions of producers through the genetic improvement of their crops, using participatory plant breeding.

The Program involves countries such as Guatemala, Honduras, Nicaragua, Costa Rica, Cuba and El Salvador, all supported by the Norwegian Development Fund (FDN), and strategic partners such as the El Zamorano Agricultural School of Honduras (EAP Zamorano) and CENTA.

## DEVELOPED VARIETIES

In El Salvador, the process of participatory plant breeding began in 2006 with beans in three locations: Chalchuapa, Guazapa and El Congo, with the direct participation of producers in the selection and improvement of bean varieties. As a result of this joint effort, each of the organizations has developed its respective bean varieties (San Nicolás FP-01, Guazapa 1 and La Presa JF), which can be cultivated in traditional areas.

## San Nicolás FP-01

This variety originated from the cross between the MILENIO 47 and CENTA San Andrés varieties. This hybrid was developed at the EAP Zamorano and introduced to El Salvador in 2007 through the Líneas Avanzadas de Frijol tipo rojo de seda (VIROS 2007) nursery. It was known experimentally as the MER 2226-35 line.

For five years, its potential for yield, resistance to diseases, and agronomic characteristics were evaluated in field trials at experimental stations and on the farms of agricultural producers. These activities were carried out directly by the producers of the 'Siete Cerros' Community Development Association in the Las Lomas canton, Chalchuapa municipality, Santa Ana department, in collaboration with technical staff from CENTA.

The San Nicolás FP-01 variety has a bright, light red grain, white flowers, green pods during grain filling, and a uniform cream color at the physiological maturity stage. It has an intermediate II-B growth habit. Its vegetative cycle ranges from 73-75 days. Flowering begins at 35-38 days, and physiological maturity occurs at 68-70 days. The potential yield is 35 qq/hectare.
