## TECHNICAL GUIDE FOR CARROT CULTIVATION

I- COMMON NAME:

Carrot

SCIENTIFIC NAME:

Daucus carota L.

## II- INTRODUCTION

This vegetable, like others in our country, has gained great importance, among the reasons being its high nutritional value, fresh consumption, use as a condiment in various dishes and salads, and its use as raw material in the food industry for the production of juices, preserves, etc.

Carrots are rich in Vitamin A, carotenoids, niacin, thiamine, pantothenic acid, and minerals.

## Origin

Carrots originated in the region of Afghanistan.

## Planting Zones

In El Salvador, carrots are planted above 1,200 m.s.n.m.; however, new cultivars are available on the market that adapt to different agroclimatic conditions, especially in low areas.

## III- CLASSIFICATION AND BOTANICAL ASPECTS

## Classification

Carrots belong to the Umbelliferae family.

## The Root

The carrot's root system consists of the main root, which tubers in the upper part (edible part), and smaller lateral roots.

## The Stem

The stem is short and flattened with a crown of tripinnate leaves.

## The Leaves

The leaves are small and simple.

## The Flower

Carrot flowers are small and pink, forming an umbel, with hermaphrodite and male flowers. Pollination is self-pollinating and entomophilous.

## The Seed

Carrot seeds are elliptical, with a convex and flat side, and retain their germination capacity for 3-4 years. Approximately 8,500 seeds are in one ounce.

## IV- CLIMATIC AND SOIL REQUIREMENTS

## Radiation

Carrots are not sensitive to day length, but require good light, which will vary depending on planting density, pruning, and trellising.

## Temperature

The optimal temperature range for carrot cultivation is 15°C to 25°C.

## Air Humidity

The relative humidity (RH) in carrot cultivation should be between 70 and 80%.

## V- GENERAL CHARACTERISTICS OF CARROT VARIETIES

These are classified specifically by root shape, including:

- 1) Heart of Ox
- 2) Chantenay

## VI- SOIL PREPARATION

## Subsoiling

Should be carried out during the dry season, one or two passes can be made in a cross direction, and it is recommended to do it every 3-5 years.

## Harrowing

Consists of removing the topsoil to depths of up to 45 cm. This practice should be repeated each time a crop is planted.

## Tilling

This practice is recommended before and after harrowing; before to incorporate crop residues or green manure, and after harrowing to break up large soil clumps.

## Surfing

Used to lift the soil and form ridges for planting.

Construction of drainage channels

This practice is used to remove excess water during the rainy season or when irrigation is used.

## VII- CULTIVATION MANAGEMENT

## Bed Preparation

In flat areas, during the rainy season, it is necessary to create raised beds and planting beds that are 1.2 meters wide by 20-30 centimeters high, avoiding soils with too much rockiness, as these tend to deform the roots, significantly reducing their quality in the market.

## Thinning

This is carried out 6-10 days after planting when seedlings have emerged. The most recommended practice is to avoid this practice by using the recommended planting spacings, as this is uneconomical due to the high labor cost of this practice.

## NUTRITIONAL REQUIREMENTS OF THE CROP

Fertilizing the crop should be based on the results of soil analysis.

The nutritional requirements of the carrot crop are 250 kg/ha for N, 150 kg/ha for P, and 180 kg/ha for K.

## ORGANIC FERTILIZATION

This is the addition of nutrients to the soil from decomposed organic matter, such as chicken manure, cow manure, compost, green manure, etc.

## WEED CONTROL IN CARROT CULTIVATION

The weeds that can cause losses in carrot cultivation are:

Grasses, sedges such as coyolillo (Cyperus rotundus), purslane (Portulaca sp), yellow flower (Baltimora recta), and others.

## CONTROL METHODS

The control methods that we can use include: Cultural control, mechanical control, chemical control.

In chemical control, we can mention some herbicides used: Dachthal, Afalón, Treflán.

## Irrigation in Carrot Cultivation

Objectives of Irrigation in Carrot

First, to meet the crop's water needs throughout its entire phenological stage, providing the necessary amount and quality of water at the right time.

Key factors for irrigation scheduling

The critical periods for carrot cultivation that require a good water supply are:

- Before and after planting
- During growth
- During fruit development
- During harvest

## Irrigation Methods

In our country, the most common method is gravity irrigation, using a system of straight or contour furrows, but in recent years, drip irrigation has been increasing.

For the scheduling of carrot irrigation in our country, the frequency of irrigation, when using the gravity method, varies between 4 to 7 days depending on the agroclimatic conditions of the area, and when using drip irrigation, the frequency of irrigation can be daily or every 3 days, depending on the water needs of the crop and external factors (operating costs, crop management, water availability, etc.).

## IX- HARVESTING AND POST-HARVEST MANAGEMENT OF CARROTS

The harvesting of carrots is largely determined by market demands, which determines the size, quality, presentation (with or without leaves, packaged, in pieces, etc.), and in large part the variety that meets all these requirements.

The harvesting process begins with the removal of plants, selecting healthy plants without damage, good color, and without deformities, washing, removing leaves (depending on the market), bundling, packaging, or in boxes.

The removal is done manually in small areas, the soil should not be too wet (field capacity of 50%) to avoid sticking too much soil to the root.

For the thermal preservation of carrots, temperatures between 0 and 2 degrees Celsius and a relative humidity of 90-95% are recommended.

The preservation of carrots depends largely on the variety and the handling of the root at the time of harvest and transport.

In our country, the only carrot that is packaged in bags is the minicarrot, which is harvested in the Las Pilas area (Chalatenango).
