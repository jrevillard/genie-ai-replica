## Application in Large Rooms

For larger quantities stored in well-sealed rooms, such as refrigerated rooms, ethylene can be generated from ethephon by adding a base of sodium hydroxide (NaOH) to the liquid. The ethephon solutions are first distributed throughout the room, then the base is added, and the room is sealed. Forced air circulation is necessary within the room to ensure proper gas distribution.

The amount of ethephon required is calculated to achieve 100 ppm of ethylene, based on the product concentration and the room volume. One liter of 480 g/l ethephon generates 74.4 liters of ethylene. Therefore, 0.13 liters of ethephon are required for every 100 m³ of room volume. If a different concentration is used, the conversion must be made.

## Benefits of Artificial Ripening

A. To accelerate ripening:

To sell a batch of fruit that is ripening too slowly on its own, the demand for ripe fruit is met, and the need for storage space is reduced because the fruit flow is higher.

B. To uniform ripening:

When dealing with fruit that is not fully ripe in different stages, artificial ripening ensures that all the fruit ripens at the same time.

C. For staged sales:

To prevent a large batch of fruit from ripening all at once, a portion is ripened every two or three days, allowing for fruit to be available for sale sooner and for a longer period.

## Limitations of Artificial Ripening

To achieve the desired effect, the fruit must be cut at the appropriate stage, with sufficient development to allow for ripening (physiological maturity), but without having already started to ripen on its own. The cutting point is crucial, and this can vary between different varieties of the same fruit.

Fruit that is not fully developed on the tree will not achieve the expected flavor and characteristics, if it ripens at all. It may ripen, but in an abnormal way.

If one liter of ethephon costs $30 and is sufficient for 1000 kg of fruit or more, the cost per quintal is 3 cents or less. This cost must also include the cost of labor and the use of the room or materials.

## Artificial Fruit Ripening

## Introduction:

Fruit is typically cut at the 'maturity' or 'immature' stage, as it is more resistant to the handling and transportation involved in harvesting. Immature fruit is better able to withstand impacts and compression when stacked. It also requires less ventilation.

Once the fruit arrives at its destination, most of it is consumed in a ripe state, as it is sweeter, softer, and more fragrant, and has the desired color for the consumer.

Ripening is a natural process in fruit, which can be spontaneous or artificially induced. The latter accelerates ripening and allows for control over the timing of when the fruit is ready for sale and consumption.

## The Ripening Process

Typical effects on the fruit:

Ripening is an active process: the fruit consumes oxygen and energy to carry out the changes, releasing CO2 and water.

| Starches            | are converted to sugars          |
|----------------------|-------------------------------------|
| Acids               | are broken down                      |
| Pectins             | are solubilized; the fruit softens |
| Phenols              | are transformed; less bitter flavor  |
| Green color          | disappears, leaving other colors and |
| odor generation     | odor generation                |

The hormone C2H4 is the 'signal' that induces ripening in climacteric fruits. Once ripening has begun, these fruits produce more ethylene, which in turn can ripen other fruits. For artificial ripening, chemical ethylene is used.

## Fruits that produce and respond to ethylene

(climacteric fruits) fruits that continue to ripen after harvest; ethylene increases the rate of respiration and, consequently, ripening.

- Bananas, plantains, guavas
- Mango
- Melon
- Papaya
- Avocado
- Sapote
- Custard apple
- Tomato

Fruits that do not produce or respond to ethylene (non-climacteric fruits), which only ripen on the plant.

- Citrus fruits
- Marañon
- Carambola
- Rambutan
- Watermelon

## Conditions for artificial ripening

Place the fruit in a location with the possibility of containing ethylene gas; this could be on plastic wrap, in a bag, or in a sealed container. If it is large, there should be the possibility of air circulation.

The temperature should be between 20 and 25°C to facilitate the process.

After 24 to 48 hours, ventilate with fresh air, open or uncover, and provide oxygen. The humidity of the air should be kept high to prevent the fruit from shriveling.

Complete ripening is achieved in 2 to 5 days, depending on the fruit and the environment.

## Gases for artificial ripening

Ethylene is a highly flammable gas that is sold in cylinders, but requires appropriate equipment for safe application. In small quantities, it is more practical to use the liquid ethefon, which generates ethylene gas. It is sold at specialized agricultural services with various brands, as it has various uses in agriculture.

Carbide, when in contact with water, generates acetylene, another gas with a similar effect to ethylene. Its use is very common among banana wholesalers, but it is prohibited in many countries because it also generates phosphine (a toxic gas) and the dust it leaves may contain arsenic, which is also very toxic.

## Application of ethefon

If the product is formulated as 72 SL, dilute 1.5 to 2 milliliters in 1 liter of water if it is for 2 to 3 quintals of fruit, otherwise, a fraction or a multiple of this amount, depending on the amount of fruit to be ripened. The dilution should be used on the same day.

According to the formulation, the concentration may vary, and the dose should be adjusted.

Apply by immersion, using a backpack pump or spraying, to thoroughly wet the fruit. Then close the container or plastic, seal it to prevent the gas from escaping.

Keep it sealed for 24 hours, then open to allow ventilation. Then maintain at room temperature until the desired degree of ripeness is reached.
