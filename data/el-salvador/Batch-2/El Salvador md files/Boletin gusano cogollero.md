## The Corn Earworm in Corn Cultivation

The corn earworm (Spodoptera frugiperda) is a significant economic pest that causes severe damage to corn crops. It attacks young plants by cutting the stalks, and during the vegetative growth stage, it causes defoliation. It can also attack the male flowers, disrupting the normal pollination process.

It also attacks by piercing the young ear. The adult lays eggs on the stigmas of the ear. When the larvae hatch, they begin to feed and concentrate in the stigmatic channels, causing direct damage to the kernels and creating entry points for other diseases that can rot the ear.

## Control for the Corn Earworm

- Good soil fertility.
- Planting at higher densities to compensate for losses.
- Crop rotation with legumes.
- Keep free of grassy weeds.
- Teflubenzuron: 200 milliliters per ear.
- Lufenuron: 140 milliliters per ear.
- Bacillus thurigiensis: 300 milliliters per ear.
