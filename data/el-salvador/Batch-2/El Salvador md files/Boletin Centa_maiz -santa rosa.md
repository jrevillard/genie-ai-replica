As CENTA SANTA ROSA is a variety of free-pollination maize, the farmer can save seeds from one harvest for another, for no more than three years; following a systematic selection process. In the fourth year, the seeds should be renewed to maintain the physical and genetic characteristics of the material.

To carry out systematic selection, please consult with the technicians at CENTA.

## ZONIFICATION AND YIELD

The disease commonly known as weevil causes significant losses in maize cultivation in the coastal area of El Salvador, especially in late-season plantings (August-September). This disease is caused by the fine stripe virus, the Río Grande spiroplasma, and the arbustive dwarf phytoplasma, all transmitted by the maize weevil, Dalbulus maidis.

In the 1980s, some research was initiated in the country, in coordination between CENTA and INTA of Nicaragua, with technical and financial support from the International Maize and Wheat Improvement Center (CIMMYT-Mexico), resulting in the CENTA SANTA ROSA maize variety.

## ORIGIN OF THE VARIETY

The original population, the basis for improvement, was Population 73 from CIMMYT. After a systematic program, experimental varieties were developed, including Santa Rosa 8073, which, after several evaluations, outperformed the other Creole varieties.

The variety was not officially released in the country but was disseminated and accepted by farmers in the Western and Eastern regions.

In 2007, the maize subprogram at CENTA collected free-pollinating maize varieties from farmers' fields, which were evaluated, and from which the CENTA SANTA ROSA variety was selected and improved.

## SEEDING SYSTEM AND SEED QUANTITY

Generally, the amount of seed, the seeding system, and the cultivation management are similar to those for traditional corn, however, to obtain optimal yields, it is recommended to leave 2 plants per hill when planting at 0.8 meters between rows and 0.4 meters between hills. 20-25 pounds of seed per acre is recommended.
