WHO PEN Protocol 2 — Cardiovascular Risk Management and Hypertension Treatment

PROTOCOL 2: HYPERTENSION MANAGEMENT

1. SCREENING AND DIAGNOSIS
- Measure blood pressure in ALL adults aged 18+ at every health facility visit
- Proper measurement technique:
  * Patient seated for 5 minutes, back supported, feet flat on floor
  * No caffeine, exercise, or smoking 30 minutes before measurement
  * Use appropriate cuff size (too small = falsely high reading)
  * Take 2 readings, 1-2 minutes apart — use the average
- Hypertension diagnosis: requires ELEVATED readings on 2 SEPARATE visits
  * Normal: SBP < 120 AND DBP < 80 mmHg
  * Elevated: SBP 120-129 AND DBP < 80
  * Stage 1 Hypertension: SBP 130-139 OR DBP 80-89
  * Stage 2 Hypertension: SBP >= 140 OR DBP >= 90
  * Hypertensive Crisis: SBP > 180 OR DBP > 120 — EMERGENCY

2. CARDIOVASCULAR RISK ASSESSMENT
- Use WHO/ISH CVD Risk Prediction Charts (specific for Sub-Saharan Africa)
- Risk factors assessed: age, sex, smoking status, blood pressure, diabetes status, total cholesterol
- Risk categories:
  * LOW (< 10% 10-year risk): lifestyle modification only
  * MODERATE (10-20%): lifestyle + consider single drug
  * HIGH (20-30%): lifestyle + single drug therapy
  * VERY HIGH (> 30%): lifestyle + combination drug therapy
- All patients with existing CVD (previous heart attack, stroke) = VERY HIGH risk

3. TREATMENT TARGETS
- General population: BP < 140/90 mmHg
- Patients with diabetes: BP < 130/80 mmHg
- Patients with kidney disease: BP < 130/80 mmHg
- Elderly (> 80 years): BP < 150/90 mmHg (less aggressive to avoid falls)

4. LIFESTYLE MANAGEMENT (FIRST-LINE FOR ALL)
- Salt reduction: < 5g per day (approximately 1 teaspoon)
  * Practical: use half a Maggi cube instead of full, cook with herbs/spices instead of salt
  * Avoid processed foods, salted fish, heavily salted sauces
  * Reduce netetu (fermented locust beans) quantity or rinse before cooking
- DASH-style diet adapted to Gambian foods:
  * Increase: moringa leaves, okra, bitter leaf, baobab fruit (bouye), hibiscus (bissap)
  * Increase: fish (fresh, not dried/salted), beans (nyebbeh), millet (chere)
  * Reduce: palm oil, groundnut oil quantities (use less oil in domoda/benachin)
  * Reduce: white rice portions, increase vegetable portions
  * Fruits: mango, papaya, orange, banana, watermelon — 2-3 servings daily
- Physical activity: 150 minutes per week moderate intensity
  * Walking briskly for 30 minutes, 5 days per week
  * Household work, farming, walking to market all count
  * Avoid heavy lifting if BP is very high (> 160/100)
- Weight management: even 5 kg weight loss can reduce BP by 5-10 mmHg
- Tobacco cessation: smoking cessation reduces CVD risk by 50% within 1 year
- Alcohol reduction: limit to 1-2 drinks per day maximum, none if possible
- Stress management: deep breathing, prayer/meditation, community support (Bantaba)

5. PHARMACOLOGICAL TREATMENT
FIRST-LINE MEDICATIONS (available on WHO Essential Medicines List):
a) ACE Inhibitors (ACEI): Enalapril 5-20mg daily, Lisinopril 10-40mg daily
   - Preferred for: diabetics, kidney disease, heart failure
   - Side effects: dry cough (10-20%), dizziness
   - Contraindicated in pregnancy

b) Calcium Channel Blockers (CCB): Amlodipine 5-10mg daily, Nifedipine 30-60mg daily
   - Preferred for: elderly, Black/African patients (often more effective)
   - Side effects: ankle swelling, headache, flushing
   - Generally well-tolerated

c) Thiazide Diuretics: Hydrochlorothiazide 12.5-25mg daily, Bendroflumethiazide
   - Preferred for: elderly, uncomplicated hypertension
   - Side effects: frequent urination, potassium loss, glucose elevation
   - Monitor potassium, watch for gout

d) ARBs (Angiotensin Receptor Blockers): Losartan 50-100mg daily
   - Alternative to ACEI if cough develops
   - Similar benefits to ACEI without cough
   - Contraindicated in pregnancy

TREATMENT ALGORITHM:
Step 1: Single drug — ACEI or CCB (CCB preferred in Black/African populations)
Step 2: If not at target after 4-8 weeks, add second drug (ACEI + CCB, or ACEI + Thiazide)
Step 3: If not at target, triple combination (ACEI + CCB + Thiazide)
Step 4: If still not controlled → REFER to specialist

IMPORTANT: WHO PEN recommends starting with LOW-dose combination therapy rather than high-dose single drug — better BP control with fewer side effects

6. HYPERTENSIVE EMERGENCIES
- BP > 180/120 with organ damage symptoms:
  * Severe headache, visual changes, chest pain, shortness of breath, confusion, seizure
  * Nosebleed that won't stop
- ACTION: Call 199 or go to EFSTH IMMEDIATELY
- While waiting: sit upright, stay calm, deep breathing, loosen tight clothing
- Do NOT give extra BP medication at home during a crisis

7. MONITORING SCHEDULE
- After starting medication: recheck BP in 2-4 weeks
- Once stable: every 3 months
- Blood tests (potassium, creatinine, glucose): every 6-12 months
- More frequent monitoring if: diabetes, kidney disease, elderly
- Home BP monitoring if available: morning and evening readings, record in diary

8. MEDICATION ADHERENCE
- Common reasons for non-adherence in Gambian context:
  * Cost of medications
  * Feeling well (no symptoms) → thinking medication not needed
  * Side effects (especially frequent urination with thiazides)
  * Running out of medication between clinic visits
  * Traditional medicine use instead of prescribed medicine
- Strategies:
  * Explain that hypertension is usually silent — "the river can erode banks quietly"
  * Align medication times with daily routine (prayers, meals)
  * Simplify regimen — once-daily medications when possible
  * Involve family member as medication buddy
  * CHW follow-up visits for adherence support

9. SPECIAL POPULATIONS
- Pregnancy: STOP ACEI/ARBs immediately if pregnancy suspected. Use Methyldopa or Labetalol.
- Breastfeeding: Amlodipine, Enalapril are generally safe. Avoid Atenolol.
- Elderly (> 65): start at half dose, titrate slowly, watch for orthostatic hypotension (dizziness on standing)
- Diabetes: target < 130/80, prefer ACEI/ARB, avoid high-dose thiazides (worsen glucose)
- Kidney disease: ACEI/ARB preferred (protects kidneys), monitor potassium carefully

10. REFERRAL CRITERIA
- BP not controlled on 3 medications at adequate doses
- Suspected secondary hypertension (young patient < 30, sudden onset, resistant to treatment)
- Hypertensive emergency (BP > 180/120 with symptoms)
- Pregnancy with hypertension
- New kidney impairment or worsening renal function
- Recurrent electrolyte abnormalities

GAMBIAN CONTEXT:
- Most common antihypertensives available at health centres: Amlodipine, Hydrochlorothiazide, Enalapril
- BP measurement equipment available at health posts (manual sphygmomanometer)
- Digital BP monitors increasingly available at health centres
- Traditional remedies often used alongside: kinkeliba tea, bitter leaf. Advise patients to inform their doctor.
- Salt intake in Gambia is HIGH due to Maggi cubes, dried fish, and sauces — salt reduction is the single most impactful lifestyle change
- Bissap (hibiscus) tea has evidence for modest BP reduction — can be encouraged as part of healthy diet
