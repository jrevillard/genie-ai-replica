## History

- Earthworm farming began in the United States in 1947
- It has been implemented in a large number of countries, primarily Switzerland, the Netherlands, Italy, Spain, Cuba, Japan, Mexico, Argentina, Colombia, and Central America.
- Earthworm farming, also known as Vermiculture
- Currently, there are between 6 and 7 thousand different species of worms, with the most well-known being Lumbricus Terrestris (earthworm)

## Vermiculture or Earthworm Farming: What is it?

<!-- image -->

Intensive breeding of worms for the production of worm castings from organic waste or recycling biodegradable organic waste. It is a natural decomposition process, similar to composting, in which organic material is also broken down by the microorganisms (fungi, bacteria, actinomycetes, yeasts, etc.) present in the natural environment, as well as by the worm's digestive system.

<!-- image -->

## Classification of the Californian Earthworm

It is known as the Californian Red Earthworm (Eisenia foetida), because it was discovered in this state of the USA, where its properties for the ecosystem were discovered and where the first breeding facilities were established.

Kingdom: Animal
Type: Annelid (segmented body)
Class: Oligochaeta (from Greek oligos, little, small, and Latin chaetae, bristles)
Order: Opistopora
Family: Lumbricidae
Genus: Eisenia
Species: foetida

## Morphology of the Earthworm

1. Possesses a mouth but no teeth.
2. Ultraviolet rays kill them quickly (photophobic)
3. Its body is cylindrical, segmented, and has between 120 and 175 segments, and is covered in a thin cuticle, with a length of 6 to 8 cm in adults and a diameter of 3 to 5 mm, with a color ranging from pink white to dark red in adults. (E. foetida)
4. Clitellum: Shaped like a ring, pinkish-white, located in the anterior third, equipped with a gland that secretes the substances that form the capsules or cocoons.
6. Respiration through the skin, must be kept moist.
6. Its circulatory system is equipped with five pairs of hearts and three pairs of kidneys.
6. It is hermaphroditic: Possesses both ovaries and testicles, and is unable to self-fertilize, so it needs to mate with another of its species.
8. Cocoons are capsules where the fertilized eggs are housed, this capsule is pear-shaped, with a diameter of 1-3 mm, and is green-yellow to green-red, and opens between 14 and 21 days in favorable conditions, humidity, temperature, etc.
9. Newborn worms measure approximately 1 mm and weigh 0.8 to 1 g as adults, and ingest 100% of their weight in decomposing organic matter daily, of which 60% is excreted as compost and 40% is assimilated and becomes worm biomass.
10. Sexual maturity is reached at 3 months, and from then on, the clitellum forms and mating occurs.
11. They live up to 15 years and are very docile to be kept in small environments. (E. foetida)
12. They have a high prolificacy of 2 to 21 worms, which with abundant food produce up to 1,500 offspring per year.
13. Calciferous glands: also known as Morren glands, are special organs that secrete calcium carbonate and have the function of controlling pH, as well as inhibiting certain fungi and bacteria found in the organic substrates they consume.
14. Regeneration: The worm has the ability to regenerate lost segments, but only if the injury or destruction affects the last part of the intestine. They are not affected by diseases caused by decomposing organic matter.
15. A worm is 80% water and 20% dry matter, and contains 65% protein.

What is worm castings?

- Worm castings are stable organic matter, meaning that it has reached a level of stability that microorganisms cannot break down further. However, what the worms produce is truly a "biodigested and microbiologically worked compost," or "vermicompost."
- Vermicompost is a mixture of chemical compounds that result from the "digestive-enzymatic" action as organic matter passes through the worms' intestines. This creates a magical transformation: waste into fertilizer, with a perfect balance and immediate availability of macro elements (N - P - K), a large amount of micro elements, and, most importantly, a gigantic bacterial load (20 billion per gram of dry matter). It is a true energy booster, enriching soils like no other.
- Worm castings are neutral, odorless, and do not harm young or delicate plants. Advantages of using worm castings
- They can be stored for a long time without undergoing any changes.

Physical and chemical characteristics of worm castings

- Worm castings are homogeneous and have a fine grain size, with a color ranging from light brown to black. They have a forest floor-like odor and a moisture content of 15 to 20%.
- They are clean, soft to the touch, and their high biological stability prevents fermentation or decomposition.
- Their neutral pH (6-7) makes them extremely reliable for use with delicate plants.
- Vermicompost contains four times more nitrogen, twenty-five times more phosphorus, and two and a half times more potassium than the same weight of cow manure.

## Physical:

1. Removal of organic waste
2. Significantly improves soil structure, compacting sandy soils and disintegrating compacted soils (porosity), which promotes water and air circulation. Soils rich in organic matter are spongy and less susceptible to drought.
1. Protects against erosion by increasing water retention, promoting root growth, and increasing water retention capacity by 5 to 30%, which translates to less frequent watering and greater resistance to drought, a fundamental characteristic of climate change.

## Chemical:

- 1. Increases the soil's cation exchange capacity, which translates to better utilization of existing or added nutrients through fertilization.
- 2. Provides plants with essential elements such as nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium (NPK). Trace elements such as iron, manganese, zinc, boron, molybdenum, copper, etc.
- 3. Stimulates biological activity through the microbial flora it contains.
- 4. Facilitates the availability or absorption of nutrients for the plant species by immediately dissolving insoluble mineral components (phosphates).

Transforms soluble nitrogen into organic nitrogen (within the bodies of microorganisms), preventing its loss through water runoff or as ammonia in the air.

- 5. Conserves and increases organic matter content.
- 6. Not only replaces any chemical fertilizer (which lack bacterial flora) but can also increase its benefits. The major mistake of modern agriculture has been to fail to plan for the reconstruction of humus, relying exclusively on chemical fertilizers.
7. Compared to other organic fertilizers derived from animal manure, one ton of earthworm compost (lombriabono) is equivalent to ten produced by cows, chickens, and pigs.
8. In addition, when using manure produced by these animals, nitrogen is lost, phosphorus is not assimilable, and a serious imbalance occurs in the soil.
9. It is the only fertilizer produced that is accepted in certified organic farms, making it the ideal fertilizer for all types of organic crops.
- 10. Earthworm compost is used to enrich soils that have become infertile due to the overuse of chemical fertilizers, or due to intensive exploitation over prolonged periods of time.
- 11. Due to its high content of humic and fulvic acids, it improves the chemical properties of the soil.

## Biological:

- 1. Earthworm compost promotes the formation of mycorrhizae, microorganisms responsible for accelerating root development.
- 2. Effectively influences the germination of seeds and the development of seedlings. Earthworm humus significantly increases the size of plants, trees, and shrubs.
- 3. Reduces the environmental impact of agrochemicals and the consumption of inorganic fertilizers.
- 4. When incorporated into plant transplants, it prevents diseases and reduces the effects of damping-off.
5. It contains approximately 20 billion bacteria per gram of earthworm compost.
6. It is rich in hormones, substances produced by the secondary metabolism of bacteria, which stimulate plant biological processes.
7. Natural decomposition, based on the activity of fungi and bacteria, lasts for at least 20 to 30 years; but through vermiculture, this process can be achieved in less than six months.
8. Natural, non-toxic, and 100% free of chemical additives.
8. Its beneficial effects last up to four years after application.
