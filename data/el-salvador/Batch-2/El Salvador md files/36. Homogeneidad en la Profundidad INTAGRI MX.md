## Ensuring Uniform Planting Depth in Corn Cultivation

Author: Intagri Editorial Team

## Introduction

The per capita consumption of shelled corn in Mexico is 297 kilograms, and approximately 7.6 million hectares are planted, producing nearly 25 million tons of grain. However, despite corn being the foundation of the Mexican diet, the average yield is only slightly above 3 tons per hectare, while the average annual yield in the United States is significantly higher, exceeding 9 tons/hectare.

Therefore, to achieve high yields, it is essential to carefully manage and maintain all the elements that are essential to the production process, from planting to harvest. These include: land preparation, hybrid selection, seed size and shape, planting depth, seeding density, and spacing between rows, planting speed, crop management, fertilization (source, dose, timing, and method), water availability, weed, pest, and disease control, and weather conditions, among others.

## Consequences of Improper Planting Depth

Shallow planting. The capacity for water and nutrient absorption through the roots is significantly reduced, and plants can develop a detrimental condition called "Rootless Corn Syndrome," which leads to losses due to lack of nodal roots, as it increases susceptibility to lodging due to reduced root development. On the other hand, it can increase damage to seedlings due to excessive exposure to herbicide residues.

## Deep Planting

Planting the seed at a depth greater than the recommended depth increases the risk of disease attacks, such as corn ear rot. In addition, the plant suffers excessive depletion of food reserves, increasing susceptibility to insects, soil pathogens, competition from weeds, or under high plant density, as well as to the active ingredients of herbicides. The risk of damage from contact with fertilizers and the possibility that leaves will sprout or not emerge due to soil surface compaction is also increased.

Figure 2. Root development and absorption are reduced as planting depth decreases. Source: Licht et al., 2011.

## Recommendations for Achieving Uniform Planting Depth

Check the planting depth in the field, which requires verifying the proper functioning and operation of the planter. This can be achieved by examining 10 meters of linear space with the planter calibrated to the desired depth and at the normal operating speed.

Ensure that the soil conditions around the seed are optimal. If the soil is drier, you can plant at a deeper depth, adjust the leveling and/or pressure, or plant normally and wait for rain.

Plant the seed at a depth of between 3.8 and 5 centimeters for optimal root development. Table 1 shows the effect of planting depth on emergence percentage and time, as well as root length.

Consider the topography (special characteristics of the land). It is recommended to perform leveling to avoid misalignments of the planter due to irregularities.
