## INTRODUCTION

One of the government's current strategies is to strengthen food sovereignty and security, promoting increased food production and employment in the agricultural sector. It is also important to address the effects of climate change. In this context, the National Center for Agricultural, Livestock and Forestry Technology "Enrique Álvarez Córdova" (CENTA) joins these efforts, in coordination with the International Maize and Wheat Improvement Center (CIMMYT), to make a synthetic maize variety with high yield potential and good agronomic characteristics, known as CENTA-ASG: Commander Arlen Siu "Guazapa" (Celia Margarita Alfaro Mancía, 1954-1985), available to producers, as a way to honor their true dedication to the social causes of our country.

This technology will benefit producers, as yellow maize in our country is primarily used for silage production and the manufacture of concentrate, with the latter increasing in small family farms. With this contribution, CENTA is fulfilling its objectives, which are focused on benefiting small and medium-sized producers in El Salvador.

## I. INTRODUCTION

Climate variability has led to early droughts at the beginning of the planting cycle, more intense rainfall in some periods of cultivation, and the appearance of diseases and pests with negative impacts on productivity and food security. Recent research, including modeling studies, indicate that the impact of global warming could cause significant reductions in the yields of maize and bean crops in the next thirty years. An increase of 1 °C in temperature could generate a 5% loss, while a 2 °C increase would result in a 20% loss in Central America. In this context, the identification of maize genetic material with the ability to adapt, resist pests and diseases is a priority (FONTAGRO 2009).

Yellow maize is the main source of energy for animal concentrate production, making it essential for this industry. Given the importance of the crop, it is necessary to generate technologies that contribute to reducing the problems of the crop, with the aim of increasing production and productivity. Within these new technologies, the availability of synthetic yellow maize varieties with high yield potential and limited humidity tolerance will allow for increased production of this grain to meet the demand of the concentrate industry.

CENTA, through the Basic Grains program, makes a new synthetic yellow maize variety with good agronomic characteristics and good yield potential available to farmers, as an alternative for environments with limited humidity. The availability of this new variety will directly reduce the import of seeds, grain and production costs, which can be translated into a readily available source of main raw material for the production of concentrates or silage (Sigüenza 2016).

## II. ORIGIN

The synthetic maize variety CENTA-ASG, initially known as line SO7TLYAB-01, is a yellow grain variety with normal endosperm and limited humidity tolerance, identified by the National Center for Agricultural, Livestock and Forestry Technology "Enrique Álvarez Córdova" (CENTA) in collaboration with the International Maize and Wheat Improvement Center (CIMMYT).

A synthetic variety is a genetically advanced generation that results from pollination between several genotypes of a plant species: These genotypes can be inbred lines, clones or selected populations through different improvement processes.

## III RESEARCH STRATEGY

The evaluation of the synthetic variety CENTA - ASG began in 2012 as part of the "Adaptation of maize and bean to climate change in Central America and the Dominican Republic: an alternative to mitigate poverty" project (PRACCA); for which a trial was established with 12 yellow synthetic varieties in fields of farmers in the municipalities of Yayantique and Conchagua in the La Unión department, both located in the so-called dry corridor, with the aim of identifying germplasm with drought tolerance, selecting line SO7TLYAB-01 for its high grain yield potential and good agronomic characteristics. During 2016, it was evaluated in 21 validation plots at the national level, where it outperformed the traditional varieties used as controls by 33% in grain yield (Table 1), also highlighting low percentages of weed infestation and rotten ears (Deras 2012).

## IV FARMER PERCEPTION OF THE SYNTHETIC VARIETY

The farmers who participated in the validation of the CENTA ASG synthetic variety expressed that the variety possesses: good plant and ear height, good size and coverage of the ear; low percentages of weeds; good grain color; grain health and high grain yield potential.

## VI SOIL PREPARATION

For proper soil preparation, it is recommended to incorporate the crop residues, as it helps to control weeds, enrich the soil, provide permeability, control pests, conserve moisture, prevent erosion, and allows for good seed germination.

In our country, the most common types of preparation are:

- Conventional tillage: where two passes with a plow are carried out to prepare a good seedbed of at least 15 cm of well-pulverized soil. This allows for proper and uniform sowing. In addition to preparing the seedbed, plowing also helps to eliminate the first generation of weeds.
- Minimum tillage: this is recommended for soils with a steep slope, and consists of mainly removing weeds through hoeing or using environmentally friendly chemicals.

## VII CROP MANAGEMENT

To obtain good yields, it is important to take the following recommendations into account.

## 1) Before Planting

- a) Use certified seeds of good quality, both physically, physiologically, genetically, and phytosanitary.
- b) To prevent the attack of soil pests, it is recommended to treat the seeds with products specifically designed for this purpose.

## 2) Planting Seasons (will depend on when the rainy season is well established)

- a) First: May 15th to June 15th
- b) Second: August 15th to August 31st

## 3) Planting Density

The recommended spacing is:

Between rows: 0.80 meters
Between plants: 0.40 meters, with two seeds per plant

With these planting densities, you will achieve a density of 62,500 plants per hectare, or 43,750 plants per acre.

## 4) Fertilization

It is recommended to conduct a soil analysis in a laboratory before planting, in order to apply the appropriate fertilization. The laboratory will indicate the type of fertilizer, the most suitable dose, and the timing for the specific conditions of the soil. It is very important to apply nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium fertilizers, as these are the essential elements for the normal development of the plant, as they participate in physiological processes that favor increased foliage and grain production.

If a soil analysis is not available, the following general recommendations apply:

- a) First fertilization: This should be applied at the time of planting, or no later than 10 days after planting. Apply 4.4 kg/ha of a 15-15-15 fertilizer.
- b) Second fertilization: Apply 4.4 kg/ha of ammonium sulfate (21% N, 24% S), 25 days after planting. If the crop is mechanized, fertilization should be applied at the time of sowing.
- c) Third fertilization: Apply 1.5 kg/ha of urea (46% N) 40 days after planting.

To maximize plant yield, it is recommended to incorporate the fertilizer into the soil.

## 5) Insect Pests

From the time of planting, corn is very susceptible to pest attacks. Climate, soil preparation, weed control, and crop rotation can favor the increase of pests or maintain them at tolerable levels, without exceeding the economic threshold and causing significant damage to the crop. It is very important to constantly monitor the crop to determine the appropriate time to apply green-band insecticides, ensuring effective pest control with minimal impact on the environment.

The corn earworm (Spodoptera frugiperda) is the most economically important pest in corn cultivation. This is a chewing insect that feeds on young plant tissue, and also acts as a cutter, causing damage during the night.

In coastal areas or in places where its appearance has been identified, it is very important to control the corn earworm (Dalbulus maidis), a vector of the corn stunt virus, which can cause losses of up to 100%. The peak incidence is in August and December.

## 6) Weeds

The problem of weeds in corn is one of the factors that has the greatest influence on reducing the final yield of the crop. Control is very important to create favorable conditions for the growth and development of the crop, thus avoiding competition for light, water, and nutrients. To carry out effective weed control, it is necessary to know the life cycle, growth habit, aggressiveness, and method of propagation of each weed.

Weed control can be manual or chemical, depending on the economic conditions of the producer.

## 7) Diseases

Corn is susceptible to several diseases that affect the normal development of plants. Diseases are favored by environmental conditions, soil type, corn susceptibility, and, in the case of viral diseases, by conditions that favor the migration, establishment, and survival of insect vectors. Foliar diseases caused by fungi are most common in the later stages of the crop, and are only important when they appear before flowering or very close to it, or when they are of an epidemic nature, such as the "black spot" disease, which occurred frequently in the 2007-2013 period in intermediate valleys with high humidity and significant temperature variations.

## 8) Harvesting

**For grain**

Generally, the harvest is carried out between 110-120 days after planting. The presence of the "black layer" in the grain is an indicator. This activity can be carried out with or without a threshing machine, depending on the preference or taste of the farmer. The threshing, can also be carried out manually. The advantage of harvesting without threshing and manual threshing is that the farmer can select healthy ears that have not been affected by fungal diseases, which, if consumed, pose a risk to human health.

It is recommended to harvest on time, not to exceed 120 days, to avoid exposing the plant to lodging, damage by rodents and birds.

As a self-pollinating variety, the farmer can save seeds from one cycle to the next, for a maximum of three cycles. The selection should be systematic. In the fourth cycle, the seed should be renewed to maintain the phenotypic and genotypic characteristics of the cultivar.

It is recommended to store the grain with a moisture content of 13% in metal silos or in bags in a dry and ventilated place. If chemical products are used to prevent grain pests, they should not be stored in residential buildings.

**For silage**

It is recommended to cut the plants when the grain is in the dough-milk stage.
