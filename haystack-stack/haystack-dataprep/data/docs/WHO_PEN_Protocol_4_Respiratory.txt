WHO PEN Protocol 4 — Diagnosis and Management of Asthma and Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease (COPD)

PROTOCOL 4: RESPIRATORY DISEASE MANAGEMENT

1. OVERVIEW
- Asthma and COPD are the two most common chronic respiratory diseases
- Asthma: reversible airway narrowing, often allergic, any age (often starts in childhood)
- COPD: progressive, largely irreversible airway damage, usually age 40+, strongly linked to smoking
- Both cause: cough, wheezing, shortness of breath, chest tightness
- In The Gambia: indoor cooking smoke (wood/charcoal) is a major cause of COPD, especially in women

2. DIFFERENTIATING ASTHMA FROM COPD

ASTHMA features:
- Usually starts before age 40 (often childhood)
- Symptoms vary day to day, often worse at night or early morning
- Triggers: dust, cold air, smoke, pollen, exercise, stress, infections
- Family history of asthma/allergies common
- Responds well to bronchodilators (symptoms fully reverse)
- Between attacks, patient may be completely normal

COPD features:
- Usually age 40+ with significant smoking history OR long-term biomass fuel exposure
- Symptoms are persistent and slowly progressive
- Productive cough (with sputum) common
- Shortness of breath on exertion that gradually worsens
- Does NOT fully reverse with bronchodilators
- History of frequent chest infections

IMPORTANT: Some patients have BOTH asthma and COPD (Asthma-COPD Overlap)

3. DIAGNOSIS
- Clinical diagnosis based on symptoms + history + response to treatment
- Peak flow meter (if available): measures maximum expiratory flow
  * Asthma: variability > 20% between morning and evening readings
  * COPD: persistently low readings
- Spirometry (if available at hospital): confirms diagnosis
  * COPD: FEV1/FVC ratio < 0.70 after bronchodilator
  * Asthma: > 12% improvement after bronchodilator

4. ASTHMA MANAGEMENT

STEP 1 — Mild intermittent (symptoms < 2x/week):
- Inhaled Salbutamol (short-acting beta-agonist) as needed only
- 2 puffs when symptoms occur, can repeat after 20 minutes
- If using Salbutamol > 2 times per week → step up

STEP 2 — Mild persistent (symptoms > 2x/week but not daily):
- Add low-dose inhaled Beclometasone (corticosteroid) 100-200 mcg twice daily
- Continue Salbutamol as needed for rescue
- Inhaler technique education is CRITICAL

STEP 3 — Moderate persistent (daily symptoms):
- Increase Beclometasone to 200-400 mcg twice daily
- Continue Salbutamol as needed
- Consider adding oral Theophylline (if inhaler therapy insufficient)

STEP 4 — Severe (symptoms throughout the day):
- High-dose inhaled corticosteroid
- Add oral Prednisolone short course (5-7 days) for exacerbations
- REFER to hospital for specialist care

ASTHMA ACTION PLAN (teach every patient):
GREEN ZONE (doing well): no symptoms, sleep well, can do normal activities
- Continue regular preventive inhaler
- Salbutamol before exercise if needed

YELLOW ZONE (getting worse): cough, wheeze, chest tightness, waking at night
- Double the Beclometasone dose
- Use Salbutamol every 4-6 hours
- If no improvement in 2 days → see health worker

RED ZONE (medical alert): severe shortness of breath, cannot speak full sentences, lips or fingernails blue
- Use Salbutamol 4-6 puffs immediately
- Start oral Prednisolone if available
- GO TO HEALTH FACILITY IMMEDIATELY or call 199
- This is an EMERGENCY

5. COPD MANAGEMENT

STABLE COPD:
Step 1: Inhaled Salbutamol as needed (2 puffs up to 4 times daily)
Step 2: If still symptomatic, add oral Theophylline 200mg twice daily
Step 3: If still symptomatic, REFER for specialist assessment

IMPORTANT: Inhaled corticosteroids are NOT routinely recommended for COPD (unlike asthma)
Exception: patients with frequent exacerbations (2+ per year) may benefit

COPD EXACERBATION (flare-up):
- Increased breathlessness, increased cough, change in sputum colour (yellow/green)
- Treatment: increase Salbutamol, add Amoxicillin 500mg 3x daily for 5-7 days (if sputum is green/yellow), short course Prednisolone 30mg daily for 5 days
- If severe (can't speak, oxygen saturation < 90%, confusion) → EMERGENCY referral

6. INHALER TECHNIQUE (CRITICAL FOR BOTH CONDITIONS)
- Poor inhaler technique is the #1 reason for treatment failure
- Teach and check technique at EVERY visit

METERED-DOSE INHALER (puffer) technique:
1. Remove cap, shake inhaler well
2. Breathe out fully
3. Put mouthpiece between lips, seal tightly
4. Start breathing in SLOWLY and deeply
5. Press the canister ONCE while still breathing in
6. Continue breathing in for 3-5 seconds
7. Hold breath for 10 seconds (or as long as comfortable)
8. Wait 30-60 seconds before second puff if needed
9. Rinse mouth with water after using corticosteroid inhaler (prevents oral thrush)

SPACER USE (recommended, especially for children and elderly):
- Attach inhaler to spacer
- Press canister once into spacer
- Breathe in and out through spacer 4-6 times
- Improves drug delivery to lungs by 50-60%
- Homemade spacer: 500ml plastic bottle with hole for inhaler — teaches with what's available

7. TRIGGERS AND PREVENTION

ASTHMA TRIGGERS to avoid:
- Tobacco smoke (active and passive)
- Indoor cooking smoke (wood, charcoal, crop residues)
- Dust and dust mites (wash bedding weekly in hot water)
- Strong scents/perfumes
- Cold air (cover nose/mouth in cold morning air)
- Respiratory infections (wash hands frequently)
- Exercise in cold/dusty conditions (use Salbutamol 15 min before)
- Certain foods (in some patients)
- Emotional stress (teach breathing exercises)

COPD PREVENTION:
- STOP SMOKING (most important intervention — reduces decline in lung function)
- Reduce indoor air pollution: improved cookstoves, ventilation, cook outdoors when possible
- Annual influenza vaccination (if available)
- Early treatment of respiratory infections
- Avoid occupational dust exposure (farming, construction — use masks)

8. WHEN TO REFER
- Diagnosis uncertain (asthma vs COPD vs other)
- Poor response to initial treatment after 4-6 weeks
- Frequent exacerbations (> 2 per year)
- Any severe/life-threatening attack
- Suspected occupational asthma
- Need for spirometry confirmation
- Child with persistent asthma (< 5 years)

9. MONITORING
- Asthma: review every 3 months when stable, 2-4 weeks after medication change
- COPD: review every 3-6 months when stable
- At every visit: check symptoms, inhaler technique, medication adherence, trigger avoidance
- Peak flow diary: if available, record morning and evening readings

GAMBIAN CONTEXT:
- Indoor cooking smoke from traditional 3-stone fires is a MAJOR cause of COPD in women
- Improved cookstoves (Jiko, rocket stoves) reduce indoor pollution by 50-70%
- Salbutamol inhaler available at most health centres (essential medicine)
- Beclometasone inhaler may need referral to regional hospital
- Spacers often unavailable — teach homemade spacer technique (plastic bottle)
- Dust during harmattan season (Dec-Feb) worsens both asthma and COPD
- Theophylline available but needs careful dosing (narrow therapeutic window)
- Traditional remedies for cough (honey, ginger, kinkeliba) — generally safe alongside prescribed medicine, but should not replace inhaler therapy
