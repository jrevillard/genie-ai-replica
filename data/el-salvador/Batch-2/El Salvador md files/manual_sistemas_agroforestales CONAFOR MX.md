## Introduction

Agroforestry systems combine the use of land with woody, agricultural, and/or animal species. The goal is to diversify production in a sustainable manner.

The increasing pressure on land due to population growth in many tropical areas can lead to land degradation. One way to address this is through agroforestry or agroforestry systems (López, 2007).

Almost all traditional agricultural systems and livestock systems have trees interspersed with crops or managed by alternating trees and crops and/or pastures; in other words, they are agroforestry systems. Agricultural landscapes still contain a large number of trees, which serve many purposes such as production (wood, firewood, fodder, fruits, and medicines, among others), as well as services (shade for crops and/or animals and protection, such as in the case of windbreaks) (Beer et al., 2004).

In the Yucatán Peninsula, the slash-and-burn system has been a traditional practice. This agricultural system has allowed rural populations to subsist through the association of corn, beans, and various types of legumes (Moya et al., 2003).

However, the sustainability of this system is threatened by the shortening of land rest periods, approximately from 20 to less than seven years, as well as by the decreasing diversity of crops integrated into the system. The same applies to the diversity of vegetation in over-exploited areas; this is decreasing, and in many cases, natural regeneration cycles are being interrupted.

The situation described clearly indicates that technology is needed to intensify land use, with practices to maintain or improve productivity levels, which can reduce the risk of monoculture and the use of external inputs, while also ensuring that social institutions that support the system are strengthened.

In this context, agroforestry systems are a productive alternative that meets the basic needs of the population, provides income in the medium and long term, and helps to improve the condition of forest resources. Furthermore, it has been estimated that for every hectare worked with this agroforestry technology, 5 to 10 hectares of forest are saved (Bandy et al., 1994). However, agroforestry systems can only be viable if they are considered as part of the overall system managed by the family farmer. Given the points mentioned above, it is necessary to transfer the knowledge and technologies of these systems to promote the technology in the communities of the region.

The "Transfer of two agroforestry modules in indigenous shelters in Yucatán" project arose from the need to disseminate the existing technology for these systems. To achieve this, the National Institute of Forest, Agricultural and Livestock Research (Inifap) was contracted to implement this project, following a diagnosis carried out by the National Forest Commission (Conafor) and the National Commission for the Development of Indigenous Peoples (CDI) in the schools of the latter.

## Land Selection

When selecting the land, the following should be considered: a) accessibility; b) the area should not be prone to flooding; c) the level of rockiness (the lower, preferably); and d) the availability of options to increase the area of cultivation of the agroforestry system.

It is necessary to conduct site visits to the areas where the agroforestry system will be established, in order to identify the spaces for the species to be used.

Once the areas for the establishment of the agroforestry system have been defined (based on the technical criteria mentioned above), it is recommended to present the final selection of areas and species to the participating producers, in order to obtain their preferences and to jointly define and adjust the methodology, the components to be implemented, and the layout of the agroforestry system.

## Land Preparation

The preparation of the planting site involves activities to control pests and prepare and clean the soil. These can be carried out manually or mechanically, depending on the type of vegetation, the level of rockiness, and the topography of the area.

The benefit of not using fire to clear the material is to protect the soil from erosion. These activities should be carried out during the dry season, in the months of April or May, one month before the rains and the planting of forest and agricultural species.

Figure 2. Land preparation in the CDI shelter in Becanchén, Yucatán.

## Agroforestry Components

To select the main agroforestry components to be established in the system, it is recommended to use the following criteria:

- They should be native to the region.
- They should provide high-quality products, with diverse uses and demand in the market.
- They should be recognized and accepted by farmers.
- They should not present problems with pests.
- They should provide products in the short, medium, and long term.

Once a list of forest, agricultural, and fruit species has been generated, it should be presented to the producers for their consideration, in order to obtain their preferences, and to jointly define and adjust the methodology, the components to be implemented, and the layout of the agroforestry system.

## Forest Species

Mahogany (Swietenia macrophylla)

Its precious wood, with excellent qualities, produces veneer and sawn timber highly appreciated in carpentry and in all types of constructions. It is exported in the form of boards or veneer (Pennington and Sarukhán, 2005).

The natural habitat of mahogany is the tropical and subtropical forest. Its natural distribution is wide, from the Atlantic region in the southeastern part of Mexico, through Central America, the northern part of South America, and in the southern part of the Amazon basin in Bolivia and Brazil.

## Cedar (Cedrela odorata)

Cedar -an important species in the forestry industry in Mexico- produces a fragrant and valuable wood with excellent characteristics. It is used to obtain sawn timber, in the production of sculptures, veneer for veneer, and to manufacture turned articles for various uses: boxes, cigar wrappers (Pennington and Sarukhán, 2005).

It is found in the humid tropical forest, the humid subtropical forest, and the dry tropical forest. It is a demanding species of light. Its natural distribution extends from Mexico to Bolivia, the northern part of Argentina, and the Caribbean. Due to its wide distribution in tropical America, it is part of the native flora of most Latin American countries.

It is a tree that grows up to 40 m in height, with a cylindrical trunk. Its shape depends on the characteristics of the soil, since in shallow soils it develops a wide root system and upright stems; while in deep and fertile areas, its roots expand more and the trunk becomes fluted. Its crown is wide and sparse (OFI-CATIE).

## Ramón (Brosimum alicastrum)

This is a truly versatile tree, from which all parts can be used. The leaves and fruits, highly palatable, are frequently used as forage for cattle, horses, pigs, goats, and sheep. It is also particularly valuable during the dry season, when it can be the only available fresh forage.

The species belongs to humid or rainy perennifolious forests and semi-montane semi-deciduous forests, as well as to riverbanks in semi-arid areas. It is distributed from southern Mexico (tropical and subtropical), throughout Central America, the Caribbean, and northern South America.

It normally reaches a height of 20 to 25 m, but can reach up to 40 m. Its trunk is straight, usually with well-developed upright stems. The branches are ascending, forming a rounded or pyramidal crown. The bark is rough, dark brown, and often with large, square and angular scales, which is one of the distinguishing characteristics of this tree (OFI-CATIE).

## Ciricote (Cordia dodecandra)

It is a species with many uses: its wood and fruits are used in the food industry and in medicine; for obtaining wood and firewood; in beekeeping, in construction, and in the production of handicrafts and tools.

Its distribution is limited, it grows in Veracruz and from the southeastern part of Mexico to the northern part of Guatemala and Belize.

It is a medium-sized tree, with heights of 10 to 20 m. Its trunk is straight, cylindrical with small spurs and ascending branches. The crown is dense

and has a rounded, young specimen, and less dense trees (over 15 years old). It has a fissured, slightly scaly bark, with grayish or whitish and smooth longitudinal pieces (OFI-CATIE).

## Huano (Sabal mexicana)

The leaves of this palm are mainly used, which, when fully developed, are used to build roofs for rural houses. The old stems are useful in the structural construction of houses and bridges.

Its distribution is typical of dry and very dry regions. Usually on shallow, well-drained, and clay-calcareous soils. It is often found in hedgerows on steep slopes with strong gradients, with moderate rockiness. It is native to Mexico and Guatemala.

It is a palm with a stem up to 15 m tall, with a root system that reaches a height of between 60 and 90 cm from the ground. Its leaves can reach up to 1 m in length (OFI-CATIE).

## Chaca (Bursera simaruba)

It does not seem to have a primary use, but is used throughout Central America to obtain firewood, charcoal, for rural construction, posts, in the production of feed, and numerous medicinal applications.

It is widely distributed from southern Florida, United States, to northern South America. It grows on both coasts of the central part of Mexico. The species is common in dry forest, rainforest, and secondary forests, requiring a tropical or subtropical climate. It can grow in extreme soil conditions, in poor soils or in very calcareous soils.

It is a medium-sized tree, deciduous, with an open and irregular crown. It reaches between 18 and 30 m, its bark is particularly characteristic, soft, reddish-brown, and peels off in thin strips, exposing a greenish-brown inner layer (OFI-CATIE).

## Fruit species

## Sweet orange (Citrus sinensis)

Among citrus fruits, the orange is the most common and best-known fruit worldwide (Toledo, 2008). The orange tree can reach a height of 8 m, its crown is rounded and extends to the ground.

It was initially used for medicinal purposes and for making sweets. It can be easily eaten with the hand, and is used in salads, fresh juices and concentrates. When dried, it is used to flavor and, in some cases, as a decoration (Toledo, 2008).

## Persian lemon (Citrus latifolia)

In cultivation, its trees are vigorous, upright in habit, and larger in size. Its leaves and branches have fewer thorns; its color tends to be bright green (Sánchez, 2011).

Fresh fruits are used in drinks and for marinating and decorating meats. Its fresh juice is used in drinks and for seasoning meats, fish and other types of food. Frozen juice is used in a similar way. The oil extracted from this species is widely used in cosmetics and in the manufacture of flavorings (Malo et al., 1994).

## Banana and banana (Musa sp.)

In Mexico, it is the second most important fruit crop, after citrus fruits, at the commercial level. The banana region is located in the coastal regions of the Pacific Ocean and the Gulf of Mexico.

The main producing states are: Chiapas, Veracruz, Tabasco, Nayarit, Michoacán, Colima, Oaxaca, Guerrero and Jalisco (Munro, 2005).

Bananas are eaten fresh and used in salads, desserts, breads and sweets. They are a good source of ascorbic acid (vitamin C), vitamin B6, and potassium. Bananas are cooked before consumption and can be baked, fried or grilled. Bananas have a similar nutritional value to bananas, but also contain vitamin A and are an excellent source of carbohydrates (starch) (Crane, 1998).

## Agricultural species

## Corn (Zea mays)

Corn is one of the most relevant crops worldwide, by its production volume. Its economic importance lies in that it is a strategic product in the food supply. In addition, in recent years it has become an essential raw material in the production of biofuels (González, 2010).

In Mexico, corn is the most important food crop. Although the protein content of the grain is low, it provides an average of 39% protein and 59% of the energy that Mexicans consume (Sierra, 2004).

## Bean (Phaseolus vulgaris)

The bean is the second most important grain crop in Mexico, after corn. It is a rich source of protein and carbohydrates (glycemic and non-glycemic), it is attributed properties to control cholesterol levels and prevent colon cancer, it contains anti-nutritional factors, it is rich in vitamins of the B complex, niacin, riboflavin, folic acid and thiamine. In addition, it is a food that is part of the basic basket in Mexico (Gómez, 2008).

## X'pelón bean (Vigna unguiculata)

It is a species that is consumed as a tender grain for the preparation of food, as animal feed and in a smaller proportion as a dry grain. It contains 23% protein when dry and 3.4% in the tender pod (Soria, 1988).

## Ib (Phaseolus lunatus)

It is used for human consumption, contains 20% dry protein and 7.5% green. The plant is used to control weeds. Its potential yield is very high and its price in the local market is higher than that of common beans (Soria, 1988).

## Cucumber (Cucumis sativus)

In Mexico, the cultivation of cucumbers is the second most important among exported vegetables, surpassed only by tomatoes (Lycopersicum esculentum L.) (Muy, D., 2004).

Despite being a low-nutritious crop with almost 100% water, it is rich in vitamins A and C, in addition to containing sulfur, which is why it is used in the cosmetic industry. Cucumbers are widely consumed in salads (Soria, M. 1988).

## Pumpkin (Cucurbita pepo)

In recent years, this pumpkin has gained importance in the Yucatan Peninsula, due to the high prices and the demand for its seeds or pepitas, both in the regional, national and even international market (Soria, M. 1988).

## Watermelon (Citrullus vulgaris)

Watermelon is a product that is spread throughout the world; it is considered a plant of temperate-warm climates. The production volumes of watermelon in Mexico place it in sixth place worldwide and as the main supplier in the Japanese market (Rojas, 2004).

In some regions of Africa, from where watermelon originated, it is considered more a water reserve than a food (although it is not insignificant as a food). In addition to being a delicious refreshing drink, it has a high content of lycopene, an antioxidant that reduces the risk of cancer and heart disease (Rojas, 2004).

## Design and topological arrangement

It is recommended to arrange the plants in a certain number of rows, intercalating forest, agricultural and fruit species. Each row will be formed by a number of individuals, depending on their characteristics and spacing, as well as on the needs of the agroforestry system. In addition, it is necessary to establish a perimeter of the agroforestry system, which can be formed by a species of multiple use.

Mahogany, cedar, guava, ironwood, and acacia

## Population Density

For forest tree species such as mahogany, cedar, guava, ironwood, and acacia used in these modules, the optimal spacing for planting depends on the productivity potential of the site. The best growth has been achieved at spacings ranging from 2.5 x 2.5 m to 3.5 x 3.5 m. This will also depend on the crops that are associated with the species and generally the most commonly used spacings range from 4.0 x 4.0 m to 12.0 x 12.0 m.

## Planting Method and Time

Once the site is prepared, planting operations begin with transporting the plant from the nursery to the field. The plant should be planted with the root ball buried to the base of the root and packed to prevent air pockets in the root zone. The planting time can vary depending on the area. In general, planting should take place during the rainy season.

## Cultural Practices

## Weed Control

In the initial growth phase, forest tree species are highly susceptible to competition from weeds, so this operation is essential until the stage is reached where they have grown enough to dominate the spontaneous vegetation. Therefore, it is recommended to carry out weeding and soil cultivation, at least during the first two years. After the second year, annual weeding is necessary until the fourth year, when the planting is established, and this weed control can be done manually, mechanically, chemically, or a combination of these methods (Evans, 1992).

In Yucatán, weed control in meliaceous tree plantations can be effectively carried out by applying glyphosate at a dose of 1.8 kg/ha to weeds up to 15 cm in height; subsequently, two weeks later, apply metribuzin at a dose of 1.2 kg/ha, or diuron at a dose of 3 kg/ha (Rivas et al., 2008; Rivas et al., 2009). In some cases, if the weed growth is vigorous, two annual applications may be required.

When weed control is done manually, the resulting grasses or legumes can be used as forage for livestock.

## Pruning

Knots are one of the most common defects in wood, therefore, controlling the growth of branches, as well as their removal, are important practices, especially when the production is intended for sawn timber or plywood.

The selection of tools for pruning depends on factors such as: thickness, angle of insertion, and height of the branches. For example, an operator with a manual saw can reach a height of 2.0 to 2.5 m from the ground. For heights greater than this, a wooden or aluminum extension is used, with the saw mounted on it. It is important to avoid using tools that cut with impact, such as machetes, for pruning operations, as their use is a frequent cause of damage due to breakage in branches and bark, and pruning is not done flush with the trunk as expected.

Pruning should begin between 12 and 18 months of age. It is recommended to keep two-thirds of the trees free of branches.

The byproducts of this activity are branches and leaves. Of these, the woody materials can be used for energy purposes (firewood or charcoal), and the succulent materials (leaves or tender branches) can be used as green manure to maintain soil fertility. And in the case of ironwood and acacia, they can be used as forage.

## Fertilization

It is recommended to fertilize between 15 and 30 days after planting. The most common doses, with satisfactory results per plant, range from 100 to 150 grams of nitrogen (N), phosphorus (P), and potassium (K), and the most commonly used formulations have been: 17-17-17, 10-34-6, 10-28-6, 5-30-10, 10-30-10, and 5-30-6.

## Pest and Disease Control

One of the pests that can affect the agroforestry plantation is the bark beetle of meliaceous trees (Hypsipyla grandella), which, when attacking the tip or end of the branches, causes the trees to fork and the subsequent delay in growth and malformation of the tree (Díaz, 1999).

## Manual Control

This operation is carried out when the attack by the bark beetle is already present and has caused the death of the main shoot, and therefore, the emission of two or more shoots is necessary to correct the damage caused by the pest. It consists of selecting the most vigorous shoot among the existing ones, as well as the most closed angle with respect to the main axis, and eliminating the remaining ones.

## Silvicultural Control

This method takes advantage of the protective effect of other species that grow spontaneously or induced between the rows of the plantation, forming a barrier that protects the cedar or mahogany trees.

## Chemical Control

The use of contact, ingestion, and systemic insecticides represents an alternative. They should be mixed with clean water, and it is recommended to apply between 1 and 2 liters of the insecticide per 200-400 liters of water per hectare, using a calibrated sprayer at low pressure. It is important to continuously stir the suspension before and during application (the latter should begin during the dry season and intensify during the rainy season).

## Biological Control

The application of living organisms for the control of this pest is an alternative, in which the fungus entomopathogen (Beauveria bassiana) stands out for its good results in controlling the pest. For this case, 250 grams of rice inoculated with the fungus are used, dissolved in 3 liters of clean water, plus 30 milliliters of an adherant. The product of this mixture is filtered and dissolved in 50 liters of water and applied directly to the foliage of each tree using a calibrated sprayer at low pressure. The applications should begin during the dry season and intensify during the rainy season.

## Organic Control

The use of neem oil sprayed on the foliage reduces the incidence of the bark beetle (Hypsipyla grandella) and the damage it causes in cedar and mahogany plantations. Neem (Azadirachta indica) has shown the same level of control as the chemicals, and due to its characteristics as an organic product, non-toxic and biodegradable, it stands out from the other methods of control, so it is suitable for forming the basis of the integrated control strategy of H. grandella (Díaz et al, 2008). 100 ml of the commercial neem product is used per 20 liters of water. The applications are made on the foliage of the plant. The application period is monthly, and in cases of severe attack, it is recommended to make applications every two weeks.

The control treatments used, together with the cultural practices of weeding, application of copper sulfate with lime, and corrective pruning, have achieved a reduction in the incidence of H. grandella in cedar odorata plantations to less than 20% (Díaz et al, 2008).

## Pest and Disease Control

## Sweet Oranges and Persian Limes

The main pests are:

Snow scale (Unaspis citri) and California red scale (Aonidiella aurantii). These are controlled with organophosphate insecticides, Diazinon at a dose of 150 ml in 100 l of water and Ethion 150 ml + 1% oil.

Brown scale (Phyllocoptruta oleivora), red spider mite (Panonychus citri) and Texas spider mite (Eutetranychus banksi). These are controlled with fungicides, with quinometionato with an active ingredient of 25 g in 100 l of water, Abamectin 1.8% C. E. 25 ml in 100 l of water + mineral oil at 1%, Dicofol, 100 ml in 100 l of water, Ethion 150 ml in 100 l of water.

Leafminer (Phyllocnistis citrella). These are controlled with Abamectin 25 ml + 100 l of water + 1% oil.

Citrus psyllid (Toxoptera citricida). This is controlled with organophosphate insecticide, Carbamate, at a dose of 50 g in 100 l of water.

Fruit fly. Aerial spraying (1 l of malathion + 4 l of food attractant + 95 l of water), for orange cultivation.

The main diseases are:

Greasy spot (Mycosphaerella citri). This is controlled with copper oxychloride (300 g + 1% oil), 30 g of Benomilo per 100 l of water.

Virus diseases (exocortis and caquexia): it is advisable to use virus-free buds and disinfect cutting tools with sodium hypochlorite (commercial chlorine).

Gummosis (Phytophthora spp). Use rootstock tolerant to this disease, plant at a height of 25-30 cm, avoid wounds on the trunk, avoid waterlogging; paint the trunk with Fosetil-Aluminio. 60 g per liter of water; Fosetil-Aluminio on the foliage 1.5 kg per hectare.

Crown gall (Ustulina deusta). Apply 60 kg of cow manure around the trunk and cover with transparent or black plastic around the affected trunk.

## Plantains

Black Sigatoka causes significant damage to the quantity and quality of the crop. When establishing a new plantation, it is important to avoid planting vegetative material that is infected with eggs or larvae.

Nematodes attack and destroy the root system of the plants, which is reflected in a general stunting of the plant and reduced weight in bunches. Black Sigatoka is the disease caused by the fungus Mycospharella fijiensis. It is characterized by spots on the leaves that partially or completely destroy the leaf. Control should be achieved through an agroecological pest and disease management program, including cultural practices (pruning, weed control, defoliation and good drainage at the time of fertilization), use of traps such as aromatic plants and natural neem preparations.

## Oranges and Persian Limes

The use of early and late varieties allows for a harvest period of almost 8 months, starting from August.

## Plantains

The bunch is cut and taken to the edge of the plantation, where it is separated into hands and prepared for transport to ripening warehouses.

## Crops

Maize, beans, ib, bean, pumpkin, watermelon and cucumber.

## Planting Method and Timing

## Maize

Planting is done in rows or furrows. Three seeds are planted in each row, with a row spacing of approximately one meter. Planting takes place from the beginning of the rainy season to June 30. Plants planted after this period are at risk of being attacked by field pests and the disease known as "corn stunt".

## Bean

The spacing between rows should be no more than one meter. Two seeds per plant and at a spacing of 30 cm should be planted. This activity is carried out at the beginning of September to take advantage of the September to November rains.

## Broad Beans

Planting is done at 1 x 1m with three or four seeds per plant, at the base of the corn plants or with stakes for climbing. It can also be planted at the same time as the corn. In this way, one bean seed will be placed for each broad bean plant. It adapts well to temporary conditions.

## X'pelon

Two seeds per plant, with each plant separated by 50 cm. It adapts well to temporary conditions, and some varieties are more tolerant of dry conditions than common beans or broad beans.

## Pumpkin

Planting is carried out in rows 3 meters wide, with a distance of 1 meter between plants, placing two to three seeds per plant. This results in a planting density of approximately 5,000 plants per hectare. It is recommended to plant at the beginning of the rainy season, until July 15.

## Watermelon

Pots are made in double rows, separated by 3 meters. The distance between pots is 1 meter, and two to three seeds are planted in each. The seed is placed 3 cm deep. It can be sown with satisfactory results from mid-December to mid-June.

## Cucumber

500 g per hectare of seed is needed, with an average germination rate of 80% when three seeds are planted per pot. These pots are made in double rows, separated by 3 meters. The distance between them is 80 cm. The seed is placed 3 cm deep. It can be sown throughout the year.

## Weed Control

Weed control should be carried out using an ecological approach through cultural practices, including pruning and weed control.

## Fertilization

Fertilization in agricultural crops includes the stages of inoculating seeds with biofertilizers and applying chemical fertilizers.

The first stage is carried out before planting, when the seeds are inoculated with biofertilizers. For this application, a biofertilizer containing bacteria, fungi, an adhesive or gel, and a mulch or plastic is required to spread the seed. Mix with a shovel.

Biofertilizers contain the amount of microorganisms for 1 hectare of medium-sized seeds, such as corn and beans, at a rate of 20 to 25 kg/ha, contained in 1 kg presentations for the case of mycorrhiza. For seeds such as watermelon, pumpkin, broad beans, x'pelon and cucumber, the maximum product amount is a medium dose, and it should always be ensured that the seeds are covered with the adhesive and biofertilizer.

The second stage of fertilization varies depending on the type of crop and the type of fertilizer to be used. For corn, the first fertilization is with 18 g of 1846-00 per plant, the second is only with urea, and the other crops are fertilized with triple 17, applying 5 g per plant. It is important to apply the doses according to the phenological stage of the crop. ## Pest and Disease Control

## Corn

The corn earworm is the pest that causes the most damage to corn, so the use of an insecticide containing chlorpyrifos at a dose of 0.5 l to 0.75 l per hectare in 300 l of water per hectare is recommended. 20 to 30 cc in 15 l of water. Another product that contains metomil at 2% in a dose of 10 kg/ha. The use of pyrethroid insecticides (lambda-cyhalothrin) at the recommended dose also controls earworms and other pests.

Figure 6. Monitoring of the corn crop in the agroforestry system, at the CDI shelter in Becanchén, Yucatán.

<!-- image -->

The most important disease for corn is corn smut, which is transmitted by a smut fungus. The peak population is in August, and it is controlled using resistant varieties and planting at the appropriate time.

## Broad Beans, X'pelon and Broad Beans

The whitefly (Bemisia tabaci) is the main cause of viral diseases and chlorotic mottling, which causes the beans to curl and reduces yield.

Smut fly (Empoasca sp). It feeds on the sap of plants, causing curling, rolling, and yellowing of the leaves. Thrips (Thrips spp.), both nymphs and adults, suck the juice from the leaves.

The main disease affecting beans is common blight. The symptoms at the beginning of the infection are watery lesions on the underside of the leaves, surrounded by a yellowish-green border, followed by brown, and eventually the leaves die.

Figure 7. Monitoring of the bean crop in the agroforestry system of the CDI shelter in Tahdziú, Yucatán.

<!-- image -->

## Watermelon, Cucumber and Pumpkin

These crops can be affected by nematodes, which can be controlled by applying 10 g of organophosphate nematicide (Fenamiphos) per pot before planting. They are also susceptible to cutworms and green aphids. The diseases that these crops suffer from are powdery mildew, which is recognized by yellow spots on the leaves. Downy mildew, which can be easily recognized by a dark brown spot near the root. And finally, the whitefly, which causes the leaves to wrinkle when attacked by its virus, before flowering.

For control, an ecological approach to pests and diseases should be carried out through cultural practices (pruning and weed control), the use of traps, which have the function of biological barriers, such as the sowing of sunflowers and aromatic plants. In addition, for the management of insect populations, it is recommended to use vegetable extracts, such as garlic, cinnamon, neem and anona seeds, among others. In parallel, vegetable oils, soaps and potassium salts can be used to control small insects in cases of high populations.

## Production and Harvests

## Corn

The harvest should be carried out as soon as the kernels on the cob start to dry, until the beginning of the leaves that cover the cob. It is also advisable to separate the leaves from the cob and visually examine the condition of the cob, and decide when to harvest.

Figure 8. Corn produced in the agroforestry system located at the CDI shelter in Becanchén, Yucatán.

<!-- image -->

## Beans, pumpkins, and squash

The harvest is carried out manually when 70% of the plants have reached their physiological maturity, and it is indicated when the leaves change color from green to yellow and fall to the ground. The harvest involves cutting, drying, threshing, and cleaning. Cutting is done manually, and drying is carried out in the field. The cutting time is around November.

## Watermelon

In plantings from December to January, the first cutting should be made at 90 days; in plantings from February to March, it will be done at 60 days, and in plantings from April to June, the cutting will be at 75 days. To determine if the fruit is ripe, you check the tendril corresponding to the peduncle, which should be completely dry.

## Pumpkin

The pumpkin cycle lasts approximately three months, and the time to make the cut is when the fruit is tender and glossy.

## Cucumber

The optimal state for cucumber harvesting is when the skin is soft and tender. The first cutting can be made approximately 60 days after planting, and the last one between 100 and 120 days.

## Conclusions

Implementing agroforestry systems in children's shelters allows for the transfer of technology developed in different research projects, such as this one. In the shelters, forest species such as cedar, mahogany, ramón, chaca, huano, and ciricote, as well as orange, lemon, and banana, were established. Similarly, production of agricultural crops was obtained, diversifying production and supporting the students' diet with vegetables such as cucumber, pumpkin, and grains (corn, beans, ib, and x'pelón, among others). Furthermore, the technology was promoted to parents and the surrounding communities.
