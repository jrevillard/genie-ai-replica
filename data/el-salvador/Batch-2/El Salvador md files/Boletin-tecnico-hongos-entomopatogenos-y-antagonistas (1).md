Within this group of entomopathogenic fungi, Beauveria sp and Metarhizium sp are highlighted for being more aggressive in the control of pest insects, and the doses used in these products are:

## For Metarhizium sp:

Dosage: 10 gr of Metarhizium / gallon of water

It is recommended to apply 2 to 3 times with a 10-day interval. Filter the solution, the spores remain in the water, and the substrate can be washed 2 to 3 times.

Dilute the water with the spores in the required amount of water. The application is done with a backpack or engine sprayer.

## For Beauveria sp:

Dosage: 10 gr / gallon of water of Bauveria

It is recommended to apply 2 to 3 times with an 8-day interval. Filter the solution, the spores remain in the water, and the substrate can be washed 2 to 3 times using a backpack sprayer or engine sprayer.

In addition, when planting bananas, the seeds or seedlings can be immersed in a solution of Beauveria spores.

## V. ANTIGENIC FUNGI

Trichoderma spp. is present in almost all soils, and are typically the most abundant. They are also found in other habitats and quickly colonize plant roots, attacking, parasitizing, and feeding on other fungi. They have developed numerous mechanisms to attack them and at the same time improve the growth of plant roots. Different strains of Trichoderma sp. have shown good control of pathogenic fungi in trials.

## Mode of Action:

Trichoderma sp. produces the rupture of the hyphal walls of the parasitic fungus, penetrates with its own hyphae, and takes advantage of the nutrients of this and breaks it down. Simultaneously, it produces antibiotic-like substances such as Tricodermin and Harzianopiridona, which cause a fungistatic effect on the pathogen, and lytic enzymes that are capable of destroying the pathogen's sclerotia or resistance structures. The inability of the pathogen to produce sclerotia in the presence of Trichoderma sp. is also observed. The pathogenic fungi that Trichoderma can control include: Rhizoctonia sp, Pythium sp, Fusarium sp, Phythostora sp, Sclerotium sp.

## Application:

The dosage is 10 gr / gallon of water and should be applied in fresh morning hours (6 to 9 am) and in the afternoon (4 to 6 pm).

The product comes in cooked rice, and to release the spores, the dose should be immersed in a gallon of water and moved to release the spores, then filtered and passed to a backpack sprayer and applied to the crop or seeds.

## I. INTRODUCTION

Entomopathogenic fungi are organisms that produce spores, are microscopic, heterotrophic (lacking photosynthesis), have chitinized cells, are typically non-motile, are found in crop residues and in nature in general as facultative saprophytes, and are able to develop well in cool, humid places with little sunlight. Once they reach a host insect, they become parasites.

They constitute the most important group in the biological control of pest insects. They have a slow action, some attack different species of insects, and are suitable for application by introduction, environmental manipulation, or inoculative increase.

The properties that make them a viable alternative for widespread use are: high pathogenicity, sufficient stability, and conservation of virulence in the preparation, before application and after a storage period.

## II. CLIMATIC AND EDAPHIC REQUIREMENTS

Ideal temperature: 27 ± 2 °C, Relative Humidity: 90 ± 5%, and low solar intensity, sandy soils, with organic matter content, organic soils.

## III. TYPES AND SPECIES OF ENTOMOPATHOGENIC FUNGI

Most entomopathogenic fungi are found in the subdivision Deuteromycotina, also known as imperfect fungi. This includes the following species: *Beauveria bassiana* and *B. brongniartii*, *Hirsutella thompsonii*, *Metarhizium anisopliae*, *Nomuraea rileyi*, *Paecilomyces spp.*, *Verticillium spp.*, and *Aschersonia spp.*

## IV. MECHANISM OF PATHOGENIC ACTION

In all cases, the infectious unit of entomopathogenic fungi is a spore, usually a conidium. The development of fungal disease is divided into the following phases: 1. Adhesion of the spore to the host: The contact of the spore with the surface of the host occurs through the cuticle, with one or more conidia. The physical and chemical characteristics of the insect cuticle and the spore are responsible for this adhesion.

2. Penetration of the integument: The penetration of the insect cuticle by germinating conidia occurs as a result of a combination of enzymatic degradation of the cuticle and mechanical pressure by the germ tube, where they typically grow in a yeast-like form called hyphal bodies or blastospores. A germ tube and a penetration peg are formed, the latter attaches to the cuticle, and with the germ tube or penetration hypha (penetrating hypha), penetration into the insect's body occurs; penetration depends on the properties of the cuticle, thickness, sclerotization, and the presence of antifungal and nutritional substances.

Penetration through open areas: Fungi can infect insects through the mouth, spiracles, and other external openings of an insect. Humidity is not a problem in the digestive tract; the spore can germinate quickly in this environment, and digestive fluids can destroy the spore or the germinating hypha. The digestion of fungal structures can cause death due to toxicity rather than fungal disease.
