## Abstract

This research project, "Morphological Characterization of Five Varieties of Heirloom Corn in the Experiment Station and Practices of the College of Agricultural Sciences, University of El Salvador, located in the municipality of San Luis Talpa, department of La Paz, El Salvador," was conducted during the months of August 2010 to January 2011, with the purpose of determining the morphological characteristics of the five landrace corn varieties: Santa Rosa, Raque, Tizate, Capulín, and Catracho. Visits were made to producers in the Lower Lempa region, in collaboration with the Mangrove Association, to collect the five varieties, which were characterized based on the corn descriptors from CIAT (International Center for Tropical Agriculture) and CIMMYT (International Maize and Wheat Improvement Center), used by CENTA (National Center for Agricultural and Forestry Technology).

The selected plants for each variety were manually pollinated and analyzed quantitatively and qualitatively. Both field measurements and laboratory measurements used descriptive statistics, based on maximum, minimum, average, standard deviation, correlation, and coefficient of variation, using the Statistical Analysis System (SAS) software. Using the absorption curves of the main elements, the critical points for N, P, and K accumulation were determined. The Catracho variety showed the best nutrient assimilation compared to the other varieties.

Morphologically, the Capulín variety was the most homogeneous for the yield variable, with a coefficient of variation of 9.39%; while for the 100-grain weight variable, the Tizate and Raque varieties were the most homogeneous, with coefficients of variation of 2.16% and 2.74% respectively. With respect to the number of rows, the Santa Rosa variety was the most homogeneous, with a coefficient of variation of 2.76%; while the Catracho variety had a low coefficient of variation for the ear diameter variable, equal to 5.27%.

Keywords: morphoagronomic, characterization, homogeneity, organic management, landraces, corn.

## Introduction

One of the challenges in corn production is that corn producers in the national context do not carry out an adequate process of selecting heirloom seeds, they do not have the technical elements to select the varieties, they do not know the advances in genetic improvement methods, as well as the methods of conserving native seeds to reuse them and obtain a production again, leading to a high degree of heterogeneity in the varieties, due to the loss of their genetic purity, which has led to heirloom corn varieties producing low yields for corn producers. (Poehlman, 1987).

Evaluations of improved and morphological characteristics of heirloom varieties have been carried out in different areas at the national level, so that the farmer can choose the corn variety that best suits him, according to the area where the plot is located, but this work has not been sufficient, as it is necessary to cover many areas where technical advice has not reached (Pablo de Rodríguez et al., 2005).

Corn is one of the main foods in the family, as it is produced by a large part of the farmers in the national context, which motivates the continued generation and transfer of technologies to meet the needs of farmers and consumers (Guerra and Osorio, 2002).

The scope of this research will be of benefit to the producers in the central region of our country, as most of the inhabitants cultivate corn.

By characterizing each of the five corn varieties, the most homogeneous varieties will be identified, in addition to determining the nutrient absorption curves for each of the varieties in the study, and recommending to the farmers the most homogeneous variety identified in the research to continue the purification process, using the mass selection method.

## Agronomic Management of Corn Cultivation

Within the traditional activities for managing corn cultivation, the following were carried out:

## Weed Control

Weed control was performed manually, with the first clearing occurring 8 days after planting, the second at 21 days, and the third at 45 days.

## Fertilization

An organic foliar fertilizer, produced at the Experimental and Practical Station, was applied. The application was 200 cc of foliar fertilizer, diluted in a 5-gallon water pump. A soil fertilization was carried out one day before planting, incorporated with biochar, using the soil analysis performed before planting as a parameter. The second fertilization was carried out 15 days after planting, and the third at 45 days after the crop was established.

## Pest Control

Pest control focused on common insects that attack corn crops, carried out 20 days after planting. Since the planting presented problems due to the presence of the European corn borer (Spodoptera frugiperda), a manual control was carried out on the planting for two weeks, applying soil over the plant's cob.

In addition, ash was applied to control the "corn earworm" (Spodoptera ornithogalli).

## Irrigation

This activity began 43 days after planting; the method used was sprinkler irrigation, with intervals of 5 days, totaling three applications.

## Reproductive Stage

This involved selecting the plants to be pollinated and covering the female flower of each plant before the stigmas emerged on the cob. Glycine bags were used for pollination control. Pollination occurred in a period of 2 to 3 days after covering the cob; for pollen collection, kraft paper bags were used, collecting pollen from several plants of the same variety. The pollen application consisted of depositing it on the stigmas and then covering them again. This activity was carried out between 7 and 9 in the morning. The glycine bags were removed one week after pollination, and the plants were marked with colored identifiers, to facilitate data collection until the end of the study (Fig. 1).

## Data Collection

## Vegetative Stage

- a) Days to germination. This refers to the number of days from planting to the emergence of roots, so the germination test between paper was used, placing 20 seeds per variety.
- b) Days to emergence. This refers to the number of days from planting to the emergence of the coleoptile on the soil surface.
- c) Predominant color of the coleoptile. This was done by comparing the color of the grain's coleoptile with the Munsell color chart, establishing a code for the color of each of the varieties evaluated.
- d) Length of the coleoptile. This was done on the seedlings using a ruler. The unit of measurement used was in centimeters.
- e) Plant height. This was measured during the emergence of the plant until pre-flowering, and refers to the distance from the soil to the base of the ear, the unit of measurement was in centimeters.
- f) Stem diameter. This was also measured during the emergence of the plant until pre-flowering, the unit of measurement was in centimeters.
- g) Number of leaves per plant. This was counted visually on each sampled plant, after flowering.
- h) Leaf width. This was also measured during the emergence of the plant until pre-flowering, and consists of measuring at the midpoint of its length.

## Flowering Stage

- a) Days to male anthesis. For this variable, the time from planting to the emission of pollen with 50% of the plants was considered.
- b) Days to female anthesis. This was taken into account as the time elapsed from the planting of the crop, until the plant presented young, visible filaments or hairs, at least 50% of the plants.
- c) Predominant color of the anthers. This was done using the Munsell color chart, taking the flowering flowers.
- d) Predominant color of the glumes. This was done using the Munsell color chart, taking the flowering flowers of the plant.
- e) Predominant color of the stigma. Using the Munsell color chart, the code was determined.
- f) Plant height. This data was obtained using a measuring tape and considered from the soil to the base of the ear.
- g) Ear height. This variable was taken from the soil to the highest node of the ear.
- h) Length of the leaf lamina. This was taken from the origin node of the leaf, using a measuring tape.
- i) Width of the leaf lamina. This was measured at the midpoint of the leaves, using a measuring tape.
- j) Area of the leaf lamina. A destructive sampling was carried out, in which all the leaves of a sample of plants were cut and placed in the Foliage Area Integrator, to determine the average area of each variety.
- k) Length of the panicle peduncle. This was measured from the base of the ear to the beginning of the first branching of the panicle, using a measuring tape.
- l) Number of secondary branches of the panicle. The axes of the panicle with double branching were visually counted.
- m) Type of ear. This was visually identified as primary, secondary or tertiary according to the description in the CIMMYT (1991) descriptor.
- n) Length of the panicle. This was measured with a measuring tape from the base of the panicle peduncle to the end of the central axis of the panicle, and is taken into account as it is a highly heritable factor in corn, and is determinant for pollen production, directly influencing the filling of grains in the ear.
- o) Total number of leaves per plant. This was counted visually on the 50 sampled plants.
- p) Leaf width. This was measured using a measuring tape, measuring at the midpoint of its length.
- q) Leaf orientation. This was visually identified as the position of the leaves with respect to the plant.
- r) Number of leaves above the highest ear. All the leaves that were above the highest ear were counted.
- s) Days to leaf senescence. This was counted from the emergence of the plant to the drying of 50% of the ears.

## Results and Discussion

The most representative variables, according to the CIMMYT and CIAT descriptors used by CENTA, for characterizing this type of material, are described below.

Plant height. At the flowering stage, the variety that showed the best values for minimum, maximum, and average was Santa Rosa, with 1.75, 2.5, and 2.12 meters, respectively, while the Tizate variety showed the lowest values for minimum, maximum, and average, with 1.3, 1.8, and 1.77 meters, respectively. Pablo de Rodríguez, et al. (2005), evaluated Capulín in San José Cortes, Ciudad Delgado, and obtained an average height of 1.8m at 60 days, and in Sitio del Niño, San Juan Opico, they evaluated Santa Rosa, which showed an average height of 2.0m at 75 days, and also evaluated Catracho in La Virgen, San Cristóbal, which showed a minimum height of 2.5m and a maximum of 3.0m at 75 days, obtaining similar results in this study.

According to CORDES (2005), the Capulín variety had a height of 2.69m, while the Raque variety had heights of up to 3.12m; in this study, the average values were 2.05m.

In other studies, conducted by CENTA (2011), it was determined that the maximum height of Santa Rosa was 2.5m, obtaining lower heights in this study, ranging from 1.75m and 2.5m. These results differ from those obtained in this study, which may be due in large part to water deficiency during the flowering stage of the crop, as rainfall did not reach the required 700 mm (SNET, 2010) for good plant growth and development; in addition, there may be variability in the seed, due to the degree of selection and conservation carried out by farmers, as Luchsinger A and Camilo E, (2008) mention that the height of a corn plant is affected by environmental and genetic conditions. In addition, according to Alas Castro, (2005), corn, being a cross-pollinating crop, exhibits a high degree of segregation over time, which can affect the uniformity of the plants.

Depending on the purpose, many people prefer high plantings for forage production and for animal consumption. On the other hand, it is important to consider that for grain production, Creole varieties have high heights due to their genetic characteristics and due to cultural practices of fertilization based on products with high nitrogen concentrations (Fuentes López, 1990). Furthermore, most producers in the Bajo Lempa region prefer plantings with an average height of 2.5m and less than 3m, as they believe that plants that are too tall are susceptible to lodging, and plants that are too short have problems with weed moisture. In addition, a relatively large planting produces ears of good size.

Stalk diameter. The Capulín and Tizate varieties showed the best values for minimum, maximum, and average, with 4.2 cm, 4.9 cm, and 4.7 cm, respectively, while the Catracho variety showed the lowest values with 3.2 cm, 4.6 cm, and 4.4 cm, respectively.

Stalk diameter showed some variation, which may be due to the genetics of the mother plant (Shenk, et al. , 1983). In addition, there are other factors that can intervene, such as water availability, as FENALACE (2008) states that the greatest water requirements occur during germination, flowering, and grain filling, with average values ranging from 4.8 to 5.4 mm/day; however, it is very important to have sufficient water availability in the soil throughout the crop cycle, so that the physiological processes are fully carried out. In addition, corn does not tolerate prolonged waterlogging, as water stress reduces the length of the reproductive stages and therefore, the yield.

Number of rows. The variety that showed the best values for minimum, maximum, and average was Santa Rosa, with 10, 18, and 16 rows, respectively, while the Catracho variety showed the lowest values with 8, 12, and 10 rows, respectively.

According to CENTA (2010), the Platino and Oro blanco hybrids had an average of 15-14 rows per ear, while the evaluated Creole corn varieties, Catracho and Raque, have the lowest number of rows per ear on average, with 10 and 12, respectively, compared to Tizate, Capulín, and Santa Rosa, which reached an average of 14 and 16, respectively, so there is no significant difference between Creole and hybrid varieties in terms of the number of rows per ear.

According to Fuentes López (1990), the number of rows in an ear is defined by the genetic characteristics, and can range from 6 to 14 rows per ear.

The Capulín, Catracho, and Santa Rosa varieties had the best correlations with values of r=0.11, r=0.04, and r=0.06, respectively, with respect to the variables number of rows and stalk diameter, indicating that as stalk diameter increases, the number of rows also increases; in addition, according to FAO (1993), the number of grains in corn is determined by the number of rows and the number of grains per row of the ear, also mentions that corn grains develop through the accumulation of products from photosynthesis, absorption through the roots, and metabolism of the corn plant in the female inflorescence called spike. This structure can contain from 300 to 1,000 grains depending on the number of rows, diameter, and length of the ear.

Grain weight. According to the CIMMYT corn descriptor (1991), this is an important data for research and characterization of materials. The varieties that showed the best values for minimum, maximum, and average were: Santa Rosa with 32.2g, 37.2g, and 35.1g, respectively, and Catracho with 31.5g, 36.4g, and 34.1g, respectively, while Raque obtained the lowest values with 19.8g, 42.64g, and 20.8g, respectively. For farmers, the weight is very important, as they cultivate these varieties because they have the characteristic of being heavy-grain corn, which favors sales. Some researchers, such as Alas Castro, S. (2005), mention that Creole corn materials have a high content of carbohydrates and starch, which makes the weight greater compared to hybrid varieties; although this theory has not been supported by other authors. The weight of the grains is determined by the hardness and the amount of starch, so heredity has a great influence on the weight of the grain, which is not always proportional to the weight, as in the case of the Santa Rosa variety, which has medium-sized grains and exceeds the average weight compared to other corn varieties; equally, the Tizate variety, showed large grains but the weight is lower than the Santa Rosa and Catracho varieties. Alas Castro, (2005), mentions that corn has characteristics of grains that are very characteristic, so it is necessary to start the process of improvement and thus achieve a homogeneous variety with desirable characteristics.

Yield. The Santa Rosa variety showed the best values for minimum, maximum, and average, with 48.36 qq/mz, 68.27 qq/mz, and 56.89 qq/mz, respectively, followed by the Capulín variety with values of 28.31 qq/mz, 39.96 qq/mz, and 33.3 qq/mz, respectively, Catracho with 55 qq/mz, 51.6 qq/mz, and 43 qq/mz, respectively, Tizate with 26.83 qq/mz, 37.87 qq/mz, and 31.56 qq/mz, respectively, and finally the Raque variety with the following values of 30.2 qq/mz, 36 qq/mz, and 35.53 qq/mz, respectively. According to CENTA (2011) research, the potential yield of Santa Rosa improved is between 60 qq/mz, while in this study, yields of 56.89 qq/mz were obtained on average, it is important to mention that the plot where the varieties were established is being worked under an organic management, as the chemical analysis carried out by CENTA showed 2.47% of organic matter. Pablo de Rodríguez, et al. (2005) indicate that the average yield of the Santa Rosa variety in San Juan Opico, San pablo Tacachico, and San Cristóbal, was 60-70 qq/mz, for the Catracho variety, it was an average of 50-60 qq/mz in the La Virgen area, San Cristóbal, while for the Capulín and Raque varieties, yields of 48 qq/mz were obtained in the San José Cortez area, Ciudad Delgado, and San Juan Opico.

When determining the correlation between the number of rows and yield, the following values were obtained for the varieties: Catracho r=0.16, Capulín r=0.24, Raque r=0.80; while for the varieties Santa Rosa and Tizate, the correlations were negative. This indicates that as the number of grains per row increases, the yield also increases, as shown by the Catracho and Capulín varieties. In addition, there is a positive correlation between the variables grain weight and yield, obtaining the following values: for the Catracho variety r=0.33, Santa Rosa r= 0.03, and Tizate r= 0.13, which indicates that the weight of the grain is directly proportional to the yield of each of the varieties, as the heavier the grain, the higher the yield.

For the variables yield and stalk diameter, the best correlations were shown by the Catracho and Santa varieties, with values of r=0.14 and r=0.13, respectively, indicating that as stalk diameter increases, the yield also increases; this is not the case for the Tizate, Capulín, and Raque varieties, whose correlations were negative.

It is likely that the results obtained in this study did not reach the yields described by other authors, due to the lack of rainfall; as FAO (2001) states that a corn crop with good irrigation transpires about 350 grams of water for each gram of dry matter produced. In the field, there are additional water losses due to soil evaporation, and only a fraction of the dry matter produced forms the grain, which means that a crop with good water availability uses about 800 to 1,000 grams of water for each gram of grain produced. It is important to note that the lack of rainfall during the flowering stage of the crop and the decrease in light hours necessary for a good filling of the ear, are factors that affected the expected yields.

Using the absorption curves of nutrients established for each corn variety, the critical points of accumulation of the main elements N, P, and K were determined for each variety according to the phenological stage. The Catracho variety showed the best nutrient absorption compared to the other varieties, as the percentage of accumulation was higher and the absorption occurred at optimal times for the crop, with the following percentages of absorbed elements: nitrogen 50% 30 days after planting; phosphorus 25% at 45 days after planting, and potassium 55% at 75 days after planting (Fig. 2). ## Homogeneity Percentage

To determine the degree of variation of the most representative variables, the coefficient of variation was used with the values presented in Table 1.

The variability indices are established based on the number of variables, and it was determined that most of the variables evaluated presented high variability indices.

For the variables "yield," "grain weight," "ear diameter," and "number of rows," which are the main characteristics to be evaluated according to CENTA (2010), it was found that the Capulín variety had the lowest coefficient of variation for the "yield" variable, compared to the others, with 9.39%, meaning that it is the most homogeneous variety in terms of average yield; while for the "grain weight" variable, the varieties with the lowest coefficient of variation are: Tizate and Raque, with 2.16% and 2.74% respectively, indicating that they are the most homogeneous in terms of this variable. For the "number of rows" variable, it was found that the Santa Rosa variety had the lowest coefficient of variation of 2.76%, achieving the highest homogeneity compared to the others in terms of this variable. For the "ear diameter" variable, the varieties with the lowest coefficient of variation are Capulín and Catracho, with 5.03% and 5.27% respectively, making them the most homogeneous with respect to the average ear diameter.

## Conclusions

With the morpho-agronomic characterization of the five Creole corn materials, it was determined that the Capulín variety was the most homogeneous for the "yield" variable, as it had the lowest coefficient of variation, equal to 9.39%; while for the "100 grain weight" variable, the varieties Tizate and Raque were the most homogeneous, with coefficients of variation equal to 2.16% and 2.74% respectively. With respect to the "number of rows" variable, the Santa Rosa variety was the most homogeneous, with a coefficient of variation equal to 2.76%; while the Catracho variety had a low coefficient of variation for the "ear diameter" variable, equal to 5.27%.

Regarding correlations, the Catracho variety had the best values, which were as follows: for the "ear diameter" and "number of rows" variables, with a value of r=0.04, for "ear diameter" and "yield" with a value of r=0.14, for the "number of rows" and "yield" variables with a value of r=0.16, and for the "number of rows" and "grain weight" variables with a value of r=0.18. Followed by the Capulín variety with the following values: for the "number of rows" and "ear diameter" variables, with a value of r=0.11, and for the "number of rows" and "yield" variables, with a value of r=0.14.

With respect to the nutrient absorption curves established for each corn variety, it can be concluded that the Catracho variety had a higher degree of nutrient assimilation compared to the other varieties, with the following percentages of absorbed elements: nitrogen 50%, 30 days after planting; phosphorus 25%, at 45 days after planting, and potassium 55% at 29 days after development. In the second place, the variety that had the best levels of assimilation of elements was Tizate, with the following values: nitrogen 30%, reaching full growth at 29 days, 25% assimilation of phosphorus, reaching its maximum at 40 days, and obtaining 50% assimilation of potassium at 29 days of development.

## Recommendations

Apply the mass selection and manual pollination technique in the Capulín and Tizate varieties for a period of at least five years, with the purpose of achieving a total homogeneity of more than 90% of these materials, for more desirable morpho-agronomic characteristics for the farmer, as they contain a higher degree of homogeneity with respect to the variables "yield," "100 grain weight," and "ear diameter," and are the ones that can be purified more quickly than the other varieties studied.

Continue to evaluate the assimilation of the elements nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium in the Catracho and Tizate varieties, with the management that the crops require for their development, through fertilization programs, to identify the critical application times for the crop and achieve the full development of the plant, while also establishing a watering program for the water needs and to achieve success in the final stage of the corn crop.

For public, private institutions, and farmers involved in the agricultural activity in our country, it is recommended to continue the studies and reproduction of these materials: Catracho, as they had the best correlations for the variables: "yield," "ear diameter," "grain weight," and "number of rows," while the Capulín variety had the lowest coefficients of variation for the variables: "yield" and "ear diameter," to achieve 90% homogeneity, as done by CENTA (2010), and to obtain materials with better characteristics and to contribute to the genetic wealth of our agriculture.
