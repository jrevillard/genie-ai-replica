WHO PEN — Lifestyle Counseling for NCD Prevention and Management

COMPREHENSIVE LIFESTYLE INTERVENTIONS FOR NON-COMMUNICABLE DISEASES

1. DIETARY GUIDELINES (WHO RECOMMENDATIONS ADAPTED FOR THE GAMBIA)

SALT REDUCTION (Target: < 5g/day = 1 level teaspoon):
- Current Gambian intake estimated at 8-12g/day (far above WHO recommendation)
- Main sources of excess salt in Gambian diet:
  * Maggi/Jumbo stock cubes: 1 cube = ~2g salt (half the daily limit)
  * Dried/smoked fish (yeet, guedj): very high salt content
  * Table salt added during cooking and at table
  * Fermented locust beans (netetu/dawadawa): moderate salt
  * Soy sauce, oyster sauce in urban cooking
- Practical reduction strategies:
  * Use HALF a Maggi cube instead of full — your family will not taste the difference
  * Rinse dried fish before cooking to remove excess salt
  * Remove salt shaker from dining table
  * Season with: lemon juice, garlic, ginger, scotch bonnet pepper, onion, locust bean (less netetu)
  * Choose fresh fish over dried/smoked when possible
  * When making benachin/domoda: reduce salt by quarter each week — gradual change

SUGAR REDUCTION:
- Added sugar target: < 25g/day (6 teaspoons)
- Main sources of excess sugar in Gambian diet:
  * Sweet attaya (green tea) — each glass can have 3-4 teaspoons sugar
  * Wonjo/bissap drink sweetened heavily
  * Soft drinks (Coca-Cola, Fanta): 1 bottle = 10+ teaspoons sugar
  * White bread and baguettes (refined carbohydrate)
  * Condensed milk in coffee/tea
- Practical strategies:
  * Reduce sugar in attaya by half — add gradually over weeks
  * Drink bissap/wonjo with less sugar or unsweetened
  * Replace soft drinks with water, unsweetened bissap, ginger water, baobab juice
  * Choose whole fruits instead of fruit juice
  * Replace white bread with whole grain when available

FAT REDUCTION AND HEALTHY FATS:
- Total fat < 30% of calories, saturated fat < 10%
- Main sources of excess fat in Gambian diet:
  * Palm oil: very high in saturated fat — dominant cooking oil
  * Groundnut paste (tigadegena): high calorie but contains healthy fats
  * Fried foods: kosam, accara, fataya
- Practical strategies:
  * Use HALF the palm oil when cooking benachin — add water to compensate
  * When making domoda: use less groundnut paste, add more vegetables
  * Grill or steam fish instead of deep frying
  * Limit fried snacks (accara, fataya) to once a week
  * Groundnut oil is healthier than palm oil for cooking (more unsaturated)
  * Eat groundnuts (raw or roasted, not fried) as a snack — healthy fats and protein

FRUIT AND VEGETABLE INTAKE (Target: 5 servings/day = 400g):
- Seasonal availability in The Gambia:
  * Year-round: moringa leaves, okra, bitter leaf, onions, tomatoes
  * Rainy season (June-Oct): mango, watermelon, papaya, sweet potato leaves, sorrel
  * Dry season (Nov-May): oranges, grapefruit, banana, cabbage
  * All year in markets: cassava leaf, jaxatu (garden egg/eggplant)
- Practical strategies:
  * Add moringa leaves to every stew — highly nutritious, available year-round
  * Eat 1 fruit with every meal (banana for breakfast, mango for lunch, orange for dinner)
  * Make supakanja (okra stew) weekly — okra is high in fiber, low in calories
  * Grow a small vegetable garden: moringa tree, okra, bitter leaf — saves money
  * At lumo (market day): buy seasonal vegetables in bulk (moringa, okra, bitter tomato)
  * Replace half the rice in benachin with mixed vegetables (carrots, cabbage, eggplant)

WHOLE GRAINS:
- Chere (millet porridge) is superior to white rice nutritionally
- Fonio is excellent — low glycemic index, good for diabetics
- Reduce white rice portions, increase millet/fonio/sorghum
- Whole grain bread when available instead of white baguette

PROTEIN:
- Fresh fish: 2-3 times per week (mackerel, bonga, sole — abundant and affordable)
- Beans: nyebbeh (black-eyed peas), lentils — cheap protein source, high fiber
- Groundnuts: healthy protein, but watch portion size (high calorie)
- Chicken/meat: moderate portions (palm-of-hand size), remove visible fat
- Eggs: affordable, complete protein — 1-2 per day is fine for most adults

2. PHYSICAL ACTIVITY (WHO Target: 150 minutes/week moderate intensity)

What counts as moderate activity:
- Brisk walking (faster than usual pace)
- Cycling at normal pace
- Farming work (hoeing, weeding, harvesting)
- Household chores (sweeping, washing clothes by hand, carrying water)
- Dancing
- Swimming

Practical strategies for Gambian context:
- Walk to nearby destinations instead of taking a gele-gele (minibus)
- Walk briskly for 30 minutes 5 days a week
- If farming: this already counts — ensure rest periods in heat
- Community walking groups: organize with Bantaba members
- Walk to the health post for check-ups instead of getting transport
- Evening walk after Maghrib: cooler temperature, social, exercise
- For women: compound cleaning, laundry, pounding grain all contribute

Special considerations:
- Diabetes: exercise helps lower blood sugar — check sugar before exercise if on insulin
- Hypertension: regular exercise can reduce BP by 5-8 mmHg
- Avoid heavy exercise if: BP > 160/100, active chest pain, severe breathlessness
- During Ramadan: light exercise after Iftar (not during fasting in heat)
- Heat safety: avoid noon-3pm, drink water before and after, wear loose clothing

3. TOBACCO CESSATION

WHO 5As Framework:
1. ASK: ask every patient about tobacco use at every visit
2. ADVISE: strongly advise all tobacco users to quit — be clear and specific
3. ASSESS: assess willingness to quit — "Would you like to try to stop?"
4. ASSIST: help willing patients make a quit plan
5. ARRANGE: schedule follow-up within 1 week of quit date

Common tobacco use in The Gambia:
- Cigarette smoking (mainly men, increasing in youth)
- Smokeless tobacco (toombak/snuff — common in some communities)
- Shisha/hookah (increasing in urban areas — equally harmful)

Quit plan structure:
- Set a quit date within 2 weeks
- Tell family and friends — get support
- Remove all tobacco products from home
- Identify triggers (after meals, with tea, during social gatherings)
- Plan alternatives: chew gum, drink water, take a walk, keep hands busy
- Manage cravings: they peak at 3-5 minutes — wait it out
- Expect withdrawal: irritability, difficulty concentrating, increased appetite — these pass in 2-4 weeks

NRT (Nicotine Replacement Therapy):
- If available: nicotine gum, patch — doubles quit success rate
- May not be widely available in The Gambia
- Alternative: gradual reduction method — reduce by 25% each week

Relapse:
- Very common — most people try 5-7 times before quitting permanently
- A slip (one cigarette) is NOT failure — get back on track immediately
- Identify what triggered the relapse and plan for next time
- Never shame a patient for relapsing — encourage them to try again

4. ALCOHOL REDUCTION

WHO recommendations:
- Best for health: no alcohol at all
- If drinking: maximum 2 standard drinks per day (men), 1 per day (women)
- No binge drinking (> 5 drinks in one sitting)
- Standard drink = 1 beer (330ml), 1 glass wine (150ml), 1 shot spirits (40ml)

Health risks of alcohol:
- Increases blood pressure
- Increases stroke risk
- Liver damage (fatty liver → cirrhosis → liver cancer)
- Increases risk of: mouth, throat, liver, breast, colorectal cancer
- Worsens diabetes control
- Interacts with many medications (BP meds, diabetes meds, pain meds)
- Weight gain (alcohol is high in calories)

5. WEIGHT MANAGEMENT

BMI categories:
- Underweight: < 18.5
- Normal: 18.5-24.9
- Overweight: 25-29.9
- Obese: >= 30

Waist circumference (abdominal obesity — stronger CVD predictor than BMI):
- Men: risk increased if > 94 cm, high risk if > 102 cm
- Women: risk increased if > 80 cm, high risk if > 88 cm
- Measure at the level of the navel, standing, at end of normal breath out

Weight loss strategies:
- Even 5% weight loss (e.g., 4kg for an 80kg person) significantly reduces NCD risk
- Reduce portion sizes: use a smaller plate/bowl
- No second servings — one plate only
- Eat slowly — take 20 minutes for a meal
- Drink water before meals (helps feel full)
- Reduce oil, sugar, refined carbohydrates
- Increase vegetables and fiber (fills you up with fewer calories)
- Regular physical activity (150 minutes/week)

6. STRESS MANAGEMENT AND MENTAL HEALTH

Stress and NCD connection:
- Chronic stress raises blood pressure
- Stress increases cortisol → raises blood sugar
- Stress leads to unhealthy coping: smoking, overeating, alcohol
- Depression and anxiety are common in NCD patients

Strategies:
- Deep breathing exercises: breathe in for 4 counts, hold for 4, out for 6 — repeat 5 times
- Prayer/meditation/dhikr — spiritual practice reduces stress
- Social connection: Bantaba gatherings, community support
- Regular sleep: aim for 7-8 hours, consistent bedtime
- Limit attaya/caffeine after 4pm (affects sleep)
- Talk to a trusted person (CHW, family member, Imam) if feeling overwhelmed
- Recognize signs of depression: persistent sadness, loss of interest, sleep changes, appetite changes, feeling hopeless — refer if present

7. SLEEP

Good sleep habits:
- 7-8 hours per night for adults
- Consistent bedtime and wake time
- Cool, dark, quiet sleeping environment
- Limit caffeine (attaya, coffee) after 4pm
- Light dinner — not heavy meal before bed
- Screen time (phone, TV) reduces sleep quality
- Poor sleep worsens: blood pressure, blood sugar, weight, mood

GAMBIAN CONTEXT:
- Dietary changes should be affordable and practical — expensive supplements NOT needed
- Moringa is a "superfood" available free in many compounds — encourage its daily use
- Community support (Bantaba) is a major asset for lifestyle change
- CHW can facilitate group walking, cooking demonstrations, quit smoking groups
- Market days (lumo) are opportunities to buy healthy foods in bulk
- Religious fasting (Ramadan, voluntary fasts) can be leveraged for health — with proper medical guidance
- Traditional foods (chere, supakanja, nyebbeh) are often healthier than imported processed foods
- Climate considerations: exercise timing, hydration, seasonal food availability
