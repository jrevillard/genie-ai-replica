## SORGHUM VARIETY FOR EARLY CYCLE GRAIN

## CENTA LIBERAL

<!-- image -->

## Introduction

Sorghum is an alternative for farming families focused on grain production due to its ability to tolerate prolonged droughts.

Its characteristics make it a viable alternative, even as a substitute for maize, despite the increasing water scarcity in recent years due to the effects of climate change.

It is a hardy crop that can produce in adverse soil conditions (except poorly drained soils), climate, and with limited agronomic management; it adapts from 10 to 1000 m above sea level.

The CENTA Liberal variety, now available to producers, is a proven alternative that addresses the problems of low grain and straw yields.

## Origin

This variety is the result of the introduction of a precocious population from the INTA-INTSORMIL program in Nicaragua in 2006.

It then underwent three individual selection cycles at the San Andrés Experimental Station, followed by validation in producer farms, from which the information about the variety now available to producers was obtained.

## Other Characteristics

In addition to the agronomic characteristics, the CENTA Liberal sorghum also has good nutritional characteristics, such as: a high crude protein content in the grain (11.74%), carbohydrates (76.12%), and low levels of tannins and phenols, making it an excellent variety for human and animal consumption.

## Validation

During the 2011 rainy season, the CENTA Liberal variety was validated in producer farms, where 16 plots were established and compared with the materials typically sown by producers.

As a result of this work, it was demonstrated that the CENTA Liberal variety outperformed the varieties used by producers by 8 kg/hectare.

## Recommended Agronomic Management

## 1- SEED TREATMENT.

Apply a seed treatment before planting with any of the existing seed treatment products on the market for controlling pests such as stalk borer, cutworm, and others.

## 2- PLANTING.

## Monoculture.

Plant in rows with 10 to 12 seeds per meter, with rows spaced 0.70 m apart, resulting in 100,000 to 120,000 plants per hectare. This density requires 20 kg of seed.

## Planting in rows with corn.

Create a furrow in the middle of the two corn rows, with a spacing of 20 cm between plants, leaving 2 or 3 plants per furrow, which results in an average of 10 to 12 plants per square meter.

It is also possible to obtain 87,500 to 105,000 plants per acre, if the corn is planted 80 cm between rows, and approximately 20 lbs of seed per acre can be used.

## FERTILIZATION.

It is recommended to have a soil analysis and fertilize based on it, but generally, it is recommended to apply 4 qq (quintals) of a 16-20-0 fertilizer at planting when planting in rows; and when planting with transplants, do so 8 days after planting with the same amount. A second fertilization, 30 days after planting, with 4 qq of ammonium sulfate or 3 quintals of urea.

## CONTROL OF LEAF PESTS.

The main pests to consider are: corn earworm, aphids, and scale insects, which can be controlled with pyrethroid insecticides.

## HARVEST.

It is carried out 10 to 15 days after the physiological maturity, i.e., between 90 and 95 days after planting.
