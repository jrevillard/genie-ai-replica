WHO PEN Protocol 3 — Cardiovascular Disease Risk Assessment and Management

PROTOCOL 3: CVD RISK ASSESSMENT

1. PURPOSE
- Identify individuals at high risk of heart attack, stroke, and other cardiovascular events
- Use WHO/ISH Cardiovascular Risk Prediction Charts for Sub-Saharan Africa
- Target those who will benefit most from preventive treatment
- Prioritize resources in low-resource settings like The Gambia

2. WHO SHOULD BE ASSESSED
- All adults aged 40+ years
- Younger adults (30+) with: known hypertension, diabetes, smoking, family history of premature CVD
- Anyone with symptoms suggestive of CVD: chest pain on exertion, shortness of breath, leg pain when walking

3. RISK FACTORS ASSESSED
a) Non-modifiable:
   - Age (risk increases with age)
   - Sex (men at higher risk, women catch up after menopause)
   - Family history of premature CVD (father < 55, mother < 65)

b) Modifiable:
   - Smoking status (current smoker = highest risk multiplier)
   - Blood pressure (measured at visit)
   - Diabetes status (known diabetes or fasting glucose >= 126)
   - Total cholesterol (if available — if not, use chart without cholesterol)
   - Physical inactivity
   - Unhealthy diet (high salt, high fat, low fruit/vegetable)
   - Harmful alcohol use
   - Overweight/obesity (BMI > 25)

4. HOW TO USE WHO/ISH RISK CHARTS
- Select chart for: Sub-Saharan Africa region
- Find the panel matching: sex + diabetes status + smoking status
- Cross-reference: age group (rows) × systolic BP (columns)
- If cholesterol available: use chart WITH cholesterol column
- If not available: use chart WITHOUT cholesterol
- Color coding:
  * GREEN (< 10%): LOW risk — lifestyle advice only
  * YELLOW (10-20%): MODERATE risk — lifestyle + consider medication
  * ORANGE (20-30%): HIGH risk — lifestyle + medication recommended
  * RED (30-40%): HIGH risk — aggressive treatment needed
  * DARK RED (> 40%): VERY HIGH risk — immediate treatment + referral

5. MANAGEMENT BY RISK LEVEL

LOW RISK (< 10%):
- Lifestyle counseling every 12 months
- Repeat risk assessment every 3-5 years
- Address individual risk factors if present

MODERATE RISK (10-20%):
- Intensive lifestyle counseling
- Treat hypertension if BP >= 140/90
- Treat diabetes if present
- Consider statin if cholesterol elevated
- Repeat assessment annually

HIGH RISK (20-30%):
- All lifestyle interventions
- Antihypertensive medication (target < 140/90, < 130/80 if diabetic)
- Statin therapy (Simvastatin 20-40mg or Atorvastatin 10-20mg)
- Aspirin 75-100mg daily (if no contraindications)
- Diabetes treatment if present
- Monitor every 3-6 months

VERY HIGH RISK (> 30%) OR EXISTING CVD:
- Maximum lifestyle interventions
- Combination antihypertensive therapy
- Statin therapy (higher dose if tolerated)
- Aspirin 75-100mg daily
- Diabetes treatment optimized
- Consider referral to specialist
- Monitor every 1-3 months

6. STROKE RECOGNITION AND RESPONSE
FAST Assessment:
- Face: ask patient to smile. Does one side droop?
- Arms: ask to raise both arms. Does one drift down?
- Speech: ask to repeat a simple sentence. Is speech slurred or strange?
- Time: if ANY of these signs → call 199 IMMEDIATELY

Additional stroke signs: sudden severe headache, sudden confusion, sudden vision loss in one or both eyes, sudden difficulty walking or loss of balance

POST-STROKE CARE:
- Rehabilitation starts in hospital
- Long-term: aspirin, statin, BP medication (ACEI preferred)
- Target BP < 140/90 post-stroke
- Monitor for depression (common after stroke)
- Prevent recurrence: medication adherence + lifestyle changes

7. HEART ATTACK RECOGNITION AND RESPONSE
Symptoms: chest pain/pressure/tightness (may radiate to left arm, jaw, back), sweating, nausea, shortness of breath, feeling of doom
- Some patients (especially women, diabetics, elderly) may have ATYPICAL symptoms: just nausea, fatigue, or upper back pain

IMMEDIATE ACTION:
- Call 199 or go to nearest hospital (EFSTH) IMMEDIATELY
- Chew one Aspirin 300mg if available (not swallow — chew for faster absorption)
- Sit upright, stay calm
- Do NOT lie flat
- Do NOT drive yourself — have someone drive you or wait for ambulance

8. PERIPHERAL VASCULAR DISEASE
- Symptoms: leg pain when walking (claudication) that improves with rest
- Advanced: pain at rest, non-healing wounds on feet/legs, colour changes
- Risk factors: same as CVD (smoking is the strongest)
- Management: stop smoking (critical), walking exercise program, aspirin, statin
- Referral: if rest pain, non-healing ulcers, or gangrene

9. PREVENTION STRATEGIES FOR THE GAMBIAN CONTEXT
Diet:
- Reduce salt: half Maggi cube, less dried/salted fish, rinse preserved foods
- Reduce fat: less palm oil in cooking, grill or steam instead of fry
- Increase fish: fresh fish 2-3 times per week (omega-3 protection)
- Increase vegetables: moringa, okra, bitter leaf, sweet potato leaves
- Whole grains: millet (chere), fonio over white rice
- Fruits: mango, papaya, orange, banana, baobab (bouye)
- Healthy drinks: bissap (hibiscus), ginger water, baobab juice

Exercise:
- Walking to market, farming, household chores all count
- Aim for 30 minutes of brisk activity most days
- Even 10-minute walks 3 times daily is beneficial
- Avoid heavy exertion in peak heat (noon to 3pm)

Smoking:
- Tobacco use is the single most modifiable CVD risk factor
- Quitting at any age reduces risk significantly
- After 1 year smoke-free: CVD risk drops by 50%
- After 5 years: stroke risk drops to near non-smoker level
- Support: 5As approach (Ask, Advise, Assess, Assist, Arrange follow-up)

10. REFERRAL CRITERIA
- 10-year CVD risk > 30%
- Existing CVD (previous heart attack, stroke, TIA)
- BP not controlled on 3 medications
- Suspected familial hypercholesterolemia
- Young patient (< 40) with CVD event
- Chest pain on exertion (needs cardiac evaluation)

GAMBIAN CONTEXT:
- WHO/ISH charts for AFR-D region (Sub-Saharan Africa, high adult mortality)
- Cholesterol testing limited to regional hospitals — use non-laboratory charts at health post level
- Aspirin and Simvastatin available on essential medicines list
- EFSTH has ECG and basic cardiac care capability
- CVD is the leading cause of death globally and rising rapidly in The Gambia due to urbanization and dietary changes
- Traditional cardiovascular protective factors in Gambian diet: fish consumption, physical labor, community social support
