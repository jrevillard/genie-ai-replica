## Boron Deficiency in Corn Cultivation

<!-- image -->

<!-- image -->

Boron is an essential micronutrient that plays a fundamental role in the normal development of plant tissues.

<!-- image -->

Boron (B) is one of the 8 essential micronutrients for plant growth and development, and it plays a crucial role in the normal development of reproductive tissues (development of silks, pollen tube growth, pollen germination, pollination, and fruit set), as well as in protein synthesis and sugar transport. It also participates in the formation and structural stability of cell walls, as well as in the apical growth of stems and roots. Read more at: Critical Functions of Boron in Crops.

Globally, boron deficiency has been recognized as the second most important micronutrient deficiency in crops, after zinc. In Mexico, boron levels in more than 70% of agricultural soils are classified as moderately low to very low, so crops show a high response to boron application in the fertilization program.

<!-- image -->

<!-- image -->

Boron requirements can vary depending on the type of plant, but the range for boron concentration in the soil solution between deficiency and toxicity is lower than for other nutrients. It has been previously reported that corn has a relatively low boron requirement compared to other cereals, however, boron is one of the micronutrients that causes more frequent deficiencies in crops like corn, which can limit the expression of the potential of current hybrids.

In corn cultivation, boron deficiency can cause the pollen tube to not function properly, which makes the embryo of the kernel not be pollinated (wrinkled kernels). Also, boron deficiency causes short and bent ears, sterile stalks, poor grain development, elongated and watery streaks that then turn white on newly formed leaves, and finally, dead growth points.

Because many other stresses can cause similar symptoms, it is important to analyze boron content in corn plant and soil samples to confirm a boron deficiency. Correct and timely diagnosis will help prevent future deficiencies in the crop, which will favor yield and grain quality.

Source: IPNI. Several factors affect boron uptake by plants, including soil type (texture, pH), organic matter content (it is the main source of boron in the soil, where boron is available as organic matter decomposes), boron concentration, moisture (high rainfall or excessive watering), and plant species. The interaction of boron with other nutrients (Nitrogen, Phosphorus, Potassium, Calcium, Aluminum, and Zinc) can be synergistic or antagonistic, which can influence the availability of boron to plants. Boron availability decreases as the soil dries due to limited rainfall or watering, due to the decrease in boron mobility in the soil by mass flow to plant roots. Boron is absorbed by plants in the form of boric acid, which is easily leached in soils with high rainfall or watering. Boron is relatively immobile in a corn plant, and its availability is essential throughout the growth cycle, particularly during flowering, pollination, and grain filling.
