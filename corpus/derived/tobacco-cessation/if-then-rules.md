# Tobacco cessation — if-then rules

Decision rules the chatbot can pattern-match against to give specific,
non-generic guidance to people who smoke or are quitting. Derived from
the WHO *Be He@lthy Be Mobile — mTobaccoCessation handbook*, the
QuitNowTXT message library, the *Gambia cessation clinical guidelines*
(2016), and WHO *FCTC Article 14* implementation guidelines.

Pharmacotherapy specifics (drug names, doses, contraindications) are
intentionally kept high-level. Specific prescribing decisions belong
with a clinician, not the chatbot.

---

## Stage of change — what to recommend

| If the person says … | Then … |
|---|---|
| "I don't want to quit." | Don't push. Acknowledge respectfully. Offer one piece of information (one health fact, one money fact, or both) and leave the door open. "If anything changes, I'm here." |
| "I might quit, but not now." | Validate. Offer to explore one motivator: financial savings, children, breathing, fitness, a specific health concern they raised. Ask if they'd like to learn about reducing harm in the meantime. |
| "I want to quit within the next month." | Walk through Quit Day planning: pick a date, identify triggers, plan substitutes, tell a supporter, remove tobacco from home/car/bag, list reasons. |
| "I want to quit right now." | Quit Day is today. Run the Quit Day morning script (see Chat 2 example): water, breathing, distraction, get rid of tobacco. Set up daily check-ins. |
| "I quit last week" / "I quit yesterday" | Acknowledge the milestone. Ask how they're feeling. Run withdrawal triage (see below). |
| "I slipped, I had one cigarette." | Reframe slip vs. relapse. Identify the trigger. Encourage immediate re-commitment without shame. Don't downplay it, don't catastrophize it. |
| "I started smoking again, fully." | Validate the difficulty. Don't lecture. Ask what happened. When appropriate, offer to plan another Quit Day. |

## Nicotine dependence — interpreting Q-1 of the Fagerström screen

The single best dependence indicator is **how soon after waking** the
person has their first cigarette.

| Time to first cigarette after waking | Dependence level | Implications |
|---|---|---|
| Within 5 minutes | Very high | Strongly recommend pharmacotherapy. Withdrawal will be intense. NRT alone may be insufficient — flag for clinician discussion of bupropion or varenicline. |
| 6-30 minutes | High | Pharmacotherapy strongly recommended. NRT (combination patch + gum) is usually adequate. |
| 31-60 minutes | Moderate | Pharmacotherapy recommended, particularly for those with prior failed attempts. |
| After 60 minutes | Low | Many can succeed with behavioural support alone; offer NRT as an option, not a default. |

## Withdrawal symptom triage (first 1-2 weeks post-quit)

| If the person reports … | Then … |
|---|---|
| intense cravings, multiple per day | Normal at this stage. Walk through the 5-D's (Delay, Deep breath, Drink water, Distract, Discuss). Recommend NRT if not already using. |
| irritability, mood swings | Normal, peaks around day 2-4. Recommend telling household members "this is the medicine working, not me", short walks, sleep priority. |
| can't sleep, restless | Common in first 1-2 weeks. Avoid caffeine after midday. Try a wind-down routine and limit screens before bed. Usually self-resolves. |
| headaches | Common. Drink water, paracetamol if needed, usually resolves within days. |
| constipation | Common, often missed. Recommend more water, fibre, walking. Self-resolves over 2-3 weeks. |
| weight gain in first weeks | Validate it's normal (most people gain 2-5 kg). Healthy snacks, water before meals, more walking. The cardiovascular gain outweighs the weight. Discourage stopping the quit attempt to "lose weight first". |
| extreme low mood, hopelessness, suicidal thoughts | **Escalate.** Recommend they speak to a health worker or mental-health professional **today**. Some people with prior depression have severe mood drops after quitting and may need temporary support or medication adjustment. Do not dismiss. |
| chest pain or severe shortness of breath | Not typical of withdrawal. Recommend they seek medical evaluation — this may be an unrelated cardiac issue surfacing. |

## Pharmacotherapy — when to suggest

| If the person … | Then … |
|---|---|
| smokes ≤ 10/day, no prior quit attempts | Behavioural support first; NRT optional. |
| smokes > 10/day | NRT or other pharmacotherapy is strongly recommended. |
| has tried to quit before without medication and relapsed | Strongly recommend medication this time. |
| has had a heart attack within the past 2 weeks | NRT requires clinician judgment. Recommend they discuss with their cardiologist. |
| is pregnant or breastfeeding | Recommend non-pharmacological support first; any medication only on clinical advice. The risk of continuing to smoke during pregnancy is generally higher than the risk of NRT, but the decision must come from a clinician. |
| has uncontrolled high blood pressure | Recommend they get the BP controlled and discuss NRT with their clinician. Smoking is still the bigger risk. |
| asks about e-cigarettes/vaping | Be honest: less harmful short-term than cigarettes but not harmless, not a first-line recommendation, keeps nicotine addiction alive. Use only on specific clinician advice after other options. |

## Trigger management

| Trigger pattern | Substitute / strategy |
|---|---|
| First cigarette of the morning | Change the morning ritual: shower first, drink water before tea, eat breakfast before going outside, do 5 push-ups, anything to break the script. |
| Cigarette with morning tea/coffee | Switch beverage temporarily (cold water, juice). After 2-4 weeks, reintroduce the original beverage in a different setting. |
| Cigarette after meals | Stand up and leave the table immediately. Brush teeth. Go for a 5-minute walk. |
| Cigarette during work breaks | Take the break, but in a different physical location (away from the smoking area). Bring water or a snack. Call a friend instead. |
| Cigarette when stressed | Practise the 4-7-8 breath (in 4, hold 7, out 8). Walk for 10 minutes. Text a supporter. Stress will not be made worse by not smoking — it will improve. |
| Cigarette when drinking alcohol | For the first 1-3 months: reduce or avoid alcohol, or only drink in places without smokers. Cravings are the strongest while drinking. |
| Cigarette around a smoking friend / family member | Tell them you've quit and ask them not to offer or smoke around you for now. If they won't respect that, limit time together for a few months. |
| Cigarette in the car | Remove the ashtray and lighter. Keep gum or sunflower seeds in the cup-holder. Sing along to music. Plan longer drives in smaller chunks with breaks. |

## Special populations

| If the person is … | Then … |
|---|---|
| pregnant | Strongly recommend quitting immediately. Smoking in pregnancy causes low birth weight, premature birth, stillbirth, SIDS. Non-pharmacological support is first-line; any medication only on clinician advice. |
| breastfeeding | Strongly recommend quitting. Nicotine passes into breastmilk. If absolutely unable to quit, recommend smoking immediately after a feed (not before) to minimise infant exposure. |
| a teenager | Use peer-relevant framing (skin, sports performance, breath/smell, money, vaping risks). Do not lecture. Offer to connect them with a youth-friendly service. |
| living with someone who smokes indoors | Address the second-hand smoke issue. Recommend they have the "outside only, please" conversation. Their quit attempt will be much harder otherwise. |
| a hookah/shisha user only | Treat as a tobacco user. Many hookah users don't think of themselves as smokers and underestimate the risk. Be specific about session-equivalent exposure (≈100 cigarettes per typical session). |
| a smokeless tobacco user (chewing, snus, etc.) | Same advice as cigarette cessation. Specific cancer risks: mouth, throat, pancreas. Encourage health-worker check of mouth and throat. |
| a recovering addict from another substance | Acknowledge they already have skills (relapse prevention, support networks). Connect quitting tobacco to those existing skills rather than framing it as a separate fight. Discuss any cross-trigger concerns with their addiction counsellor. |

## Relapse-prevention rules

| If it is … | Then … |
|---|---|
| Quit Day | Daily check-in. Validate the milestone. Run the morning script. |
| Day 2-7 | Daily check-in. Withdrawal triage as needed. Reinforce that this is the hardest week and it gets easier from here. |
| Week 2-4 | Check in every few days. Cravings are still common; focus on trigger management. Begin weighting the long-term framing ("you're past the worst part"). |
| Month 2-3 | Weekly check-in. Most relapses happen in this window. Probe specifically for social and alcohol triggers, life events. |
| Month 4-6 | Monthly check-in. Acknowledge milestones at 3 months and 6 months. |
| Month 6+ | Less frequent. Frame as "ex-smoker." Offer to re-engage if a stressful life event arises (loss, job change, illness). |

## Things to never do in the conversation

- Never moralise or shame someone for smoking, slipping, or relapsing.
- Never claim a specific cigarette type, brand, or alternative ("light",
  "natural", e-cigarette, shisha) is safe. None are.
- Never recommend a specific dose of pharmacotherapy — that is a
  clinician's call.
- Never minimise withdrawal symptoms. Acknowledge them as the price of
  healing.
- Never dismiss reports of severe low mood or suicidal thoughts after
  quitting — escalate to clinical support.
- Never give up on someone who has relapsed multiple times. Most
  successful ex-smokers needed several attempts.
