## How to Improve Grain Storage

The drier the grain, the better. Ideally, it should have a moisture content below 13%, as this prevents fungal growth.

All storage silos should be enclosed and in a shaded area to prevent the walls and grain from overheating.

The reduction of oxygen and the accumulation of CO2 needed to stop insects and fungi can be achieved by:

- 1) Placing lit candles in a dish on top of the grain, then sealing the silo. The candles generate up to 4% CO2 before burning out. This may be sufficient if the grain is already dry.
- 2) Also, placing damp grains in a plastic bottle converts oxygen into CO2, with the advantage that more oxygen is consumed, creating better protection for the grain throughout the storage period.

## Various Recommendations

The best practice is to fill the granary completely, and after opening it, to empty it as soon as possible. Avoid frequent openings. Each time you open it, make sure to seal it properly.

Before filling the silo, ensure it is clean and dry. If it is damp, expose it to the sun. A layer of lime at the bottom helps to control any remaining fungi.

## Why is it important to properly seal the metal silo?

Whether using hydrogen phosphide tablets or hermetic storage without tablets, it is important that gases do not escape from the granary, and that moisture and oxygen do not enter.

Proper sealing of the silo significantly reduces the risk of damage from insects and fungi during storage. Oxygen accelerates the deterioration of the grain, especially if it contains live pests or is not properly dried.

In the case of seeds, a good sealing of the granary maintains the germination power during storage, even when the humidity and temperature of the environment are high.

A grain insect stored can consume up to 10 milliliters of oxygen during its life; and if the grain is dry, it also needs it to obtain water. Without water, the insect dies dehydrated.

## How to achieve a good seal?

The quality of the silo or granary's welds determines whether it can be used for hermetic storage.

Metal silos of the 'p oscosecha' type are designed to be properly sealed.

A good seal cannot be achieved with poorly maintained granaries or those with loose lids or seams without tin. In this case, the use of tablets initially controls insects, but moisture will enter the granary, and over time, fungi will damage the grain.

To properly seal the lids, rubber strips from tires or wide adhesive tape are used.

If the seam of a sheet or its lid has grooves, they should be filled with silicone or putty to prevent gas leakage.

## How to tell if the silo is properly sealed?

## Before filling the silo, you can:

Look inside for any light leaks.

Empty a bucket of water into the silo and observe if it comes out from the inside, and from where (it will need to be thoroughly dried afterwards). Leaks can be sealed with tin, silicone, tar, "sealing tape," or putty.

## Once filled and sealed:

When using tablets, if you smell the tablets, it means there is a leak.
