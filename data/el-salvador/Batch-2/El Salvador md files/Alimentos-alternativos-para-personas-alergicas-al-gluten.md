## Alternative foods for people with gluten allergies

<!-- image -->

Ing. Jared López / Food Technology Laboratory Technician

The global market for gluten-free foods has seen significant growth in recent years, largely due to the high demand from people with celiac disease.

Gluten is a group of proteins found in grains such as wheat, barley, and rye. It is primarily composed of two classes of proteins: gliadin (a prolamine) and glutenin (a glutelin). The fraction that causes celiac disease belongs to the prolamine group.

Gluten is not nutritionally important, but it is from a technological perspective; in baking, gluten acts as a binder, it is sticky, flexible, and holds ingredients together, and is responsible for the lightness, elasticity, and crust of bread. That is why gluten is added to many foods in the food industry, for example, in the production of pasta, pizza, cakes, cookies, and snacks.

autoimmune condition that damages the lining of the small intestine. When a person with this condition consumes gluten-containing foods, their immune system reacts, causing damage to the villi in the small intestine. Due to this damage, the villi are unable to absorb nutrients properly, leading to stomach pain, fatigue, weight loss, bloating, diarrhea, gas, headaches, anemia, and other health complications. Therefore, people affected by this condition must avoid consuming gluten.

The condition can manifest at any time in life, from infancy to old age, and is more common in women.

Fortunately, there are other grains that do not contain gluten, such as corn, rice, sorghum, and beans. These can be used to make a variety of foods, such as bread, cookies, and snacks.

CENTA, through the Food Technology Laboratory, makes available to producers, entrepreneurs, and entrepreneurs a variety of technologies for processing and preserving fruits, basic grains, roots, and tubers, which can be used to produce a variety of bakery and snack products, such as tortillas, chips, salty and sweet cookies, sticks, croutons, pasta, and beverages.

Some of the raw materials used in the development of these gluten-free alternative foods include flours made from banana, sorghum, rice, beans, yuca, malanga, sweet potato, cassava, and corn. These foods are a good source of fiber, protein, vitamins, and minerals.
