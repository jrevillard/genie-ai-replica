Once the husk of the cacao bean is transformed into flour, it was subjected to analysis to determine its nutritional, sensory, microbiological, and functional quality characteristics. The results concluded that:

- It is an excellent source of protein, fiber, and minerals such as phosphorus and calcium, making it ideal for the development and evaluation of functional foods.
- Sensory evaluation determined that the cookie made with 90% wheat flour and 10% cacao husk flour was the most preferred.
- The flour is safe; it was found to be free of pathogenic bacteria such as Salmonella and coliforms; molds and yeasts, and the count of mesophilic bacteria did not exceed the maximum limit established by the Salvadoran Standard for Wheat Flour in 2001.
- The high water absorption capacity and swelling capacity of the flour make it functionally suitable for inclusion in the food industry, with a particular emphasis on the meat industry.
- The cost analysis of the flour production revealed a price of USD $1.75/lb.

In addition to the production and characterization of cacao husk flour, prototypes of simple and quick-to-prepare foods were developed, using maize, rice, panela (un type of sugar), fruits, edible leaves, and other ingredients. Among the products, horchata (a traditional drink), smoothies, and other items stand out.

The products and equipment are accessible, using locally sourced and popular ingredients such as: can pasties, empanadas, nachos, a variety of sweet and savory cookies, cold and hot beverages, and atoles (a traditional drink).

## PRODUCTION OF CACAO HUSK FLOUR

(Theobroma cacao)

In the production and processing of cacao, large volumes of pulp and husks are generated, creating serious environmental problems. The husk is considered a breeding ground for Phytophthora spp, the main cause of economic losses in the cacao industry.

The food industry, pursuing sustainable development, is increasingly applying alternatives to reduce its environmental impact, with the valorization and utilization of by-products.

In July 2019, at the Food Technology Laboratory, cacao husk flour was produced from the Trinitario cacao bean, with the aim of finding a technological alternative to utilize this agroindustrial by-product and incorporate it into the development of foods.
