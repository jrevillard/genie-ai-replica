## INTRODUCTION TO SORGHUM AND CORN MILLING

## Importance in nutrition and food security

Sorghum is a valuable grain, often used as a substitute for milk, and is also used as a food source for people who are ill or recovering from illness. The fact that sorghum helps to improve the growth of livestock is another reason why it is considered a nutritious and valuable crop.

In the context of reducing malnutrition, sorghum is a beneficial grain because it is a very nutritious grain that grows in unfavorable conditions. This is why it is grown in areas where other crops do not thrive, and where agricultural and overall development opportunities are limited.

## About the development of sorghum varieties at CENTA

Convinced of the benefits of sorghum, CENTA conducts research, training, and publication activities on sorghum, including programs for selecting and improving varieties, research on nutritional aspects, and the development of food products. New sorghum varieties have been introduced in Central America, and efforts have been made to promote the cultivation and use of sorghum. CENTA has released improved sorghum varieties, such as RCV, Soberano, and others. Training is provided on transforming sorghum into food products for human consumption, and tests have been conducted on milling, as described in this manual.

The non-profit organization "COMPATIBLE TECHNOLOGY INTERNATIONAL" from Minnesota, USA, worked on designing and proposing appropriate technology, meaning basic technology that helps to improve the quality of life in rural areas of countries in the southern hemisphere. They have threshers, granaries, water purification systems, etc. CTI mills are designed to process grains on a small scale, at a low cost. Currently, CTI is part of Bountifield International, an organization that primarily works in Africa.

## Characteristics and quality of the grain

Sorghum has been cultivated for many years in countries such as India and China, and in many other countries in Africa. It is not known when the plant was first introduced to the Western Hemisphere. The first seeds were likely brought to the Western Hemisphere on slave ships from Africa. The plant thrives in tropical conditions, with plenty of sunlight and limited water and fertilizer. This is why it is often grown as a co-crop or in rotation, meaning it is planted alongside or after the main crop, as it can utilize the nutrients that the main crop did not fully utilize. For the same reason, sorghum is often neglected, and not given proper agronomic care, and is therefore poorly paid at the end, despite its high nutritional value.

## Grain Cleaning

The corn is cleaned well and can be processed and stored without further cleaning when it is threshed using threshing machines with blowers. However, if the corn has been trampled in the field without a cover, it may contain stones, lumps, plant material, dust, and other undesirable materials.

There are equipment for cleaning grains, which typically consist of a screen that removes large debris and allows the grain to pass through, and a second screen with smaller holes that allows sand, dust, and small insects to fall through. Air is also used to blow away dust and debris.

To produce flour, it is preferable not to buy grain that has been contaminated, and if that is not an option, the grain must be cleaned carefully with screens, air, and, as a last resort, water, before it can be milled.

Washing grain in water can be done at home in a trough. The debris and hollow grains float to the surface, and the soil, stones, and other heavy materials sink to the bottom and can be separated. Often, the washing process is repeated until the water runs clear.

After washing, the grain must be dried to reduce the moisture quickly, otherwise, mold will attack the grain or ruin the flour due to the high moisture content.

## Grain Storage

To ensure the availability of raw materials when the harvest is only once a year, the grain is stored in granaries or silos until it is consumed. To preserve the quality as much as possible, the grain must be clean and dry (below 12%) and the granaries must be sealed hermetically. Before sealing the granary, aluminum phosphate tablets or another insecticide are added, it is sealed, and it is left to fumigate for three days if using aluminum phosphate. Corn is very susceptible to attack by insects, which normally come with the grain, so the fumigation must be effective. If insects are detected during an inspection, another fumigation should be carried out.

## Initial grinding

Corn kernels are round and small, which makes them easy to enter the grinding stones quickly, which receive a lot of product. Because of this, the motor works harder in the first pass.

Therefore, in the first pass, it is recommended to limit the amount of grain entering the mill by feeding the grain gradually, either manually or by placing a plate with a hole on top of the grinding stone. The thin grinding stone should be used, and the grinding stones should not be tightened too much.

When filling the hopper with grain and the grinding stones are slightly tightened, the grinding stones will jam quickly in the first pass if nothing is done to prevent it. If the grinding stones jam, the motor may overheat, the belt may slip, or the grinding stone may deform.

Start the motor without any grain and observe if the grinding stones are turning normally. If everything is going well, you can fill the hopper. Then, you can tighten it slightly with your fingers, always checking that the motor is not working too hard. This can be detected by ear, and by checking that the part with the spout next to the grinding stones does not move.

Depending on the amount of product entering, the grain will either be coarsely ground or ground into a coarse flour. It is preferable to feed little and not tighten too much to avoid problems in the first pass. The noise level is high, so it is more necessary to use earplugs.

To determine the position of the nut that tightens the grinding stones, it is helpful to mark a notch with nail polish and record or remember the correct position at each step. This way, next time you only need to put the nut in the same position as before, without having to search much.

It is preferable for the grain to be dry, because wet grain sticks more to the grinding stones and causes jamming more easily.

## 2. Reducing particle size

With the ground grain, it is less likely that the grinding stones will jam, but always be careful not to tighten them too much. If you need to reduce the amount of material entering the grinding stones, you can.

In the second pass, the flour comes out a little warm, and the temperature of the flour is a good indicator of whether the grinding stones are tight enough or too tight. The temperature can increase by 20-30°C in the first three passes. If the flour feels too hot to touch, the grinding stones are definitely too tight.

When tightening the nut with your fingers while the mill is running with product, you can feel how tight it is working. Be very careful when using a wrench to tighten it more, as this can cause jamming or wear on the grinding stones.

## 3. Tuning the flour

Depending on the tightness and feeding given in the first passes and the hardness of the grain, the flour may be acceptable after three passes for making tortillas and other recipes that do not require very fine flour.

To find the right tightness, tighten without product until the grinding stones touch each other and you can turn them almost without force, and then tighten another quarter turn. When passing the product, adjust slightly according to the temperature of the flour that comes out and observe.

## 4 Final refining

Depending on how the flour came out in the previous pass and the use you will give it, you can refine it one or two more times. Be careful if the flour gets too hot, because this generates condensation of steam in some colder parts of the mill, and it can cause the flour to stick together, forming balls or flakes that then harden. It is better to let the flour cool down a bit between passes. Do not cover it. A partially open cover for the grinding stones also helps.

Fine flour tends to compact and stop flowing. To prevent this, you need to remove the flour from the hopper. A large grinding stone piece attached to a thin one can remove the flour more easily. Always be careful not to tighten the grinding stones too much.

If the grain is of a hard type, the flour may be slightly coarse after four passes. You will need to refine it more.

The finished flour should be left to cool before packaging to prevent condensation. Paper bags will absorb some water, while plastic bags will not. Keep the flour in a clean place and protect it from insects or other forms of contamination before packaging.

There are many ways to combine milling and sieving. For example:

In the first example, the initial sieving aims to separate large pieces of bran and husk before grinding. The second sieving aims to separate the fiber and coarse particles. Depending on the mesh size of the final sieve, the flour can be sifted twice during milling.

Since sieves with 30 and 40 mesh are relatively open, they can be sifted by hand or with a simple sieve.

In the second example, a much finer sieve is used, so the sifted flour will be fine enough for use. The coarse flour and bran are sifted again during milling and passed through the same sieve, resulting in a slightly darker and less fine flour. The bran remains on the sieve and is removed.

An 80-mesh sieve is quite fine, so the sieve needs a strong agitation, such as with brushes, to ensure the flour passes quickly.

Because the fine flour has been separated, the sifting process does not cause problems in the hopper. It is not necessary to monitor it, as the flour does not clog and flows easily.

## 8. Tempering

To obtain purer bran, it is possible to pre-soak the sorghum grain. This process is called tempering. After cleaning the grain, a sufficient amount of water is added to bring the moisture content to approximately 15%.

Formula: Amount of water to add = (Initial grain weight x 15% initial moisture) / 100

It is necessary to mix the added water evenly throughout the grain. Then, the grain should rest for a couple of hours before milling.

Moisture makes the husk more resistant, resulting in larger pieces, which allows for better separation from the flour. When milling tempered grain, it is important to avoid pressing the discs too hard, as the moisture makes the material stickier, and the discs are more likely to jam.

Always, with the aim of obtaining pure bran and minimizing fiber in the flour, it is better to press the discs less and sift more.

When using sieves, it is possible to obtain well-refined flours and others with more fiber, allowing for different qualities to suit customer preferences. This also allows for a reasonable yield, to avoid ending up with too much bran: The expected bran percentage will be between 10 and 20 percent, with 15 percent being the fraction of husk in the grain.

To prevent the risk of mold growth in tempered grain flour, it should be ventilated to allow it to dry during the process and then cooled before packaging. In rainy weather, it is not recommended to wet the grain, as it is difficult for it to dry sufficiently during milling, and mold can develop.

## 9. Fat Mixtures

People who make powdered horchata using CTI mills do so in a special way to prevent the discs from jamming. Seeds of morro, ayote, mani, ajonjolí, and cacao have a high fat content, which makes the flour sticky, causing the discs to jam.

In summary, the key points to consider are:

- Do not put in too many fatty seeds, no more than an equal amount with the cereal.
- Store in sealed containers to prevent re-wetting.
- Cook over low heat, enough to reduce moisture and develop even color and flavor, throughout the seed (preferably in a toaster, oven, comal, or tortilla pan)
- First coarse grinding of the mixed seeds and cereal flour.
- Avoid overheating the horchata. Ventilate and, if necessary, cool before sifting.
- When the horchata is somewhat fine, ensure that it does not clog the sifter: Use a hand-held tool, a stick, or a brush to help it flow.

As with any food processing, grinding flour requires hygienic conditions to ensure the quality and safety (freedom from contamination) of the product.

Since the equipment is small and easily disassembled, cleaning can be done quickly and easily. A brush, thin sticks, compressed air, or blowing and other cleaning tools are used. WATER SHOULD NOT BE USED TO CLEAN THE MILL because water can damage the bearings or drip onto the motor and wet it. Only the discs that have been disassembled can be wet to soften the crust. They must be dried immediately to prevent oxidation (mold formation).

For cleaning and removing all residue, the front plate and disc are removed, and the screen is taken out. See the CTI manual with photos). Do not disassemble the shaft for cleaning in a motor mill.

## REMEMBER TO DISCONNECT THE MOTOR FROM THE PLUG OR THE THERMOSTAT BEFORE DISASSEMBLING THE MILL

Cleaning should be done after use, not before. If not, the equipment will be contaminated with insects such as weevils, flour beetles; cockroaches; ants, and even mice, which will come to feed on the residue, leaving eggs, excrement, hair, etc. No matter how thorough the cleaning is, the product will always be contaminated, and it will not have the expected shelf life.

In addition to the equipment, the place and surroundings must be kept clean to ensure a quality product and avoid disappointing customers.

FINAL RECOMMENDATIONS FOR ADJUSTING THE GRINDING PROCESS

If the flour is coarse, it is re-ground in the mill. If you want to reduce the fiber content, pass it through a sifter to remove pieces of bran and husk.

Reducing the moisture content of flour is more difficult than in grain. If the grain is too moist, the best approach is to dry it in the sun before grinding. Grinding will slightly dry the flour by heating between the discs, as long as the ambient air is dry, there is ventilation, and the falling flour is exposed to the air. If the moisture content of the flour is above 15%, mold will grow on it, and it will appear after a few days or weeks. The flour should be consumed as soon as possible, preferably if the ambient temperature is high.

A slightly damp flour should not be packed hot, and certainly not in plastic bags; this can cause condensation. Leaving the bag open while it cools reduces the risk somewhat, but there will still be some moisture.
