## Genetic Control of Sorghum Grain Color

## INTRODUCTION

There is currently a great interest in using sorghum grain as a source of energy in the production of food for human and/or animal consumption, replacing wheat in bread making and yellow corn in the production of concentrated foods; for this purpose, it is necessary to know the genetic control of grain color to improve its quality.

The objective of this information is to briefly describe the main genetic characteristics, currently known and that objectively affect the appearance of sorghum grain.

The color of the pericarp and the glume of the flower affect the color of the food products made from the grain. At least ten pairs of genes have been identified so far that affect the color of sorghum grain. The color of the pericarp is mainly determined by the genes: R\_, Y\_, I\_, S\_, B 1 \_, B 2 \_. The genes P\_, Q\_, and Tp\_ also affect the expression of this basic set.

## SORGHUM GRAIN:

Sorghum grain (Fig. 1) is simply the fertilized and mature ovary, which essentially consists of an embryo, endosperm, and the pericarp. The pericarp, in which the genes that control the color are concentrated, defines the color of the grain.

## III - MESOCARP

The thickness of the mesocarp layer is determined by the genes Z\_ and zz (Fig. 10). The dominant gene Z\_ results in a thin pericarp and a waxy grain. The thin pericarp is translucent, allowing the color of the testa and the endosperm to affect the appearance of the grain. The homozygous recessive (zz) exhibits a thick pericarp with a waxy appearance due to the higher concentration of starches.

## IV - PLANT COLOR

The color of the plant is controlled by the genes PP and QQ, where P\_Q\_ for purple, P\_ qq for red, pp Q\_ for dark cinnamon, and pp qq for light cinnamon color of the plant. The color of the glume and the plant is generally associated with these gene pairs (Fig. 11, 12, 13 and 14). The genes P\_Q\_ affect the maternal tissue of the grain and modify the effects of other direct color factors.

## V. IMPORTANT INTERACTIONS

- Crossing a white grain with another white grain can result in F1 grains that are white, beige, red, or purple, depending on whether the R\_yy, rr yy, B 1 \_B 2 \_, S\_, I \_, tp tp genes interact.
- A white grain can be affected by the presence of the Z\_ gene. When dominant, the endosperm is thin, and the grain has a pearl-like appearance. However, if homozygous recessive, the endosperm is thick, and the grain has a white, chalky appearance.
- Crossing a white grain (R\_yy) with a pigmented testa (B 1 \_B 2 \_), thick endosperm (zz zz), with another yellow grain (rr Y\_), with a stalk (S\_), intensifier (I \_), and testa (tp tp), will result in F1 grains that are purple.
- The presence of B 1 \_B 2 \_ genes indicates the presence of tannins in the grain testa, which can increase towards the pericarp if they interact with the I \_, S\_, zz zz, and tp tp genes.
- The interaction of the I \_ gene with genes for red or yellow color intensifies the color, but not with white grains.
- The P\_ Q\_ genes, which affect plant color, are generally correlated with glume color, but there are plants with glumes of a different color than the plant itself.

This means that there are inter-allelic interactions that change the basic expression of grain color, and that explain why crossing white grains by white grains results in beige or purple grains. Crossing red grains results in purple grains, etc…
